How to Get There

From Little Rock (mi) Get on I-630 E. Follow I-40 W to AR-363 S in Pottsville. Take exit 88 from I-40 W. Take AR-247 N to State Hwy 155 in Dardanelle Township

Overview

Mount Nebo State Park sits atop a flat-topped mountain that rises about 1,350 feet above the valley near Dardanelle, with views of Lake Dardanelle, the Arkansas River and the surrounding ridges. The mountain was a summer retreat from the late nineteenth century and became a state park in 1928, with much of its early development carried out by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s. The CCC-built pavilion and cabins are on the National Register of Historic Places. People visit for the overlooks, the Rim Trail, rustic cabins, camping, mountain biking and hang gliding launches.

Access

The park is on the mountaintop about 5 miles west of Dardanelle, reached by Highway 155 from the Dardanelle area. The road climbs steeply with sharp turns, so drive slowly and check ahead if you are pulling a trailer or driving a large RV. Russellville is about 8 to 13 miles away, and Little Rock is roughly 1.5 hours to the east along I-40. Almost all visitors drive.

Seasons

The summit is cooler than the river valley in summer, making it a popular place to escape the heat, and the park swimming pool is open in the warm months. Spring brings wildflowers and greening forests, and fall brings colorful hardwoods and clear views. Winter can bring ice or snow to the mountain, and the road may be slick or closed temporarily. Hang gliding is most active when winds are favorable, and the visitor center has extended hours on some summer weekends.

Things to Do

Enjoy Panoramic Views from the Mountaintop

Mount Nebo State Park sits atop a flat-topped mountain rising more than 1,300 feet above the Arkansas River Valley. Visitors can enjoy spectacular overlooks that provide sweeping views of the surrounding valleys, Lake Dardanelle, and distant mountain ranges, making the park one of Arkansas's premier scenic destinations.

Explore Historic CCC Structures

The park contains numerous structures built by the Civilian Conservation Corps during the 1930s, including cabins, pavilions, trails, and stonework that contribute to the park's historic character. Visitors can learn about the CCC's role in developing Arkansas's state park system.

Watch Hang Gliders and Paragliders

Mount Nebo is one of the most popular locations in Arkansas for hang gliding and paragliding. The mountain's steep cliffs and favorable wind conditions often allow visitors to watch pilots launch from scenic overlooks and soar above the valley below.

Top Trails

Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 3.6 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal with some rolling terrain
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

The park's signature trail circles the mountain's edge, providing continuous views of the Arkansas River Valley, sandstone bluffs, forests, and overlooks. It is widely considered one of the most scenic hikes in Arkansas.

Bench Trail

Length: Approximately 3.0 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet descent/ascent
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This historic trail descends the mountain through forests and rocky terrain, connecting the summit to the valley below while offering impressive views and a glimpse into the rugged landscape surrounding Mount Nebo.

Visitor Centers

The park visitor center provides information, maps and exhibits on the mountain's history, the CCC and the surrounding area. It is open daily during the day year-round, with extended Friday and Saturday hours in the summer season. Park interpreters offer programs and can point out the best overlooks and trails. The historic CCC pavilion, cabins and stonework are close to the visitor center and easy to see on foot.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers 15 rentable cabins, including several historic CCC-built cabins, and 25 campsites. Both fill quickly in summer and on fall weekends, so reserve ahead. There is no hotel on the mountain. Hotels and restaurants can be found in Russellville, about 8 to 13 miles away, and in Dardanelle. Morrilton and Conway are about an hour to the east, and Mount Magazine State Park, with its lodge, is within a drive of roughly an hour.
