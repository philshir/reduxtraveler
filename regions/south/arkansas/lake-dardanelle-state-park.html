Lake Dardanelle State Park

★5.0
  1. South
  2. Arkansas
  3. Lake Dardanelle State Park

Lakeside recreation area featuring fishing, boating, camping, wildlife viewing, interpretive exhibits, and shoreline access along a large Arkansas River reservoir.

How to Get There

From Russellville (4 mi): Take Marina Road and follow signs to State Park Drive, continuing directly to the park entrance on the shore of Lake Dardanelle.

Overview

Lake Dardanelle State Park is a lakeside state park on Lake Dardanelle, a large reservoir on the Arkansas River that is part of the McClellan-Kerr Arkansas River Navigation System, which allows barge traffic from the Mississippi River to the Port of Catoosa in northeastern Oklahoma. The lake covers about 34,000 acres and reaches roughly 50 miles upstream, with more than 300 miles of shoreline. The park has two sites, one in Russellville and one in Dardanelle, each with picnic areas, boat ramps, pavilions and playgrounds. It is popular with anglers and boaters and for its visitor center, which has aquariums and natural and cultural history exhibits.

Access

The Russellville site is on the lake near town and is reached by Marina Road and State Park Drive from the I-40 area. The Dardanelle site is on the lake in the town of Dardanelle. The park is about 75 miles northwest of Little Rock and roughly 80 miles east of Fort Smith along I-40. Most visitors drive, and parking is available at the visitor center and boat ramps.

Seasons

Spring through fall is the best time for fishing, boating, birdwatching and picnicking, with summers hot and humid. Fishing events and tournaments can make the boat ramps busy on some weekends. Fall brings pleasant weather and migrating birds, while winters are mild and quiet, with waterfowl often seen on the lake. The park is open year-round, though the visitor center and some facilities keep set daytime hours.

Things to Do

Fish and Boat on Lake Dardanelle

The lake is a well-known fishing destination, with bass, catfish and crappie among the popular catches, and both park sites have boat ramps. Boating and kayaking are also popular in the warmer months.

Visit the Visitor Center Aquariums

The Russellville visitor center has six aquariums and displays on the natural and cultural history of the Arkansas River valley. It is a good stop for families and for learning about the lake and its wildlife.

Picnic and Watch Wildlife

Both park sites have picnic areas, pavilions and playgrounds, and the lakeshore attracts birds, including waterfowl in the cooler months.

Visitor Centers

The Russellville site features a visitor center of more than 10,000 square feet with six aquariums, natural and cultural history displays, classrooms and a gift shop. It is open daily during the day and is also the park office. Staff provide maps, information on fishing and boating, and educational programs. The Dardanelle site is mostly a recreation area with boat ramps, picnic areas and pavilions.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers camping at its lakeside sites, and the Corps of Engineers also operates campgrounds around Lake Dardanelle. Hotels, motels and restaurants are available in Russellville, about 4 miles from the main site, and Dardanelle has some additional services. Conway and Morrilton are about 45 minutes to an hour east along I-40, and the Little Rock area is roughly 1.25 hours away. Mount Nebo and Mount Magazine state parks are also within about an hour and offer cabins and lodge rooms.
