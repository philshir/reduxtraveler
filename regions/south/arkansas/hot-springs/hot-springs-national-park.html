Drive to US-70 BUS/E Grand Ave in Hot Springs

Overview

Hot Springs National Park protects the thermal springs at the heart of the city of Hot Springs, Arkansas, in the Ouachita Mountains. Set aside by Congress in 1832 as a federal reservation, before the idea of a national park existed, it is the oldest park area in the National Park Service system. The springs flow from the western slope of Hot Springs Mountain at about 143 °F, and the park manages them to keep the water clean for public use. Its centerpiece is Bathhouse Row, a National Historic Landmark District with the country's grandest collection of Gilded Age bathhouses.

Hot Springs was redesignated as a national park in 1921, and it is one of the most accessible parks in the system, because it includes part of downtown. People visit to soak in the thermal water at a traditional bathhouse, tour the restored Fordyce Bathhouse museum, fill jugs with spring water at the public fountains, hike in the wooded hills above the city, and enjoy the historic town with its shops, restaurants, and horse racing. The park is also a favorite stop for visitors to the Ouachita Mountains region and Lake Ouachita.

Park Sections

The park is small and has two main parts. The downtown section includes Bathhouse Row along Central Avenue, the Grand Promenade behind it, the Fordyce Bathhouse Visitor Center, and the thermal spring displays. The mountain section, behind the bathhouses, covers Hot Springs Mountain, West Mountain, North Mountain, and Sugarloaf Mountain, with forests, hiking trails, and scenic drives. The Gulpha Gorge area on the east side has the park's campground. The park is surrounded by the city, and most of its attractions are within walking distance of each other.

Sightseeing and Exploring the Park

The best sightseeing starts with a walk along Bathhouse Row, where eight historic bathhouses line the street. The Grand Promenade, a brick walkway, runs behind the bathhouses and past a number of spring displays, and the Display Spring and Hot Water Cascade show where the thermal water emerges. The 216-foot Hot Springs Mountain Tower gives views of the mountains, city, and lakes. Hot Springs Mountain Drive and West Mountain Drive are scenic drives with overlooks over the town. Several mineral springs, including Happy Hollow Spring and Mountain Valley Spring, offer free drinking water.

Hiking

The park has about 26 miles of trails, from a flat promenade to steep mountain paths. The Grand Promenade (about 0.5 miles) is an easy, paved walk behind Bathhouse Row. The Dead Chief Trail, Peak Trail, and Goat Rock Trail climb through the woods to overlooks and are moderate. The Sunset Trail, about 10 miles, is the longest and loops around the north side of the park, and it is more demanding. Most trails are in hardwood and pine forest and are usually shaded. See the Hot Springs hiking trails for more information.

Other Activities

Bathing is the park's signature activity. The Buckstaff and Quapaw bathhouses offer traditional thermal baths and spa treatments, and others offer massages and facials. Nearby, you can visit the Arlington Hotel, the Mid-America Science Museum, Garvan Woodland Gardens, Oaklawn Racing Casino Resort, Lake Ouachita, and the Gangster Museum of America. Ranger programs and tours are offered, and the Hot Springs Mountain Tower can be visited. The park has no cave tours, but nearby lakes offer boating and fishing.

Flora and Wildlife

The park lies in the Ouachita Mountains, with forests of shortleaf pine, oak, and hickory, and flowering trees such as dogwood and redbud in spring. Fall brings red and gold foliage. Wildlife is typical of the southern Ouachitas, with white-tailed deer, which are often seen near the roads, along with raccoons, squirrels, wild turkeys, bobcats, and many songbirds. The thermal springs support specialized algae and bacteria. Wildlife viewing is on your own, mostly along the trails and in the woods.

Access

The park is in the city of Hot Springs, in central Arkansas, reached by US-70, US-270, and Highway 7. Little Rock is about 55 miles northeast, a drive of about an hour, and Little Rock National Airport is the nearest major airport, with Hot Springs Memorial Field serving smaller aircraft. Dallas is about 4.5 hours southwest, and Memphis about 3.5 hours east. Bathhouse Row is in downtown, and parking is available in lots and on the street. Most visitors arrive by car, and a trolley circulates in town.

Seasons

The park is open all year. Spring is a favorite season for flowering dogwoods and redbuds. Summer is hot and humid, and the soaking baths are often most welcome in the cooler months. Fall has bright foliage and mild weather, and the bathhouses and trails are busy on weekends. Winter is mild, with occasional ice and snow, and the hot water is especially inviting. Racing season at Oaklawn, from January to May, brings crowds to town. The bathhouses are open year-round, with seasonal hours.

Camping and Lodging

The park has one campground, Gulpha Gorge Campground, with about 40 sites near the east entrance, and it takes reservations. There is no lodging in the park other than hotels along the edge of Bathhouse Row. The city has many places to stay, including the historic Arlington Resort Hotel & Spa, Hotel Hale, which is located in a former bathhouse, and chain hotels and motels. Nearby state parks, including Lake Ouachita State Park, Lake Catherine State Park, and DeGray Lake Resort State Park, have campgrounds and lodges within 30 minutes.

Visitor Centers

The Fordyce Bathhouse Visitor Center on Bathhouse Row is the park's main visitor center. The restored 1915 bathhouse has exhibits on the history of bathing, a museum with original rooms, a film, a bookstore, and rangers who can advise on trails, baths, and programs. The Hot Springs Mountain Tower and the park's other sites have limited information services. Hours vary by season, so check the National Park Service website.
