How to Get There

From Little Rock (55 mi): Take I-30 west to US-70 west, then continue southwest into Hot Springs via Central Avenue and the downtown historic district.

Overview

Hot Springs is a city in the Ouachita Mountains of central Arkansas, and it is famous for its natural thermal springs, which are the centerpiece of Hot Springs National Park, the smallest national park in the country and the only one located in the middle of a city. The elegant bathhouses of Bathhouse Row were built in the early twentieth century to take advantage of the 143-degree water, and they made the city one of the most popular health resorts in America.

Visitors come to Hot Springs to see Bathhouse Row, to soak in the thermal waters at one of the two bathhouses that still offer treatments, and to hike in the national park. They also come for the Oaklawn racetrack and casino, for Garvan Woodland Gardens, and for boating and fishing on Lake Ouachita and Lake Hamilton, and the city is a popular getaway from Little Rock, Dallas, and Memphis.

History

Native Americans considered the springs neutral ground where they could heal in peace, and Hernando de Soto may have visited in 1541. In 1832, Congress set aside the area as a federal reservation, the first protected area in the national park system, and it became Hot Springs National Park in 1921. The bathhouses on Bathhouse Row were built from 1892 to 1923, and the city grew as a spa and entertainment center. In the 1920s through the 1940s it was also known for gambling and organized crime, with visitors such as Al Capone, and the Arlington Hotel was a favorite of these. President Bill Clinton grew up in Hot Springs and graduated from the city's high school in 1964.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Bathhouse Row is a collection of eight historic bathhouses along Central Avenue in a variety of architectural styles, and the Fordyce Bathhouse is now the park's visitor center with restored interiors. The Arlington Hotel is a grand historic hotel, and the Hot Springs Mountain Tower offers views over the city and the Ouachita Mountains. Visitors can also fill jugs of thermal water at the public fountains along the street. Downtown has the Hot Springs Documentary Film Festival and the Gangster Museum of America.

Culture and Museums

The Fordyce Bathhouse Visitor Center has exhibits on the history of bathing and health treatments, and the Mid-America Science Museum has hands-on exhibits. The National Park Service runs programs and tours of the bathhouses, and the Gangster Museum of America tells the story of the city's illicit past. The Hot Springs Documentary Film Festival, held each October, is one of the oldest documentary festivals in the country.

Parks

Hot Springs National Park has more than 26 miles of hiking trails and a scenic drive, and the Grand Promenade behind Bathhouse Row is a pleasant walk. Garvan Woodland Gardens, a botanical garden of the University of Arkansas, has a glass chapel and thousands of plants, and Lake Ouachita State Park, Lake Catherine State Park, and DeGray Lake Resort State Park offer boating, fishing, and camping in the area.

Seasons and Weather

Hot Springs has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and a few inches of snow. Spring and fall are the best times to visit, and the thoroughbred racing season at Oaklawn runs from January to May, including the Arkansas Derby in spring.
