Hemmed In Hollow Falls

★Rating:5.0

Strenuous wilderness trail leading through steep Ozark terrain to a towering seasonal waterfall that drops nearly 210 feet from a sandstone bluff.

How to Get There

From Harrison (18 mi): Take AR-43 south toward Compton, turn onto the gravel wilderness access road across from the Compton community, follow signs for approximately one mile, and continue to the Hemmed-In Hollow Trailhead parking area.

Overview

Hemmed-In Hollow Falls drops about 210 feet over a sandstone bluff in the Ponca Wilderness of Buffalo National River, the tallest waterfall between the Appalachians and the Rocky Mountains. The hike to the base of the falls is one of the most popular and challenging in Arkansas. Hikers come for the waterfall, best after rain, and for views across the Buffalo River valley and the wild Ozark hills.

Getting to the Trailhead

The trailhead is in Compton, about 18 miles south of Harrison. From AR-43, turn onto the road to Compton and follow the signs about a mile on gravel to the Compton Trailhead. The gravel road is suitable for passenger cars in dry weather but can be rough after heavy rain. The parking area is small and fills on spring and fall weekends.

Trail Description

From the Compton Trailhead, the trail descends steeply through hardwood forest toward the Buffalo River, passing views of the valley and the surrounding hills. Near the bottom, it turns into Hemmed-In Hollow and follows the creek a short distance to the base of the falls, which pour into a box canyon surrounded by high bluffs. Hikers return the same way, for about 5 miles round trip. Several trails meet near the bottom, including trails to the Buffalo River and to the Centerpoint Trail, so follow the signs for Hemmed-In Hollow.

Trail Conditions and Difficulty

The hike is strenuous, with about 1,400 feet of elevation change, and the climb back out is long, steep, and rocky. No scrambling is required. Summer is hot and humid, so start early and carry plenty of water; the falls may be just a trickle in dry weather. The rocks around the falls are slippery, and ticks and chiggers are common in warm months.
