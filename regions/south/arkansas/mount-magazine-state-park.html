How to Get There

From Little ( 114mi) Get on I-630 E. Follow I-40 W to AR-363 S in Pottsville. Take exit 88 from I-40 W. Take AR-247 N, AR-27 S and AR-10 W to AR-309/Mount Magazine Scenic Byway in Mountain Township

Overview

Mount Magazine State Park protects the highest point in Arkansas and is the highest state park in the state. The park takes in the summit area, called Signal Hill, and Mossback Ridge, and it is home to The Lodge at Mount Magazine, cliffside cabins, trails and a hang gliding launch. The mountain was inhabited from the 1850s and passed between the Ouachita and Ozark National Forests before becoming a state park. Visitors come for the sweeping views of the Petit Jean River Valley, hiking, camping, hang gliding, butterflies and the quiet mountaintop lodge.

Access

The park sits on a flat-topped mountain in western Arkansas and is reached by the Mount Magazine Scenic Byway, Highway 309, which climbs from the surrounding valley. Approaches are from the north near Paris and from the south near Havana. The drive from Little Rock takes roughly two hours, and from Fort Smith about 1.5 hours. The mountain road is steep and winding, so allow extra time, especially with a trailer or RV.

Seasons

The mountain is noticeably cooler than the valley in summer, which makes it a popular escape from the heat. Spring brings wildflowers and migrating birds, and summer is prime time for butterflies, including the park's annual butterfly festival. Fall color along the ridges and overlooks is a major draw. Winter can bring fog, ice and snow to the summit, which may make the road and some trails difficult, so check conditions before driving up.

Things to Do

Stand on Arkansas's Highest Peak

Mount Magazine State Park is home to Mount Magazine, the highest point in Arkansas at 2,753 feet. Visitors can enjoy breathtaking views from numerous overlooks, taking in panoramic vistas of the Arkansas River Valley, Blue Mountain Lake, and the surrounding Ozark and Ouachita Mountains.

Experience Outdoor Adventure

The park is one of Arkansas's premier destinations for hiking, mountain biking, rock climbing, rappelling, horseback riding, and hang gliding. Its rugged terrain and scenic landscapes provide opportunities for both casual recreation and challenging outdoor adventures.

Visit the Lodge and Visitor Center

The park's mountaintop lodge offers spectacular views, dining, exhibits, and educational programs that highlight the natural history, geology, and wildlife of Mount Magazine. Visitors can also learn about the mountain's unique ecosystems and conservation efforts.

Top Trails

Signal Hill Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail leads to Signal Hill, the highest point in Arkansas. The route passes through scenic forests and provides visitors with the opportunity to stand atop the state's highest elevation.

Benefield Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 8.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

One of the park's premier hiking routes, the Benefield Loop traverses forests, mountain slopes, streams, and scenic ridges while showcasing the diverse landscapes and wildlife habitats of Mount Magazine.

Visitor Centers

The park visitor center is the main interpretive facility and is open daily during the day. It has exhibits on the mountain's natural history, geology and wildlife, along with maps and information about trails and programs. Park interpreters lead hikes, butterfly programs and other activities. The lodge area adds a restaurant and overlooks, and the park also has picnic areas and trailheads near the summit.

Camping and Lodging

The Lodge at Mount Magazine, which opened in 2006, has 60 guest rooms, an indoor pool and a restaurant, and the park also has cliffside cabins. Cameron Bluff Campground offers camping, and all lodging and camping should be reserved well ahead for fall weekends. Hotels and restaurants are available in Paris, Ozark and Danville, and Russellville and Fort Smith offer a larger range within about an hour to an hour and a half. Mount Nebo State Park is another mountaintop option within a short drive.
