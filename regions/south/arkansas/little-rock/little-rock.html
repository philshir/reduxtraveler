Little Rock Travel Guide

Capital of Arkansas offering a blend of history, culture, and natural beauty. Visitors can explore the historic River Market District, visit the William J. Clinton Presidential Library, and enjoy outdoor activities at Pinnacle Mountain State Park.

Places to See in Little Rock

Arkansas Museum of Fine Arts

★ 5.0

Contemporary arts center offering rotating exhibitions, live performances, art classes, and community events in a striking architectural space.

Arkansas State Capitol

★ 5.0

Grand neoclassical building featuring a gilded dome, marble interiors, and historic legislative chambers open for public tours.

Big Dam Bridge

★ 5.0

At 4,226 feet (1288 m) in length the bridge is the longest pedestrian/bicycle bridge in North America that has never been used by trains or motor vehicles and rises to 90 feet (27 m) over the Arkansas River

Historic Arkansas Museum

★ 5.0

Museum featuring preserved frontier buildings, artisan workshops, and galleries dedicated to regional art and cultural history.

Little Rock Central High School National Historic Site

★ 5.0

Site of forced desegregation in 1957 after the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that segregation of public schools was unconstitutional three years earlier

Lake Willastein Park

★ 5.0

Lakeside recreational area featuring a paved walking trail, fishing opportunities, picnic facilities, playgrounds, scenic water views, and outdoor event spaces.

Old State House Museum

★ 5.0

Historic Greek Revival building housing exhibits on Arkansas’s political, cultural, and social history, including artifacts from governors and first families.

The Old Mill

★ 5.0

Historic re-creation of an 1880's water-powered grist mill known for being in the opening scenes of the classic movie "Gone With The Wind"

River Market

★ 5.0

City park and network of walkways along the banks of the Arkansas River lined by bars, shops, restaurants, nature, public artwork, and the five historic missions

Riverfront Park

★ 5.0

Expansive urban park featuring riverfront walkways, public art installations, playgrounds, plazas, fountains, bridges, gardens, and outdoor entertainment venues.

Rock Creek Trail

★ 5.0

Wooded multi-use pathway following a winding creek through natural green space with boardwalks, bridges, wildlife viewing opportunities, and shaded terrain suitable for walking, running, and cycling.

Two Rivers Bridge Park

★ 5.0

Large riverside recreation area featuring paved and natural trails, wildlife habitat, an inclusive playground, picnic areas, boating access, and scenic views where two waterways converge.

William J. Clinton Library and Museum

★ 5.0

Presidential library of Bill Clinton, the 42nd president of the United States (1993-2001), including the Clinton Presidential Library, the offices of the Clinton Foundation, and the University of Arkansas Clinton School of Public Service

Overview

Little Rock is the capital and largest city of Arkansas, on the south bank of the Arkansas River in the central part of the state, and it is known for its role in the civil rights movement and as the home of the Clinton Presidential Library. In 1957, nine Black students entered Central High School under the protection of federal troops, a key moment in the desegregation of public schools, and the school is now a National Historic Site. The city is also known for its river trails, its downtown River Market, and its Southern food.

Visitors come to Little Rock for the Little Rock Central High School National Historic Site, the William J. Clinton Presidential Center, the Arkansas State Capitol, and the Old State House Museum. They also come for the River Market District, the Big Dam Bridge, and the parks and trails along the river, and it is a good base for visiting Hot Springs, about an hour southwest.

History

The city takes its name from a small rock outcropping on the Arkansas River that the French explorer Bernard de la Harpe called "La Petite Roche" in 1722, to distinguish it from a larger bluff upriver. It was a trading post and then a town in the early 1800s, became the capital of Arkansas Territory in 1821, and was the state capital after Arkansas gained statehood in 1836. During the Civil War, the city was captured by Union troops in 1863. In 1957, Governor Orval Faubus used the National Guard to block the integration of Central High School, and President Eisenhower sent the 101st Airborne Division to escort the Little Rock Nine to class. Bill Clinton, the 42nd president, was governor of Arkansas and his presidential library opened in Little Rock in 2004.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Little Rock Central High School National Historic Site has a visitor center across the street from the school, and the Clinton Presidential Center is a modern building on the riverbank with a replica of the Oval Office. The Arkansas State Capitol, built in 1915 and modeled on the U.S. Capitol, offers free tours, and the Old State House Museum is the oldest standing state capitol west of the Mississippi. The River Market District has restaurants and a farmers market, and the Old Mill at T. R. Pugh Memorial Park was featured in Gone with the Wind.

Culture and Museums

The Arkansas Museum of Fine Arts in MacArthur Park has a collection of American and European art, and the Historic Arkansas Museum has a collection of frontier buildings and art. The Mosaic Templars Cultural Center tells the story of Black history in the state, and the Little Rock Central High School site, the Clinton Center, and the Old State House Museum are major history museums. The Robinson Center hosts the Arkansas Symphony Orchestra and touring shows, and the Arkansas Repertory Theatre is a local theater company.

Parks

Little Rock has a large park system along the Arkansas River. The Arkansas River Trail is a 17-mile loop that connects Riverfront Park and Two Rivers Park, and the Big Dam Bridge, at 4,226 feet, is the longest pedestrian and bicycle bridge in North America built for that purpose. Pinnacle Mountain State Park has hiking trails with views of the Ouachita foothills, and Rock Creek Trail and Lake Willastein Park are local trails.

Seasons and Weather

Little Rock has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and about 5 inches of snow. Spring and fall are the best seasons for walking and biking, and thunderstorms and tornadoes are possible in spring.
