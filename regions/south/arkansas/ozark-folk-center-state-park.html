How to Get There

From Mountain View (2 mi): Follow Park Avenue north from downtown Mountain View and continue along the marked route directly to the state park entrance.

Overview

Ozark Folk Center State Park is a living-history park in Mountain View, Arkansas, dedicated to preserving the traditional crafts, music and ways of life of the Ozark Mountains. It opened in the early 1970s as a state park to keep these traditions alive. Skilled artisans work in the craft village, demonstrating blacksmithing, pottery, weaving, woodworking, quilting and other trades. Visitors also come for folk, bluegrass and old-time music performances, the heritage herb gardens and the workshops and festivals held through the season.

Access

The park is on the edge of Mountain View in north-central Arkansas, about 2 miles from the courthouse square. Mountain View is reached by state highways, including AR-5 and AR-9, and the drive from Little Rock takes roughly two hours. There is no public transportation, so nearly all visitors arrive by car. Parking is near the entrance, and the craft village is a short walk through the grounds.

Seasons

The craft village and music programs operate seasonally, generally from spring through fall, with the best weather in spring and early fall. Summers are warm and humid, and the village has shaded areas and indoor workshops. Fall brings color to the surrounding hills and special events, including music festivals and craft fairs. Winters are quiet, with reduced programming, so check the park's schedule before visiting during the off-season.

Things to Do

Visit the Craft Village

Artisans demonstrate traditional crafts such as blacksmithing, pottery, weaving, woodworking and quilting, and visitors can usually watch the work and talk with the craftspeople. Many of the pieces are sold in the park's shops.

Hear Ozark Music

The park hosts folk, bluegrass, gospel and old-time mountain music performances during the season, and Mountain View itself is known as a center of Ozark folk music. Evening shows and festivals are a highlight.

Tour the Herb Garden and Take a Workshop

The heritage herb gardens show plants used for cooking, medicine and dyes in Ozark households, and the park offers classes and workshops in crafts, gardening and music.

Visitor Centers

The park's entrance building provides admission, schedules, maps and information on demonstrations and performances. Staff can explain which crafts and music programs are running on a given day. The grounds also include shops selling handmade goods, a restaurant and performance venues. Hours are generally Tuesday through Saturday during the season, though they change during festivals and special events.

Camping and Lodging

The park has lodging on site, and there is a restaurant on the grounds. Mountain View, about 2 miles away, has motels, cabins, bed-and-breakfasts and restaurants, many of which book up during festivals and the fall season. Blanchard Springs Caverns and Ozark National Forest campgrounds are within a short drive. Larger lodging selections are in Batesville and Heber Springs, each roughly 45 minutes to an hour away.
