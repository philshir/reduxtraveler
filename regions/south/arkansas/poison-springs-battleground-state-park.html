Poison Springs Battleground State Park

★5.0

Preserved Civil War battlefield featuring interpretive exhibits, memorial areas, picnic sites, and historic landscapes associated with one of the most significant engagements of the Camden Expedition.

How to Get There

From Camden (15 mi): Take US-79 south toward Hampton, then follow AR-76 west toward Chidester and continue to the battlefield entrance located along Highway 76.

Overview

Poison Springs Battleground State Park preserves the site of the April 18, 1864, Battle of Poison Spring, one of the most consequential engagements of the Camden Expedition in southern Arkansas. Confederate forces attacked a Union foraging train, captured supplies and inflicted heavy casualties. The battle is especially remembered for the role of the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry, whose soldiers suffered heavily in the fighting and its aftermath. Interpretive signs and a memorial in a quiet forest let visitors reflect on this important event in Black military history and the Civil War in Arkansas.

Access

The park is in Ouachita County, about 15 miles west of Camden along AR-76 in the piney woods of southern Arkansas. Visitors reach it by car from US-278 and AR-24, and the roads are paved. The park is open during daylight hours and admission is free. The site is rural, so bring water and food, and note that there is no public transportation.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with spring and fall the most comfortable seasons for exploring the grounds. Spring brings wildflowers and green forest, while summers are hot and humid. Fall has mild temperatures and color, and winters are mild but wet. Anniversary events in April sometimes feature talks and reenactments, so check ahead if you want to attend.

Things to Do

Read the Interpretive Signs

Signs explain the course of the battle, the Camden Expedition and the people involved.

Remember the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry

Memorials and exhibits honor the African American soldiers who fought and died here.

Walk the Battlefield Grounds

The forested grounds offer a quiet landscape for reflection.

Picnic Under the Pines

Picnic sites provide shade and a rest stop on a driving tour of the Camden Expedition.

Explore Camden and the Camden Expedition Sites

The nearby town has museums and other markers related to the 1864 campaign.

Top Trails

Battlefield Interpretive Path

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path connects the memorial, signs and the area where the fighting took place.

Visitor Centers

There is no staffed visitor center at the park. Interpretive panels and a memorial provide the main information on site. The Arkansas State Parks website and the Camden area museums offer additional background and maps.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the park, but the nearby Poison Springs State Forest has primitive camping and trails. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Camden, about 15 miles east. Magnolia and Hope offer additional options within an hour, and Lake Columbia and the Ouachita National Forest are also nearby.
