Overview

Baton Rouge is the capital of Louisiana, on the east bank of the Mississippi River in the south-central part of the state, and it is the second-largest city in Louisiana. It is known for the Louisiana State Capitol, the tallest state capitol in the country, for Louisiana State University and the LSU Tigers, and for its petrochemical industry along the river. The city blends the Cajun and Creole cultures of south Louisiana with the Southern traditions of the north, and its food, music, and festivals reflect both.

Visitors come to Baton Rouge for the Capitol and its observation deck, for the Old State Capitol, and for the battleship-era USS Kidd on the river. They also come for LSU football in Tiger Stadium, one of the loudest stadiums in college sports, for Cajun and Creole restaurants and festivals, and for nearby plantations and swamp tours, and it is about an hour from New Orleans and Lafayette.

History

The French explorer Pierre Le Moyne d'Iberville saw a red pole marking the boundary between the hunting grounds of two Native American tribes at this spot in 1699 and named the place "le bâton rouge," or "red stick." The French built a fort in the 1720s, and the area was later ruled by Britain and Spain before the West Florida Rebellion of 1810 and its incorporation into the United States. Baton Rouge became the state capital in 1849 and was occupied by Union forces in 1862 during the Civil War. In the 1930s, Governor and Senator Huey Long built the new Capitol, completed in 1932, and was assassinated there in 1935. The city grew with oil and chemical industries and with LSU.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Louisiana State Capitol, a 450-foot Art Deco skyscraper, has an observation deck on the 27th floor with views of the Mississippi River and the city, and Huey Long's grave is in the gardens in front. The Old State Capitol is a Gothic Revival castle on a bluff over the river and now houses a museum of political history. The USS Kidd, a WWII destroyer, is open for tours next to the Louisiana Veterans Memorial Museum, and Riverfront Plaza and Galvez Plaza are downtown public spaces.

Culture and Museums

The Old State Capitol has exhibits on Louisiana politics, and the Louisiana State Museum and the Capitol Park Museum show the state's history and culture. The LSU Museum of Art, the Shaw Center for the Arts, and the LSU Rural Life Museum, an outdoor museum of Louisiana folk life, are other cultural sites. The USS Kidd Veterans Museum honors the military, and the Magnolia Mound Plantation shows life in French Creole Louisiana. The city hosts events such as the Baton Rouge Blues Festival and Mardi Gras parades.

Parks

Baton Rouge has many parks and lakes. City Park has lakes and walking trails near LSU, and the LSU Lakes, with oak trees and walking paths, are a local favorite. Bluebonnet Swamp Nature Center and the Baton Rouge Zoo offer nature experiences, and the Mississippi River levee has a walking and biking trail. The Atchafalaya Basin, with swamps and bayous, is a short drive to the west, and plantation homes along the river are popular day trips.

Seasons and Weather

Baton Rouge has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F and almost no snow. Fall is the most popular season, because of LSU football, and spring is pleasant. Hurricane season runs from June to November, and heavy rain and flooding can occur, as in August 2016.
