General Information

New Orleans, LA Weather Forecast

Mardi Gras, or Fat Tuesday, refers to events of the Carnival celebration, beginning on or after the Christian feasts of the Epiphany (Three Kings Day) and culminating on the day before Ash Wednesday, which is known as Shrove Tuesday. Mardi Gras is French for "Fat Tuesday", reflecting the practice of the last night of eating rich, fatty foods before the ritual Lenten sacrifices and fasting of the Lenten season.

Overview

The holiday of Mardi Gras is celebrated in all of Louisiana, most famously in the city of New Orleans. Celebrations are concentrated for about two weeks before and through Shrove Tuesday, the day before Ash Wednesday (the start of lent in the Western Christian tradition). Usually there is one major parade each day (weather permitting); many days have several large parades. The largest and most elaborate parades take place the last five days of the Mardi Gras season. In the final week, many events occur throughout New Orleans and surrounding communities, including parades and balls (some of them masquerade balls).

The parades in New Orleans are organized by social clubs known as krewes; most follow the same parade schedule and route each year. The earliest-established krewes were the Mistick Krewe of Comus, the earliest, Rex, the Knights of Momus and the Krewe of Proteus. Several modern "super krewes" are well known for holding large parades and events, such as the Krewe of Endymion (which is best known for naming celebrities as grand marshals for their parades), the Krewe of Bacchus (similarly known for naming celebrities as their Kings), as well as the Zulu Social Aid & Pleasure Club—a predominantly African American krewe. Float riders traditionally toss throws into the crowds. The most common throws are strings of colorful plastic beads, doubloons, decorated plastic "throw cups", Moon Pies, and small inexpensive toys, but throws can also include lingerie and more sordid items. Major krewes follow the same parade schedule and route each year.

While many tourists center their Carnival season activities on Bourbon Street and in New Orleans and Dauphin, major parades originate in the Uptown and Mid-City districts and follow a route along St. Charles Avenue and Canal Street, on the upriver side of the French Quarter. Mardi Gras day traditionally concludes with the "Meeting of the Courts" between Rex and Comus.

Endymion Parade
★ 5.0

Massive Mardi Gras procession renowned for its enormous illuminated floats, celebrity grand marshals, high-energy atmosphere, and one of the largest crowds of the Carnival season.

Orpheus Parade
★ 5.0

Renowned Mardi Gras super krewe parade featuring spectacular illuminated floats, musical themes, celebrity appearances, and a grand finale celebration unique to Carnival season.

Rex Parade
★ 5.0

Historic Mardi Gras procession led by the King of Carnival, featuring elaborately themed floats, centuries-old traditions, and many of the symbols most closely associated with New Orleans Carnival.

Mills Baker, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Zulu Parade
★ 5.0

Historic Mardi Gras procession celebrated for its rich African American heritage, vibrant traditions, elaborately decorated floats, and the famous hand-painted coconuts thrown to spectators.
