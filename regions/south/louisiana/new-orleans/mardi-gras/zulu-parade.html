Zulu Parade

Historic Mardi Gras procession celebrated for its rich African American heritage, vibrant traditions, elaborately decorated floats, and the famous hand-painted coconuts thrown to spectators.

General Information
Date:
Held annually on Mardi Gras Day (Fat Tuesday) and traditionally begins in the morning before the Rex Parade
Location:
Starts at South Claiborne Avenue and Jackson Avenue, New Orleans, LA, Ends at Broad Street and Orleans Avenue, New Orleans, LA
Website:
kreweofzulu.com

Parade Route

General Route: The parade begins at South Claiborne Avenue and Jackson Avenue, travels down Jackson Avenue to St. Charles Avenue, follows St. Charles Avenue through Uptown New Orleans and around Harmony Circle, continues to Canal Street, turns onto Basin Street, proceeds onto Orleans Avenue, and concludes at Broad Street.

Overview

The Krewe of Zulu, officially known as the Zulu Social Aid & Pleasure Club, is one of the most beloved and culturally significant organizations in New Orleans Mardi Gras. Founded in 1909, the krewe emerged from the city's African American community and developed into one of Carnival's most recognized traditions. What began as a small social club evolved into one of Mardi Gras' premier parades, blending pageantry, music, humor, and community pride. The parade attracts enormous crowds each year and serves as a powerful expression of the city's cultural heritage.

Zulu is best known for its coveted hand-painted coconuts, among the most sought-after throws in all of Mardi Gras. Spectators line the route in hopes of catching one of these unique souvenirs while enjoying colorful floats, marching groups, and the krewe's distinctive characters, including the Witch Doctor, Big Shot, and Ambassador. The organization has also played an important role in New Orleans civic life, with notable figures such as jazz legend Louis Armstrong serving as Zulu King. Combining history, tradition, and celebration, the Zulu Parade remains one of the defining experiences of Mardi Gras Day.
