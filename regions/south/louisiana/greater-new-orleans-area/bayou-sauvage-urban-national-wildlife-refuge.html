Bayou Sauvage Urban National Wildlife Refuge

★5.0
  1. South
  2. Louisiana
  3. Greater New Orleans Area
  4. Bayou Sauvage Urban National Wildlife Refuge

Expansive urban marshland preserve featuring bayous, lagoons, and forested wetlands that provide habitat for hundreds of bird species and diverse wildlife.

General Information
Hours:
Open daily during daylight hours
Trails and access points may close temporarily for safety or maintenance
Fees:
Free admission
Optional Federal Duck Stamp ($25) for hunting and conservation support
Pet Policy:
Dogs are allowed
Closest cities with hotels:
New Orleans (about 19 mi)
Seasons:
All year (best wildlife viewing in fall, winter, and early spring)
Location:
17160 Chef Menteur Hwy, New Orleans, LA 70129
Website:
fws.gov/refuge/bayou-sauvage-urban

How to Get There

From New Orleans (19 mi): Take I-10 East to I-510 South, exit onto US-90 East (Chef Menteur Highway), and continue for several miles until reaching the refuge entrance along the highway.

Overview

The Bayou Sauvage Urban National Wildlife Refuge is one of the largest urban wildlife refuges in the United States, protecting more than 20,000 acres of marsh, swamp, bottomland forest and low ridges within the city limits of New Orleans. It was established in 1990 by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to protect the wetlands east of the city, which lie along the Mississippi Flyway and shelter large numbers of migratory waterfowl and other birds. The refuge also acts as a natural buffer against storm surge from Lake Pontchartrain and Lake Borgne. Visitors come for birdwatching, fishing, paddling and photography, and for the chance to see alligators and wading birds just minutes from a major city.

Access

The refuge sits in eastern New Orleans along US-90 (Chef Menteur Highway), about 19 miles from downtown by the route described above, and nearly all visitors arrive by car. Trailheads and parking areas are found along the highway, and anglers and paddlers reach the bayous and canals from public access points. Facilities are basic, so bring water, insect repellent and sun protection. Trails and access points can close after storms or high water, so check the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service website for current conditions before you go.

Seasons

Fall through early spring is the best time to visit, when cooler, drier weather makes walking comfortable and large numbers of waterfowl winter in the marshes. Spring brings migrating songbirds and nesting wading birds. Summers are hot, humid and buggy, with frequent afternoon thunderstorms, and alligators are most active in the warm months. Hurricane season, from June through November, can bring flooding and temporary closures to the refuge.

Things to Do

Explore Louisiana's Largest Urban Wildlife Refuge

Bayou Sauvage Urban National Wildlife Refuge protects more than 20,000 acres of wetlands, marshes, bayous, and lagoons within the city limits of New Orleans. Visitors can experience diverse coastal ecosystems while enjoying wildlife viewing, photography, fishing, and nature observation in one of the nation's largest urban wildlife refuges.

Watch Birds and Wildlife

The refuge is a premier destination for birdwatching and supports hundreds of species of migratory birds, wading birds, waterfowl, alligators, turtles, and other wildlife. Its location along the Mississippi Flyway makes it especially important for migratory species throughout the year.

Enjoy Fishing and Paddling

Visitors can fish, paddle, and explore the refuge's network of bayous, canals, and marshes while experiencing the natural landscapes of coastal Louisiana. The refuge provides excellent opportunities to observe wetland habitats and learn about conservation efforts in the region.

Top Trails

Marsh Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This boardwalk trail leads to an observation platform overlooking the marsh, providing excellent opportunities to view birds, alligators, and native wetland vegetation while learning about the refuge's ecosystems.

Ridge Trail

Length: Approximately 1.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The Ridge Trail follows one of the area's natural levee ridges and offers visitors a chance to experience a variety of habitats, including forests and wetlands, while observing wildlife and enjoying a peaceful nature walk.

Visitor Centers

Bayou Sauvage is a day-use, largely self-guided refuge with limited staffed facilities, and I am not certain a visitor center is regularly open on site. Trailheads have parking and some interpretive signs, and the refuge is managed by the Southeast Louisiana National Wildlife Refuge Complex, which can provide maps and current information. Contact the complex or check the refuge website before your trip to confirm what is open.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the refuge. New Orleans offers the full range of hotels, from French Quarter properties to chain hotels along I-10 in the eastern part of the city, and Slidell, about 25 miles away across Lake Pontchartrain, has additional chain hotels and motels. For camping, Fontainebleau State Park near Mandeville, St. Bernard State Park and Bayou Segnette State Park are each within roughly an hour's drive.
