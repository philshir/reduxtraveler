Fairview-Riverside State Park

★5.0
  1. South
  2. Louisiana
  3. Greater New Orleans Area
  4. Fairview-Riverside State Park

Shaded riverside park featuring historic architecture, towering oak trees, and scenic waterways ideal for outdoor recreation.

How to Get There

From Slidell (29 mi): Take I-12 West toward Covington, exit onto LA-21 South and then LA-22 East toward Madisonville, and follow local roads to Fairview Drive leading into the park.

Overview

Fairview-Riverside State Park is a Louisiana state park on the Tchefuncte River in Madisonville, on the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain. The park is known for its shaded grounds of live oaks and Spanish moss, its river frontage and the historic Otis House, a late 19th-century home that is now a museum furnished with period pieces. The land was set aside as a state park to preserve both its natural setting and its historic house. Visitors come for picnics, fishing and boating on the river, short nature walks, and tours of the Otis House.

Access

The park is at 119 Fairview Drive in Madisonville, about 6 to 10 miles from Covington and roughly 45 minutes to an hour from New Orleans by car via the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway or I-12. From Slidell it is about 29 miles. There is no public transportation to the park, and the entrance station collects a small per-person day-use fee. Seniors and young children are admitted free, according to the General Information box.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, with mild temperatures that suit picnics, river walks and house tours. Summers are hot, humid and buggy, though shade from the oak trees helps, and the river is busiest with boaters and anglers at this time. Winters are mild and quiet. Hurricane season, from June through November, can cause flooding along the river and temporary closures.

Things to Do

Tour the Historic Otis House Museum

Fairview-Riverside State Park is home to the historic Otis House, a late nineteenth-century residence overlooking the Tchefuncte River. The museum features period furnishings, local history exhibits, and displays that highlight life along Louisiana's Northshore during the region's timber and river trade era.

Enjoy the Tchefuncte River

The park's scenic location along the Tchefuncte River makes it a popular destination for boating, fishing, paddling, wildlife viewing, and photography. Visitors can relax along the riverfront while enjoying views of one of Louisiana's most picturesque waterways.

Picnic and Explore the Riverfront Grounds

Fairview-Riverside State Park offers shaded picnic areas, open green spaces, and beautiful live oak trees that create a peaceful setting for family outings and nature observation. Its convenient location near Madisonville makes it a popular day-trip destination.

Top Trails

Riverside Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic trail follows portions of the riverfront and park grounds, providing opportunities to observe native plants, birds, and views of the Tchefuncte River while learning about the area's natural environment.

Otis House Walking Path

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors can stroll the historic grounds surrounding the Otis House, enjoying interpretive displays, historic landscaping, and river views while learning about the cultural history of the property and the surrounding region.

Visitor Centers

The Otis House Museum serves as the park's main interpretive facility, with exhibits and furnishings that explain life along the Tchefuncte River in the late 19th century. The entrance station and park office provide maps and information. Museum tours are scheduled at set times rather than all day, so contact the park or check the Louisiana State Parks website to confirm hours.

Camping and Lodging

Fairview-Riverside has an area for camping, and I believe reservations are made through Louisiana State Parks; confirm the current options before you go. Hotels and motels are available in Covington and Mandeville, each about 15 to 25 minutes away, and the town of Madisonville has a small number of guest options, with more in Slidell and New Orleans. Fontainebleau State Park, about 20 minutes east, also offers campsites and cabins.
