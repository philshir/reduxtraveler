Overview

The Greater New Orleans Area encompasses a diverse collection of cities, historic communities, wetlands, and waterfront destinations centered around one of America's most distinctive cultural capitals. Extending beyond the famous streets of New Orleans itself, the region includes neighboring communities such as Metairie, Kenner, Gretna, Chalmette, Mandeville, Covington, and communities throughout the Northshore, River Parishes, and coastal areas. Together, these destinations offer a remarkable blend of architecture, history, cuisine, music, outdoor recreation, and cultural traditions that have evolved from French, Spanish, African, Caribbean, and American influences.

The Greater New Orleans Area extends far beyond the city's famous historic districts into a diverse landscape of wetlands, bayous, barrier islands, and outdoor recreation destinations. Visitors can explore vast natural areas such as Bayou Sauvage Urban National Wildlife Refuge, one of the nation's largest urban wildlife refuges, where marshes and lagoons provide habitat for hundreds of bird species. Nearby preserves and swamps offer opportunities for kayaking, hiking, airboat tours, wildlife photography, and birdwatching, while scenic communities along the Northshore provide access to lakefront parks, historic districts, and protected natural areas. The region's extensive network of waterways and wetlands showcases the unique ecosystems that have shaped southeastern Louisiana for centuries.

Outdoor enthusiasts will find a remarkable variety of destinations surrounding the metropolitan area, including Fontainebleau State Park along the shores of Lake Pontchartrain, Bayou Segnette State Park with its mix of marsh and swamp habitats, and Grand Isle State Park on a barrier island along the Gulf Coast. Wildlife refuges, state parks, swamp tours, fishing communities, and nature trails allow visitors to experience everything from cypress forests and freshwater marshes to coastal beaches and migratory bird habitats. Together, these attractions create a region where nature, recreation, and conservation are as important to the visitor experience as the culture and history for which New Orleans is famous.
