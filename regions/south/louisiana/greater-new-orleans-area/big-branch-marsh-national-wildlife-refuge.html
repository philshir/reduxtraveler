How to Get There

From Slidell (10 mi): Take US-190 West for several miles, turn left onto LA-434 South, and continue about 3 miles to the refuge entrance.

Overview

The Big Branch Marsh National Wildlife Refuge protects more than 18,000 acres on the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain, where pine flatwoods, freshwater and brackish marshes, bayous and cypress-tupelo swamp meet. It was established in 1994 by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to preserve one of the last large tracts of undeveloped habitat along the lake. The refuge is home to the endangered red-cockaded woodpecker, which nests in its open pine forests, and it hosts large numbers of wintering waterfowl and migrating birds. Visitors come for birdwatching, short boardwalk and nature walks, paddling, photography and wildlife viewing.

Access

The refuge is near Lacombe, about 10 miles east of Slidell by the directions above, and is reached by car on LA-434. A small refuge office and visitor area sit on LA-434, and nature trails and boardwalks start from nearby parking areas. Public transportation is not available, so most visitors drive from Slidell, Mandeville or New Orleans. Check the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service website for current access, as some areas close for management or after storms.

Seasons

Late fall through winter brings waterfowl and mild, comfortable walking weather, and late winter and spring offer migrating songbirds and wildflowers in the pine savannas. Summers are hot, humid and buggy, with frequent thunderstorms, so mornings are the best time to visit. Hurricane season, from June through November, can cause flooding and closures, as the refuge helps absorb storm surge from the lake.

Things to Do

Explore Louisiana's Pine Forests and Marshes

Big Branch Marsh National Wildlife Refuge protects more than 18,000 acres of pine flatwoods, freshwater marshes, cypress-tupelo swamps, bayous, and coastal habitats along the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain. Visitors can experience one of Louisiana's most diverse natural landscapes while enjoying wildlife viewing, photography, and nature observation.

Watch Birds and Wildlife

The refuge is a premier birdwatching destination and provides important habitat for migratory songbirds, wading birds, waterfowl, bald eagles, and numerous other species. Wildlife enthusiasts may also encounter alligators, turtles, river otters, and a variety of native mammals throughout the refuge.

Enjoy Paddling and Nature Photography

Visitors can explore portions of the refuge by canoe or kayak while experiencing scenic bayous, marshes, and forested waterways. The refuge's diverse habitats and abundant wildlife make it a favorite destination for photographers and outdoor enthusiasts.

Top Trails

Boy Scout Road Boardwalk Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This accessible boardwalk trail winds through marsh and wetland habitats, providing opportunities to observe birds, native plants, and other wildlife while learning about the refuge's unique ecosystems.

Bayou Lacombe Trail

Length: Approximately 2.0 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic trail follows portions of Bayou Lacombe and passes through forests and wetland habitats, offering excellent opportunities for birdwatching, photography, and experiencing the natural beauty of coastal Louisiana.

Visitor Centers

The refuge has a visitor center and office on LA-434 at the Lacombe site, and the General Information box lists it as open Thursday to Saturday. Exhibits and staff or volunteers explain the habitats and wildlife, and maps and trail information are available. The Southeast Louisiana National Wildlife Refuge Complex headquarters is also in the Lacombe area, and hours can change, so confirm with the refuge before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the refuge. Chain hotels and motels are available in Slidell, about 10 miles away, and also in Mandeville, Covington and New Orleans, each within roughly 30 to 45 minutes. Fontainebleau State Park near Mandeville offers campsites and cabins and is about 30 minutes away, with additional campgrounds around Slidell and Pearl River.
