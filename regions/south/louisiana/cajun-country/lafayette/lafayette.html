How to Get There

From New Orleans (135 mi): Take I-10 West across southern Louisiana, continue through the Atchafalaya Basin, and follow signs into downtown Lafayette.

Overview

Lafayette is the heart of Acadiana, the Cajun and Creole region of south-central Louisiana, and it is known as the "Happiest City in America" in some surveys and for its zydeco and Cajun music, its spicy food, and its joie de vivre. It is the fourth-largest city in Louisiana and a center for the oil and gas industry, healthcare, and the University of Louisiana at Lafayette, home of the Ragin' Cajuns. The city also hosts Festival International de Louisiane in April, one of the largest French-language festivals in the United States.

Visitors come to Lafayette for the food, such as boudin, gumbo, étouffée, and crawfish, for live music at dance halls and festivals, and for cultural sites such as Vermilionville, the Acadian Cultural Center, and the Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist. They also come for swamp tours of the Atchafalaya Basin, a visit to the Tabasco factory on Avery Island, and Mardi Gras, one of the biggest outside New Orleans.

History

The area was home to the Chitimacha and Attakapas peoples before the arrival of Acadians, French-speaking exiles from Nova Scotia who were expelled by the British in the 1750s and settled in south Louisiana beginning in the 1760s. They became known as Cajuns, and they farmed the prairies and fished the bayous. The town was founded in 1821 as Vermilionville and renamed Lafayette in 1884 for the Marquis de Lafayette, the French hero of the American Revolution. The arrival of the railroad in 1880 and the oil boom of the twentieth century helped the city grow, and in the 1960s the state created the Council for the Development of French in Louisiana to preserve the language and culture.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Vermilionville is a living history village on the Vermilion River with restored buildings, costumed interpreters, and music, and the Acadian Cultural Center, part of Jean Lafitte National Historical Park, has exhibits on Acadian history. The Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist has an adjacent 500-year-old oak tree, and the Hilliard Art Museum at the university has a collection of art and Louisiana artifacts. The Atchafalaya Basin, the largest swamp in the country, is a short drive away, and Avery Island, home of Tabasco, is about 30 minutes south.

Culture and Museums

Lafayette's culture is built around Cajun and Creole traditions. The Hilliard Art Museum shows Louisiana and international art, and Vermilionville and the Acadian Cultural Center interpret the area's history. The city hosts Festival International de Louisiane in April, Festivals Acadiens et Créoles in October, and Mardi Gras, and dance halls such as Randol's and Vermilionville offer live music. The Paramount Theatre and the Acadiana Center for the Arts have exhibits and shows.

Parks

Lafayette has a number of parks and nature areas. Acadiana Park Nature Station has trails through the woods, and Girard Park has a lake and playgrounds. Lake Martin, near Breaux Bridge, is a swamp with a large rookery of egrets and herons and tours, and Lake Fausse Pointe State Park, Palmetto Island State Park, and the Atchafalaya Basin offer paddling, camping, and wildlife viewing. The Atchafalaya National Wildlife Refuge protects some of the largest bottomland hardwood forests in the country.

Seasons and Weather

Lafayette has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F and almost no snow. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons, and festival season runs from February through November. Hurricane season runs from June to November.

Acadian Cultural Center

★ 5.0

Interactive museum and visitor center exploring the history, migration, and culture of the Acadian people through exhibits, films, and ranger-led programs.

Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist

★ 5.0

Historic cathedral featuring Romanesque Revival architecture, intricate stained glass, and a centuries-old oak tree on its grounds.

Hilliard Art Museum

★ 5.0

University art museum showcasing rotating exhibitions of regional, national, and international artists alongside a permanent collection of modern and contemporary works.

Vermilionville Historic Village

★ 5.0

Living history museum and folklife park featuring restored 18th- and 19th-century homes, costumed interpreters, and cultural demonstrations of Acadian, Creole, and Native American heritage.
