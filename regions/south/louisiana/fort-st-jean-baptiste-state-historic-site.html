Fort St. Jean Baptiste State Historic Site

★5.0
  1. South

Reconstructed eighteenth-century French colonial fort featuring period buildings, living history demonstrations, and exhibits exploring trade, military life, and early settlement in the Lower Mississippi Valley.

How to Get There

From Natchitoches: Travel through the historic downtown district, follow College Drive to Rue Jefferson, and continue to 155 Rue Jefferson near Cane River Lake where the historic site is located.

Overview

Fort St. Jean Baptiste State Historic Site in Natchitoches is a reconstruction of the French colonial fort founded in 1714, the first permanent European settlement in what became the Louisiana Purchase. The fort was a strategic military outpost and trade center that linked French settlers, the Caddo people and Spanish territories to the west. Inside the wooden palisades are furnished buildings, exhibits and living history demonstrations. Visitors come to learn about colonial life and trade on the Cane River and to explore Natchitoches, a charming historic town famous for its Christmas festival and Steel Magnolias film locations.

Access

The fort is at 155 Rue Jefferson in downtown Natchitoches, in northwest Louisiana, about 75 miles southeast of Shreveport and 65 miles northwest of Alexandria. Visitors reach it by car from I-49 and LA-1, and parking is available nearby. The historic district along Cane River Lake is easy to explore on foot. There is no regular public transportation, though Shreveport has an airport.

Seasons

The fort is open Wednesday through Sunday year-round and closed on Mondays, Tuesdays and major holidays. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for visiting, with mild weather and festivals. Summers are hot and humid, and winters are mild, with the Natchitoches Christmas Festival of Lights from late November through early January drawing large crowds. Check the calendar for living history weekends.

Things to Do

Tour the Reconstructed Fort

Palisades, barracks, a guardhouse and a chapel recreate the 1700s fort with exhibits.

Watch Costumed Interpreters

Interpreters demonstrate cooking, crafts and military life and explain the relationships among French, Native and Spanish communities.

Learn About Trade and Native Heritage

Exhibits describe the Caddo, French trade and the role of Natchitoches in the Lower Mississippi Valley.

Walk the Historic District and Cane River Lake

Brick streets, shops and restaurants make for a pleasant stroll after the tour.

Visit Cane River Creole National Historical Park

Oakland and Magnolia Plantations are a short drive and tell later stories.

Top Trails

Fort Grounds Walk

Length: Approximately 0.25 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk around the palisades and buildings of the fort.

Cane River Riverbank Walk

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved path along Cane River Lake in Natchitoches passes shops, historic homes and shaded benches.

Visitor Centers

The fort has a visitor center and gift shop, with exhibits and staff who can answer questions about the fort and local history. The Natchitoches Welcome Center downtown has maps, brochures and tips for planning a day in the historic district. Admission to the fort is a small fee.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the fort. Natchitoches has many historic bed-and-breakfasts, inns and hotels in the downtown historic district, along with chain hotels near I-49. Camping is available at Kisatchie National Forest campgrounds, about 20 minutes away. Shreveport and Alexandria are about an hour to an hour and a half away and have a wide range of hotels.
