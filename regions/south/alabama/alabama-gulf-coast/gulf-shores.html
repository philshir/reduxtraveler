How to Get There

From Mobile (52 miles): Take N Water St and Conti St to Government St. Take I-10 E and Baldwin Beach Express to Co Rd 32 in Baldwin County. Take AL-59 S to destination in Gulf Shores.

Overview

Gulf Shores is a resort city on the Gulf of Mexico in southern Alabama, with 32 miles of sugar-white sand beaches along the Alabama Gulf Coast. It is known for its warm, clear water, its family-friendly vacation atmosphere, its seafood restaurants, and Gulf State Park, a large state park with a long fishing pier, trails, and a lake. Together with neighboring Orange Beach, it is Alabama's main beach destination and attracts millions of visitors every year, many from the Midwest.

Visitors come to Gulf Shores for the beaches, for fishing and boating in the Gulf and the bays, and for attractions such as the Gulf State Park pier, the Alabama Gulf Coast Zoo, and the amusement parks and mini-golf courses. They also come for seafood, live music at venues such as the Hangout, and history at Fort Morgan, and it is about an hour from Mobile and Pensacola.

History

The Gulf Shores area was home to Native American peoples for thousands of years, and in the nineteenth century it was a quiet, isolated stretch of barrier island and peninsula, with Fort Morgan guarding the entrance to Mobile Bay. The Gulf Shores area was largely undeveloped until the 1950s, when a bridge and road were built, and the city of Gulf Shores was incorporated in 1958. Hurricanes, including Frederic in 1979, Ivan in 2004, and Sally in 2020, have struck the coast and led to rebuilding, and today the city is a year-round resort community with large condominium towers and a growing population.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The beaches are the main attraction, especially the public beach at the Gulf Place and the beaches of Gulf State Park, which has a 1,540-foot fishing pier, one of the longest on the Gulf Coast. The Hangout is a beachfront restaurant and music venue, home to the Hangout Music Festival in May, and the Alabama Gulf Coast Zoo is a small local zoo. Fort Morgan, a brick fort from 1834 on the western tip of the peninsula, played a role in the Battle of Mobile Bay in 1864, and a ferry runs from there to Dauphin Island.

Culture and Museums

Gulf Shores has fewer traditional museums than other cities, but it has an active events calendar. The Hangout Music Festival draws top acts each May, and the National Shrimp Festival in nearby Gulf Shores each October is one of the biggest events on the coast. The Gulf Shores Museum shows local history, and Fort Morgan has a museum with artifacts from its history. The Gulf Coast also has a tradition of seafood restaurants and the Flora-Bama bar on the state line.

Parks

Gulf State Park covers more than 6,000 acres with 2.5 miles of beach, freshwater lakes, a nature center, a campground, and more than 25 miles of trails for biking and walking. Bon Secour National Wildlife Refuge, to the west, protects some of the last undeveloped beach on the Alabama coast and is a stop for migratory birds, and the Backcountry Trail in Gulf State Park is popular with cyclists. The Gulf Shores Beach has lifeguards and facilities in season.

Seasons and Weather

Gulf Shores has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F and warm water, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F. The beach season runs from spring break in March to October, and the water is warm enough for swimming from May to October. Hurricane season runs from June to November, and visitors should watch the forecast.
