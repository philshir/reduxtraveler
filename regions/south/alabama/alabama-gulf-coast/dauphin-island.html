How to Get There

From Mobile (38 miles): Take I-10 W and AL-193 S to destination in Dauphin Island. Turn left.

Overview

Dauphin Island is a small barrier island town at the mouth of Mobile Bay in southern Alabama, about 30 miles south of the city of Mobile, and it is known for its quiet beaches, its historic fort, and its role as one of the best bird-watching sites on the Gulf Coast. The island is only about 14 miles long and has fewer than 2,000 year-round residents, and it is a more relaxed and natural alternative to the busy resorts of Gulf Shores and Orange Beach.

Visitors come to Dauphin Island for the Fort Gaines historic site, the Estuarium at the Dauphin Island Sea Lab, and the beaches. They also come for bird watching during the spring and fall migrations at the Audubon Bird Sanctuary, for fishing and shelling, and for the ferry to Fort Morgan, and it is a good day trip from Mobile or Gulf Shores.

History

The island was home to Native Americans who left large shell middens, and French explorers landed here in 1699, naming it Massacre Island after finding human bones. The island served as the first capital of French Louisiana, and it was later renamed Dauphine Island for the wife of the French dauphin. Fort Gaines, built in the 1850s at the eastern end of the island, played a role in the Battle of Mobile Bay in 1864, when Admiral David Farragut's fleet entered the bay. In the twentieth century the island became a beach community, and Hurricane Frederic in 1979 and Katrina in 2005 caused severe damage and led to major rebuilding.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Fort Gaines is a well-preserved brick fort with tunnels, cannons, and a museum, and it is one of the best-preserved Civil War forts on the Gulf. The Dauphin Island Sea Lab is a marine research center with an aquarium and nature trails, and the Estuarium shows the habitats of Mobile Bay. The Audubon Bird Sanctuary has boardwalks through woods and marshes, and the Dauphin Island Beach and Pier offer views of the Gulf. A ferry runs across the bay to Fort Morgan.

Culture and Museums

The Fort Gaines museum has exhibits on the Civil War and the Battle of Mobile Bay, and the Estuarium tells the story of the environment of the Mobile Bay and the Gulf of Mexico. The island's events include the Alabama Deep Sea Fishing Rodeo, the Dauphin Island Race, and a Mardi Gras parade. The island is also well known as a destination for Alabama nature lovers.

Parks

The Audubon Bird Sanctuary is one of the best places in the country for watching migrating songbirds in spring, and it has a lake with alligators and a boardwalk through the dunes. The beaches along the Gulf have shells and sand dunes, and the Bay side has calm water for kayaking. Dauphin Island Park and Campground has camping, a nature trail, and a beach, and the island is also a popular fishing destination for flounder and speckled trout.

Seasons and Weather

Dauphin Island has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F. The best time for bird watching is in April and in the fall, and the beach season is from May to October. Hurricane season runs from June to November, and the island is vulnerable to storm surge.
