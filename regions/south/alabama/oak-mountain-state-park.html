How to Get There

From Birmingham, AL (20 mi): Take I-65 S to Exit 246, turn left onto AL-119 S, then right onto State Park Rd and follow signs to the main entrance on Terrace Dr.

Overview

Oak Mountain State Park is Alabama's largest state park, with roughly 10,000 acres of forested ridges, lakes and trails just south of Birmingham. It was created to protect a large block of ridge-and-valley forest close to the state's biggest metropolitan area and to give residents a place for outdoor recreation. The park is known for its network of trails, which are popular with hikers, trail runners, mountain bikers and horseback riders, and for Peavine Falls, Double Oak Lake and the Alabama Wildlife Center. It also has a golf course, a swimming beach, a BMX track, an archery range, camping and cabins, making it one of the most visited parks in the state.

Access

The park is near Pelham, about 20 miles south of downtown Birmingham, and is easy to reach from I-65 and AL-119. Most visitors drive, and the main entrance is off State Park Road, with an entrance fee collected at the gate. The park is large, and driving is needed to reach different areas such as the lakes, trailheads, golf course and campgrounds. Trails and facilities are busiest on spring and fall weekends, so arrive early to find parking.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the best seasons for hiking, biking and events, with mild temperatures, spring wildflowers and fall color on the ridges. Summers are hot and humid, and the swimming beach and lake activities become the main attractions. Peavine Falls is strongest after rain, in late winter and spring. Winter is quiet, with cool days and open views through the bare trees, and heavily used trails can be muddy after rain, so check conditions before riding.

Things to Do

Explore Alabama's Largest State Park

Oak Mountain State Park is Alabama's largest state park and offers a wide variety of outdoor activities, including hiking, mountain biking, camping, fishing, swimming, horseback riding, and wildlife viewing. Its diverse landscapes of forests, ridges, lakes, and streams make it one of the state's most popular outdoor destinations.

Visit the Alabama Wildlife Center and Treetop Nature Trail

The park is home to the Alabama Wildlife Center, which rehabilitates injured native birds and provides educational opportunities for visitors. Nearby, the Treetop Nature Trail features elevated boardwalks, wildlife exhibits, and scenic views designed to connect visitors with the natural environment.

Enjoy the Lakes and Recreation Areas

Oak Mountain features several lakes that provide opportunities for kayaking, canoeing, paddleboarding, fishing, swimming, and relaxing along the shoreline. The park also offers golf, archery, and family-friendly recreation areas throughout its extensive grounds.

Top Trails

King's Chair Trail

Length: Approximately 4.0 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 900 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

One of the park's most popular hikes, this trail climbs to King's Chair, a dramatic rock outcrop that provides sweeping views of the surrounding valley and forested ridges. The overlook is considered one of the best viewpoints in the park.

Red Road Trail

Length: Approximately 3.0 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This versatile trail is popular with hikers, trail runners, and mountain bikers. The route winds through forests and rolling terrain while connecting many of the park's recreation areas and trail systems.

Visitor Centers

The Oak Mountain Interpretive Center offers exhibits and nature programs about the park's ecology and history, and it is a good place to pick up trail maps. The Alabama Wildlife Center, which cares for injured birds of prey and has an adjacent Treetop Nature Trail, operates its own visitor facilities within the park. The park office and gatehouse also provide maps and current information, and hours vary by season.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers campsites, cabins and group camping, and reservations are recommended for spring, fall and holiday weekends. Outside the park, Pelham and Alabaster, just a few minutes away, have many chain hotels and restaurants. Birmingham, about 20 miles north, offers the widest range of hotels, from downtown properties to suburban chain hotels, and Hoover is also a short drive away.
