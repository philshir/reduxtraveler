How to Get There

From Huntsville, AL (75 mi): Take US-431 S to AL-75 S, continue to Fort Payne, then follow signs to AL-35 and the preserve entrance on Little River Trail NE.

Overview

Little River Canyon National Preserve is a National Park Service unit on top of Lookout Mountain in northeastern Alabama, protecting one of the deepest canyons east of the Mississippi. The Little River flows for much of its length along the top of the mountain before dropping into a sandstone gorge, making it one of the few rivers in the country to flow for so long atop a mountain. Congress established the preserve in 1992 to protect the canyon, its waterfalls and its rare plants and animals, and it is managed with an emphasis on conservation and recreation. Visitors come for the scenic Little River Canyon Rim Parkway and its overlooks, Little River Falls, hiking, rock climbing, kayaking and swimming.

Access

The preserve is near the towns of Fort Payne and Mentone in DeKalb County. Most visitors use the Little River Canyon Rim Parkway (AL-176), which runs along the western rim and links the main overlooks, and AL-35 leads to the Little River Canyon Center near the preserve's northern end. Canyon Mouth Park, at the south end of the canyon, provides river access and a picnic area. From Huntsville the drive is roughly an hour and a half, and Chattanooga, Tennessee is about the same. A personal vehicle is needed, as there is no public transportation.

Seasons

Waterfalls are strongest in late winter and spring after rain, and may dwindle to a trickle in late summer and fall. Spring also brings wildflowers and blooming mountain laurel along the rim. Summer is warm and humid, and Canyon Mouth Park is popular for swimming and picnicking. Fall is a favorite for colorful hardwoods along the rim drive, and winter offers clear views through bare trees, along with occasional ice that can affect roads and trails. Whitewater paddling in the canyon depends heavily on water levels and is for experienced paddlers only.

Things to Do

Explore Little River Canyon

Little River Canyon National Preserve protects one of the deepest river-cut canyons in the eastern United States. Visitors can enjoy spectacular overlooks, dramatic cliffs, waterfalls, and scenic drives while experiencing the rugged beauty of northeast Alabama's Appalachian Plateau.

Visit Little River Falls

Little River Falls is the preserve's most iconic attraction. The waterfall plunges into the canyon below and provides excellent opportunities for sightseeing, photography, and enjoying the area's unique geology. The falls can be viewed from several easily accessible overlooks.

Enjoy Outdoor Adventure

The preserve offers exceptional opportunities for hiking, rock climbing, kayaking, fishing, wildlife viewing, birdwatching, and nature photography. The Little River, which flows for much of its length atop Lookout Mountain before entering the canyon, creates a landscape unlike any other in the Southeast.

Top Trails

Eberhart Point Trail

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This short but scenic trail descends into the canyon and provides spectacular views of Little River, the surrounding cliffs, and one of the preserve's most dramatic overlooks. It is a favorite destination for photographers and sightseers.

Little River Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This family-friendly trail provides access to viewpoints overlooking Little River Falls and portions of the canyon. Visitors can enjoy close-up views of the waterfall while learning about the area's geology and natural history.

Visitor Centers

The Little River Canyon Center on AL-35 is the preserve's main visitor facility, with exhibits on the canyon's geology, plants and wildlife, and rangers who can provide maps and advice. It is open on a posted schedule, which is usually Tuesday through Sunday, so check the National Park Service website before you go. Canyon Mouth Park has picnic facilities and restrooms, and the overlooks along the rim parkway have interpretive signs.

Camping and Lodging

The preserve has no developed campground or lodge, so most visitors camp or stay overnight nearby. DeSoto State Park, just a few miles away, has a lodge, chalets, cabins and campgrounds. Fort Payne, about 5 miles away, has chain hotels and restaurants, and Mentone, on the mountain, is known for its cabins, inns and bed and breakfasts. Gadsden, Chattanooga and Huntsville offer additional lodging within about 1 to 1.5 hours.
