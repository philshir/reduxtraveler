How to Get There

From Huntsville, AL (65 mi): Take US-431 S to AL-227 S, continue through Geraldine, then follow County Roads 402 and 19 to the park entrance on County Road 174.

Overview

Buck's Pocket State Park is an Alabama state park of more than 2,000 acres in the Appalachian foothills of northeastern Alabama, built around a rugged, tree-covered canyon, or "pocket," carved by South Sauty Creek. It is known for the view from Point Rock Overlook, a network of hiking trails of more than 15 miles, and Alabama's first dedicated State Park off-highway-vehicle trail. The park was set aside by the state to protect this remote canyon landscape and make it available for outdoor recreation, and it remains one of the quieter parks in the Alabama system. Visitors come to hike to overlooks and small waterfalls, ride the OHV trail, fish, camp and watch birds, since the park is part of the North Alabama Birding Trail.

Access

The park is in a rural part of northeastern Alabama near the community of Grove Oak, about an hour's drive from Huntsville and roughly 15 to 20 miles from Scottsboro and Guntersville. The final miles are on county roads, so it is wise to follow the signs to the park entrance rather than relying only on a navigation app. There is no public transportation, and a personal vehicle is needed. The park office is open limited days, so plan around its posted hours, and bring food and water because services nearby are sparse.

Seasons

Spring brings wildflowers and the best flow in the park's small waterfalls, particularly after rain, while summer is hot and humid, with shady creekside trails the most comfortable choice. Fall is a favorite season, as the hardwood forests turn color and the overlooks look out over the canyon. In winter the leaves are down and the views open up, though temperatures can be cold, and ice or snow is possible. Trails and the OHV trail can be wet or closed after heavy rain, so check with the park before you go.

Things to Do

Hike the park’s trail system: Explore more than 15 miles of hiking trails ranging from easy nature walks to challenging canyon hikes, with routes leading to overlooks, waterfalls, streams, and forest scenery.

Visit Point Rock Overlook: Enjoy one of the park’s signature viewpoints with panoramic views of the canyon, surrounding mountains, and hardwood forests.

Explore South Sauty Creek: Discover the rugged landscape carved by water over millions of years while enjoying peaceful creekside scenery.

OHV riding: Ride Alabama State Parks' first dedicated off-highway vehicle trail featuring approximately 6 miles of forest and ridge terrain.

Visitor Centers

Buck's Pocket does not have a large visitor center. The park office handles registration, camping check-in and information, and staff can provide trail maps and advice on conditions. Office hours are limited, and the office is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays, so plan to pick up maps in advance or download one before arriving.

Camping and Lodging

The park campground offers about two dozen RV sites with hookups and a number of primitive tent sites, with reservations recommended on spring and fall weekends. There is no lodge in the park, so most visitors who want a room stay in Scottsboro or Guntersville, each roughly 15 to 20 miles away, where chain hotels and motels are available. Lake Guntersville State Park, near the town of Guntersville, has a lodge, cabins and a campground, and Huntsville, about an hour away, offers the widest selection of hotels.
