Overview

Frankfort is the capital of Kentucky, on the Kentucky River in the central part of the state, and it is one of the smallest state capitals in the United States, with a population of about 28,000. It is known for its Beaux-Arts State Capitol, for its historic downtown, for the bourbon produced at distilleries such as Buffalo Trace, and for being the final resting place of Daniel Boone. The hills and river setting give the city a scenic backdrop.

Visitors come to Frankfort for the Kentucky State Capitol and the Old State Capitol, for the Kentucky Historical Society and its museums, and for distillery tours at Buffalo Trace, Castle & Key, and Woodford Reserve nearby. They also come for historic homes such as Liberty Hall, for the views from Fort Hill, and for the grave of Daniel Boone.

History

Frankfort was founded in 1786 by General James Wilkinson on land at a ford of the Kentucky River where Stephen Frank was killed by Native Americans, and the name is said to come from "Frank's Ford." It was chosen as the capital in 1792, when Kentucky became the 15th state, because of its central location, and it grew as a river port and a center of government. During the Civil War, the city was occupied by Confederate troops for a short time in 1862, and the Battle of Frankfort was fought on its outskirts. The distilling industry grew in the area in the nineteenth century, and the Buffalo Trace Distillery, founded in 1869, is one of the oldest in the country.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Kentucky State Capitol, completed in 1910, has a dome modeled on the Capitol in Washington and a floral clock on the lawn, and it is open for free tours. The Old State Capitol, built in 1830 in the Greek Revival style, has a famous freestanding spiral staircase, and the Governor's Mansion and Old Governor's Mansion are also open for tours. Daniel Boone's grave is in the Frankfort Cemetery, with views of the river, and River View Park is a riverfront park.

Culture and Museums

The Kentucky Historical Society operates the Thomas D. Clark Center for Kentucky History, the Old State Capitol, and the Kentucky Military History Museum, and its collections cover the state's past. Liberty Hall was the home of U.S. Senator John Brown and is now a museum with historic gardens. The Capital City Museum, and the Josephine Sculpture Park have more arts and history, and Frankfort offers bourbon tastings and tours at nearby distilleries.

Parks

Leslie Morris Park on Fort Hill has a Civil War fort site with views over the city and trails through woods, and River View Park has a boat ramp and a trail along the Kentucky River. Cove Spring Park has a nature preserve with a spring and trails, and the Kentucky River offers paddling and fishing. The Franklin County Greenway and the Capital Avenue Walk are popular for walking.

Seasons and Weather

Frankfort has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are cool, with highs in the 40s°F and about 10 inches of snow. Spring and fall are the best times to visit, with fall color in the river hills.
