Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park

★5.0
  1. South

Memorial site preserving early American frontier life through historic structures, interpretive trails, and exhibits honoring the formative years of a U.S. president.

Location:
2995 Lincoln Farm Road, Hodgenville, KY 42748

How to Get There

From Bardstown, KY (30 mi): Take US-31E S toward Hodgenville, then turn left onto Lincoln Farm Road to reach the park entrance.

Overview

Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park commemorates the Kentucky beginnings of the 16th president and is operated by the National Park Service. Lincoln was born in 1809 on Sinking Spring Farm near Hodgenville, and a neoclassical Memorial Building, reached by 56 steps for the years of his life, now shelters a symbolic log cabin representing his birthplace. Philanthropists of the Lincoln Farm Association preserved the site in the early 1900s, and the property became a national park in 1916 before being redesignated a national historical park in 2009. The park also includes the Boyhood Home Unit at Knob Creek, where the family lived for several years. Visitors come to visit the memorial and cabin, see the Sinking Spring that gave the farm its name, and walk the grounds of Lincoln's earliest home.

Access

The Birthplace Unit is about three miles south of Hodgenville off KY-61 on Lincoln Farm Road, and the Boyhood Home Unit is a separate site along US-31E, roughly 10 miles from Hodgenville. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, with Elizabethtown about 15 miles away and Bardstown about 30 miles from the main unit. From Bardstown, take US-31E south toward Hodgenville and follow the signs to Lincoln Farm Road. Admission is free, and dogs on short leashes are welcome outdoors but not inside buildings.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and spring is especially pleasant, with wildflowers and green woodlands around the Sinking Spring. Summers are warm and humid, and the 56 steps to the Memorial Building can feel long in midday heat. Fall brings colorful foliage and cooler weather that suits walking the trails. Winter is quiet, with fewer visitors, though the visitor center and memorial stay open. The park is closed on New Year's Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, and the Knob Creek visitor center is open only seasonally.

Things to Do

Climb to the Memorial Building

The marble and granite Memorial Building stands at the top of 56 steps, one for each year of Lincoln's life. Inside, a symbolic log cabin represents the birthplace cabin, and the structure is a National Park Service landmark in its own right.

See Sinking Spring

A short walk leads to the spring that gave Sinking Spring Farm its name and supplied water to the Lincoln family. Paths and interpretive signs explain the farm and its landscape.

Explore the Visitor Center

The visitor center has exhibits on Lincoln's early life, frontier Kentucky and his legacy, along with a film and ranger information. Junior Ranger activities are available for children.

Visit the Boyhood Home at Knob Creek

The separate Knob Creek unit, a short drive away, preserves the farm where Lincoln spent part of his childhood, with a reconstructed cabin and trails across the valley.

Visitor Centers

The main visitor center at the Birthplace Unit is open daily, typically from 9am to 5pm, and offers museum exhibits on Lincoln's early life and the frontier, a park film, a bookstore and Junior Ranger materials. The Memorial Building, with the symbolic cabin, is open daily until about 4:30pm. At the Knob Creek unit, the Knob Creek Tavern Visitor Center is a smaller, seasonal facility with exhibits on Lincoln's boyhood there. Hours can change, so confirm them with the National Park Service before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the park. Hodgenville, about three miles from the Birthplace Unit, has limited lodging and restaurants, while Elizabethtown, about 15 miles away, has a broad choice of chain hotels. Bardstown, about 30 minutes away, offers historic inns and bed-and-breakfasts, and My Old Kentucky Home State Park there has a campground. Louisville, roughly an hour north, has a large selection of hotels and other services.
