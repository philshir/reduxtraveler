Camp Nelson National Monument

How to Get There

From Lexington, KY (20 mi): Take US-27 S toward Nicholasville, turn right onto KY-34 W, then left onto Old Danville Loop 2 Road to reach the monument entrance.

Overview

Camp Nelson National Monument in Jessamine County, Kentucky, preserves a Union Army supply depot established in 1863 that became one of the most important recruitment and training centers for African American soldiers in the Civil War. Thousands of enslaved men enlisted here in the United States Colored Troops, and their wives and children sought refuge at the camp, making it a pivotal site in the story of emancipation. The land includes earthworks, historic buildings, a national cemetery and miles of interpretive trails, and it is also part of the National Underground Railroad Network to Freedom. The site was proclaimed a national monument in 2018 and is managed by the National Park Service, and visitors come to learn about the soldiers and refugees who found freedom here.

Access

The monument is in Jessamine County just south of Nicholasville and about 20 miles south of Lexington, and it is reached by car. From Lexington, take US-27 south through Nicholasville and follow the signs to the monument entrance. The drive typically takes 30 to 40 minutes from downtown Lexington. Admission is free, and pets are welcome on the grounds and trails but not inside buildings.

Seasons

The grounds are open daily from sunrise to sunset year-round. Spring and fall are the best times to walk the trails, with mild temperatures, spring wildflowers and fall color across the rolling hills. Summers are hot and humid, and much of the landscape is open, so plan for morning visits and bring water. Winters are cold and quiet, and trails can be muddy or icy. The visitor center, museum and barracks are open only Wednesday through Sunday, so plan accordingly, and seasonal ranger programs and special events are offered at various times of year.

Things to Do

Explore the Historic Camp Nelson Site

Camp Nelson National Monument preserves one of the most significant Civil War recruitment and training centers for African American soldiers in the United States. Originally established as a Union Army supply depot, Camp Nelson became a major center for the enlistment of formerly enslaved men into the United States Colored Troops and played an important role in the fight for freedom and citizenship.

Visit the Visitor Center and Museum

The visitor center features exhibits, artifacts, films, and interpretive displays that explore the history of Camp Nelson, the Civil War, emancipation, and the experiences of African American soldiers and refugee families who lived at the site.

Learn About Emancipation and Civil Rights History

Visitors can explore reconstructed and preserved historic areas, including fortifications and interpretive landscapes that tell the story of Camp Nelson's transformation into a center of freedom for thousands of formerly enslaved people seeking refuge behind Union lines.

Top Trails

Heritage Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This interpretive trail connects key historic locations throughout the monument and provides information about military operations, recruitment efforts, refugee settlements, and daily life at Camp Nelson during the Civil War.

Fort Jones Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors can walk to the remains of Fort Jones and other defensive features while learning about the military significance of Camp Nelson and its role in protecting Union supply lines and personnel.

Visitor Centers

The visitor center and museum provide the best introduction to Camp Nelson, with exhibits, artifacts, a short orientation film and information on the soldiers, refugees and abolitionists connected to the camp. A reconstructed barracks building on the grounds shows how soldiers lived. The visitor center, museum and barracks are open Wednesday through Sunday from 9am to 5pm and closed Monday and Tuesday, while the grounds and trails are open daily from sunrise to sunset. Junior Ranger materials are available for children.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the monument. The closest hotels and restaurants are in Nicholasville, about 6 miles away, and in Lexington, about 20 miles to the north, which has a full range of lodging and dining options. Danville and Harrodsburg lie to the south and west, and the Shaker Village of Pleasant Hill, near Harrodsburg, offers historic lodging within about an hour. The Kentucky Horse Park Campground in Lexington is a convenient camping option within 30 to 40 minutes.
