Big Bone Lick State Historic Site

How to Get There

From Cincinnati, OH (27 mi): Take I-71 S/I-75 S toward Lexington/Louisville, then take exit 175 for KY-338/Richwood Road; follow KY-338 W for about 7 miles to Beaver Road and turn left to reach the park entrance.

Overview

Big Bone Lick State Historic Site in Boone County, Kentucky, is known as the "Birthplace of American Vertebrate Paleontology." For thousands of years, natural salt springs here drew mammoths, mastodons, giant ground sloths, bison and other Ice Age animals, and their bones accumulated in the marshy ground. Naturalists collected fossils here in the 1700s, and President Thomas Jefferson sponsored William Clark's 1807 excavation of the site, which shipped bones east for study. Kentucky now preserves the site as a state park, where visitors can see the salt springs, a recreated Ice Age marsh, a small bison herd and a museum of fossils and history. People come to learn about Ice Age life and early American science, to hike short trails and to camp.

Access

The site is in northern Kentucky, about 15 miles southwest of Florence and about 27 miles from downtown Cincinnati, and nearly everyone arrives by car. From I-75, take the Richwood Road (KY-338) exit and follow KY-338 west for roughly seven miles, then turn onto Beaver Road to reach the park. Free parking is available near the museum and visitor center. Pets are welcome in outdoor areas but not inside buildings.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the most pleasant times to visit, with wildflowers, migrating birds and fall color along the trails. Summers are hot, humid and buggy, so bring water and insect repellent, but it is also the best time for the museum's full schedule. Winters are cold, with the museum open reduced hours, and the trails can be muddy, icy or snowy. Spring rains often make low areas near the creek and springs wet. The park grounds are open daily year-round, and the bison are visible in any season.

Things to Do

See the Ice Age Fossil Site

Big Bone Lick State Historic Site is known as the "Birthplace of American Vertebrate Paleontology." The site became famous for the discovery of Ice Age fossils, including mammoths, mastodons, giant ground sloths, and other prehistoric animals that were attracted to the area's natural salt springs.

Visit the Museum and Discovery Trail

The park's museum features exhibits on paleontology, geology, archaeology, and the history of fossil discoveries at Big Bone Lick. Visitors can learn about the scientific expeditions that helped shape America's understanding of prehistoric life and explore interpretive displays throughout the park.

See the Bison Herd

A herd of American bison resides within the park, providing visitors with a glimpse of an animal that once roamed Kentucky's landscapes. Observation areas allow guests to safely view and photograph these iconic animals while learning about their ecological importance.

Top Trails

Discovery Trail

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This interpretive trail leads visitors through the fossil discovery area and natural salt springs while explaining the site's paleontological significance, ancient wildlife, and geological history.

Gobbler's Trace Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic trail travels through woodlands and natural habitats within the park, offering opportunities for wildlife viewing, birdwatching, and experiencing the landscape that helped attract prehistoric animals to Big Bone Lick.

Visitor Centers

The park's museum and visitor center is the best first stop, with exhibits on Ice Age mammals, the paleontological history of Big Bone Lick and the people who studied it. From March 16 to November 12, the museum is open daily from 8:30am to 4pm, and from November 13 to March 15 it is open Monday through Saturday from 9am to 3pm. Admission to the park and museum is free. Staff can provide trail maps and information on programs and the bison herd.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground, which offers a convenient base for a weekend visit. Lodging and restaurants are also available in Florence, about 15 miles away, with additional chain hotels in the Cincinnati and northern Kentucky area, including Covington and Newport, about 30 to 45 minutes away. General Butler State Resort Park in Carrollton, roughly 45 minutes to an hour away, has a lodge, cottages and a campground. Rooms near Cincinnati can fill during major events, so book ahead.
