John James Audubon State Park

★5.0

Woodland park featuring a museum, nature center, hiking trails, wetlands, and recreational lake with opportunities for birding, fishing, and wildlife observation.

How to Get There

From Evansville, IN (8 mi): Take Veterans Memorial Parkway E to US-41 S, cross the Ohio River into Kentucky, and continue south for about 5 miles; the park entrance will be on your right.

Overview

John James Audubon State Park in Henderson, Kentucky, honors the naturalist and artist John James Audubon, who lived in Henderson from about 1810 to 1819 and studied and painted many of the birds that later appeared in his famous Birds of America. The park is known for its Audubon museum and nature center, which hold one of the world's largest collections of Audubon's artwork and personal items, in a stone building dating to the Depression era. Kentucky established the park as a state park to commemorate Audubon, and it now includes a lake, wooded trails, a campground and a 649-acre wetlands area. Visitors come for the museum, birdwatching, hiking and a quiet nature setting just outside Henderson.

Access

The park is just north of Henderson, Kentucky, on US-41, about two miles from town and roughly 8 miles from Evansville, Indiana. Most visitors arrive by car; from Evansville, take US-41 south across the Ohio River into Kentucky and follow the signs to the park. The grounds are open daily from dawn to dusk, and parking is available near the museum and nature center. Pets are allowed in the park, but only on the Eagle Glen Pet Trail and the Audubon Wetlands Trail among the trails.

Seasons

Spring migration is a highlight, when warblers and other songbirds pass through the woods and the wildflowers bloom. Summers are hot and humid, with insects and thick foliage, but the lake and campground are busiest then. Fall brings cooler weather, colorful hardwoods and the return of migrating birds, and winter offers quiet trails, waterfowl on the lake and wetlands, and the possibility of bald eagles. The park grounds are open year-round, while the museum is closed Sundays and Mondays and the trails can be muddy after rain.

Things to Do

Visit the Audubon Museum

John James Audubon State Park is dedicated to the life and legacy of famed naturalist, artist, and ornithologist John James Audubon. The park's museum features one of the world's largest collections of Audubon artwork, exhibits on his life and travels, and displays highlighting his contributions to bird conservation and wildlife illustration.

Explore Nature and Wildlife Viewing Areas

The park's forests, lakes, and natural habitats provide opportunities for birdwatching, wildlife observation, photography, and environmental education. Visitors can experience landscapes similar to those that inspired Audubon's studies of North American birds and wildlife.

Enjoy Recreational Activities

The park offers fishing, boating, kayaking, picnicking, and nature programs throughout the year. Its scenic setting in western Kentucky makes it an ideal destination for families, outdoor enthusiasts, and anyone interested in natural history.

Top Trails

Woodpecker Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail winds through mature forests and offers opportunities to observe native birds, wildflowers, and wildlife while learning about the ecosystems that inspired Audubon's work.

Warbler Road Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Named for its abundant birdlife, this scenic trail explores woodlands and natural habitats throughout the park, making it a favorite destination for hikers, birdwatchers, and nature photographers.

Visitor Centers

The Audubon Museum and Nature Center is the park's main visitor facility. The museum displays original Audubon paintings, prints, books and personal items, and the nature center has a wildlife observation area looking over feeders, along with access to boardwalks and trails. The museum and nature center are open Tuesday through Saturday from 10am to 5pm, and admission fees apply. Staff can provide trail maps, birding information and details on programs and festivals.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground, with nightly rates that depend on the type of site. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Henderson, about two miles away, and in Evansville, Indiana, about 8 miles away, which has a large range of lodging. Owensboro, about 30 miles east, is another option for hotels. Campsites can fill during the spring and fall birding seasons and the Ohio Valley Birding Festival, so reserve ahead.
