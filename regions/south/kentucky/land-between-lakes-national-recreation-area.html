How to Get There

From Paducah, KY (35 mi): Take I-24 E to US-62 E, then follow KY-453 S to the North Welcome Station entrance at Land Between the Lakes.

Overview

The Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area is a 170,000-acre peninsula of forest, wetlands and open land in western Kentucky and Tennessee, lying between Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley. It was created by the Tennessee Valley Authority in 1963 as a demonstration in outdoor recreation and environmental education, and it has been managed by the U.S. Forest Service since 1998. The recreation area is popular for its hundreds of miles of shoreline, boating and fishing, hiking, biking and horseback trails, and one of the largest developed campground systems in the region. Visitors also come for the Elk & Bison Prairie, the Golden Pond Planetarium and Observatory, the Woodlands Nature Station, and The Homeplace 1850s Working Farm, a living-history site with costumed interpreters.

Access

Land Between the Lakes runs about 40 miles north to south, with the Woodlands Trace National Scenic Byway, known as The Trace, running its length. The North Welcome Station is near Grand Rivers, Kentucky, and can be reached from Paducah by taking I-24 to US-62 and then KY-453 south. The South Welcome Station is near Dover, Tennessee, on the Tennessee side. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and distances inside the recreation area are long, so plan on driving between attractions.

Seasons

Spring brings wildflowers, wild turkey activity and good fishing, with mild weather that is ideal for hiking and biking. Summer is the peak season for boating, swimming and camping, but it can be hot and humid, with biting insects near the water. Fall offers colorful foliage along The Trace and comfortable hiking temperatures, while winter is a quiet time known for bald eagle watching along the lakes. Many attractions, including The Homeplace and some campgrounds and programs, operate seasonally and are typically open from spring through fall, so check current hours before visiting.

Things to Do

Explore the Lakes and Water Recreation

Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area spans more than 170,000 acres between Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley, offering exceptional opportunities for boating, fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, swimming, and wildlife viewing. The area's extensive shoreline and forests make it one of the premier outdoor recreation destinations in Kentucky and Tennessee.

Visit the Elk & Bison Prairie

One of the recreation area's most popular attractions is the Elk & Bison Prairie, a large enclosed habitat where visitors can drive through and observe herds of elk and bison roaming in a natural setting. The prairie provides a unique opportunity to experience wildlife that once inhabited much of North America.

Discover Nature and History

Visitors can explore attractions such as The Homeplace 1850s Working Farm, the Woodlands Nature Station, scenic overlooks, historic sites, and educational exhibits. These destinations offer insight into the region's natural ecosystems, cultural heritage, and pioneer life.

Top Trails

Hematite Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 2.2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic trail circles Hematite Lake and travels through forests, wetlands, and wildlife habitat. It is a popular route for hiking, birdwatching, photography, and enjoying the peaceful natural beauty of the recreation area.

North/South Trail

Length: Approximately 58 miles total
Elevation Gain: Varies by section
Difficulty: Moderate

The North/South Trail is the recreation area's premier long-distance hiking route, traversing forests, lakeshores, ridges, and backcountry landscapes. Hikers can choose shorter day hikes or complete longer segments to experience the diverse scenery of Land Between the Lakes.

Visitor Centers

The Golden Pond Visitor Center, near the center of the recreation area, offers exhibits, information and the Golden Pond Planetarium and Observatory. The North and South Welcome Stations at the two entrances provide maps, trip-planning help and current information. The Woodlands Nature Station focuses on live native animals and nature education, and The Homeplace has its own interpretive staff. Hours vary by season, so confirm them before you visit.

Camping and Lodging

Land Between the Lakes has developed campgrounds such as Hillman Ferry, Piney and Energy Lake, as well as primitive and backcountry camping areas, boat-in campsites and horse camping. Reservations are strongly recommended in summer. For lodging outside the recreation area, Grand Rivers has hotels, cabins and inns close to the north entrance, and Kentucky Dam Village State Resort Park in Gilbertsville and Lake Barkley State Resort Park near Cadiz offer lodge rooms, cottages and campgrounds. Murray, Cadiz, Benton and Paducah in Kentucky and Dover in Tennessee all have additional hotels within about an hour.
