Overview

Louisville is the largest city in Kentucky, on the Ohio River at the Falls of the Ohio, and it is world-famous as the home of the Kentucky Derby, held every year on the first Saturday in May at Churchill Downs. It is also known as the birthplace of boxing legend Muhammad Ali, as the home of the Louisville Slugger baseball bat, and as the center of the bourbon industry. Louisville is a mix of Southern hospitality, Midwestern friendliness, and a creative food and arts scene.

Visitors come to Louisville for the Derby and the Kentucky Derby Museum, for the Louisville Slugger Museum & Factory, and for the Muhammad Ali Center. They also come for bourbon on Whiskey Row and the Urban Bourbon Trail, for the Waterfront Park, the Victorian homes of Old Louisville, and the restaurants of NuLu and Butchertown.

History

Louisville was founded in 1778 by George Rogers Clark and named for King Louis XVI of France in gratitude for his support in the American Revolution, and it grew around the Falls of the Ohio, a stretch of rapids that forced boats to stop and unload. It became a major river port and by 1850 was one of the largest cities in the country, with a thriving trade in tobacco, hemp, and whiskey. The first Kentucky Derby was run in 1875 at Churchill Downs, and the city has remained a center for horse racing, bourbon, and manufacturing. Louisville was also an important city in the civil rights era, and the Louisville Slugger bat, made here since 1884, became a symbol of American baseball.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Churchill Downs, with its twin spires, is the home of the Derby, and the Louisville Slugger Museum & Factory has a 120-foot bat leaning against the building. The Big Four Bridge, a former railroad bridge, is now a pedestrian bridge over the Ohio River, and Waterfront Park is a large downtown park with a fountain and playground. The Belle of Louisville, a 1914 steamboat, offers river cruises, and Union Station, 4th Street Live, and Whiskey Row are other downtown landmarks.

Culture and Museums

The Kentucky Derby Museum has exhibits on the history of the race and tours of Churchill Downs, and the Muhammad Ali Center celebrates the life of the boxer and his humanitarian work. The Speed Art Museum is the oldest and largest art museum in Kentucky, and the Frazier History Museum covers the story of the state and the U.S. in a downtown building. The Farmington Historic Home and Cave Hill Cemetery, with graves such as Colonel Sanders, and Waverly Hills Sanatorium, a haunted tuberculosis hospital, are other attractions.

Parks

Louisville is known for its park system, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, which includes Cherokee Park, with trails and a scenic drive, and Iroquois and Shawnee Parks. Waterfront Park is an 85-acre park on the Ohio River with views of Indiana, and the Louisville Loop is a trail system that is planned as a 100-mile loop around the city. The Falls of the Ohio State Park across the river in Indiana has fossil beds, and the Louisville Zoo is a popular family attraction. Thunder Over Louisville, the largest annual fireworks show in the country, opens the Derby Festival each April.

Sports

Louisville's professional team is Racing Louisville FC of the National Women's Soccer League, which plays at Lynn Family Stadium. The city is better known for the Kentucky Derby and for the University of Louisville Cardinals, whose basketball and football games draw large crowds.

Seasons and Weather

Louisville has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are cool, with highs in the 40s°F and about 12 inches of snow. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, and the Derby in early May is the busiest time of the year, so hotels should be booked well in advance.
