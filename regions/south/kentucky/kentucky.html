Overview

Kentucky is an Upland South state of about 4.5 million people, famous for bourbon, horses, bluegrass music, and the world's longest cave system. Frankfort is the capital, Louisville is the largest city, and Lexington is the heart of the horse country.

Visitors come for the Kentucky Derby at Churchill Downs, the Kentucky Bourbon Trail, and Mammoth Cave National Park. Others hike the cliffs and arches of the Red River Gorge, tour the Shaker village at Pleasant Hill, visit Daniel Boone's frontier, or take in the faith-based attractions near Cincinnati.

Geography

Kentucky borders the Ohio River to the north and the Mississippi River to the west, and it has more miles of navigable water than any other state in the contiguous U.S. The Appalachian Cumberland Plateau covers the east, with rugged hollows and ridges, and the rolling Bluegrass region sits in the center, with fertile limestone soil. West of the Bluegrass are the Pennyroyal and the Western Coal Fields, and the Jackson Purchase lies in the far west. Black Mountain, at 4,145 feet (1,263 m), is the highest point.

History

The Shawnee, Cherokee, and other peoples hunted in what is now Kentucky when Daniel Boone and other settlers crossed the Cumberland Gap in the 1770s. Kentucky County, Virginia, became the 15th state on June 1, 1792. A border state during the Civil War, it was the birthplace of both Abraham Lincoln and Jefferson Davis. The state grew on tobacco, coal, horse breeding, and distilling, and Kentucky produces the great majority of the world's bourbon.

Louisville

Louisville, on the Ohio River, is Kentucky's largest city and home to the Kentucky Derby, run on the first Saturday in May at Churchill Downs since 1875. The Kentucky Derby Museum next to the track tells the story of the race, and the city also has the Louisville Slugger Museum & Factory, marked by a 120-foot bat, and the Muhammad Ali Center, honoring the hometown boxing champion. Whiskey Row is the center of bourbon tasting, and the Victorian houses of Old Louisville form one of the largest preserved Victorian districts in the country. Waterfront Park and the Big Four Bridge offer riverfront walks, the Speed Art Museum is the state's largest art museum, and the Belle of Louisville paddlewheel steamboat still cruises the river. Cave Hill Cemetery is the resting place of Colonel Sanders.

Lexington

Lexington is the "Horse Capital of the World," the center of Kentucky's thoroughbred industry and the heart of the Bluegrass region. The Kentucky Horse Park is a working horse farm and equestrian center with the International Museum of the Horse, and Keeneland is a historic racecourse and thoroughbred auction house where visitors can watch morning workouts and spring and fall race meets. The city also has Ashland, the estate of statesman Henry Clay, the Lexington Cemetery, the Arboretum, and the University of Kentucky, whose Wildcats basketball team has one of the most passionate fan bases in college sports. Downtown has a growing food, bourbon, and brewery scene, and the surrounding countryside is lined with miles of black plank fences.

The Bluegrass Region

The Bluegrass region of central Kentucky is a rolling landscape of limestone-rich soil, which produces nutrient-rich pasture grass and strong bones in young horses, and is the reason it became the thoroughbred capital of the world. The famous "blue" grass is actually a type of Poa pratensis that appears bluish-purple when it blooms in spring. Horse farms with white barns, stone walls, and plank fences surround Lexington, Versailles, and Paris, and many welcome visitors for tours. History runs deep in the region, with Fort Boonesborough, where Daniel Boone built a frontier fort in 1775, the Shaker community of Pleasant Hill, and the state capital of Frankfort. Nature is also nearby at Raven Run Nature Sanctuary, the Red River Gorge, and the Muir Valley climbing area.

Kentucky Bourbon Trail

Kentucky produces about 95 percent of the world's bourbon, and the Kentucky Bourbon Trail is a tour of the state's best-known distilleries, with a passport program that rewards visitors for getting stamps. Bourbon must be made in the United States from at least 51 percent corn and aged in new charred oak barrels, and the limestone-filtered water of Kentucky is credited with giving the spirit its character. The trail includes well-known names such as Maker's Mark, Jim Beam, Woodford Reserve, Buffalo Trace, and Four Roses, and the Kentucky Bourbon Trail Craft Tour covers smaller producers. Bardstown, the "Bourbon Capital of the World," has the Oscar Getz Museum of Bourbon History and the nearby My Old Kentucky Home, and Louisville's Whiskey Row and Lexington round out the trail. Visitors must be 21 to taste, and designated drivers are strongly recommended.

National Protected Areas

Kentucky's national park sites cover caves, history, and rivers. Mammoth Cave National Park protects the longest known cave system in the world, with more than 400 miles of mapped passages. Cumberland Gap National Historical Park marks the pass that opened the frontier, and Abraham Lincoln Birthplace National Historical Park preserves his early boyhood home near Hodgenville. Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area, the Big South Fork National River and Recreation Area, and Mill Springs Battlefield National Monument also lie in the state.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Kentucky's state parks include a number of resort parks with lodges and golf, among them Natural Bridge State Resort Park, which has a sandstone arch, and Cumberland Falls State Resort Park, home of the "moonbow." Big Bone Lick is a Pleistocene fossil site, Breaks Interstate Park shares a canyon with Virginia, and Bad Branch Falls is a state nature preserve. Other highlights include Bernheim Forest and Arboretum, Raven Run, Muir Valley, and the Abbey of Gethsemani.

Seasons and When to Visit

Spring is the busiest season, with the Kentucky Derby on the first Saturday in May, blooming redbuds and dogwoods, and horse farm tours. Summer is hot and humid and ideal for lakes, caves, and festivals, while fall brings spectacular foliage in the Red River Gorge and the Bourbon Trail's busiest weeks. Winters are cool, with some snow in the mountains, and caves stay a constant temperature of about 54 degrees all year, making them a good winter or summer escape.
