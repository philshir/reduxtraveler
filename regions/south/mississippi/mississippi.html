Mississippi Travel Guide

Trace the blues' birthplace along Mississippi's Delta, explore historic Natchez mansions, and relax on Gulf Coast beaches near vibrant Jackson.

Places to See in Mississippi

Mississippi Gulf Coast

Area of southern Mississippi along the Gulf of Mexico including the original French settlements in Biloxi and Ocean Springs, as well as Waveland, Bay St. Louis, Pass Christian, Long Beach, Gulfport, D'Iberville, Gautier, and Pascagoula.

Mississippi Delta

Region in the northwestern part of the state of Mississippi, known for its rich cultural heritage, particularly in blues music, and its unique geography as a flat, fertile plain formed by the Mississippi River. The area is often referred to as the "Birthplace of the Blues" due to its significant influence on the development of this genre of music. The Delta is also home to a diverse population and has a complex history, including its role in the Civil Rights Movement. Visitors to the Mississippi Delta can explore historic sites, enjoy live music, and experience the vibrant culture that has shaped this iconic region.

Natchez

City located on the Mississippi River was a prominent city in the antebellum years, a center of cotton planters and Mississippi River trade.

Overview

Mississippi is a Deep South state of about 2.9 million people and the birthplace of the blues, deeply connected to American music, literature, and the civil rights movement. Jackson is the capital and largest city, and the state is named for the Mississippi River, whose Ojibwe name means "great river."

Visitors come to follow the Blues Trail through the Mississippi Delta, to see the Civil War landmarks of Vicksburg and Natchez, and to visit the homes of William Faulkner in Oxford and Elvis Presley in Tupelo. Others come for the beaches and casinos of the Mississippi Gulf Coast and the Natchez Trace Parkway.

Geography

Mississippi lies in the Gulf Coastal Plain, with the Mississippi Alluvial Plain, or Delta, along the western edge, a flat and fertile floodplain. The loess bluffs near Natchez and Vicksburg rise above the river, the rolling hills of the north and east are drained by the Tombigbee and Pearl Rivers, and the southern coast is a strip of sandy beaches along the Gulf of Mexico. Woodall Mountain, at 806 feet (246 m), is the highest point.

History

The Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Natchez peoples lived in the region, and earlier Mississippian cultures built large mound centers. Hernando de Soto crossed in 1540, and France founded settlements on the Gulf Coast and at Natchez in the early 1700s. Mississippi became the 20th state in 1817, and cotton plantations and slavery dominated the economy until the Civil War, when Vicksburg fell to the Union in 1863. The 20th century brought Jim Crow, the Freedom Summer of 1964, and the civil rights movement, and the state produced writers such as Faulkner, Welty, and Wright and musicians from Robert Johnson to Elvis Presley.

Mississippi Gulf Coast

The Mississippi Gulf Coast stretches about 62 miles along the Gulf of Mexico, with a long public beach, live oaks, casinos, and fresh Gulf seafood. Biloxi is the best known of its cities, with the historic Biloxi Lighthouse, the Maritime and Seafood Industry Museum, and the Beauvoir home of Jefferson Davis, while Gulfport has the Mississippi Aquarium and ferries to the barrier islands. The Ohr-O'Keefe Museum of Art in Ocean Springs honors the eccentric "Mad Potter of Biloxi," and the artsy towns of Bay St. Louis and Pass Christian are known for their galleries and restaurants. Gulf Islands National Seashore protects unspoiled barrier islands, including Ship Island and its Civil War fort. The coast was devastated by Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and has since rebuilt.

Mississippi Delta and the Blues

The Mississippi Delta is the flat, fertile floodplain between the Mississippi and Yazoo Rivers, and it is widely regarded as the birthplace of the blues. The music grew out of the work songs and spirituals of Black sharecroppers and was carried by artists such as Robert Johnson, Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, and B.B. King. Today the Mississippi Blues Trail marks historic sites across the region. In Clarksdale, the Delta Blues Museum displays instruments and memorabilia, and the "Crossroads" of Highways 61 and 49 is rooted in the legend of Robert Johnson. Indianola is home to the B.B. King Museum and Delta Interpretive Center, and the GRAMMY Museum Mississippi in Cleveland tells the broader story of American music. Juke joints, catfish dinners, and cotton fields complete the Delta experience, and Highway 61 is the main route through the region.

Oxford and Tupelo

Oxford, in northern Mississippi, is home to the University of Mississippi ("Ole Miss") and is one of the South's best-known literary towns. It was the home of Nobel Prize-winning author William Faulkner, whose house, Rowan Oak, is open to visitors, and his grave is in the city cemetery. The town square has Square Books, restaurants, and the historic Lafayette County Courthouse, and the University of Mississippi Museum holds fine art and antiquities. About an hour east, Tupelo is the birthplace of Elvis Presley, and visitors can tour the Elvis Presley Birthplace, the tiny house where he was born in 1935, and the nearby church and museum. Tupelo is also on the Natchez Trace Parkway, with a visitor center and the Tupelo National Battlefield.

Civil Rights Movement Sites and Museums

Mississippi was a focal point of the civil rights struggle, from the murder of Emmett Till in 1955 to Freedom Summer in 1964, and many sites have been preserved. The Mississippi Civil Rights Museum in Jackson, which opened in 2017, has eight galleries on the movement. The Medgar and Myrlie Evers Home National Monument preserves the house where the NAACP field secretary was assassinated in 1963, and the Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley National Monument marks sites associated with Till's murder and his mother's activism. The Smith Robertson Museum in Jackson tells African American history, and Tougaloo College was a key center of organizing. In Natchez, Forks of the Road was one of the largest slave markets in the Deep South, and the William Johnson House tells the story of a free Black businessman.

Jackson

Jackson, the state capital and largest city, sits on the Pearl River in the center of the state and is the cultural and political hub of Mississippi. The Mississippi State Capitol, finished in 1903 in Beaux-Arts style, and the Old Capitol Museum, which houses the state's history museum, anchor downtown. The city has the Mississippi Museum of Art, the Mississippi Museum of Natural Science, and the Agriculture and Forestry Museum, and the Governor's Mansion is the second oldest continuously occupied governor's residence in the country. Jackson is also known for the writer Eudora Welty, whose home is a museum, for its Southern and soul food restaurants, and for LeFleur's Bluff State Park, a wooded park on the city's edge.

National Protected Areas

Mississippi's national park sites are rich in history. The Vicksburg National Military Park preserves the site of the 1863 siege that split the Confederacy, Brices Cross Roads National Battlefield marks a 1864 Confederate victory, and the Natchez Trace Parkway follows an ancient trail from Natchez to Nashville. Natchez National Historical Park, Tupelo National Battlefield, Medgar and Myrlie Evers Home National Monument, and Gulf Islands National Seashore also lie in the state.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Mississippi's state parks offer lakes, forests, and history. Tishomingo State Park has rock formations on the Natchez Trace, Clarkco State Park and Percy Quin State Park are popular for camping, and Buccaneer State Park sits on the Gulf Coast. Historic sites include Winterville Mounds, Emerald Mound, and the Mississippi Civil Rights Museum in Jackson. Visitors also tour the antebellum mansions of Natchez and the Blues Trail markers across the Delta.

Seasons and When to Visit

Spring and fall are the best seasons, with mild weather, azaleas and dogwoods in March and April, and fall festivals and football in October. Summer is hot and humid, with the busiest beach season on the Gulf Coast. Winters are mild, good for visiting the Delta and the Natchez Trace, and the Natchez Pilgrimage tours of historic homes take place in spring and fall. Hurricane season runs June through November.
