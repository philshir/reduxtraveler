How to Get There

From Natchez, MS (0–3 mi): Follow local streets to US Highway 84 and Canal Street, continue to the Natchez National Historical Park headquarters and visitor area at 640 South Canal Street, with additional park sites located throughout downtown Natchez.

Overview

Natchez National Historical Park is a National Park Service unit that tells the story of Natchez, Mississippi, one of the oldest and most historically significant towns on the lower Mississippi River. Congress established the park in 1988 to preserve and interpret places that show the city's role in French, British, Spanish and American history and in the life of the antebellum South. The park includes Fort Rosalie, the site of an 18th-century French fort; Melrose, an estate with a Greek Revival mansion and its slave quarters; the William Johnson House, home of a prominent free Black barber whose diary survives; and Forks of the Road, once one of the largest slave markets in the Deep South. Visitors come to see how enslaved and free people, planters and townspeople lived in the cotton-era Natchez.

Access

The park's sites are spread around Natchez, and most visitors drive or walk between them. Park headquarters and the main visitor facilities are at 640 South Canal Street in downtown Natchez, near the Mississippi River bluffs. Melrose sits on its own grounds a short drive away, the William Johnson House is in the downtown historic district, and Forks of the Road is on the edge of town. Natchez is reached by US-61 and US-84 and lies near the southern end of the Natchez Trace Parkway, and the nearest large cities are Baton Rouge and Jackson, each roughly 1.5 to 2 hours away. Check the park's website for site hours, since schedules vary from site to site and Melrose tours are by reservation.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, but the seasons shape the experience. Spring is the most popular time to visit, with mild weather, blooming gardens and the Natchez Pilgrimage season, when many private historic homes also open to the public. Summers are hot and humid, so plan outdoor visits in the morning and enjoy the air-conditioned interiors, and afternoon thunderstorms are common. Fall brings pleasant weather and fewer crowds, and winters are mild and quiet, with the city decorated for the holidays. Some site hours and tour schedules change by season.

Things to Do

Tour Melrose

Guided tours of the Greek Revival mansion and slave quarters explain how the McMurran family and the people they enslaved lived and worked on the estate. The collections include period furniture and household objects.

Visit the William Johnson House

William Johnson was a free Black barber and businessman whose diary gives a rare firsthand picture of life in Natchez before the Civil War. The house contains furnishings and exhibits about his family.

Learn at Forks of the Road

This site marks what was once one of the busiest slave markets in the Deep South, and its exhibits and markers help visitors understand the domestic slave trade.

See the Fort Rosalie Site

Fort Rosalie was built by the French and later controlled by Britain, Spain and the United States. Visitors can learn about the site from the nearby bluffs and from information at the visitor facilities.

Explore Downtown Natchez and the Natchez Trace

The park is a good starting point for walking the downtown historic district, enjoying views over the Mississippi River and driving the southern end of the Natchez Trace Parkway.

Visitor Centers

The park's main visitor facilities are at the headquarters on South Canal Street in downtown Natchez, where rangers provide maps, information and orientation and can help plan a visit among the park's sites. The William Johnson House has exhibits about his life and the free Black community of Natchez, and Melrose has a visitor contact area and guided tours of the mansion. Check current hours before you go, as individual sites may operate on different schedules.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the park. Natchez has a wide range of hotels and motels, as well as historic inns and bed-and-breakfasts in restored homes, and Vidalia across the river in Louisiana has additional motels. Natchez State Park north of town has campsites and cabins, and the Natchez Trace Parkway has primitive campgrounds farther north. Within one to two hours, Vicksburg, McComb, Port Gibson and Baton Rouge offer more places to stay, with Baton Rouge and Vicksburg having the widest selection.
