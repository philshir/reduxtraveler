How to Get There

From Biloxi, MS (28 mi): Take US-90 W along the Gulf Coast through Gulfport and Pass Christian, continuing straight to reach the Bay St. Louis Bridge.

Overview

Pascagoula is a city on the eastern end of the Mississippi Gulf Coast, near the Alabama border, and it is the seat of Jackson County and a major shipbuilding center. It is home to Ingalls Shipbuilding, one of the largest shipyards in the country and the largest private employer in Mississippi, which builds ships for the U.S. Navy. The city also has a rich natural setting at the mouth of the Pascagoula River, one of the last free-flowing river systems in the lower 48 states.

Visitors come to Pascagoula for the Pascagoula River Audubon Center, for fishing and boating on the river and the Mississippi Sound, and for the quiet beaches and lighthouse sites. They also come for the legend of the "Singing River," in which the river is said to hum on still nights, and for seafood, and it is a good stop on the Mississippi Gulf Coast between Mobile and Biloxi.

History

The name Pascagoula comes from a Native American word that is said to mean "bread eaters" and refers to the Pascagoula people, who lived along the river. The French explorer Iberville visited the area in 1699, and the first European settlement was established in the early 1700s. The city grew as a lumber and fishing port in the nineteenth century, and shipbuilding began here during World War I and expanded with Ingalls Shipbuilding, which was founded in 1938 and became an important builder of Navy ships. In 1973, two men claimed to have been abducted by aliens while fishing on the Pascagoula River, which made the city famous in UFO lore.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Pascagoula's waterfront has a harbor with shrimp boats and views of the Ingalls shipyard, and Beach Park has a beach and a pier with views of the Mississippi Sound. The Pascagoula River Audubon Center, a few miles north, is on the river and offers a nature center and trails, and the Round Island Lighthouse replica stands on the waterfront. The Scranton Floating Museum and the Pascagoula Public Library are other cultural stops, and the Pascagoula River Basin offers kayaking and boating tours.

Culture and Museums

The Scranton Floating Museum, a project of the Jackson County community, has exhibits about the culture of the river and the Gulf, and the Pascagoula Public Library and the Jackson County Historical Society cover local history. The Singing River Island and the legend of the singing river are part of local lore, and the city hosts festivals such as the Pascagoula Mardi Gras and the Pascagoula Fourth of July celebration. The Ingalls shipyard also offers a close look at modern shipbuilding.

Parks

The Pascagoula River Basin is one of the largest unimpounded river systems in the lower 48 states, with swamps, bayous, and forests that provide habitat for alligators, birds, and other wildlife. The Pascagoula River Audubon Center has trails and a boardwalk, and the Pascagoula River Wildlife Management Area is popular for hunting and fishing. Beach Park and Bayou Casotte offer waterfront recreation, and the barrier islands of Gulf Islands National Seashore are accessible by boat.

Seasons and Weather

Pascagoula has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F and almost no snow. The beach and fishing seasons run from spring through fall, and hurricane season runs from June to November.
