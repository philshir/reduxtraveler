How to Get There

From Biloxi, MS (13 mi): Take US-90 W along the coast through Edgewater and Long Beach to reach downtown Gulfport.

Overview

Gulfport is the second-largest city in Mississippi and the largest on the Mississippi Gulf Coast, a port city that is the gateway to the Gulf Islands National Seashore and the barrier islands. It is known for its working harbor, which handles tropical fruit from Central America, for its beaches along the Mississippi Sound, and for the Mississippi Aquarium, which opened in 2020. The city was rebuilt after Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and has a growing downtown and waterfront.

Visitors come to Gulfport for the Mississippi Aquarium, for the beaches and the casinos, and for the ferry to Ship Island, where they can explore Fort Massachusetts and the white sand beaches of the Gulf Islands National Seashore. They also come for fresh seafood, for fishing charters from the Gulfport Small Craft Harbor, and for the events at the Mississippi Coast Coliseum, and it is a good base for exploring the Gulf Coast.

History

The city was founded in 1887 by William Hardy, a railroad builder who built a railroad from Hattiesburg to the Gulf and a deep-water port, and the port opened in 1902. Gulfport grew as a shipping center for lumber and later for bananas and other fruit from Latin America, and the Gulfport Harbor remains one of the busiest banana ports in the country. The city was struck hard by Hurricane Camille in 1969 and by Hurricane Katrina in 2005, when a storm surge destroyed much of the waterfront. Since then it has rebuilt with new hotels, casinos, a downtown redevelopment, and the aquarium.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Mississippi Aquarium has a large collection of sea creatures, including sharks, dolphins, and sea turtles, with a focus on the Gulf Coast and the world's oceans. Ship Island Excursions runs ferry trips to Ship Island, a barrier island in the Gulf Islands National Seashore with a Civil War-era fort and clear water. Gulfport's beach, the Centennial Plaza, and the Gulfport Harbor are popular for strolls, and the Island View Casino and the Mississippi Coast Coliseum and Convention Center host concerts and events. Jones Park is another local landmark.

Culture and Museums

The Mississippi Aquarium is the main cultural attraction, and the Gulfport Little Theatre, the Gulf Coast Symphony, and the Mississippi Gulf Coast Community College offer performing arts. The Infinity Science Center, at the nearby Stennis Space Center, has NASA exhibits, and the Maritime & Seafood Industry Museum in Biloxi covers the history of the coast. The Gulfport Mardi Gras parades and the Cruisin' the Coast classic car festival in October are popular events.

Parks

The beaches of the Mississippi Gulf Coast offer swimming, fishing, and views of the Gulf, and Jones Park is a favorite spot. The Gulf Islands National Seashore has beaches and historic forts on Ship Island, and the Gulfport Small Craft Harbor has charter fishing and boat tours. The De Soto National Forest is about 30 minutes to the north.

Seasons and Weather

Gulfport has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F and almost no snow. The beach season runs from April to October, and hurricane season runs from June to November.
