Overview

Oxford is a small city in northern Mississippi that is the home of the University of Mississippi, known as "Ole Miss," and the hometown of Nobel Prize-winning novelist William Faulkner. It is famous for its literary heritage, its picturesque courthouse square, its independent bookstores such as Square Books, and the Grove, the campus tailgating area where Ole Miss football fans gather before games in a festive scene. The city is regularly ranked among the best small towns in America.

Visitors come to Oxford to see Rowan Oak, Faulkner's home, and his grave in St. Peter's Cemetery, to browse the Square and its historic courthouse, and to see the University of Mississippi campus. They also come for the Southern restaurants, such as City Grocery, for Ole Miss football and baseball games, and for events such as the Oxford Film Festival and the Double Decker Arts Festival.

History

The town was founded in 1837 on land that had been taken from the Chickasaw people, and it was named after Oxford, England, in the hope of attracting the state university. The University of Mississippi opened in 1848, and during the Civil War its students formed a Confederate company, and the town was burned by Union troops in 1864. In 1962, the university was the site of a riot when James Meredith became the first Black student to enroll, requiring federal troops to restore order. William Faulkner, who lived in Oxford from the age of five, wrote most of his famous novels here, setting them in the fictional Yoknapatawpha County, and he won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1949.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Rowan Oak, Faulkner's Greek Revival home from 1930 to 1962, is preserved with his study and the outline of the plot of A Fable written on the wall, and his grave is in St. Peter's Cemetery. The Lafayette County Courthouse and Oxford City Hall anchor the Square, which has bookstores, restaurants, and bars, and Ammadelle is a Victorian mansion. The University of Mississippi campus has the Lyceum, the Grove, and the University of Mississippi Museum.

Culture and Museums

Oxford has a strong literary tradition. Square Books and Off Square Books host readings, and the Faulkner and Yoknapatawpha Conference and the Oxford Conference for the Book celebrate writers, and the University of Mississippi Museum has a collection of Southern art and Greek and Roman antiquities. The Ole Miss campus also has the Center for the Study of Southern Culture and the Blues Archive, with a large collection of blues recordings. The Oxford Film Festival and the Double Decker Arts Festival in April are popular events.

Parks

Oxford has a number of parks and green spaces. Avent Park and Lamar Park have playgrounds and trails, and the Oxford-University Trail connects the city and campus. The Grove and the Ole Miss campus are good places to walk, and Sardis Lake and Enid Lake, north of the city, offer boating, fishing, and camping. The Holly Springs National Forest is about 30 minutes to the north.

Seasons and Weather

Oxford has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and a few inches of snow. Fall is the most popular season because of football, and spring is also pleasant. Thunderstorms and tornadoes are possible in spring.

Explore the Historic Oxford Square

The Oxford Square serves as the cultural heart of the city, featuring locally owned shops, restaurants, art galleries, and historic landmarks. Visitors can browse the renowned Square Books, enjoy Southern cuisine, and experience the vibrant atmosphere that has made Oxford one of Mississippi's most beloved college towns.
