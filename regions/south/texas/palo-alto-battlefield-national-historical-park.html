How to Get There

From Brownsville (9 mi): Take US-77/83 north from downtown, exit toward FM 1847, then follow Paredes Line Road to the park entrance located on the outskirts of the city.

Overview

Palo Alto Battlefield National Historical Park preserves the site of the first major battle of the Mexican-American War, fought on May 8, 1846. It was established as a national historic site in 1992 and redesignated a national historical park in 2009, and it is the only unit of the National Park System dedicated to interpreting that war. The landscape remains largely open coastal prairie, so visitors walk across ground much like what the soldiers crossed, with interpretive trails and exhibits explaining troop movements and the stakes of the engagement. The park also protects a rare stretch of native coastal prairie, and ranger programs and visitor center displays blend history and environment into a worthwhile stop.

Access

The park is on the northern edge of Brownsville, about 9 miles from downtown, at 7200 Paredes Line Road. Visitors usually drive, taking US-77/83 north and following FM 1847 (Paredes Line Road) to the entrance. Admission is free. The trails are flat and open with little shade, so bring water and sun protection.

Seasons

Winter is the most pleasant season in the Rio Grande Valley, with mild, dry weather that draws many seasonal visitors. Spring and fall are also comfortable for walking the trails. Summers are hot and humid, with intense sun and little shade on the prairie, and mosquitoes can be a nuisance after rains. Hurricane season runs from June through November, and heavy rain can leave low spots wet.

Things to Do

Walk the Battlefield Trail
Follow the paved trail across the historic landscape where U.S. and Mexican forces fought, with side spurs to the positions representing each army's battle lines.

Visit the Visitor Center and Museum
See exhibits on the causes, events, and consequences of the Mexican-American War, along with artifacts, weapons, and maps tied to the battle.

Visit Resaca de la Palma Battlefield
Continue learning about the war at the nearby site of the second major engagement, fought the day after the Battle of Palo Alto.

Top Trails

Battlefield Trail
Distance: Approximately 1 mile round trip plus side trails
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Historic battle positions, interpretive exhibits, open prairie scenery, and key locations from the first major battle of the Mexican-American War.

Battle Line Spur Trails
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Access to locations representing the American and Mexican troop lines during the battle.

Visitor Centers

The visitor center offers exhibits on the causes, events, and consequences of the Mexican-American War, along with artifacts, weapons, and maps tied to the battle. Rangers can explain the troop movements and offer programs, and the center is the best place to start before heading out onto the battlefield trail. Hours can vary by season, so check with the park before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. The closest hotels and restaurants are in Brownsville, about 9 miles away, and Harlingen is a short drive farther. Many visitors also base themselves at South Padre Island and Port Isabel, roughly 45 minutes away. Campgrounds and RV parks can be found around Brownsville and Harlingen, and the Rio Grande Valley is well set up for seasonal RV visitors.
