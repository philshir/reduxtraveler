How to Get There

From La Porte (5 mi): Take TX-225 toward Independence Parkway, exit at Independence Parkway, and follow signs to One Monument Circle and the monument grounds.

Overview

San Jacinto Battleground State Historic Site marks the ground where Sam Houston's army defeated Santa Anna's forces on April 21, 1836, winning Texas its independence from Mexico in a battle that lasted less than 20 minutes. The battlefield was set aside to preserve and commemorate that victory and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960. It is operated by Texas Parks and Wildlife, and its centerpiece is the 567-foot San Jacinto Monument, with the San Jacinto Museum of History in its base. The grounds also hold preserved prairie and marsh along the Houston Ship Channel, and the museum ship USS Texas is associated with the site. Visitors come for the history, the museum, and the sweeping views from the monument's observation deck.

Access

The site is near La Porte in the Houston area, about 20 miles east of downtown Houston along the Houston Ship Channel. Most visitors drive, taking Highway 225 and following signs to the battleground and One Monument Circle. Parking is available on the grounds near the monument. The site is spread out, so allow time to move between the monument, the battlefield, and the waterfront.

Seasons

The site is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for walking the battlefield. Summers on the upper Texas coast are hot, humid, and prone to afternoon thunderstorms, so shade and water matter on the open grounds. Winters are mild, with occasional cold fronts. The anniversary of the battle in April is marked with special events, and hurricane season from June through November can affect operations. The museum and monument close on select holidays.

Things to Do

Ride to the Observation Deck

An elevator carries visitors up the San Jacinto Monument for views across the battlefield, the Houston Ship Channel, and the Houston skyline.

Tour the San Jacinto Museum of History

Exhibits in the base of the monument cover the Battle of San Jacinto, the Republic of Texas, and the Spanish, Mexican, and Native American influences on Texas history, and a theater screens a short film on the battle.

Walk the Battleground

Walk the open grounds and reflecting pool around the monument, where interpretive markers help explain the positions of the armies and how the battle unfolded.

See the USS Texas

The battleship is a historic World War I-era vessel and museum ship. Its location and visitor access have changed in recent years because of repair work, so check current status before planning a visit.

San Jacinto Monument

Rising 567 feet above the battlefield, the San Jacinto Monument is the tallest masonry column in the world — taller even than the Washington Monument. Built between 1936 and 1939 as part of the Texas Centennial and dedicated on the battle's anniversary, the octagonal, limestone-faced shaft is topped with a 220-ton star representing the Lone Star of Texas. A 1,750-foot reflecting pool in front of the monument mirrors the entire column.

The San Jacinto Museum of History fills the monument's base, with exhibits on the Battle of San Jacinto, the Republic of Texas, and the Mayan, Spanish, and Mexican influences on Texas culture. The 160-seat Jesse H. Jones Theatre screens a short film on the battle.

USS Texas

Docked nearby along the ship channel, USS Texas (BB-35) is a decommissioned battleship turned museum ship — the first battleship to become a permanent museum and the only surviving dreadnought from World War I. Commissioned in 1914, she saw action in both World Wars, escorting Atlantic convoys and supporting landings at Normandy, Iwo Jima, and Okinawa before earning five battle stars and National Historic Landmark status. The ship is periodically closed to visitors for conservation work, so it's worth checking status before a visit.

Visitor Centers

The San Jacinto Museum of History in the base of the monument serves as the main visitor facility, with exhibits, a theater, ticketing for the observation deck, and a gift shop. Staff can explain current hours and what is open, including the status of the USS Texas. There is no separate visitor center elsewhere on the grounds.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. The closest hotels and restaurants are in La Porte, about 5 miles away, with more options in Pasadena, Baytown, and the Kemah and Seabrook area. Downtown Houston, roughly 20 miles away, offers a full range of hotels. Camping is limited close by, though RV parks are scattered around the Houston area, and Galveston Island State Park, about an hour away, has campsites.
