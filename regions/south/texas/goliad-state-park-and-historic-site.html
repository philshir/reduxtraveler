Goliad State Park & Historic Site

★5.0

Historic mission and park complex featuring Spanish colonial architecture, restored structures, museum exhibits, and cultural landmarks that interpret the origins of Texas settlement.

How to Get There

From Victoria (30 mi): Take US-59 N toward Goliad, enter the historic town center, then follow Park Road 6 to 108 Park Road 6 at Goliad State Park & Historic Site.

Overview

Goliad State Park & Historic Site preserves one of the most significant colonial-era landscapes in Texas, centered on the restored Mission Espíritu Santo, founded by Spanish missionaries in the 18th century on the San Antonio River. The Civilian Conservation Corps rebuilt the mission buildings in the 1930s, and the park also includes the ruins of Mission Rosario, the birthplace of General Ignacio Zaragoza and a campground along the river. Goliad itself is known for Presidio La Bahía and the 1836 Goliad Massacre. Visitors come for the Spanish colonial architecture, the history of ranching and the Texas Revolution, and quiet camping beside the river.

Access

The park is on US-183/77A, about 1 mile south of the town of Goliad and 30 miles from Victoria in the Texas Coastal Bend. From San Antonio it is about 95 miles southeast, and from Corpus Christi about 85 miles north. Visitors usually drive on US-59 or US-77 and follow signs for Goliad State Park. Roads are paved, and there is no public transportation.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and fall through spring is the most comfortable season, with mild days, wildflowers in spring and fewer insects. Summers are very hot and humid, so plan outdoor visits for mornings. Winters are mild, with occasional cold snaps. Events such as the Goliad Massacre commemoration in March and Christmas festivities bring crowds, so reserve campsites ahead.

Things to Do

Tour Mission Espíritu Santo

The restored mission buildings include the church, workshops and granary, with exhibits on the Spanish mission system and daily life.

Visit Mission Rosario and Zaragoza Birthplace

Nearby ruins and the birthplace of the Mexican hero of the Battle of Puebla add to the story.

Walk or Bike the Angel of Goliad Trail

A hike and bike trail connects the park, the mission and Presidio La Bahía.

Camp, Fish and Paddle on the San Antonio River

The campground, picnic areas and river access offer a quiet outdoor setting.

Learn About Goliad in the Texas Revolution

Presidio La Bahía and the Fannin Memorial are a short drive away.

Top Trails

Angel of Goliad Hike and Bike Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved path links the park and mission with the town and Presidio La Bahía along the San Antonio River.

Park Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path through woods and along the river with signs about the plants and wildlife.

Visitor Centers

The park headquarters and Mission Espíritu Santo visitor center have exhibits, a gift shop and staff to provide maps and tour information. Rangers lead programs on mission history and the Texas Revolution. Hours vary by season, and park admission is free to the mission grounds, with a day-use fee at some times.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground with RV and tent sites, screened shelters and a group area, which can be reserved through Texas Parks and Wildlife. Hotels, motels and bed-and-breakfasts are in Goliad, about a mile away, and in Victoria, about 30 miles northeast, which has many chains. Refugio and Beeville offer smaller options, and the coast is about an hour away.
