How to Get There

From Mineral Wells (26 mi): Take US-180 W to Palo Pinto, turn north on FM 4, continue to PK Lake Road and State Park Road 33, and follow the signs to the park entrance.

Overview

Possum Kingdom State Park sits in the Palo Pinto Mountains and Brazos River Valley, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1940s and opened to the public in 1950. Its main draw is Possum Kingdom Lake, a 20,000-acre reservoir on the Brazos River formed by Morris Sheppard Dam in 1941 and famed for some of the clearest water in Texas. The lake has more than 300 miles of shoreline winding 65 miles down the river, with limestone cliffs and wooded coves. Its signature landmark is Hell's Gate, a narrow channel cutting between a cliff-lined island and the shore, best seen from the water. Visitors come to swim, boat, fish, dive, camp, and hike.

Access

The park is near the community of Caddo in Palo Pinto County, about 26 miles from Mineral Wells and roughly 80 miles west of Fort Worth. From Mineral Wells, take US-180 west to Palo Pinto, then turn north on FM 4 and follow signs to State Park Road 33 and the park entrance. Most visitors drive, and boat access is a key part of the lake experience, so many bring their own boats or use local rentals. Gate access is available around the clock, which is helpful for early fishing and late camping arrivals.

Seasons

Summer is the peak season for swimming, boating, and diving, and the park and lake can be crowded on weekends and holidays from Memorial Day through Labor Day. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking and camping, with milder temperatures and fewer people. Winter is quiet and cool, and some water activities slow down. Lake levels can vary with rainfall and drought, and spring thunderstorms can roll through quickly.

Things to Do

Swim in Clear Blue Water
Possum Kingdom Lake is known for having some of the clearest water in Texas. Enjoy designated swimming areas and cool off in the lake's scenic coves and shoreline areas.

Go Boating
Launch a boat and explore more than 300 miles of shoreline, secluded coves, and open-water sections of Possum Kingdom Lake.

Kayak and Paddle
Explore the lake's calm coves and scenic shoreline by kayak or paddlecraft while enjoying views of the surrounding hills and cliffs.

Scuba Dive and Snorkel
The lake's unusually clear water makes it one of Texas' most popular inland destinations for scuba diving and snorkeling.

Hike Scenic Trails
Walk trails that wind through rugged terrain, forested areas, rocky ridges, and overlooks that provide views of the lake and Brazos River Valley.

Visit Hell's Gate
Take a boat trip to the famous narrow canyon opening known as Hell's Gate, one of the most recognizable landmarks on Possum Kingdom Lake.

Top Trails

Possum Kingdom Lakeview Trail
Distance: Approximately 1.6 miles
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Beautiful lake overlooks, shoreline scenery, open meadows, and shaded sections through native vegetation.

Longhorn Loop
Distance: Approximately 1.4 miles
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate
Highlights: Elevated lake views, rocky terrain, and scenic ridgeline overlooks.

Chaparral Ridge Trail
Distance: Approximately 0.6 miles
Difficulty: Moderate
Highlights: Rocky outcrops, rugged terrain, and panoramic viewpoints overlooking the lake.

Possum Kingdom Hike and Bike Trail
Distance: Up to 8.7 miles
Difficulty: Moderate
Highlights: Rolling hills, forested terrain, lake views, wildlife observation, and scenic overlooks.

Visitor Centers

The park headquarters and office is open daily during the day and is where visitors check in, ask about trail and lake conditions, and pick up maps. It is a modest facility rather than a large visitor center with a museum. Staff can point visitors to boat ramps, swimming areas, and the best trails for lake views.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers camping, including campsites with water and electricity, and reservations are a good idea in summer and on weekends; check the Texas Parks and Wildlife website for the current choices of campsites and other overnight accommodations. Outside the park, lakeside resorts, vacation rentals, and cabins are scattered around the lake near Caddo and Graford. Larger towns such as Mineral Wells, about 26 miles away, and Graham and Weatherford have chain hotels. Lake Mineral Wells State Park, about 30 miles away, also has camping.
