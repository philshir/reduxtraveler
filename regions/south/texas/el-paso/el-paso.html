El Paso Travel Guide

City located in the westernmost part of the state, known for its rich history, diverse culture, and stunning desert landscapes. With its unique blend of Mexican and American influences, El Paso offers a wide range of attractions and activities for visitors to enjoy. From exploring the historic downtown area to hiking in the nearby Franklin Mountains, there is something for everyone in this dynamic city. Whether you're interested in art, history, or outdoor adventures, El Paso has it all.

Overview

El Paso is a city in far West Texas on the Rio Grande, directly across the border from Ciudad Juárez, Mexico, and it is the largest U.S. city on the border. It is known for its desert mountain setting, with the Franklin Mountains rising in the middle of the city, for its blend of Mexican and American cultures, and for its Tex-Mex and Southwestern cuisine. Famous for its sunshine, with more than 300 sunny days a year, El Paso is also home to Fort Bliss, one of the largest U.S. Army posts, and the University of Texas at El Paso.

Visitors come to El Paso for the Franklin Mountains State Park, for the Wyler Aerial Tramway, and for the historic San Jacinto Plaza downtown. They also come for the El Paso Museum of Art, for the Mission Trail, which includes some of the oldest missions in Texas, and for the food, and Juárez and New Mexico's Las Cruces are within easy reach.

History

The area was home to Native Americans, including the Manso and Suma peoples, when the Spanish arrived in the late 1500s, and in 1598 Juan de Oñate crossed the Rio Grande here, calling the place "El Paso del Norte," or "the pass of the north." Spanish missions were founded in the 1600s, including the Ysleta Mission in 1682, the oldest continuously operating parish in Texas. The American city of El Paso was established in 1859 after the Mexican–American War, and the arrival of the railroads in 1881 made it a major trading hub. In the early twentieth century, El Paso played a role in the Mexican Revolution, with figures such as Pancho Villa operating nearby, and it has since grown as a center of trade and border commerce.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Wyler Aerial Tramway carries visitors to the top of Ranger Peak in Franklin Mountains State Park, with panoramic views of the city, the border, and parts of three states. San Jacinto Plaza, known for its alligator sculptures, is the central plaza downtown, and the Plaza Theatre, built in 1930, is a restored movie palace. The Chamizal National Memorial commemorates the settlement of a border dispute between the U.S. and Mexico, and the Mission Trail has the Ysleta, Socorro, and San Elizario missions.

Culture and Museums

The El Paso Museum of Art has a collection of European and Southwestern art, and the Chamizal National Memorial has a museum, a theater, and an annual festival. The University of Texas at El Paso, with its Bhutanese-style architecture, is a cultural center, and the Plaza Theatre hosts concerts and shows. El Paso also has the El Paso Museum of History, the El Paso Holocaust Museum, and the Magoffin Home State Historic Site, a nineteenth-century adobe house.

Parks

Franklin Mountains State Park is the largest urban park in the United States, with more than 24,000 acres of desert mountains, 100 miles of trails, and rock climbing within the city limits. The Wyler Aerial Tramway offers a ride to the top of the range, and the Hueco Tanks State Park and Historic Site, about 30 miles east, has rock climbing and ancient pictographs. Memorial Park, Ascarate Park, and the Rio Grande Trail offer additional recreation.

Seasons and Weather

El Paso has a hot desert climate. Summers are hot, with highs in the upper 90s°F and low humidity, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and occasional light snow. Rain is scarce, about 9 inches a year, and most falls in the summer monsoon, so spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons.
