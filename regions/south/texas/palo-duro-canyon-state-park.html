How to Get There

From Amarillo (30 miles): Head east on E Amarillo Blvd/U.S. Rte 66 toward N Pierce St. Turn right onto N Pierce St. Use the right 2 lanes to take the ramp to I-27/I-40. Continue onto US-287 S/US-60 W/US-87 S. Continue straight onto I-27 S. Take exit 122B for Farm to Market Rd 1541 toward Washington St. Merge onto Canyon Dr. Turn left onto S Washington St

Overview

Palo Duro Canyon State Park protects part of the second-largest canyon in the United States, which cuts roughly 120 miles through the Caprock Escarpment near Amarillo. Carved by the Prairie Dog Town Fork of the Red River, the canyon reaches depths of 800 feet or more and widths up to 20 miles, with multicolored rock layers and mesa walls that have earned it the nickname "The Grand Canyon of Texas." The park opened in 1934, and Civilian Conservation Corps crews built much of its infrastructure by 1937, with many of their stone structures still in use today. Painter Georgia O'Keeffe, who taught nearby in the late 1910s, called it "a burning, seething cauldron, filled with dramatic light and color." Visitors come to hike, bike, ride horses, camp, drive the canyon road, and see the Lighthouse formation.

Access

The park is about 25 to 30 miles south of Amarillo and roughly 12 miles east of the town of Canyon, entered by Highway 217 and Park Road 5. The paved road drops from the canyon rim to the canyon floor, with scenic overlooks along the way and low-water crossings in places. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and reservations are highly recommended for both day use and camping, since the park can reach capacity on busy weekends. Plan to enter early to find parking at popular trailheads.

Seasons

Spring and fall bring the most comfortable temperatures for hiking, with spring storms and wildflowers and clear, crisp fall days. Summer is hot, and visitors should start hikes early and carry plenty of water, though the park is busy then because of the TEXAS outdoor musical. Heavy rain can cause flash flooding that closes low-water crossings and trails, so check conditions after storms. Winters are cold, with occasional snow or ice that can make roads hazardous, but the canyon is quiet and scenic.

Things to Do

Drive the Canyon Road
Follow the scenic paved park road from the rim to the canyon floor, with panoramic overlooks along the way.

Hike, Bike, or Ride to the Lighthouse
Explore the park's trail network through red rock canyons, mesas, and grasslands, including the trail to Lighthouse Rock, a towering spire that's become the symbol of Palo Duro Canyon.

Explore the Visitor Center
Learn about the canyon's geology, wildlife, and Indigenous cultures through exhibits and interpretive displays.

See Historic CCC Structures
Discover the stone buildings and infrastructure built during the 1930s by the Civilian Conservation Corps that still serve park visitors today.

Watch the TEXAS Outdoor Musical
Catch this outdoor historical drama, performed each summer since it premiered in 1966 at the park's Pioneer Amphitheatre — one of the best-attended outdoor history dramas in the country, with live music, dance, and special effects.

Learn the Canyon's Human History
Visitor center exhibits and historic sites around the park cover the area's Indigenous history, ranching heritage, and the events of the Red River War that took place within the canyon.

Top Trails

Lighthouse Trail
Distance: 5.3 miles round trip
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate
Highlights: The park's most famous hike leading to the iconic Lighthouse rock formation and some of the canyon's best scenery.

Rock Garden Trail
Distance: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Difficulty: Challenging
Highlights: Steep sections, dramatic rock formations, and elevated views across the canyon.

Upper Comanche and Kiowa Trail Loop
Distance: Approximately 6.6 miles
Difficulty: Moderate
Highlights: Colorful canyon walls, diverse terrain, and expansive views of the surrounding landscape.

CCC Trail
Distance: Approximately 0.9 miles
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Historic Civilian Conservation Corps features and beautiful canyon overlooks.

Juniper, Sunflower, and Rojo Grande Loop
Distance: Approximately 3.1 miles
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Family-friendly hiking with excellent views of canyon formations and native vegetation.

Visitor Centers

The park's visitor center has exhibits and interpretive displays on the canyon's geology, wildlife, and Indigenous cultures, and it is a good first stop for trail maps and current conditions. A park store inside the canyon offers snacks, supplies, and souvenirs. Staff can also explain the park's CCC-era history and its role in the Red River War story.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers tent and RV camping on the canyon floor, equestrian camping with horse corrals, and rim-top cabins with sunrise views over the canyon, and all fill quickly, so book well in advance. For hotels, the closest large city is Amarillo, about 25 to 30 miles away, with a full range of chain hotels, and the smaller town of Canyon is closer and has motels and inns. About two hours away, Caprock Canyons State Park near Quitaque also offers camping.
