How to Get There

From Lawton (15 mi): Take I-44 west to Exit 45, follow OK-49 west toward the refuge entrance, then continue on refuge roads to the visitor center at 21088 State Highway 115.

Overview

Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge is a National Wildlife Refuge of about 59,000 acres in southwestern Oklahoma near Lawton, protecting 500-million-year-old granite mountains, mixed-grass prairie and small lakes. It began as the Wichita Forest Reserve in 1901, was redesignated a game preserve by President Theodore Roosevelt in 1905, and became a wildlife refuge under an act of 1936, making it the oldest managed wildlife area in the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service system. The refuge is one of the best places in the state to see free-roaming American bison, elk and Texas longhorn cattle, along with prairie dogs and more than 275 species of birds. Visitors come for wildlife viewing, the scenic drive to the summit of Mount Scott, hiking, rock climbing, fishing in its 13 lakes and camping, and the Holy City of the Wichitas hosts a long-running Easter pageant.

Access

The refuge sits just northwest of Lawton and Fort Sill, about 15 miles from Lawton. From I-44, take exit 45 onto OK-49 and go west about 7 miles to the refuge gate or 14 miles to the visitor center; from US-62, take the Cache exit and go north on Highway 115 to the refuge gate. The visitor center is at 21088 State Highway 115 in Cache. Roads inside the refuge are paved, and a narrow, winding 3-mile road leads to the Mount Scott summit. Parking areas, pullouts, side roads and trailheads close from sunset to sunrise, and wildlife often stands near the road, so drive slowly. Admission is free.

Seasons

The refuge is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking and wildlife viewing. Summers in southwestern Oklahoma are hot, so hike early in the morning and bring plenty of water. Winter can bring cold snaps and is the quietest season for visitors. The Holy City of the Wichitas holds its annual Easter pageant in spring. Dry spells can lead to burn bans that affect campfires, so check conditions before arriving.

Things to Do

See Free-Roaming Bison, Elk, and Longhorn Cattle

Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge is one of the best places in Oklahoma to view large native mammals in a natural setting. Visitors can observe free-roaming American bison, elk, Texas longhorn cattle, prairie dogs, and a wide variety of birds and wildlife throughout the refuge's rugged landscape.

Explore the Wichita Mountains

The refuge protects some of the oldest exposed rock formations in North America, featuring granite mountains, scenic lakes, prairie grasslands, and dramatic landscapes. Visitors can enjoy photography, wildlife viewing, rock climbing, fishing, and scenic drives through one of Oklahoma's most iconic natural areas.

Visit the Holy City of the Wichitas

Located within the refuge, the Holy City of the Wichitas is a unique outdoor replica of ancient Jerusalem that serves as the site of annual Easter pageants and provides visitors with an interesting cultural and historical attraction surrounded by beautiful mountain scenery.

Top Trails

Elk Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 4.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 800 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

One of the refuge's most popular hikes, Elk Mountain Trail climbs through rugged terrain and granite outcrops to provide panoramic views of the Wichita Mountains, nearby lakes, and surrounding prairie landscapes.

Charon's Garden Wilderness Trail

Length: Approximately 5.0 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail explores the Charon's Garden Wilderness Area, known for its granite boulder formations, native wildlife, seasonal wildflowers, and opportunities for solitude in one of Oklahoma's premier wilderness settings.

Visitor Centers

The Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge Visitor Center on Highway 115 is open daily from 9 am to 5 pm and is the best first stop. Its exhibits and bookstore cover four habitats of the refuge: rocklands, aquatic, mixed-grass prairie and cross timbers. Staff can provide maps and information on trails and roads. The refuge headquarters office is open weekdays from 8 am to 4:30 pm.

Camping and Lodging

The refuge's Doris Campground offers non-electric, electric and walk-in tent sites plus group sites, with reservations through Recreation.gov, and limited backcountry camping is allowed in the Charon's Garden Wilderness with a refuge permit. Motels and hotels are found in Lawton, about 15 miles away, with smaller choices in Cache and the resort town of Medicine Park near the refuge. Oklahoma City and Wichita Falls, Texas, offer the widest selection within roughly 1.5 to 2 hours.
