How to Get There

From Enid (40 mi): Take US-60 west toward Cherokee, continue to Jet, then follow signs north and west to Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge on Harper Road near Great Salt Plains Lake.

Overview

Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge protects about 32,000 acres of salt flats, wetlands, prairie and woodland in Alfalfa County, northern Oklahoma, along Great Salt Plains Lake on the Salt Fork of the Arkansas River. It was established on March 26, 1930, by executive order of President Herbert Hoover as a resting and breeding ground for migratory birds, and it was designated a National Natural Landmark in 1983. The refuge is best known for its roughly 11,000 to 12,000 acres of glaring white salt flats and for being the only place in the world where visitors can dig for hourglass selenite crystals, a form of gypsum that traps a band of sand in the shape of an hourglass. It is also one of the most important shorebird habitats in the Western Hemisphere, with the entire refuge designated as critical habitat for the endangered whooping crane, and it draws birders, anglers and families who come to dig for crystals.

Access

The refuge is in north-central Oklahoma, near the small town of Jet and about 40 miles from Enid, the closest sizable city with hotels. Nearly everyone arrives by car: from Enid, follow US-60 west toward Cherokee and Jet, then follow signs to the refuge headquarters on Harper Road, with the refuge reached from State Highway 11 by way of County Road 710. The crystal digging area is a separate entrance on Garvin Road near Cherokee, where a gate leads about a mile to the marked dig area. Roads on the refuge are mostly paved or dirt and there is no public transportation, so plan to drive. The refuge is open daily from sunrise to sunset and admission is free.

Seasons

Selenite crystal digging is open only from April 1 through October 15, which makes summer the busiest time for visitors despite the heat and almost no shade on the flats. Spring and fall are the best seasons for bird migration, with shorebirds and waterfowl in large numbers, whooping cranes passing through mostly in fall, and American white pelicans arriving on the lake in late summer. Winter brings bald eagles and waterfowl, and the refuge is quiet and cold, with the dig area closed. Trails may close during controlled hunts, prescribed burns and maintenance, so check current conditions before visiting.

Things to Do

See the Famous Salt Flats

Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge is best known for its vast salt flats, a rare landscape that covers thousands of acres and creates one of the most unique natural environments in Oklahoma. The bright white plains offer exceptional opportunities for sightseeing, photography, wildlife observation, and experiencing a landscape unlike almost anywhere else in North America.

Dig for Selenite Crystals

The refuge is one of the only places in the world where visitors can legally collect unique hourglass-shaped selenite crystals. Crystal digging is a popular seasonal activity that attracts visitors from across the country who search for these distinctive geological formations found within the salt flats.

View Migratory Birds and Wildlife

Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge provides critical habitat for migratory birds along the Central Flyway. Visitors can observe sandhill cranes, shorebirds, waterfowl, bald eagles, and numerous other species throughout the year, making the refuge one of Oklahoma's premier birdwatching destinations.

Top Trails

Salt Flats Observation Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy trail provides access to observation areas overlooking the salt flats and surrounding wetlands. Visitors can enjoy panoramic views of the refuge while observing birds, wildlife, and the unique salt-encrusted landscape.

Sandhill Crane Viewing Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail offers opportunities to view migratory birds and wetland habitats while learning about the refuge's role in wildlife conservation and the importance of the Central Flyway migration corridor.

Visitor Centers

The refuge visitor center and headquarters is on Harper Road in Jet. The refuge's trailheads for the Sandpiper and Eagle Roost trails are nearby, but the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service currently lists the building as closed for a remodel with no reopening date, so call ahead at (580) 626-4794 before counting on it. The salt flats can also be viewed from an observation tower when the digging area is closed, and the refuge website has current information on trails, digging rules and closures.

Camping and Lodging

The refuge does not advertise camping, but Great Salt Plains State Park on Great Salt Plains Lake has RV and tent campsites, cabins, a swimming beach and boat ramps, and is the closest place to stay. Motels and hotels are available in Enid, about 40 miles away, with a smaller selection in Alva and Cherokee nearby. Services in Jet are very limited.
