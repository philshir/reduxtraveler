How to Get There

From Oklahoma City (90 mi): Take I-44 W toward Lawton, exit onto Rogers Lane (US-62 W), turn right on Sheridan Road, enter Fort Sill at Bentley Gate, continue to Randolph Road, turn left, then right on Chickasha Road, and immediate left onto Quanah Road to reach the museum.

Overview

Fort Sill National Historic Landmark and Museum is the museum and historic district of Fort Sill, an active U.S. Army installation just north of Lawton in southwestern Oklahoma. Major General Philip Sheridan staked out the post on January 8, 1869, during the campaigns on the Southern Plains, and it was named for Brigadier General Joshua Sill. The Old Post area was designated a National Historic Landmark on December 19, 1960. It is famous as the place where the Kwahadi Comanche leader Quanah Parker arrived in 1875, ending the fighting on the South Plains, and where Geronimo and other Chiricahua Apache prisoners of war were held, and both Geronimo and Quanah Parker are buried on post. It is also the home of the Army Field Artillery School, founded here in 1911. The museum, which is free, preserves the original stone buildings around the Old Post Quadrangle, including the guardhouse and post chapel, and presents the history of the fort, the Plains tribes and the Army.

Access

Fort Sill is a working military base north of Lawton, at the museum's address of 435 Quanah Road, and about 85 to 90 miles southwest of Oklahoma City. All visitors over 16 must stop at the Visitor Control Center and complete a background check before entering the post, and a government photo ID is needed, so allow extra time and check current requirements. From Lawton, visitors head north on Sheridan Road to Bentley Gate and follow signs to the museum. Most visitors arrive by car, and large groups should arrange tours with the museum weeks in advance. Pets are allowed on the grounds but not inside museum buildings.

Seasons

The museum is open year-round, Tuesday through Saturday, and is closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. Spring and fall are the most comfortable times to explore the outdoor sites around the Old Post Quadrangle, with mild temperatures. Summers in southwestern Oklahoma are hot, so plan outdoor walks for the morning, and winters are cool with occasional cold snaps and ice. Special events, such as the annual Candlelight Stroll, are held on select dates, and military operations or heightened security can affect access to the base, so check before traveling.

Things to Do

Explore the Old Post Quadrangle

The quadrangle is the historic heart of the fort, surrounded by 19th-century stone buildings, including the old guardhouse, post chapel and original barracks. Self-guided tours are available with an audio guide or brochure.

Visit the Museum Exhibits

The museum's exhibits tell the story of Fort Sill and the Southern Plains, from the Plains tribes to the Field Artillery, and include artifacts, weapons and uniforms.

Walk the Cannon Walk

Outdoor displays of artillery pieces from different eras show the evolution of field artillery.

See Where Geronimo and Quanah Parker Are Buried

Geronimo's grave is on post, and Native American leaders including Quanah Parker are buried at Chief's Knoll in the Post Cemetery.

Visit the Other Fort Sill Museums

Fort Sill is also home to the U.S. Army Field Artillery Museum and the U.S. Army Air Defense Artillery Museum, which are free to the public.

Top Trails

Old Post Quadrangle Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This self-guided walk circles the Old Post Quadrangle and passes the guardhouse, post chapel, stone barracks and other 19th-century buildings. It is flat, suitable for all ages and a good way to see the historic district between museum visits.

Cannon Walk

Length: Less than 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short outdoor stroll passes artillery pieces from different eras and shows how field artillery evolved. Some outdoor sites on post may be restricted, so follow posted signs and the guidance of staff.

Visitor Centers

The Fort Sill Visitor Control Center is the first stop for non-military visitors and handles background checks and the visitor pass needed to enter the post. The museum has its own visitor center at the southeast corner of the Old Post Quadrangle, where staff provide brochures and audio tours and answer questions. The museum phone number is 580-442-5123.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the museum. The nearest hotels, motels and restaurants are in Lawton, just a few miles south of the post. Duncan and Chickasha, about an hour away, have additional lodging, and campgrounds can be found in the wider Lawton area.
