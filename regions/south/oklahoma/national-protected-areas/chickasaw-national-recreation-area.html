Chickasaw National Recreation Area

★5.0

Recreation area in the foothills of the Arbuckle Mountains in south-central Oklahoma and includes the former Platt National Park and Arbuckle Recreation Area

How to Get There

From Sulphur (1 mi): From downtown Sulphur, head west on OK-7/W 1st Street and follow signs into Chickasaw National Recreation Area, with access points located throughout the Platt Historic District and Lake of the Arbuckles areas.

Overview

Chickasaw National Recreation Area is a National Park Service unit of nearly 10,000 acres in the foothills of the Arbuckle Mountains near Sulphur in south-central Oklahoma. It is known for its cool springs, clear streams and lakes in a part of the state that is otherwise dry, and it includes the former Platt National Park and the Arbuckle Recreation Area. The Chickasaw and Choctaw nations sold the springs and a 640-acre buffer to the federal government in 1902, creating Sulphur Springs Reservation, which was renamed Platt National Park in 1906. In 1976 Platt National Park, the Arbuckle Recreation Area and additional lands were combined and renamed to honor the Chickasaw Nation's role in the park's creation, and there is no entrance fee. Visitors come to swim and wade in Travertine Creek, hike the forested creek bottoms, see the bison herd, camp, and boat and fish on Lake of the Arbuckles.

Access

The recreation area adjoins the town of Sulphur at 901 W. 1st Street (OK-7), south of Oklahoma City and roughly 30 miles northeast of Ardmore. It has two main parts. The Platt Historic District, with the Travertine Nature Center, Travertine Creek and the old park's springs and trails, is on the west edge of town, while Lake of the Arbuckles lies about 8 miles southwest of Sulphur. A narrow road circles the Platt district, with parking areas near the springs, picnic areas and swimming holes, which can fill quickly on summer weekends. There is no public transportation, so most visitors arrive by car. Do not drive through flooded creek crossings or low-water crossings, which can flood quickly after heavy rain.

Seasons

Activities run year-round. Summer is the busiest season, when the cold, clear streams and swimming holes and the lakes draw crowds, and Lake of the Arbuckles attracts many boaters, though summers are hot and humid. Spring brings wildflowers and small waterfalls along Travertine and Rock creeks, but thunderstorms are common from April through July and can cause flooding. Fall is cooler and brings colorful foliage, and it is a pleasant time for hiking. Winters are mild and quieter. Some campgrounds, including Rock Creek and parts of the Buckhorn campground, are open only in summer, while others stay open all year, and the Travertine Nature Center has seasonal hours.

Things to Do

Swim and Wade in Travertine Creek

Springs feed cold, clear water into Travertine and Rock creeks, which drop over small travertine ledges and form swimming holes that are popular in summer. Swimming and wading are prohibited east of Little Niagara, and visitors should never swim alone.

Boat and Fish at Lake of the Arbuckles

The 2,350-acre lake offers motorboating, water skiing, sailing, fishing and swimming in designated areas, and a boat launch permit is required. Crappie, catfish, largemouth bass, white bass and bluegill are among the fish caught here, and Veterans Lake is another place to fish.

Visit the Springs and Historic Structures

The Platt Historic District has springs such as Buffalo, Antelope and Pavilion springs, along with rustic stone pavilions, bridges and spring enclosures built by Civilian Conservation Corps workers in the 1930s. Lincoln Bridge, one of the oldest structures, crosses Travertine Creek.

See the Bison

A bison herd lives in a fenced pasture in the park and can be seen from the Bison Pasture Trail. Visitors must stay out of the fenced area.

Camp, Picnic and Join Ranger Programs

The park has campgrounds, picnic areas and pavilions, and the Travertine Nature Center hosts nature walks, ranger talks and seasonal programs.

Top Trails

Antelope and Buffalo Springs Trail

Length: 1.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy trail follows Travertine Creek upstream to Antelope and Buffalo springs, where the water emerges at about 5 million gallons per day to feed the creek.

Travertine Creek Trail

Length: 1.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The trail runs between Pavilion Springs and the Travertine Nature Center, passing swimming holes, small waterfalls and shaded creek bottomland.

Bison Pasture Trail

Length: 1.9-mile loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This loop skirts the park's bison pasture and offers a chance to see the herd. Keep your distance and stay outside the fence.

Veterans Lake Trail

Length: 2.9-mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A concrete-paved loop through forest, prairie and lakeside beaches, suitable for walking, running and cycling.

Rock Creek Multi-Use Trails

Length: Four trails, 2.1 to 4.4 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Varies
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

These unpaved trails are open to hikers, mountain bikers and horseback riders and explore the prairie and woodland near Lake of the Arbuckles.

Visitor Centers

The Travertine Nature Center is the park's main visitor center, in the Platt Historic District near Travertine Creek. It has exhibits on the area's nature, history, wildlife and geology, a park film, a bookstore and live animals, and it hosts nature walks, ranger talks and seasonal programs. Hours vary with the season, so check the park website before visiting. The park headquarters at 901 W. 1st Street in Sulphur also provides information.

Camping and Lodging

Chickasaw National Recreation Area has six campgrounds with more than 400 sites, all with tables, grills, restrooms and water. In the Platt Historic District, Cold Springs is open year-round and Rock Creek and Central are summer campgrounds, all without electric hookups. At Lake of the Arbuckles, Buckhorn and The Point have lakeside sites, some with electric hookups, and some loops are open year-round. Some sites can be reserved on recreation.gov, while others are first come, first served, and the maximum stay is two weeks. Hotels and motels are in Sulphur, next to the park, including the Chickasaw Nation's Artesian Hotel, Casino & Spa on West 1st Street, with additional lodging in Davis and in Ardmore, about 30 miles southwest.
