How to Get There

From Oklahoma City (120 mi): Take I-35 N to US-60 W, continue west through Lamont and Pond Creek, then north on OK-38 to Jet; follow signs to the park entrance on S Spillway Drive.

Overview

Salt Plains State Park is an 840-acre Oklahoma state park in Alfalfa County in north-central Oklahoma, on the shore of Great Salt Plains Lake. It lies next to the Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge, where about 11,000 acres of bright white salt flats are the only place in the world to dig for hourglass-shaped selenite crystals. The park is the recreation base for the area, with a shallow, salty lake of about 9,300 acres that is popular for fishing, boating and kayaking, and for birding, since the region lies on a major migration corridor. Visitors come to camp, swim, hike and bike, and to dig for crystals in the refuge's designated area.

Access

The park is about 8 miles north of Jet on State Highway 38 and about 12 miles east of Cherokee, with its entrance on S Spillway Drive. Access is by car on paved roads, and there is no public transportation. From Oklahoma City, the drive is about 120 miles by way of I-35 and US-60. The crystal digging area is in the refuge, reached by access roads about 6 miles west of Jet or 3 miles south of Cherokee.

Seasons

Spring through fall is the best time to visit. Crystal digging is open from April 1 to October 15, and summer brings swimming, fishing and boating but also heat with little shade on the salt flats. Spring and fall are the best seasons for bird migration, including shorebirds and waterfowl. In winter the lake and park are quiet, the dig area is closed, and bald eagles and waterfowl can be seen. The park is open year-round from sunrise to sunset.

Things to Do

Explore the Great Salt Plains

Salt Plains State Park sits adjacent to the unique Great Salt Plains, one of Oklahoma's most unusual natural features. Visitors can experience vast salt flats, wide-open landscapes, and scenic views unlike anywhere else in the state while enjoying opportunities for photography and sightseeing.

Enjoy Great Salt Plains Lake

The park provides access to Great Salt Plains Lake, a popular destination for boating, fishing, kayaking, swimming, birdwatching, and other outdoor recreation. The lake's expansive shoreline and open waters attract anglers and water enthusiasts throughout the year.

Watch Wildlife and Migratory Birds

Located near Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge, the park offers excellent opportunities to observe migratory waterfowl, shorebirds, bald eagles, and other wildlife. The region lies along a major migration corridor and is known for outstanding birdwatching opportunities.

Top Trails

Jet Recreation Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy walking trail provides access to scenic viewpoints of Great Salt Plains Lake and surrounding grasslands while offering opportunities for wildlife observation and photography.

Lake Shore Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail follows portions of the lake shoreline and natural habitats, providing visitors with opportunities to observe birds, native plants, and the unique ecosystems associated with the Great Salt Plains region.

Visitor Centers

The park does not have a visitor center. The park office is open daily from 8:00 am to 4:00 pm and can be reached at 580-626-4731, and staff can provide camping information and directions to the crystal dig area. The Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge headquarters and visitor center is nearby in Jet, though the refuge lists it as closed for remodeling.

Camping and Lodging

The park has RV sites with water and 30- and 50-amp electric hookups, tent sites, comfort stations with showers, six cabins, and equestrian camps. All campsites are reserved online. The closest towns with services are Jet and Cherokee, with motels in Enid, about 45 miles away, and Alva, about 50 miles away.
