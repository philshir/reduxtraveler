How to Get There

From Waynoka, OK (5 mi): Take US-281 south from Waynoka, continue approximately 5 miles, and follow signs to Little Sahara State Park at 8951 US Highway 281.

Overview

Little Sahara State Park, also called Waynoka Dunes, is an Oklahoma state park in Woods County that protects about 1,600 acres of sand dunes ranging from roughly 25 to 75 feet tall. It is named for its resemblance to the Sahara Desert, and the dunes formed from terrace deposits left when the Cimarron River flowed across the area in prehistoric times. The state bought the first 339 acres in 1960, and the park had grown to its present size by 1963. It is one of the best-known off-highway vehicle destinations in the region, drawing riders of motorcycles, ATVs, UTVs, side-by-sides, dune buggies, SUVs and Jeeps, and the tallest dunes, named Competition Hill and Buttercup, are in the western and northwestern part of the park. Camping, picnicking and an observation deck make it a stop for spectators and families as well.

Access

The park is in northwest Oklahoma on US-281, about 5 miles south of the town of Waynoka. Access is by car on paved highway and there is no public transportation. Waynoka has groceries and fuel, but the park has no concessions, so stock up before arriving. Pedestrians are not allowed in the riding area, and spectators can watch from the observation deck at Buttercup Campground. Riders need a daily dune permit, whips with orange flags and other safety equipment, and alcohol is prohibited in the riding area.

Seasons

The dune riding area is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and the park is open year-round without seasonal closures. Spring and fall are the most popular seasons because milder temperatures make riding and camping more comfortable. Summer days are hot with no shade on the dunes, while winter is quiet and cold.

Things to Do

Ride the Sand Dunes

Little Sahara State Park is one of the premier off-road recreation destinations in the United States, featuring more than 1,600 acres of sand dunes that range in height from approximately 25 to 75 feet. Visitors enjoy riding ATVs, dune buggies, motorcycles, and other off-highway vehicles across the expansive dune landscape.

Enjoy Camping and Outdoor Recreation

The park offers camping facilities, picnic areas, and open spaces that allow visitors to experience Oklahoma's unique dune environment. Many visitors stay overnight to enjoy multiple days of riding and outdoor adventure.

Explore the Unique Sand Dune Ecosystem

Beyond its recreation opportunities, Little Sahara protects a distinctive landscape formed by ancient river deposits and wind-blown sands. Visitors can observe specialized plants and wildlife that have adapted to life in this dynamic dune environment.

Top Trails

Sand Dune Riding Area

Length: More than 1,600 acres of open riding terrain
Elevation Gain: Varies throughout the dunes
Difficulty: Beginner to Advanced

The park's primary attraction is its vast network of open sand dunes. Riders can choose gentle rolling dunes or tackle steeper slopes and larger formations depending on their skill level and vehicle type.

Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short trail provides an opportunity to experience the dune ecosystem on foot while learning about native plants, wildlife, and the geologic history that created Little Sahara's unique landscape.

Visitor Centers

Little Sahara does not have a visitor center. The park office is open daily from 8:00 am to 4:00 pm and is where riders buy permits, annual passes ($300 plus tax) and information, and the office phone is 580-824-1471. Permits can also be paid at kiosks at Competition and Buttercup campgrounds when no gate attendant is on duty. The observation deck at Buttercup Campground is the best place to see the dunes without entering the riding area.

Camping and Lodging

The park has camping at its Competition and Buttercup campgrounds, with RV sites that offer water, electric and sewer hookups, tent sites, picnic areas and comfort stations. All campsites must be reserved online or through the state park reservation center. The closest motels and restaurants are in Waynoka, about 5 miles away, with more choices in Alva and Woodward, each within about an hour.
