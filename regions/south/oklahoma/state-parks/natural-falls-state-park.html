How to Get There

From West Siloam Springs (5 mi): Take US-412 west from West Siloam Springs, turn south onto OK-10, continue toward Colcord, then follow signs to Natural Falls State Park via E 578 Road.

Overview

Natural Falls State Park is a 120-acre Oklahoma state park in the Ozark Highlands of Delaware County, near the Arkansas state line. It protects a 77-foot waterfall that drops through rock formations at the bottom of a narrow, V-shaped valley. The property was privately owned and known as Dripping Springs, with a swimming pool, gardens and a roadside stop for travelers on US-412, until the state bought it in 1990, and it was used in the filming of the 1974 movie Where the Red Fern Grows. As the water falls, evaporation cools the air by about ten degrees, creating a moist microclimate where ferns, mosses and liverworts grow beneath maples and oaks. Visitors come to see the falls from an observation platform and a deck at its base, to hike short trails and to camp in a wooded setting.

Access

The park is near the small town of Colcord in northeast Oklahoma, within about 5 miles of US-412 and just west of West Siloam Springs and the Arkansas border. Access is by car on paved roads, with the park entrance on E 578 Road, and there is no public transportation. The park is open from dawn to dusk, 7 days a week, though campers may enter at any hour. A parking fee applies for day visitors. The park is an easy drive from northwest Arkansas.

Seasons

The park is open year-round. Spring is the best time to see the waterfall at its fullest, along with wildflowers and lush green forest, and the flow varies with recent rainfall. Summer is the busiest season for camping, and the shaded valley stays cooler than the surrounding country. Fall brings color to the Ozark hardwoods, and winter offers quiet hiking and clear views of the falls with fewer visitors.

Things to Do

See Natural Falls

Natural Falls State Park is home to one of Oklahoma's most beautiful waterfalls, a 77-foot cascade that plunges into a narrow, forested valley. The waterfall is the park's centerpiece and provides excellent opportunities for photography, sightseeing, and enjoying the natural beauty of the Ozark Highlands region.

Explore Scenic Forests and Overlooks

The park features mature hardwood forests, scenic overlooks, picnic areas, and peaceful natural settings that make it a popular destination for hiking, birdwatching, and wildlife observation. Visitors can enjoy panoramic views of the waterfall and surrounding landscape from multiple vantage points.

Enjoy Camping and Outdoor Recreation

Natural Falls State Park offers camping, hiking, horseback riding, volleyball, and nature programs. The park's tranquil setting and diverse recreational opportunities make it a favorite destination for families, photographers, and outdoor enthusiasts.

Top Trails

Ghost Coon Trail

Length: Approximately 1.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 200 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This scenic trail leads visitors through the park's forests and natural areas while providing access to multiple overlooks of Natural Falls. Along the route, hikers can enjoy wildlife viewing and views of the surrounding Ozark landscape.

Dripping Springs Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short trail offers a pleasant walk through shaded woodlands and provides access to scenic viewpoints, interpretive features, and additional views of the park's natural features.

Visitor Centers

Natural Falls does not have a traditional visitor center. The park office handles information, camping and group arrangements and can be reached at 918-422-5802, and the Red Fern Reunion Center is used for group events. An observation platform and picnic pavilion overlook the falls.

Camping and Lodging

The park has RV and tent sites with a comfort station and showers, five yurts that can be reserved online, and a hike-in camping area called Fox Meadow that requires a permit from the park office. Motels and restaurants are available in West Siloam Springs, about 5 miles away, and in nearby Siloam Springs, Arkansas, with larger selections in Bentonville and Fayetteville, Arkansas, within about an hour.
