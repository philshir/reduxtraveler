How to Get There

Take US-75 N into downtown Tulsa, exit at 7th Street, turn right onto S Boston Ave, and continue north to the depot near 1st Street.

Overview

The Tulsa Union Depot, also known as Tulsa Union Station, is a striking example of Art Deco architecture and a symbol of Tulsa’s golden age of rail travel. Opened in 1931, the depot unified several rail lines and quickly became a bustling hub, serving up to 36 trains a day. Designed by architect R.C. Stephens and built by Manhattan Construction, the building features classic Deco motifs like winged wheels and sunbursts. It was a beacon of hope during the Great Depression, funded by a 1927 bond issue and celebrated with a grand opening attended by over 60,000 people.

After closing to passenger service in 1967, the depot underwent several transformations, including a major renovation in the 1980s and a stint as the home of the Oklahoma Jazz Hall of Fame. As of 2025, the building is undergoing further renovations with plans to reopen as a cultural venue and event space. Though currently closed to the public, the depot remains a cherished landmark in downtown Tulsa, admired for its architectural beauty and historical significance.
