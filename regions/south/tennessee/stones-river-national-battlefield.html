Stones River National Battlefield

★5.0
  1. Northeast
  2. Tennessee
  3. Stones River National Battlefield

Preserved Civil War battlefield featuring historic fields, monuments, walking trails, and interpretive exhibits commemorating one of the war's bloodiest and most strategically important Union victories.

Visitor Center: 9am to 4pm November through February

How to Get There

From Murfreesboro (3 mi): Take Thompson Lane north from downtown Murfreesboro and continue to the battlefield entrance at 1563 North Thompson Lane, following signs to the visitor center and tour road.

Overview

Stones River National Battlefield preserves the site of the Battle of Stones River, fought from December 31, 1862, to January 2, 1863, near Murfreesboro. It was one of the bloodiest battles of the Civil War in proportion to the number of men who fought, and the Union victory gave a much-needed boost to morale after Fredericksburg. The park includes the Hazen Brigade Monument, the oldest intact Civil War monument in the nation, the Stones River National Cemetery and the remains of Fortress Rosecrans. People come to follow the tour road, walk the battlefield and learn how the battle shaped Tennessee and the war.

Access

The battlefield is just northwest of Murfreesboro, about 30 miles southeast of Nashville, with the visitor center on Old Nashville Highway. Visitors drive from I-24 and US-41/70S to the park, and the roads are paved. Parking is free, and admission is free. There is no public transportation, though the Music City Star train runs between Nashville and Lebanon, and Nashville International Airport is about 30 minutes away.

Seasons

The park is open daily year-round, and the tour road is open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. in spring and summer and 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. in winter. Spring and fall are the most pleasant times for walking the battlefield, with wildflowers in spring and fall color. Summers are hot and humid, and winters are mild but can be cold and damp. The anniversary of the battle at the end of December and early January brings special programs.

Things to Do

Drive the Tour Road

A self-guided tour stops at key battle sites such as the Cotton Field, the Slaughter Pen and the Round Forest.

Visit the Hazen Brigade Monument

This stone monument, built in 1863, is the oldest intact Civil War memorial.

Explore the Visitor Center Museum

Exhibits, a film and artifacts explain the battle and its soldiers.

Walk Fortress Rosecrans

The earthworks of the supply depot show the scale of the Union fortifications.

Visit the National Cemetery

The cemetery holds the graves of thousands of Union soldiers.

Top Trails

Hazen Brigade Monument Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk to the Hazen Brigade Monument and the surrounding battlefield.

Fortress Rosecrans Earthworks Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail follows the remains of Union fortifications and gives views of the Stones River valley.

Visitor Centers

The visitor center, open daily from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in spring and summer and to 4 p.m. in winter, has a museum, film, bookstore and rangers who offer maps, talks and tours. Admission is free. The visitor center is the best starting point for the tour road and trails.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Murfreesboro has many hotels, restaurants and shops, only a few minutes away, and Nashville, about 30 miles northwest, offers the widest range. Camping is available at Cedars of Lebanon State Park, about 25 miles northeast, and at the Corps of Engineers campgrounds on Percy Priest Lake, about 15 minutes away.
