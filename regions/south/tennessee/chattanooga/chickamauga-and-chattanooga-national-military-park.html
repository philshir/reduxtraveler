Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park

Park reserves the sites of two major battles of the American Civil War: the Battle of Chickamauga and the Siege of Chattanooga

General Information

Hours:
Park grounds: Sunrise to sunset daily
Chickamauga Battlefield Visitor Center: 8:30am to 5:00pm daily
Lookout Mountain Visitor Center: Generally 9:00am to 6:00pm daily
Fees:
Most areas of the park are free to visit.
Point Park at Lookout Mountain: $10 per person (ages 16 and older).
Children age 15 and under: Free.
Pet Policy:
Pets are permitted on a leash in outdoor areas of the park. Owners must clean up after their pets and maintain control at all times.
Closest cities with hotels:
Chattanooga, Tennessee (approximately 8 mi)
Seasons:
Open year-round. Spring and fall are the most popular seasons due to mild temperatures and scenic views. Summer is warm and ideal for outdoor touring, while winter offers smaller crowds.
Location:
3370 LaFayette Rd, Fort Oglethorpe, GA 30742
Website:
nps.gov/chch

How to Get There

From Chattanooga, Tennessee (8 mi): Take US-27 S from downtown Chattanooga, continue toward Fort Oglethorpe, turn onto Battlefield Parkway (GA-2), then follow LaFayette Road south to the Chickamauga Battlefield Visitor Center at 3370 LaFayette Rd.

Overview

The Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park preserves the sites of two major battles of the American Civil War, the Battle of Chickamauga in September 1863 and the Chattanooga campaign that followed in November. Spread across Georgia and Tennessee, the park protects battlefield landscapes that decided control of Chattanooga, a key rail hub often called the gateway to the Deep South. Congress established it in 1890 as the nation's first national military park, and it became a model for later battlefield preservation. Chickamauga, one of the bloodiest battles of the war, is preserved in northwest Georgia, while Lookout Mountain, Missionary Ridge, Orchard Knob and other sites overlook Chattanooga. Visitors come for the hundreds of monuments, the scenic overlooks and the chance to walk historic ground.

Access

The park has several separate units. The Chickamauga Battlefield and its main visitor center are near Fort Oglethorpe, Georgia, about 8 miles south of downtown Chattanooga, and a self-guided auto tour road runs through the battlefield. Point Park on Lookout Mountain, the Lookout Mountain Battlefield visitor center, Orchard Knob and Missionary Ridge are in and around Chattanooga, each with its own parking and access. Chattanooga is on I-24 and I-75, and Atlanta and Knoxville are each about two hours away. Plan on at least a full day to see the main units, and allow extra time for the mountain roads and traffic around Lookout Mountain.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the most popular seasons, with mild weather, flowering trees and wildflowers in spring and fall color on Lookout Mountain and the Georgia battlefield. Summers are warm to hot and humid, and the shaded forest roads and overlooks make touring more comfortable early in the day. Winters are mild with occasional ice or snow, fewer visitors and clearer views from Lookout Mountain, and some programs and facilities operate on reduced schedules. The park grounds are open from sunrise to sunset year-round, and the fee area at Point Park has set hours.

Things to Do

Drive the Chickamauga Battlefield Auto Tour

A self-guided tour road passes monuments, tablets and key battlefield sites such as Snodgrass Hill, where Union troops made a famous stand. Allow a couple of hours with stops.

Climb Wilder Tower

The Wilder Brigade Monument is a tall stone tower at the Georgia battlefield with views over the forest and the surrounding countryside when it is open.

Visit Point Park on Lookout Mountain

Point Park has monuments and spectacular views of Chattanooga, the Tennessee River and the surrounding ridges, and it was the site of the Union victory known as the Battle Above the Clouds.

See Missionary Ridge and Orchard Knob

These sites tell the story of the Union assault that broke the Confederate siege of Chattanooga in November 1863.

Join a Ranger Program or Hike the Trails

Rangers offer talks and tours in season, and hiking trails in the Georgia battlefield and on Lookout Mountain explore woods, ridgelines and historic ground.

Visitor Centers

The Chickamauga Battlefield Visitor Center near Fort Oglethorpe has exhibits, a film, a bookstore and the Fuller Gun Collection of historic firearms, and it is the best place to begin a tour of the Georgia battlefield. On Lookout Mountain, the Lookout Mountain Battlefield visitor facilities at Point Park provide exhibits and information about the Chattanooga campaign. Hours vary by unit and season, so check the National Park Service website before you go.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the park units. Fort Oglethorpe and Ringgold, Georgia, have motels near the Chickamauga Battlefield, while Chattanooga offers the widest choice of hotels, inns and restaurants. Camping options near the park include Cloudland Canyon State Park in Georgia, Harrison Bay State Park in Tennessee and Raccoon Mountain Campground near Chattanooga. Within one to two hours, Dalton and Rome, Georgia, and Cleveland, Tennessee, provide additional lodging.
