How to Get There

From McMinnville (15 mi): Take US-70S E from McMinnville, turn right onto TN-287 S, and follow the signs into Rock Island State Park to reach the visitor area, overlooks, and waterfall access points.

Overview

Rock Island State Park is a Tennessee state park on a peninsula in Warren and White counties, formed where the Collins River meets the Caney Fork just above Great Falls Dam and the headwaters of Center Hill Lake. It is best known for the Great Falls gorge, where a series of plunge and cascade waterfalls spill into the Caney Fork, and for Twin Falls, which pours from a cavern in the rock below the dam. Historic structures near the falls include the 1892 Great Falls Cotton Mill, its "Spring Castle" spring house, and the Great Falls Dam, built in 1915-1916, all listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The area was set aside as a Tennessee state park in the late 1960s to protect the river scenery and mill ruins and to provide public recreation. Visitors come for waterfall viewing, swimming, paddling, fishing, hiking and photography.

Access

The park is in the Highland Rim and Cumberland Plateau region of Middle Tennessee, near the small town of Rock Island and about 15 miles from McMinnville. Most visitors arrive by car from McMinnville along US-70S and TN-287, and the park is within a roughly one to two hour drive of Nashville and Chattanooga. There is no public transportation to the park. Some riverside areas near the dam can be affected by water releases, so check conditions and posted warnings before entering the water or walking along the river.

Seasons

Spring is often the best time for waterfalls, when rain keeps the falls and river running strongly and wildflowers bloom in the woods. Summers are warm and humid, and swimming, paddling and fishing are at their most popular, so the riverfront areas can be busy on weekends. Fall brings comfortable temperatures and colorful foliage over the gorge and lake. Winter is quiet and cool, with unobstructed views of the falls and gorge and fewer visitors, though some facilities may have reduced services. Water levels and the appearance of the falls change with rainfall and dam operations in any season.

Things to Do

See Great Falls and Twin Falls

Rock Island State Park is famous for its spectacular waterfalls, including Great Falls, an approximately 30-foot waterfall that spans nearly 100 feet across the Caney Fork River. Nearby Twin Falls, created by water flowing from the Great Falls Dam, is another iconic attraction and one of Tennessee's most photographed waterfalls.

Enjoy River Recreation

The park offers excellent opportunities for kayaking, fishing, swimming, boating, and whitewater paddling. The Caney Fork, Collins, and Rocky rivers converge near the park, making it a popular destination for water-based recreation and outdoor adventure.

Explore Scenic Overlooks and Natural Areas

Visitors can enjoy overlooks above the gorge, rugged river scenery, limestone cliffs, and forested landscapes throughout the park. Wildlife viewing, photography, picnicking, and nature observation are popular activities year-round.

Top Trails

Downstream Trail

Length: Approximately 1.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 200 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail follows the Caney Fork River below Great Falls and provides excellent views of the gorge, river rapids, and surrounding rock formations. It is one of the park's most popular hiking routes.

Blue Hole Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short trail leads to the Blue Hole area, a scenic swimming and recreation spot along the river. Visitors can enjoy beautiful water views and easy access to one of the park's most popular natural features.

Visitor Centers

Rock Island State Park has a park office and visitor center where staff can provide maps, current information on trails and water conditions, and answers about the falls and historic mill. Interpretive signs near the falls and historic structures explain the area's river, mill and dam history. It is best to stop in early in your visit and confirm hours, which can vary by season.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground with tent and RV sites, as well as picnic areas and a swimming beach on Center Hill Lake. Check with Tennessee State Parks for current campground availability, amenities and any cabin or lodging offerings. Hotels, motels and restaurants are available in McMinnville, about 15 miles away, and in nearby Sparta and Smithville. Larger selections of lodging can be found in Cookeville and Tullahoma, each within roughly an hour's drive, and in Murfreesboro and Nashville within about two hours. Other state parks and lake campgrounds in the Center Hill Lake area offer additional camping options.
