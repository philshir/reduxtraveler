Hollenberg Pony Express Station State Historic Site

★5.0
  1. Midwest
  2. Kansas

Preserved frontier-era way station featuring original Pony Express history, trail-era artifacts, and exhibits highlighting overland travel across the American West.

How to Get There

From Marysville (25 mi): Take US-36 W toward Hanover, turn north onto K-148, continue about 4 miles, then travel east on K-243 for approximately 1 mile to reach the Hollenberg Pony Express Station State Historic Site.

Overview

Hollenberg Pony Express Station State Historic Site preserves the only unaltered Pony Express station that still stands on its original site. Built around 1857 by Gerat and Sophia Hollenberg along a heavily traveled emigrant route, the wood-frame building served as a store, tavern, post office and lodging place for travelers on the Oregon and California Trails. When the Pony Express began in 1860, it became a relay stop where riders changed horses and carried the mail across the frontier. Visitors come to walk through the original building, learn about the riders and settlers who passed through and see open prairie that looks much as it did more than 160 years ago.

Access

The site is in northeastern Kansas near the town of Hanover in Washington County. From Marysville, visitors take US-36 west toward Hanover, turn north on K-148, and then follow K-243 east for about a mile. The roads are paved or well-maintained gravel, and the site is reached by car. It is about 25 miles from Marysville and is a rural location, so bring food and water.

Seasons

The site is open seasonally, usually from late spring through early fall, and then closes for winter. Summer is the best time to visit, with warm weather and the full schedule of tours, but it can be hot and humid. Spring and fall offer cooler temperatures and a quieter experience. Hours are limited to Wednesday through Sunday and the site is closed on state holidays, so check ahead.

Things to Do

Tour the Original Pony Express Station

The wood-frame station remains largely unchanged and shows how a way station served riders, emigrants and merchants. Guided and self-guided tours explain the building's history.

Visit the Interpretive Center

Exhibits describe the Pony Express, Oregon and California Trail travel and daily life on the frontier, with artifacts and photographs.

Learn About the Pony Express Riders

Displays tell the story of the riders and the short but legendary service that sped mail across the continent before the transcontinental telegraph.

See the Prairie Landscape

The surrounding prairie closely resembles what travelers saw in the 1860s, making it a good place for photographs and reflection.

Explore Nearby Trail Sites

The area is part of the Oregon-California Trail corridor, and nearby Alcove Spring near Marysville is another important stop along the route.

Top Trails

Station Grounds Walk

Length: Less than 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk around the historic station and the surrounding grounds offers a view of the original building and the open prairie landscape.

Visitor Centers

A visitor center near the station provides exhibits, a short introduction and information about the Pony Express and overland trails. Staff can answer questions about the station and nearby historic sites. The site is open Wednesday through Sunday during the season, with free admission.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. The nearest hotels and motels are in Marysville, about 25 miles away, and in Washington, Kansas. Beatrice, Nebraska, about 45 miles north, offers a larger selection of hotels and restaurants and is home to Homestead National Historical Park. Camping is available at several county and city parks in the Marysville area.
