How to Get There

From Colby, KS (90 mi): Take US-83 S to K-95 near Scott City, then follow signs north to the park entrance on Scott Lake Drive.

Overview

Lake Scott State Park is a canyon oasis in western Kansas that combines natural beauty with remarkable history. The 1,020-acre park surrounds a spring-fed lake in a wooded canyon of limestone bluffs, and it is home to deer, wild turkey, beaver and many birds. It is best known for the El Cuartelejo ruins, the northernmost Native American pueblo in the United States, built in the 1600s, and for the nearby Steele Homestead, a preserved pioneer site. Visitors come to hike, fish, paddle, swim and camp in a setting that feels very different from the surrounding High Plains, and to learn about more than two dozen documented archaeological sites in the area.

Access

The park is in Scott County, about 10 miles north of Scott City along K-95. From Colby it is roughly 90 miles by way of US-83 and K-95, and from Garden City it is about an hour away. Most visitors arrive by car on paved roads, and there is no public transportation. Services are available in Scott City, but the park is rural and distances to larger towns are long, so plan ahead for fuel and supplies.

Seasons

Spring through fall is the best time to visit, when the canyon is green, wildlife is active and the lake is open for fishing, paddling and swimming. Summer brings the warmest weather and the most visitors, and the shade of the canyon helps on hot days. Fall offers cooler hiking and some color in the trees, and winter is quiet, with fewer services and occasional icy trails. Some amenities, such as the swimming beach and rentals, operate seasonally, so check hours before visiting.

Things to Do

Visit El Cuartelejo, the Northernmost Pueblo in the U.S.

The ruins of El Cuartelejo preserve a pueblo built by Taos Indians in the 1600s and are a National Historic Landmark. Interpretive displays tell the story of the people who lived on the High Plains and how Spanish, Native and later settlers interacted here.

Tour the Steele Homestead

The Steele Homestead gives a look at pioneer life in western Kansas. Historic buildings and exhibits show how early settlers lived and worked in the canyon.

Fish, Paddle and Swim

Scott State Fishing Lake is stocked for fishing, and seasonal canoe and paddleboat rentals are available. The park also has a swimming beach for summer visits.

Hike Through the Canyon

Trails climb along limestone bluffs and wooded slopes and offer views over the lake and canyon. They are a good way to see the area's plants and wildlife.

Camp and Enjoy the Outdoors

Camping, picnicking and horseback riding make the park a good base for a weekend trip in western Kansas.

Top Trails

Lake Scott Lakeshore Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This gentle path follows the shoreline through trees and along the water, with chances to watch birds and other wildlife. It is a good choice for families.

Canyon Bluff Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This route climbs onto the bluffs above the canyon for views of the lake and the surrounding prairie. Hikers should expect some uneven terrain and wear sturdy shoes.

Visitor Centers

The park office is the main information stop and handles permits, camping reservations and questions about trails and history. The El Cuartelejo site and the Steele Homestead have interpretive signs and displays, and the park office and nearby museums in Scott City provide further information on the area's archaeology. Office hours vary by season, so check with the park before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

Lake Scott has campsites ranging from primitive to utility sites, as well as cabins that can be reserved through the Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks. The nearest hotels and motels are in Scott City, about 10 miles from the park. Garden City (about an hour away) offers the widest selection of hotels, and Oakley and Colby are options within about 1.5 hours.
