Overview

Topeka is the capital of Kansas, on the Kansas River in the northeastern part of the state, and it is best known for its role in the civil rights movement. The Supreme Court's 1954 decision in Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka, which ruled that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional, began with a case filed here, and the former Monroe Elementary School is now a National Historical Park. The city is also known for the Kansas State Capitol and for being home to the Menninger Clinic, a leader in psychiatric care.

Visitors come to Topeka for the Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park, the Kansas State Capitol, the Kansas Museum of History, and the Great Overland Station. They also come for aviation museums such as the Combat Air Museum and the American Flight Museum, and for Gage Park and the Topeka Zoo, and the city is an affordable and quiet stop for travelers along Interstate 70.

History

Topeka was founded in 1854 by free-state settlers, who chose the name, thought to come from a Native American word meaning "a good place to dig potatoes," and it became the territorial capital and then the state capital when Kansas was admitted to the Union in 1861. The Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway was headquartered here and made the city an important railroad center, with shops that employed thousands for decades. Dr. Karl Menninger and his family founded the Menninger Clinic here in 1925, which made Topeka a national center for psychiatry. In 1954, the Supreme Court decided Brown v. Board of Education, and the city has since commemorated the legacy of the case.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Kansas State Capitol, completed in 1903, offers tours up to the dome with views over the city, and its walls have murals including John Steuart Curry's "Tragic Prelude" about John Brown. The Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park is housed in the former Monroe Elementary School and has free exhibits, and the Great Overland Station is a restored Union Pacific depot with a railroad museum. The NOTO Arts District, with galleries and restaurants, and Gage Park with a rose garden and carousel, are also popular.

Culture and Museums

The Kansas Museum of History has an extensive collection that covers prehistory to the present, and the Museum of the Kansas National Guard tells the story of the state's military history. The Combat Air Museum has more than 40 aircraft, and the American Flight Museum has restored vintage planes. The Topeka and Shawnee County Public Library and the Mulvane Art Museum at Washburn University are other attractions, and the Topeka Performing Arts Center hosts concerts and shows.

Parks

Topeka has about 100 parks. Gage Park has the Topeka Zoo, the Reinisch Rose Garden, a carousel, and a miniature train, and Lake Shawnee, Cedar Crest, and the Kansas River Trail offer boating and walking. The Shunga Trail is a paved trail that winds through the city, and the Topeka Zoo and Conservation Center is well known for its tropical rain forest. Ward-Meade Historic Site has a botanical garden and a historic mansion.

Seasons and Weather

Topeka has a humid continental to humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s°F and about 20 inches of snow. Spring and fall are mild, and the city is in Tornado Alley, with severe weather most likely from April to June. The city had a major tornado in 1966.
