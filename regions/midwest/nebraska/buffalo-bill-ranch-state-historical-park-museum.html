Buffalo Bill Ranch State Historical Park Museum

A historic estate featuring a restored 19th‑century mansion, barn exhibits, and extensive memorabilia connected to frontier-era life and entertainment.

How to Get There

Follow US‑26 East from Scottsbluff, merge onto I‑80 East toward North Platte, then exit and continue north to Scouts Rest Ranch Road to reach the park entrance.

Overview

Buffalo Bill Ranch State Historical Park preserves Scouts Rest Ranch, the home of William F. "Buffalo Bill" Cody, the frontier scout and showman who created the famous Wild West shows. The 1886 mansion is furnished with period items and family belongings, and a restored barn displays saddles, wagons, posters and other memorabilia from Cody's traveling productions. The grounds once covered thousands of acres of ranchland, and today they offer a peaceful setting for walking and picnicking. Visitors come for a glimpse of one of America's best-known entertainers and for the story of the Wild West in popular imagination.

Access

The park is at 2921 Scouts Rest Ranch Road in North Platte, in west-central Nebraska, a few miles north of I-80. From Lincoln it is about 3.5 hours by interstate, and from Scottsbluff it is about 3 hours via US-26 and I-80. Roads are paved, and nearly all visitors arrive by car. There is no public transportation, but North Platte offers a full range of services.

Seasons

The mansion and barn museum are open from late spring through early fall, generally May 1 to October 31, and the grounds are open year-round. Summer is the best time to visit and may include special events such as the Nebraskaland Days celebration in North Platte in June. Spring and fall are mild and quieter, while winter is cold, with the buildings closed but the grounds available for walks.

Things to Do

Tour the Cody Mansion

The 1886 mansion is furnished with period pieces and shows both the personal and public lives of Buffalo Bill and his family.

See the Barn Exhibits

The restored barn displays saddles, wagons, posters and artifacts from Cody's Wild West shows, offering a rare look at 19th-century entertainment.

Stroll the Ranch Grounds

Self-guided walks through the grounds give a sense of the working ranch Cody ran, with shaded paths and picnic areas.

Attend Seasonal Programs

Demonstrations and educational events highlight ranch life, horsemanship and frontier history during the summer season.

Visit North Platte Attractions

Bailey Yard, the world's largest railroad classification yard, and the Golden Spike Tower are nearby.

Top Trails

Scouts Rest Ranch Grounds Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This gentle route circles the mansion, barn and nearby grounds, with signs about ranch life. It is flat and suitable for families.

Visitor Centers

The museum and mansion are the main visitor stop, and staff provide tours, exhibits and information about Buffalo Bill and the ranch. There is a small gift shop and information about the park. The mansion is open Wednesday through Sunday from May through October, with the grounds open year-round, so check hours before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the park. North Platte, just a few miles away, has many hotels, motels and restaurants along I-80 and in town. Camping is available at Lake Maloney State Recreation Area, about 8 miles south, and Lake McConaughy, about an hour and a half west. Kearney and Ogallala are about 1 to 1.5 hours away and offer additional options.
