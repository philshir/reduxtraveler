How to Get There

From Louisville (3 mi): Take NE-50 south, turn west onto 346th Street, and continue to the park entrance at 14421 346th St.

Overview

Platte River State Park is a public recreation area encompassing 453 acres (183 ha) on the southern bluffs of the Platte River two miles (3.2 km) west of Louisville, Nebraska. The state park has a relatively steep, rolling topography compared to the surrounding region, with much of it forested.

Things to Do

Enjoy Outdoor Adventure in Eastern Nebraska's Wooded Hills

Platte River State Park is a popular outdoor recreation destination located between Omaha and Lincoln near Louisville, Nebraska. Nestled among rolling hills, forests, and scenic creek valleys, the park offers hiking, fishing, camping, mountain biking, wildlife viewing, and family-friendly activities. Its location along the Platte River corridor makes it one of Nebraska's most accessible and beloved state parks.

Visit the Park's Scenic Waterfalls

The park is home to two picturesque waterfalls, including the well-known Stone Creek Falls and Coon Creek Falls. These man-made cascades blend beautifully with the natural landscape and are popular attractions for photographers, hikers, and visitors seeking scenic views.

Climb the Observation Towers

Two observation towers provide panoramic views of the surrounding forests, hills, and Platte River Valley. The park's towers offer excellent opportunities for photography, birdwatching, and enjoying Nebraska's changing seasons from an elevated vantage point.

Experience the Schramm Park Area Nearby

Many visitors combine their trip with nearby Schramm Park State Recreation Area and the Eugene T. Mahoney State Park complex. Together, these destinations create one of Nebraska's premier outdoor recreation regions, offering nature centers, aquariums, trails, and educational exhibits.

Enjoy Year-Round Recreation

Platte River State Park offers hiking, horseback riding, mountain biking, fishing, paddle sports, camping, archery, and winter activities. The park's cabins and facilities also make it a popular destination for family vacations and weekend getaways.

Top Trails

Stone Creek Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This short trail leads visitors to Stone Creek Falls, one of the park's signature attractions. The route passes through wooded terrain and offers scenic views of the waterfall and surrounding forest.

Coon Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail explores forested hillsides and creek valleys while providing access to scenic overlooks, natural habitats, and one of the park's waterfall areas.

Observation Tower Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Hikers climb through wooded terrain to reach observation towers offering sweeping views of eastern Nebraska's forests, bluffs, and river valley landscapes.

Mountain Bike Trail System

Length: More than 10 miles of interconnected trails
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Platte River State Park features one of eastern Nebraska's most popular mountain biking networks. Riders enjoy rolling hills, wooded single-track routes, and varied terrain suitable for multiple skill levels.

History

On August 13, 1982, the park was created from three formerly separate areas: Harriet Harding Campfire Girls Camp, Camp Esther K. Newman, and a woodland tract of 104 acres (42 ha). Vintage cabins from the earlier campgrounds are still in use.

Activities and amenities

Two observation towers provide views of the Platte River Valley; the taller rises 85 feet (26 m) above its base. The park has a spray park, tennis courts and ten miles (16 km) of hiking trails. Guided trail rides are offered in summer. Jenny Newman Lake provides fishing opportunities. Paddleboats are offered for rent. The park has a visitor center, arts and crafts center, picnicking areas, ballfield, tent camping, tepees, and cabins. A rifle, trap, and archery shooting range is part of the Roger G. Sykes Outdoor Heritage Complex.
