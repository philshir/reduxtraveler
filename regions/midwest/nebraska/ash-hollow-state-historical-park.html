Overview

Ash Hollow State Historical Park is located five miles (8.0 km) south of Lewellen in Garden County, Nebraska. The park comprises two attractions located 2.5 miles (4.0 km) from each other: Ash Hollow Cave and Windlass Hill.

Ash Hollow Cave

A spring in the vicinity of Ash Hollow Cave made it an attractive site for periodic human habitation. Archaeological explorations of the cave have revealed that at least four distinct indigenous cultures occupied this area, during a period of more than 1,500 years. These include the Apache from A.D. 1675-1725; the Central Plains Tradition from A.D. 900-1450; the Woodland Tradition from A.D. 0-1100; and the Late Archaic Tradition from 1000 B.C.-A.D. 500. The cave was used as a base camp for hunting and food collecting.

From the early 18th century, this became an area predominately of Lakota Sioux occupation. The September 1855 Battle of Ash Hollow took place near here. The United States Army, with 600 troops, made a punitive attack on a Brule Sioux encampment, killing a total of 86 people. The attack on the Lakota Sioux was launched as a result of The Grattan Massacre of 1854 near Ft Larmie, Wyoming.

In 1962, the site was protected as part of a 312-acre Nebraska state park. Ash Hollow Cave was named as a National Historic Landmark in 1964, and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1966. The surrounding area was named the Ash Hollow Historic District in 1975. In 1978, a visitor center was built overlooking the canyon.

Windlass Hill

Windlass Hill is located along the Oregon-California Trail. The hill marked the entrance from the high table lands to the south into the Ash Hollow area and the North Platte River valley. Wagon ruts are visible on the hill. The name "Windlass Hill" was not used by the emigrants, and the source of the name is unknown.

Things to Do

Follow the Historic Oregon, California, and Mormon Trails

Ash Hollow State Historical Park is one of Nebraska's most significant historic and natural sites. Located near Lewellen in the North Platte River Valley, the park preserves an important landmark along the Oregon, California, Mormon, and Pony Express trails. For thousands of pioneers traveling west during the nineteenth century, Ash Hollow served as a critical stop offering water, shelter, and grazing lands before continuing across the Great Plains.

Explore Pioneer and Native American History

The park contains evidence of human activity dating back thousands of years. Visitors can learn about Indigenous peoples who lived in the region long before European settlement, as well as the emigrants who crossed the area during westward expansion. Interpretive exhibits explain the strategic importance of Ash Hollow to both Native Americans and pioneer travelers.

Visit Windlass Hill

One of the park's most famous attractions is Windlass Hill, where visible wagon ruts remain from thousands of westbound emigrants. The steep descent challenged wagon trains traveling from the higher plains into the North Platte Valley, making it one of the most memorable sections of the historic overland trails.

See Ash Hollow Cave and Natural Features

The park features scenic cliffs, springs, wooded ravines, and Ash Hollow Cave. These natural features provided vital resources for travelers and continue to attract visitors interested in geology, archaeology, and natural history.

Enjoy Wildlife Viewing and Photography

The combination of prairie, river valley, and woodland habitats supports deer, wild turkeys, songbirds, hawks, and numerous native plant species. The scenic landscapes and historic settings make Ash Hollow a popular destination for photography and nature observation.

Top Trails

Windlass Hill Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail leads visitors to the historic Windlass Hill area, where original wagon ruts from the Oregon Trail remain visible. The route offers excellent views of the North Platte Valley and opportunities to learn about pioneer travel.

Ash Hollow Cave Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk provides access to Ash Hollow Cave and interpretive information about the site's archaeological significance and natural history.

Interpretive Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail explores prairie and woodland habitats while highlighting the area's geology, wildlife, and role in westward migration across the Great Plains.

Historic Overland Trails Exploration

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

Additional walking opportunities throughout the park allow visitors to explore trail remnants, historical markers, scenic overlooks, and cultural landscapes associated with the Oregon, California, and Mormon Trails.
