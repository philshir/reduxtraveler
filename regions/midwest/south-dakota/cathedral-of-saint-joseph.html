How to Get There

From Sioux Falls (2 mi): Travel north toward downtown Sioux Falls, follow Minnesota Avenue to 5th Street, turn onto Duluth Avenue, and continue to the cathedral at 521 N Duluth Ave.

Overview

The Cathedral of Saint Joseph is a Catholic cathedral and parish church located in Sioux Falls, South Dakota. Since 1974 the cathedral has been a contributing property in the Cathedral Historic District on the National Register of Historic Places.

Overview

Rising atop a hill just west of downtown Sioux Falls, the Cathedral of Saint Joseph commands attention long before visitors reach its grand stairway. Designed by renowned French architect Emmanuel Masqueray and dedicated in 1919, this landmark blends French Renaissance and Romanesque influences into one of South Dakota’s most striking architectural treasures. Twin limestone towers soar above the city skyline, while intricate carvings, a magnificent rose window, and elegant stonework create a sense of timeless grandeur. Stepping inside, travelers are greeted by soaring arches, luminous stained-glass windows, and richly detailed artwork that transform the cathedral into both a place of worship and a work of art.

Much like a National Geographic journey into the heart of a destination, a visit to the Cathedral of Saint Joseph reveals the cultural story of Sioux Falls itself. The cathedral serves as the seat of the Diocese of Sioux Falls and stands as a symbol of the region’s growth from frontier settlement to modern city. Restored to its original splendor through an extensive renovation completed in 2011, the building offers visitors an immersive experience filled with history, craftsmanship, and quiet reflection. Whether admired from the surrounding historic district or explored through a self-guided tour, the cathedral remains one of Sioux Falls’ most inspiring attractions and a must-see stop for travelers seeking beauty, heritage, and architectural excellence.
