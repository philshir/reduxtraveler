How to Get There

From Columbia, MO (5 mi): Take Providence Road south to Route K, then turn left onto Highway 163 and follow signs to the park entrance.

Overview

Rock Bridge Memorial State Park is a Missouri state park of about 2,270 acres just south of Columbia, known for its karst scenery of caves, sinkholes, springs and a natural limestone bridge. Its best-known feature is Devil’s Icebox, a cave where an underground stream flows beneath the rock bridge, reached by a boardwalk trail. The park was established in 1967 and named a memorial after a young girl was killed in a 1961 hit-and-run accident near the site; it was created in her memory and to provide a safe place for children. The area was once home to the milling community of Rockbridge Mills, and the park is now popular for hiking, cycling, picnicking and cave viewing, including a look into Connor’s Cave from its entrance. The park’s Gans Creek Wild Area also draws hikers and horseback riders seeking a quieter day in the woods.

Access

The park is about 5 miles south of Columbia, Missouri, off Highway 163, and most visitors drive in from Columbia, which is also easy to reach from Interstate 70. Because the park is so close to town, the busiest times are weekends and nice-weather days, and the Devil’s Icebox boardwalk can be crowded. There is no public transportation to the park.

Seasons

The park is open daily year-round, and each season has its appeal. Spring brings wildflowers and flowing creeks and springs, and the forest canopy shades the trails in summer, though the weather is hot and humid. Fall color on the wooded hills is a highlight, and winter offers quieter trails and open views through bare trees. Trails can be muddy after rain, and some may close temporarily because of weather or maintenance, so check the park’s advisories before you go. Visitor center hours vary by season.

Things to Do

Explore Rock Bridge and Devil's Icebox

The park's most famous attractions are the natural limestone rock bridge and the Devil's Icebox, a dramatic karst feature that includes a cave system, sinkholes, and an underground stream. Visitors can view these unique geologic formations from trails and boardwalks throughout the area.

Discover Missouri's Cave and Karst Landscape

Rock Bridge Memorial State Park protects an extensive karst environment featuring caves, springs, sinking streams, and limestone formations. Interpretive signs along the trails help visitors understand the geology and natural processes that shaped the landscape.

Visit the Nature Center

The park's nature center offers exhibits on local wildlife, ecosystems, cave environments, and conservation. Educational programs and displays provide insight into the natural history of central Missouri.

Top Trails

Devil's Icebox Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail leads visitors through the park's most scenic area, including the Devil's Icebox, sinkholes, boardwalks, caves, and views of the underground stream that disappears into the karst landscape.

Gans Creek Wild Area Trail

Length: Approximately 4.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This longer trail traverses wooded hills, stream valleys, and natural areas within the park, offering opportunities for wildlife viewing, wildflower observation, and a quieter hiking experience away from the busiest attractions.

Visitor Centers

The park’s visitor center and nature center is the place to start, with exhibits on the area’s wildlife, caves and karst geology, plus trail maps and information from park staff. The park also runs interpretive programs and guided hikes throughout the year, and the park naturalist can answer questions about programs. Hours vary by season, so confirm them with the park before arriving.

Camping and Lodging

The park has no regular campground or lodging; only special-use camping is offered, with details available from the park. The closest hotels, motels and restaurants are in Columbia, about 5 miles away, which has a wide selection of chain hotels and inns. Jefferson City, the state capital, is about 30 miles away and also has a range of hotels. Campers looking for developed campgrounds can check the Missouri State Parks website for other parks within an hour or two of Columbia.
