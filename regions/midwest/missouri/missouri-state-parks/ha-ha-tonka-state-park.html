How to Get There

From Camdenton (5 mi): Take US-54 to State Road D and follow signs south into the park entrance near the castle ruins and trail areas.

Overview

Ha Ha Tonka State Park is a Missouri state park of more than 3,700 acres on the Niangua arm of the Lake of the Ozarks, about five miles south of Camdenton. It is known for the stone ruins of an early 20th-century mansion modeled on European castles, and for a rare concentration of karst features including a natural bridge, caves, sinkholes, towering bluffs and one of Missouri’s largest springs. Kansas City businessman Robert McClure Snyder began the castle in 1905, and the state bought the castle and grounds in 1978 and opened them as a park; a 70-acre portion was designated the Ha Ha Tonka Karst Natural Area in 1981. Visitors come to hike boardwalks and trails to the castle ruins, spring and overlooks, and to enjoy the lake.

Access

The park is at 1491 State Road D, about five miles southwest of Camdenton in the Lake of the Ozarks region of central Missouri, roughly 2.5 hours from Kansas City and about 2.5 hours from St. Louis. Most visitors drive from US-54 to State Road D, and there is no public transportation. Parking is near the castle and at lower trailheads, and the lot closest to the castle has limited spaces reserved for visitors with disabilities and older visitors, while others walk up from the lower lot. Several trails involve stairs and boardwalks.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with longer daily hours from April through October. Spring brings wildflowers and a full spring and waterfalls after rain, while summer is hot and busy, with boating and swimming on the lake and a crowd of families at the castle. Fall foliage over the lake and bluffs is a highlight and the castle overlook is especially popular then. In winter the park is quiet, and ice can make boardwalks and stairs slippery, and some trail sections may close for safety, so check conditions.

Things to Do

Explore the Castle Ruins

The park's most iconic attraction is the ruins of a turn-of-the-century stone castle perched atop a bluff overlooking the Lake of the Ozarks and Ha Ha Tonka Spring. The castle offers impressive views and a unique glimpse into the area's history.

See the Natural Wonders

Ha Ha Tonka State Park is known for its remarkable geologic features, including a large natural bridge, caves, sinkholes, towering bluffs, and Missouri's 12th-largest spring. Boardwalks and trails provide easy access to many of these scenic landmarks.

Hike the Scenic Trail System

A network of trails and boardwalks allows visitors to explore the park's woodlands, rocky glades, historic sites, overlooks, and geologic formations. The trail system provides some of the best hiking opportunities in the Lake of the Ozarks region.

Top Trails

Castle Trail

Length: Approximately 0.4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short, popular trail leads directly to the famous castle ruins and scenic overlooks above the lake and spring valley, making it one of the park's most visited routes.

Spring Trail

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile one way
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet descent and ascent
Difficulty: Moderate

The Spring Trail descends from the blufftop castle area to Ha Ha Tonka Spring, providing views of the spring, boardwalks, forests, and unique karst features along the way.

History

Construction of the Ha Ha Tonka castle was started in 1905 by Robert McClure Snyder, Sr., a Kansas City businessman who purchased the large property after first visiting there in 1903.

Following Snyder's death in an auto accident in 1906, the castle was completed by his sons Robert, Jr., LeRoy, and Kenneth Snyder in the early 1920s before the Stock Market Crash. The building was used as a summer and weekend home by the Snyder family, who lived in Kansas City. In the late 1930s, it was used as a hotel; however, it was destroyed by fire in 1942.

The state purchased the castle and grounds in 1978, adapting them for use as a state park and opening them to the public.

Activities and amenities

The park has 15 miles of hiking trails leading to caves, sinkholes, natural bridges, and the castle. The park also features boating, fishing, and swimming.

Visitor Centers

The park has a visitor center with information on the castle ruins and the area’s karst geology, staffed by park personnel who can provide maps and trail advice, and the park office can be reached at 573-346-2986. Hours vary seasonally, so check the Missouri State Parks website before you visit. Interpretive programs are offered at times of the year.

Camping and Lodging

The park does not offer regular campgrounds or lodging; the only camping listed is a special-use camping area, with details on the Missouri State Parks website. Hotels, motels, resorts and restaurants are in Camdenton, about five miles away, and throughout the Lake of the Ozarks area, including Osage Beach and Lake Ozark, within about 30 minutes. For camping, Lake of the Ozarks State Park offers campsites and cabins, and there are many private campgrounds and resorts around the lake. Rooms fill up on summer weekends, so book ahead.
