How to Get There

From Cape Girardeau (19 mi): Take MO-34 W toward Jackson, continue to Route HH near Burfordville, and follow signs to Bollinger Mill Road and the historic site entrance.

Overview

Bollinger Mill State Historic Site centers on a four-story brick gristmill beside the Whitewater River in Burfordville, Cape Girardeau County, and on the 1868 Burfordville Covered Bridge just downstream. George Frederick Bollinger, who received a Spanish land grant in 1797, built the first mill here around 1800; the present brick mill, completed by Solomon Burford in 1867 on an 1825 limestone foundation, replaced one burned during the Civil War. It ground grain until 1953 and passed to the State of Missouri in 1967, and the mill and bridge are both listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Visitors come to tour the mill and its machinery, photograph the Howe truss covered bridge, and picnic along the river.

Access

The site is at 113 Bollinger Mill Road in Burfordville, about 19 miles west of Cape Girardeau and a short drive from the town of Jackson, in southeast Missouri. Most visitors arrive by car on paved state routes, and parking is next to the mill. The grounds are small, flat and easy to walk, and the covered bridge is open to foot traffic only. It combines easily with a trip to Cape Girardeau or with other historic stops in the area, such as the nearby Trail of Tears State Park.

Seasons

The grounds are open every day from sunrise to sunset throughout the year, while mill tours and the museum follow seasonal schedules, with the most days open from spring through fall. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, with green banks or autumn color reflected in the mill pond and river. Summer is warm and humid, and winter brings fewer visitors and shorter hours inside the mill. Milling demonstrations with the original equipment depend on river water levels, and the site hosts occasional events such as music concerts and displays of restored farm equipment.

Things to Do

Tour the Historic Bollinger Mill

Bollinger Mill State Historic Site preserves a four-story water-powered gristmill that dates to the 1860s. Visitors can explore the mill's original machinery and learn how grain was processed to serve local communities in southeast Missouri.

Visit the Museum and Interpretive Exhibits

The site features exhibits highlighting the history of the Bollinger family, the operation of the mill, and the role milling played in the economic development of the region. Artifacts and displays help bring Missouri's agricultural history to life.

Enjoy the Covered Bridge and Scenic Grounds

A picturesque covered bridge spans the Whitewater River near the mill, creating one of the site's most photographed features. Visitors can stroll the grounds, enjoy river views, and appreciate the historic setting along the waterway.

Top Trails

Mill and Covered Bridge Walking Path

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short walk connects the historic mill, covered bridge, and riverfront areas while providing interpretive information about the site's history and operation.

Whitewater River Grounds Walk

Length: Varies throughout the site
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors can explore the historic grounds along the Whitewater River, enjoy views of the mill and bridge, and learn about the area's agricultural and transportation history.

Visitor Centers

The mill itself serves as the visitor center and museum. Guided tours take visitors through the working floors and the original milling equipment, while first-floor exhibits explain how milling changed over time and how the Bollinger family and the mill shaped the community. Inside the mill, hours are typically Wednesday through Sunday, though they vary by season, so confirm the schedule on the Missouri State Parks website before you go.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the historic site. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Jackson and in Cape Girardeau, about 19 miles away, which has the widest selection in the region. For camping, Trail of Tears State Park near Cape Girardeau has a campground and is roughly a 30-minute drive, and other campgrounds can be found along the Mississippi River and in the Mark Twain National Forest within one to two hours. Check each park’s website for availability and reservations.
