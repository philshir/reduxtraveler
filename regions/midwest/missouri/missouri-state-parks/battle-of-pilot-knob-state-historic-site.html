How to Get There

From Farmington (9 mi): Take US-67 S to Pilot Knob, turn onto Route 221 and follow signs to East Maple Street, where the historic site entrance and visitor center are located.

Overview

The Battle of Pilot Knob State Historic Site preserves Fort Davidson, the earthen Union fort at the center of one of the largest and hardest-fought battles on Missouri soil. On September 27, 1864, Confederate Major General Sterling Price led an army of about 8,000 men against the fort in the Arcadia Valley of Iron County as part of his Missouri raid. The Union garrison held off repeated assaults, then slipped away overnight after destroying the fort’s powder magazine, and the delay helped derail Price’s plan to capture St. Louis. Today the site is a Missouri State Parks historic site where visitors can see the surviving fort earthworks, tour a visitor center and museum, and walk a short interpretive trail beneath the surrounding St. Francois Mountains.

Access

The historic site is in the small town of Pilot Knob (118 E. Maple Street), in the Arcadia Valley of Missouri’s eastern Ozarks, about an hour and a half’s drive south of St. Louis. Most visitors arrive by car on paved state highways, and there is no public transportation. Parking is at the visitor center and fort, and the main walking areas are short and mostly level. The nearby towns of Ironton and Arcadia are only minutes away for meals and supplies.

Seasons

The grounds are open from sunrise to sunset year-round, while the visitor center keeps seasonal hours, with more days open from mid-April into the fall than in winter. Spring brings green hillsides and dogwood and redbud in the valley, and fall foliage on the surrounding mountains is a highlight. Summers are warm and humid, and the park notes that ticks and chiggers are abundant from May through mid-October. Winter is quiet and cold, and the anniversary of the battle in late September is a popular time for living-history and interpretive programs.

Things to Do

Tour Fort Davidson

Battle of Pilot Knob State Historic Site preserves Fort Davidson, the earthen fort at the center of the Battle of Pilot Knob fought on September 27, 1864. Visitors can explore the fort, learn about the Union defense against a much larger Confederate force, and see one of Missouri's most significant Civil War battlefields.

Visit the Visitor Center and Museum

The visitor center features exhibits, artifacts, weapons, uniforms, maps, and interpretive displays that explain the events of the battle and its importance in the Civil War. A museum and theater provide additional historical context for visitors.

Explore Battlefield Overlooks and Historic Sites

Walking trails and interpretive stops throughout the park lead to key battlefield locations, monuments, and overlooks. Visitors can follow the course of the battle while enjoying views of the Arcadia Valley and surrounding mountains.

Top Trails

Fort Davidson Walking Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short interpretive trail circles Fort Davidson and provides information about the battle, military strategy, and the soldiers who fought at the site. Visitors can explore the fort's earthworks and historic features along the route.

Shepherd Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 2.0 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 400 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs Shepherd Mountain, one of the key locations involved in the battle. The route offers scenic overlooks of Fort Davidson, the Arcadia Valley, and the surrounding St. Francois Mountains while highlighting important battlefield positions.

Visitor Centers

The visitor center and museum interpret the battle with exhibits, audiovisual presentations and a fiber-optic diorama of the fighting, and staff offer tours of the fort and the grounds. The visitor center is open daily during the main season and on fewer days in winter, and it is closed on some holidays, so check the hours on the Missouri State Parks website before you go. The grounds themselves, including the fort and trail, are open every day from sunrise to sunset.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the historic site. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Ironton and Arcadia a few minutes away, and in Farmington, roughly 30 minutes north. Campers can head to nearby state parks in the St. Francois Mountains, such as Johnson’s Shut-Ins State Park and Taum Sauk Mountain State Park, or to campgrounds in the Mark Twain National Forest, all within about an hour’s drive. Check each park’s website for current campground availability and reservations.
