How to Get There

From Farmington (4 mi): Take US-67 S to Park Hills, exit onto Missouri Route 32 and State Highway P, then follow signs to the historic site entrance.

Overview

Missouri Mines State Historic Site preserves Federal Mill No. 3, a former lead and zinc ore-processing mill in Park Hills, St. Francois County, that anchors the story of southeast Missouri’s Old Lead Belt. Federal Lead Company built the mill in 1906 to 1907, St. Joseph Lead Company bought it in 1923, and it closed in 1972. The land was donated to the state in 1975 and the historic site was established in 1976. The old power building now houses a museum and interpretive center on geology, minerals and the history of mining in the region, and the surrounding mill structures and equipment show the scale of the industry. Visitors come for the museum, the rock and mineral collections and the outdoor industrial remains.

Access

The site is at 4000 State Highway P in Park Hills, about 4 to 6 miles from Farmington and about an hour and a half south of St. Louis, and is reached by paved roads from US-67. Most visitors drive and there is no public transportation. Parking is next to the museum, and the museum and grounds are easy to explore on foot. It combines easily with other stops in the area, such as nearby St. Joe State Park, Elephant Rocks and Bonne Terre.

Seasons

The museum is open year-round and closed on only a few holidays, which makes the site a good choice for rainy, hot or cold days. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for walking the outdoor mill grounds, with mild temperatures and clear views of the industrial structures. Summers are warm and humid, with limited shade around the mill, and winter weather occasionally makes the grounds icy. Because the structures are old, some areas may be fenced off at times.

Things to Do

Explore the Mining Museum

Missouri Mines State Historic Site preserves the history of lead mining in the Old Lead Belt, one of the most significant mining regions in the United States. The museum features mining equipment, exhibits, photographs, minerals, and artifacts that tell the story of the area's industrial heritage and mining communities.

Tour Historic Mining Facilities

Visitors can view preserved structures from the former St. Joe Minerals Corporation, including the impressive powerhouse, milling facilities, and other industrial buildings that illustrate the scale of mining operations that shaped southeast Missouri for more than a century.

Learn About Geology and Mineral Resources

Interpretive exhibits explain how lead ore was formed, extracted, processed, and used. The site provides insight into Missouri's rich mineral resources and the important role mining played in the state's economic development.

Top Trails

Mine Interpretive Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short self-guided trail passes historic mining features and interpretive exhibits, helping visitors understand the site's industrial history, mining processes, and the lives of the people who worked there.

Historic Site Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1.0 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors can follow walking routes throughout the historic site to view preserved industrial buildings, mining equipment, and exhibits while learning about the development of the Old Lead Belt and Missouri's mining legacy.

Visitor Centers

The museum, in the former power building, serves as the visitor center. Exhibits cover geology, minerals, mining equipment, photographs and the people of the Old Lead Belt, with a large collection of rocks and minerals. It is open daily, typically from 10am to 4pm, but is closed on New Year’s Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, so confirm hours with Missouri State Parks. Staff can provide information about the grounds and programs.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the historic site. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Farmington, Park Hills and Bonne Terre, all within 15 minutes. The adjoining St. Joe State Park has a campground, and Johnson’s Shut-Ins State Park, Hawn State Park and Mark Twain National Forest campgrounds are within about an hour. Check each park’s website for availability and reservations.
