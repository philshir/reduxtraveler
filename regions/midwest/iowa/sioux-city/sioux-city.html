How to Get There

From Omaha (98 mi): Take I-29 N toward Sioux City, continue along the Missouri River corridor, then follow downtown exits into central Sioux City.

Overview

Sioux City is a city in northwest Iowa at the meeting of the Missouri, Big Sioux, and Floyd Rivers, where Iowa, Nebraska, and South Dakota come together. It is the largest city in the region known as Siouxland and has a history tied to the Lewis and Clark Expedition, steamboats, and meatpacking. The city is best known for the Sergeant Floyd Monument, the first National Historic Landmark in the United States, and for its riverfront museums and historic downtown.

Visitors come to Sioux City for the Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center, the Sergeant Floyd River Museum, and the Betty Strong Encounter Center, as well as the Sioux City Art Center and Trinity Heights. It is also a stop for travelers on Interstates 29 and 129 and a base for exploring the Loess Hills and the Missouri River valley, with a laid-back and affordable Midwestern feel.

History

Sergeant Charles Floyd, the only member of the Lewis and Clark Expedition to die on the trip, died near here in August 1804 and was buried on a bluff above the river, and the monument marking his grave was dedicated in 1901. The city was founded in 1854 and grew with steamboat traffic on the Missouri, and the railroads arrived in the 1860s and 1870s. By the early twentieth century the Sioux City Stockyards were one of the largest in the country, and meatpacking was a leading industry, and the city's population also included large immigrant groups. In recent decades the city has diversified into food processing, healthcare, and distribution, and the riverfront has been redeveloped with museums and parks.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Sergeant Floyd Monument, a 100-foot obelisk on a bluff south of downtown, is a National Historic Landmark, and the Sergeant Floyd River Museum is housed in a retired Army Corps of Engineers riverboat on the Missouri River. Trinity Heights is a religious site with a 33-foot statue of Our Lady of Peace and a Stations of the Cross walk, and the Historic Fourth Street District has restored brick buildings with shops and restaurants. The Orpheum Theatre and the Hard Rock Hotel & Casino are other attractions.

Culture and Museums

The Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center has exhibits on the expedition and its time on the Missouri River, and the Betty Strong Encounter Center tells the story of the Native peoples and the Lewis and Clark journey. The Sioux City Public Museum is housed in a Romanesque mansion, and the Sioux City Art Center shows regional and national art. The Mid-America Museum of Aviation & Transportation has exhibits on the 1989 crash of United Flight 232, and the Sioux City Symphony performs regularly.

Parks

Sioux City has many parks and trails. Stone State Park in the Loess Hills has hiking and equestrian trails, and Riverfront Park and the Chris Larsen Park along the Missouri have walking trails and a sculpture and a flowing fountain. The Loess Hills National Scenic Byway runs east of the city, and Dorothy Pecaut Nature Center has displays and trails. The Missouri River offers fishing and boating.

Seasons and Weather

Sioux City has a humid continental climate with hot summers and cold winters. Summer highs are in the upper 80s°F, winter highs are in the 20s and 30s°F, and snowfall averages about 30 inches. Spring and fall are mild, and the city can be windy, with severe thunderstorms in late spring and summer.
