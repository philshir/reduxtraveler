How to Get There

From Maquoketa (6 mi): Take US-61 north from Maquoketa, turn west onto 98th Street, and continue directly to the entrance of Maquoketa Caves State Park.

Overview

Maquoketa Caves State Park is a state park located in Jackson County. Iowa. The park contains more caves than any other state park in Iowa. A trail system links the caves, formations, and overlooks while providing a scenic hiking experience.

The park's limestone caves, arches and chimneys including Dancehall Cave, Hernado's Hideaway, Shinbone Cave, Wye Cave, and an unmarked cave within the Dancehall Cavern locally known as Steelgate Cave.

Things to Do

Explore Iowa's Premier Cave and Geological Wonderland

Maquoketa Caves State Park is one of Iowa's most unique natural attractions, located near Maquoketa in eastern Iowa. The park is famous for its extensive cave system, dramatic limestone formations, rugged bluffs, natural bridges, and scenic hiking trails. It contains more caves than any other state park in Iowa and offers visitors an exciting combination of geology, outdoor adventure, and natural beauty.

Discover the Caves

The park features more than a dozen caves that visitors can explore, ranging from small passageways to larger caverns. Dancehall Cave is the largest and most accessible cave in the park, with a spacious chamber large enough to accommodate groups of visitors. Other caves provide opportunities for exploration and a firsthand look at Iowa's karst landscape.

Explore the Natural Bridge and Rock Formations

In addition to its caves, the park is known for impressive limestone bluffs, balanced rocks, narrow passages, and a striking natural bridge. These geological features have been formed over thousands of years through erosion and create a landscape unlike anywhere else in Iowa.

Enjoy Camping and Nature Observation

The park offers campgrounds, picnic areas, and opportunities for wildlife viewing. Visitors may encounter white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, songbirds, bats, and a variety of native plants while exploring the park's forests and ravines.

Learn About Iowa's Geology

Interpretive exhibits and trail signage help visitors understand the geological processes that created the caves, limestone formations, and karst topography that make Maquoketa Caves State Park a nationally recognized natural destination.

Top Trails

Maquoketa Caves Trail System

Length: Approximately 6 miles of interconnected trails
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

The park's trail network connects most major caves, overlooks, and geological features. Visitors can customize their hikes while exploring forests, ravines, limestone formations, and cave entrances throughout the park.

Dancehall Cave Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular route provides access to Dancehall Cave, the park's largest cave. The trail passes through scenic woodlands and offers an excellent introduction to the park's unique cave environment.

Natural Bridge Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail leads to the park's natural bridge and passes several noteworthy limestone formations. It is a favorite route for photographers and visitors interested in geology.

Raccoon Creek Valley Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following the wooded valley and creek corridor, this trail provides opportunities to observe wildlife, native vegetation, and scenic limestone bluffs while experiencing some of the park's quieter areas.
