How to Get There

From Des Moines, IA (45 mi): Take I-35 N to IA-210 W, then head north on US-169 and west on 250th St; follow signs to the park entrance on P Ave near Boone.

Overview

The Ledges State Park is one of Iowa’s most scenic natural areas, known for its towering sandstone cliffs carved by Pea’s Creek over thousands of years. The park’s dramatic terrain includes a winding stream that flows through a forested canyon, creating shallow fords that visitors can wade through. Stone bridges, staircases, and picnic shelters built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s add historic charm to the rugged landscape. Popular hiking trails like the Canyon Road and Table Rock Trail offer panoramic views and access to the park’s most iconic formations.

Established in 1924, Ledges is a favorite destination for families, hikers, and photographers seeking a blend of natural beauty and outdoor recreation. The park features a modern campground, group campsites, and seasonal interpretive programs. In autumn, the foliage transforms the canyon into a vibrant tapestry of color, while spring brings wildflowers and rushing waters. Whether you're exploring the creek bed or relaxing at a scenic overlook, Ledges State Park offers a timeless escape into Iowa’s geological and ecological heritage.

Things to Do

Explore Iowa's Most Unique Canyon Park

Ledges State Park is one of Iowa's most distinctive and scenic state parks, located near Boone in central Iowa. The park is famous for its dramatic sandstone cliffs, deep canyons, shaded ravines, and the Des Moines River Valley. Established in 1924, Ledges offers visitors a rare landscape unlike most of Iowa and is a favorite destination for hiking, picnicking, wildlife viewing, and photography.

Discover the Sandstone Ledges

The park's signature feature is a series of sandstone cliffs and ravines carved by Pea's Creek over thousands of years. These towering rock formations, known as the "Ledges," rise as much as 100 feet above the canyon floor and create some of the most spectacular scenery in Iowa.

Drive Through the Canyon Crossings

One of the park's unique attractions is its historic roadway that crosses Pea's Creek multiple times through low-water stone bridges. During normal conditions, visitors can drive or walk through these scenic crossings while enjoying views of the surrounding canyon walls.

Enjoy Picnicking and Nature Observation

Ledges State Park features picnic shelters built by the Civilian Conservation Corps, open recreation areas, and numerous scenic overlooks. The park's rich woodlands provide habitat for white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, songbirds, owls, and a variety of native wildflowers.

Experience Fall Colors and River Valley Views

The park is especially popular during autumn when hardwood forests display vibrant colors. Scenic overlooks atop the bluffs provide sweeping views of the Des Moines River Valley and surrounding landscapes.

Top Trails

Canyon Road and Ledges Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This popular route follows the heart of the canyon system and provides excellent views of the sandstone ledges, creek crossings, and forested ravines. It is the best introduction to the park's unique geology.

Lost Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 1.7 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The trail travels through wooded terrain and leads to Lost Lake, offering opportunities for wildlife observation, birdwatching, and experiencing quieter areas of the park.

Table Rock Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail climbs to elevated viewpoints overlooking the canyon and Des Moines River Valley. The route offers some of the park's best panoramic views and excellent photography opportunities.

Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The Rim Trail follows the upper edges of the canyon system and connects several overlooks, forested ridges, and scenic vistas. It provides a broader perspective of the park's geological features and natural beauty.
