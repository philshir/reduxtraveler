How to Get There

From Cedar Rapids (74 mi): Take I-380 N toward Waterloo, merge onto US-20 E toward Dubuque, then continue into downtown Dubuque along the Mississippi River corridor.

Overview

Dubuque is the oldest city in Iowa, on the western bank of the Mississippi River in the northeastern corner of the state, where Iowa, Illinois, and Wisconsin meet. It is known for its dramatic setting among steep limestone bluffs, its well-preserved nineteenth-century architecture, and attractions such as the Fenelon Place Elevator, the shortest and steepest scenic railway in the world, and the National Mississippi River Museum & Aquarium. The city calls itself the "Masterpiece on the Mississippi."

Visitors come to Dubuque for the National Mississippi River Museum & Aquarium, the Fenelon Place Elevator, Eagle Point Park, and the Mississippi Riverwalk. They also come for the historic downtown and the Millwork District, the casinos, and the scenic drives through the bluffs, and it is close to Galena, Illinois, and the Field of Dreams movie site in nearby Dyersville.

History

The Meskwaki (Fox) people lived in this area when the French-Canadian trader Julien Dubuque arrived in 1788 and obtained permission to mine lead, and the city is named for him. American settlers began to arrive after the Black Hawk Purchase in 1833, and Dubuque was incorporated in 1841 and became a prosperous river port, lumber center, and manufacturing town. Irish and German Catholic immigrants built many of its churches and its early institutions, and its high bluffs and river location were used by industries such as sash and door manufacturing. In the late twentieth century the city reinvented itself with riverfront development, tourism, and technology jobs.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Fenelon Place Elevator is a funicular railway built in 1882 that climbs a 189-foot bluff and offers views of three states, and Eagle Point Park has a limestone tower, a pavilion, and views of the river and the lock and dam. The Dubuque County Courthouse, built in 1893, is a Beaux-Arts landmark, and the Shot Tower is a reminder of the lead industry. The Cathedral Square, the Cable Car Square, and the Mathias Ham House are also worth a visit, and the Mines of Spain State Recreation Area is the site of Julien Dubuque's grave.

Culture and Museums

The National Mississippi River Museum & Aquarium has exhibits on the river's wildlife and history and a large aquarium, and the Dubuque Museum of Art shows regional and national art. The Grand Opera House and the Five Flags Center host performances, and the Dubuque Symphony Orchestra is one of the oldest in the Midwest. The Dubuque Arboretum has seasonal garden events.

Parks

The Dubuque Arboretum and Botanical Gardens is a large botanical garden with themed gardens and an annual flower show, and the Mississippi Riverwalk has a trail with views of the river and the port. The Heritage Trail is a 26-mile rail trail that runs southwest of the city, and Mines of Spain has trails and a nature center. The nearby Mississippi Palisades and Maquoketa Caves State Park offer more outdoor options.

Seasons and Weather

Dubuque has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. Spring and fall are mild, and the bluffs are at their most colorful in mid October. Summer is the best time to visit the riverfront and the casinos, while winter is a quiet season with fewer crowds.
