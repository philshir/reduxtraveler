Ward Charcoal Ovens State Historic Park

★5.0

How to Get There

From Ely (18 mi): Take US-6 west from Ely, turn south onto County Road 45 near the Ward mining district, and follow the paved route to the park entrance in Willow Creek Canyon.

Overview

Ward Charcoal Ovens State Historic Park preserves six beehive-shaped stone charcoal ovens, each about 30 feet tall, that were built in the 1870s to supply fuel for the silver smelters of the nearby Ward mining district. The ovens converted pinyon and juniper wood into charcoal and are among the best-preserved examples of such structures in the West, and over the years they have also sheltered stagecoach travelers, outlaws and local ranchers. The park sits in a quiet pinyon-juniper canyon in the Egan Range, a scenic backdrop that adds to the appeal. Visitors come for the unusual architecture, the mining history and the peaceful camping and picnicking close to Ely and Great Basin National Park.

Access

The park is in eastern Nevada about 18 miles south of Ely, off US-93, in Willow Creek Canyon, and is reached by a county road that leaves the highway and climbs into the Egan Range. Most visitors arrive by car from Ely, and the route is generally passable for passenger vehicles in good weather, although conditions can change after storms. The park is about an hour's drive from Great Basin National Park and about 4 hours from Las Vegas, so it is best treated as a stop on a longer Loneliest Road trip.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, but conditions change with the season. Late spring through fall is the most popular time, when the weather is pleasant and the road and campsites are accessible. Summer days can be warm, but the elevation of about 7,000 feet keeps nights cool. Winter brings snow, cold temperatures and sometimes difficult road conditions, and camping may be limited, so check ahead if visiting in the cold season. Fall color from aspens and other trees in the canyon adds to the scenery.

Things to Do

See the Six Beehive Charcoal Ovens

The park's stone ovens stand in a row in a quiet canyon, each with a large arched door and a cone-like shape. Visitors can walk up to them, peer inside and imagine the work that went into making charcoal for the silver mines.

Learn About Ward and the Silver Mining Era

Interpretive signs explain the history of the Ward mining district, which boomed in the 1870s and faded in just a few years. The story shows how mining towns depended on wood and charcoal and how fast fortunes changed.

Walk the Interpretive Paths

Short paths lead around the ovens and through the surrounding pinyon-juniper woodland. The walks are easy and give a chance to see birds and other wildlife.

Picnic, Fish and Camp in the Canyon

The park has picnic areas and a campground, and Willow Creek offers a shaded and pleasant spot. It is a peaceful place to spend an evening away from traffic and city lights.

Combine With Ely and Great Basin National Park

The park fits well into a trip that includes the Nevada Northern Railway Museum in Ely and Great Basin National Park. It is a convenient and scenic stop for travelers following US-50 and US-93.

Top Trails

Charcoal Ovens Interpretive Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short walk visits the six ovens and the interpretive signs in the canyon. It is easy and suitable for families and takes less than an hour.

Willow Creek Canyon Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A relaxed path follows the canyon floor and creek through pinyon, juniper and cottonwood. It offers shade, birdwatching and scenic views of the canyon walls.

Visitor Centers

Ward Charcoal Ovens State Historic Park does not have a staffed visitor center. Interpretive signs at the ovens and in the day-use area explain the history of the structures and the mining district, and brochures and park information may be available at the entrance or through Nevada State Parks. In nearby Ely, the Ely Renaissance Village and local visitor information can help with maps, road conditions and area attractions.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground with tent and RV sites, fire rings, picnic tables and restrooms, and camping fees apply, but there is no lodging in the park. Ely, about 18 miles north, has the nearest hotels, including the historic Hotel Nevada and Gambling Hall, the Prospector Hotel and Casino, the Jailhouse Motel and Casino and several chain hotels. About an hour away, Baker has small motels and inns close to Great Basin National Park, and Eureka, about 1.5 hours north, has the historic Jackson House Hotel. Ely is also the best base for dining and supplies, so stock up before heading to the canyon. Check each property's website for current availability.
