Grimes Point / Hidden Cave Archaeological Site

★5.0

Archaeological area featuring ancient petroglyphs, prehistoric cultural sites, interpretive trails, and a significant Native American cave that preserve thousands of years of human history in the Great Basin.

How to Get There

From Fallon (7 mi): Take US-50 East for about seven miles and follow signs to the Grimes Point Archaeological Area parking area located directly off the highway.

Overview

Grimes Point and Hidden Cave Archaeological Site protects one of the largest concentrations of petroglyphs in the Great Basin, scattered over boulders on a rocky slope above the dry bed of ancient Lake Lahontan. The carvings were made over thousands of years by people who lived along the lake and its marshes, and the nearby Hidden Cave, which was used as a storage site, has produced important archaeological discoveries about Native life. The site is managed by the Bureau of Land Management and offers an easy self-guided trail among the boulders as well as guided tours of Hidden Cave. People come for the rock art, the archaeology, the open views over the Carson Sink and the chance to touch deep time in the desert.

Access

Grimes Point is on US-50 just east of Fallon in Churchill County, about 60 miles east of Carson City. Most visitors drive from Fallon and turn off the highway at the signed access road to the parking area. The route is paved to the site, and the drive from Fallon takes about 15 minutes. Hidden Cave is reached by a separate guided tour, and visitors should contact the BLM or the Churchill County Museum in Fallon in advance to arrange a visit.

Seasons

The site is open year-round, and the trail can be walked in any season. Spring and fall bring comfortable temperatures and clear views of the valley, and wildflowers can appear after wet winters. Summer is very hot, and the boulder-strewn slope has little shade, so go early in the morning. Winter days can be pleasant but windy and cold, and the trail can be icy at times. Hidden Cave tours are scheduled by arrangement and may be limited seasonally, so check ahead.

Things to Do

Explore a Great Basin Rock Art Landscape

Grimes Point Archaeological Area is covered with boulders carved with spirals, lines, dots and animal figures. The petroglyphs are among the oldest and most numerous in Nevada and give a glimpse into ancient lakeshore life.

Walk the Self-Guided Trail

An easy loop trail with interpretive signs leads visitors through the densest groups of petroglyphs. The walk is short and informative, and it offers views over the valley and old lake basin.

Take a Guided Tour of Hidden Cave

Hidden Cave was a storage place for Native peoples and has yielded tools, baskets and other artifacts that help tell the story of human use of the area. Tours are guided because of the cave's fragile resources.

Learn About Lake Lahontan

Interpretive displays explain how a vast lake once filled this basin and how people adapted to its marshes and shores. The ancient shoreline is visible in the landscape.

Visit the Churchill County Museum

In Fallon, the Churchill County Museum has exhibits on local Native history, pioneer days and the Hidden Cave excavation. It is a natural companion stop to the archaeological site.

Top Trails

Grimes Point Archaeological Trail

Length: Approximately 0.8 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This interpretive loop winds among boulders with rock art panels and lookout points over the valley. The path has rocky sections, so wear sturdy shoes, and stay on the trail to protect the petroglyphs.

Hidden Cave Guided Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The walk to Hidden Cave is part of the guided tour and goes from the parking area to the cave entrance. Tours are led by BLM staff or volunteers and explain the cave's archaeology and history.

Visitor Centers

There is no staffed visitor center at the site, but interpretive signs and a small shelter near the parking area give information about the petroglyphs and Hidden Cave. The Churchill County Museum in Fallon is the best place to learn more, with exhibits and information about tours, and the BLM Carson City District provides official site information. Because Hidden Cave is accessible only by guided tour, contact the BLM or museum before you go.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site itself. The nearest hotels are in Fallon, about 10 to 15 minutes west, where chain properties such as the Best Western Plus Fallon Inn and Suites and Holiday Inn Express offer rooms along with restaurants and local shops. Fernley and Dayton, about an hour west, and Carson City, about an hour away, provide more options, and Reno is about 1.5 hours away. Dispersed camping is available on nearby public lands, and Sand Mountain Recreation Area to the east has primitive camping. Check each property's website for current availability.
