Valley of Fire State Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Nevada
  3. Valley of Fire State Park

Fiery red sandstone formations, ancient petroglyphs, and scenic desert trails offer a dramatic backdrop for hiking, photography, and camping.

General Information

Hours:
Open daily from sunrise to sunset
Visitor Center: 8:30am to 4:30pm (closed Christmas)
Fees:
Day Use (NV Vehicles): $10 per vehicle
Day Use (Non-NV Vehicles): $15 per vehicle
Camping: $20 per night + $10 for utility hookups
Annual Pass: $75
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed (max 6 feet); not permitted in the visitor center
Seasons:
Best visited October through April
Location:
29450 Valley of Fire Hwy, Overton, NV 89040
Website:
parks.nv.gov

How to Get There

From Las Vegas (58 mi): Take I-15 N to Exit 75, then head east on Valley of Fire Highway for 17 miles to the west entrance.

Overview

Valley of Fire State Park is Nevada's oldest and largest state park, covering about 46,000 acres of brilliant red Aztec sandstone formations that appear to glow as if on fire in the afternoon sun. Established in 1935, the park protects dramatic rock formations that were formed from shifting sand dunes about 150 million years ago, along with petroglyphs carved by Ancestral Puebloan people thousands of years ago. It is famous for features such as the Fire Wave, Elephant Rock, the Seven Sisters and Atlatl Rock. People come for hiking, photography, scenic drives, rock art and camping in one of the most colorful desert landscapes in the American Southwest.

Access

Valley of Fire is about 50 to 60 miles northeast of Las Vegas and just south of Overton, near the northern arm of Lake Mead National Recreation Area. Most visitors take I-15 north to Exit 75 and then drive about 17 miles east on Valley of Fire Highway to the west entrance, while others arrive from Overton and the east entrance. The road through the park is paved and well maintained, and the main sites are easy to reach by car. There is no public transportation, and cell service is limited, so plan ahead and carry water.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and the best time to visit is from October through April, when temperatures are comfortable for hiking. Summers are extremely hot, often over 110°F, and the park's trails have little shade, so heat is a real danger. Spring can bring desert wildflowers, and winter nights are cold even though days are mild. Thunderstorms can bring flash floods, which occasionally close parts of the park roads, and sunrise and sunset provide the richest colors on the rocks.

Things to Do

Explore Nevada's Most Colorful Desert Park

Valley of Fire State Park is a remarkable landscape of red, orange, pink and white sandstone shaped by wind and water. Scenic roads and short trails make it easy to see many highlights in a single day.

Hike to the Fire Wave

The Fire Wave is one of the park's signature features, a swirling sandstone formation of red, white and pink stripes. A short hike leads there, and the formation is especially beautiful at sunrise and sunset.

See Ancient Petroglyphs at Atlatl Rock and Petroglyph Canyon

Atlatl Rock has a staircase to a panel of well-preserved petroglyphs, while Mouse's Tank Petroglyph Canyon offers rock art along a short walk. These carvings provide a glimpse into the lives of ancient peoples who lived in the region.

Drive the Scenic Roads

Valley of Fire Highway and White Domes Road wind through colorful formations with many pullouts. The Nevada Scenic Byway section of the main road connects the east and west entrances and offers wide views.

Visit Elephant Rock and Rainbow Vista

Elephant Rock is a natural formation resembling an elephant near the east entrance, and Rainbow Vista offers sweeping views over multicolored sandstone. Both are easy stops that are popular for photography.

Top Trails

Fire Wave Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail leads across slickrock and sand to the swirling red-and-white Fire Wave formation. It is a relatively short hike with little shade, so go early or late in the day.

White Domes Trail

Length: Approximately 1.1 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This loop passes through sandstone formations and a short slot canyon, and visits the remains of an old movie set. It is a diverse and scenic hike that suits most families.

Mouse's Tank Petroglyph Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk through a sandy canyon leads to a natural water tank and several panels of ancient petroglyphs. The trail is popular, and visitors should avoid touching the rock art.

Atlatl Rock Trail

Length: Less than 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A staircase climbs to a large rock face with a well-known panel of petroglyphs, including a depiction of an atlatl, an ancient spear-throwing tool.

Visitor Centers

The Valley of Fire Visitor Center is located along the main park road and is the best place to begin. It has exhibits on the park's geology, ecology and human history, a bookstore, restrooms, water and rangers who can provide maps and trail conditions. Displays introduce the desert wildlife, such as bighorn sheep and desert tortoises, and explain the rock art. The center is open daily during standard hours, and it closes on Christmas Day.

Camping and Lodging

Valley of Fire has two developed campgrounds, Atlatl Rock and Arch Rock, with tent and RV sites, picnic tables, fire rings and restrooms, plus several historic stone cabins used as shelters. There is no lodging inside the park. The closest hotels are in Overton and the Moapa Valley, about 20 minutes away, and in Mesquite, about 45 minutes away, where casino resorts such as CasaBlanca Resort and Eureka Casino Resort offer rooms and dining. North Las Vegas and the Las Vegas Strip are about an hour away with the widest range of lodging. Check each property's website for current availability.
