Overview

Carson City is the capital of Nevada, a compact city of about 58,000 people set at roughly 4,800 feet in the Eagle Valley at the foot of the Sierra Nevada, about 30 miles south of Reno and 15 miles from Lake Tahoe. It is known for its Victorian historic district, its museums, and its role at the center of Nevada’s silver-mining history. Unlike most Nevada cities, it is quiet, walkable, and more closely tied to government and history than to gambling.

Visitors come to tour the State Capitol and the old Mint, to walk the Kit Carson Trail through the historic district, and to use Carson City as a base for Lake Tahoe, Virginia City, and the Carson Valley. The city is also an easy day trip from Reno, and its location between the Sierra Nevada and the Great Basin offers hiking, skiing, and lake recreation within a short drive.

The site was a campsite for the Washoe people and a stop on the California Trail before Abraham Curry bought the land in 1858 and laid out a town around a plaza, naming it for the scout Kit Carson. The Comstock Lode silver strike of 1859 nearby made it a supply center, and it became the capital of Nevada Territory in 1861 and of the state when Nevada was admitted to the Union in 1864. The U.S. Carson City Mint, which opened in 1870, struck the famous "CC" coins until 1893, and the Virginia & Truckee Railroad shops employed many residents.

The Nevada State Capitol, a sandstone building with a silver-colored dome completed in 1871, anchors the historic downtown, and the Nevada Governor’s Mansion, built in 1909, stands a few blocks away. The Kit Carson Trail, a self-guided walking route marked by a blue line on the sidewalk, passes more than 50 historic buildings, including Victorian homes and churches. Nearby, Mormon Station State Historic Park in Genoa and the historic mining town of Virginia City are short drives away.

The Nevada State Museum is housed in the old Carson City Mint building and presents exhibits on the state’s natural history, Native peoples, and mining past, including a walk-through replica of a mine and coin presses that still strike commemorative medals. The Nevada State Railroad Museum preserves locomotives from the Virginia & Truckee line, and the Brewery Arts Center hosts concerts, theater, and exhibitions. The annual Nevada Day parade each October celebrates statehood.

Carson City has a surprising amount of open space. The Carson River, Mexican Dam, and Silver Saddle Ranch have riverside trails, Ash Canyon and Prison Hill Recreation Area offer hiking and mountain biking above town, and Fuji Park hosts events and gardens. Washoe Lake State Park lies just north, and the Lake Tahoe Nevada State Park, with Spooner Lake and the East Shore beaches, is a short drive west.

Carson City has a high desert climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s and 90s °F and cool nights, and winters are cold, with highs in the 40s °F and occasional snow, since the Sierra Nevada blocks much of the moisture. Spring is breezy and mild, and autumn is crisp and clear, with fall colors in the Carson Range.

Things to Do

Explore Nevada's Historic Capital City

Carson City, the capital of Nevada, blends rich Old West history with scenic mountain landscapes and modern attractions. Nestled between the Sierra Nevada and the Carson Range, the city offers visitors a mix of museums, historic landmarks, outdoor recreation, and easy access to nearby Lake Tahoe.

Visit the Nevada State Museum

Located in the historic Carson City Mint building, the Nevada State Museum is one of the city's premier attractions. Visitors can explore exhibits covering Nevada's mining heritage, Native American history, geology, and the famous Carson City Mint, which once produced silver and gold coins from the Comstock Lode.

Tour the Nevada State Capitol

Built in 1871, the Nevada State Capitol is one of the state's most important historic landmarks. Visitors can tour the grounds, learn about Nevada's government, and admire the preserved architecture and surrounding gardens.

Discover Historic Downtown Carson City

Downtown Carson City features historic buildings, local shops, restaurants, breweries, art galleries, and museums. Walking tours allow visitors to explore the city's role in Nevada's development during the mining boom of the nineteenth century.

Ride the Virginia & Truckee Railroad

The historic Virginia & Truckee Railroad offers scenic excursions through the Carson River Canyon, connecting visitors with the region's railroad and mining heritage. The route provides beautiful views and educational experiences that highlight Nevada's transportation history.

Explore Nearby Lake Tahoe and Outdoor Recreation

Carson City serves as a convenient gateway to Lake Tahoe, the Sierra Nevada, and numerous outdoor recreation areas. Visitors can enjoy hiking, mountain biking, fishing, kayaking, boating, camping, wildlife viewing, and scenic drives throughout the region.

Top Trails

Kings Canyon Waterfall Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 800 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

One of Carson City's most popular hikes, this trail leads to a seasonal waterfall surrounded by pine forests and granite cliffs. The route provides beautiful views of Eagle Valley and the surrounding mountains.

Ash Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This scenic trail climbs into the Carson Range and offers panoramic views of Carson City, Washoe Valley, and the Sierra Nevada. Wildlife sightings and spring wildflowers are common along the route.

Clear Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following a historic corridor through the mountains, this trail offers peaceful forest scenery, seasonal streams, and access to higher-elevation terrain near the Carson Range.

Prison Hill Recreation Area Trails

Length: Various routes from 1 to 10+ miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This extensive trail network is popular with hikers, trail runners, and mountain bikers. Visitors enjoy excellent views of Carson City, surrounding valleys, and nearby mountain ranges while exploring a variety of loop and out-and-back routes.

Featured Locations

Kit Carson Trail
★ 5.0

A self-guided historic walking route featuring nearly 50 landmarks, Victorian-era homes, museums, churches, and public buildings that showcase the development of Nevada’s capital during the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Nevada Governor's Mansion
★ 5.0

Neoclassical residence built in 1909 featuring Ionic columns, wraparound porches, and historic furnishings used by Nevada’s First Families.

Nevada State Capitol Building
★ 5.0

Government building featuring a silver-domed rotunda, Alaskan marble interiors, and a museum chronicling the state's political and cultural evolution.

Nevada State Museum
★ 5.0

Natural and cultural history museum featuring exhibits on prehistoric life, mining, Native American heritage, and the historic Carson City Mint.
