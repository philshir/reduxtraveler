Bellagio Hotel & Casino, Las Vegas, Nevada

Inspired by the Lake Como town of Bellagio in Italy and houses the Fountains of Bellagio, a large dancing water fountain synchronized to music

General Information
Average Room Rates with Resort Fee:
Roughly $200–$300 per night on weeknights, including the resort fee of about $62.36 per night (with tax); rates vary widely by season, room type and events.
Average Room Rates Weekends and Holidays with Resort Fee:
Roughly $320–$500 per night on Friday–Saturday nights, holidays and major events, including the resort fee of about $62.36 per night (with tax).
Parking Rates:
Self-parking is $20 per day and valet is $40 per day; free self-parking for MGM Rewards Pearl tier and above, and Nevada residents get 3 free hours with ID.
Pet Policy:
Dogs only, up to two per room (about 100 pounds combined), in designated pet-friendly rooms; reported fee of about $150 per night. Pets are not allowed on the casino floor, in restaurants or at the pools. Service animals are welcome.
Smoking Policy:
Smoking is allowed on most of the casino floor, with a designated non-smoking section. Nevada law exempts casinos from its indoor smoking ban. Guest rooms are predominantly non-smoking; confirm when booking.
Budget:
Expensive
Location:
3600 S Las Vegas Blvd, Las Vegas, NV 89109
Website:
bellagio.mgmresorts.com

Overview

Bellagio is one of the most elegant and best-known resorts in Las Vegas. Opened in 1998 on the site of the old Dunes and inspired by the Italian lakeside town of Bellagio on Lake Como, it draws visitors who come to see the Fountains of Bellagio, the Conservatory and Botanical Garden, the Gallery of Fine Art, and the casino and restaurants.

Hotel Design and Rooms

The resort's design is refined and upscale rather than flashy, with Italian-style architecture, a lake out front, and a lobby ceiling covered with the Chihuly glass-flower sculpture. Its roughly 3,900 rooms and suites are luxurious, with elegant decor and many views of the fountains or the pools.

Special Attractions

The Fountains of Bellagio put on free water shows on the lake every 30 minutes in the afternoon and every 15 minutes at night. The Conservatory and Botanical Gardens, free to visit, change displays with the seasons, and the Bellagio Gallery of Fine Art hosts rotating exhibitions.

Dining Options

Bellagio has some of the best-known fine dining in Las Vegas, including Picasso, which earned two Michelin stars during the years the guide covered Las Vegas, along with steakhouses, Italian and French restaurants, and a range of cafes and casual spots. Restaurants change over time, so check current listings.

Las Vegas Shows

The resort is home to "O" by Cirque du Soleil, an acrobatic and aquatic show that has been a Strip favorite since it opened with the resort in 1998. It is performed in a theater built around a pool holding about 1.5 million gallons of water, with a stage that can change from solid floor to deep water in seconds.

Pool Options

Bellagio has several pools set in Mediterranean-style courtyards, with cabanas and gardens. Some pools are seasonal, so check which are open in the cooler months.

Nightlife Options

The resort has lounges and bars, including bars overlooking the lake and casino. The Mayfair Supper Club pairs dinner with live entertainment and views of the fountains, the Petrossian Bar in the lobby serves cocktails and caviar, and the Baccarat Bar is a quieter spot near the casino. Bellagio is more of a place for a stylish cocktail than a large club scene.

Shopping Options

Via Bellagio is a luxury shopping promenade with designer brands such as Chanel, Tiffany, and Hermès, along with the resort's gift and specialty shops.

Special Amenities

Spa Bellagio offers a full range of treatments, with a salon and fitness center, and the resort also has wedding chapels and luxury suites and villas.

Nearby Attractions

Bellagio sits at the center of the Strip, within walking distance of Caesars Palace, the LINQ and High Roller, the Cosmopolitan, ARIA, and the Eiffel Tower Viewing Deck at Paris Las Vegas.

Featured Attractions

Bellagio Conservatory and Botanical Gardens
★ 5.0

Spanning approximately 14,000 square feet just off the Bellagio’s main lobby, the Bellagio Conservatory & Botanical Gardens is a horticultural masterpiece showcasing elaborate, themed installations that change five times annually; these displays feature thousands of flowers, sculptural plant artistry, fountains, interactive elements, immersive soundscapes, and captivating scents to evoke the character of each season.

Bellagio Gallery of Fine Art
★ 5.0

From Los Angeles, take I‑15 North into Las Vegas, exit at Flamingo Road, then follow signs to Bellagio on Las Vegas Boulevard.

Fountains of Bellagio
★ 5.0

Conceived by WET Design and debuted in 1998 alongside the opening of the resort, the Fountains of Bellagio are an engineering marvel featuring over 1,200 nozzles and nearly 4,800 lights choreographed to music; water jets can soar up to 460 feet above the 8.5-acre man-made lake, creating a sweeping spectacle that captivates audiences daily and has become an enduring symbol of Las Vegas flair.

O by Cirque du Soleil
★ 5.0

A mesmerizing Cirque du Soleil production featuring aquatic acrobatics, synchronized swimming, and aerial stunts performed on and above a massive water stage.
