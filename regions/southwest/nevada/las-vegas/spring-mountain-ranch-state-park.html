Spring Mountain Ranch State Park

★5.0

Historic desert ranch nestled in Red Rock Canyon offering scenic hiking trails, shaded picnic areas, and preserved 19th-century buildings with seasonal outdoor theater.

How to Get There

From Las Vegas, take Charleston Blvd west into Red Rock Canyon, continue on NV-159, and the park entrance will be on your left just past Blue Diamond.

Overview

Spring Mountain Ranch State Park is a historic ranch set against the red cliffs of the Wilson Range, just beyond Red Rock Canyon west of Las Vegas. The ranch grew around natural springs that drew Native people, early ranchers and a series of owners, including the German heiress Vera Krupp and, later, the reclusive billionaire Howard Hughes, before it became a state park in the 1970s. Today the park pairs an easy-going ranch atmosphere with the dramatic scenery of the Spring Mountains, and visitors come for the historic buildings, green lawns, hiking trails and summer outdoor theater. It is a relaxing alternative to the busier scenic loop at Red Rock Canyon.

Access

The park is on NV-159 (the Red Rock Scenic Drive extension) about 15 miles west of Las Vegas, just past the Red Rock Canyon Visitor Center and near the community of Blue Diamond. Most visitors drive west on Charleston Boulevard and continue along NV-159, a scenic and paved road with plenty of pullouts. The road is open year-round, and the park entrance is clearly signed on the left. Public transportation is not available, so a vehicle is the practical choice.

Seasons

The park is open year-round with hours that change seasonally, with longer days in summer and shorter hours in winter. Spring and fall are the best times for hiking, ranch-house tours and picnicking, with mild temperatures and clear air. Summer days can be hot, but the park's shade trees and higher elevation help, and the outdoor Super Summer Theatre draws crowds on warm evenings. Winter is cool and can bring a dusting of snow on the surrounding peaks, and wet weather can make trails muddy.

Things to Do

Step Into a Historic Desert Ranch

Spring Mountain Ranch State Park preserves a working-ranch landscape with a historic ranch house, outbuildings, cottonwood trees and grassy meadows beneath towering red cliffs. The setting offers a lovely contrast to the open desert nearby.

Tour the Ranch House and Grounds

Visitors can see the main house and other historic structures and learn about owners such as Vera Krupp and Howard Hughes. Interpretive displays and periodic ranger programs describe the ranch's long history.

Hike the Park Trails

Short trails leave from the ranch area and lead to viewpoints and small ponds, with sweeping views of the Wilson Cliffs. The trails are gentle and rewarding for families and casual hikers.

Attend Summer Theater and Special Events

The park hosts outdoor theater in summer and a range of seasonal events and living history programs. Check the calendar for current performances and activities.

Picnic and Relax in the Shade

Large lawns and picnic areas provide an inviting place to relax after a day at Red Rock Canyon. The setting is especially pleasant in the evening, when the cliffs glow.

Top Trails

Lake Harriet Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short, gentle walk leads from the ranch area to a small pond framed by trees and the red cliffs beyond. It is a good option for families and for birdwatching in the cooler parts of the day.

Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A modest climb above the ranch leads to an overlook with views of the ranch, the valley below and the Wilson Cliffs. The trail is well suited to sunrise or sunset photography.

Visitor Centers

The park's visitor center, located near the ranch house, is the best first stop. It offers exhibits on the history of the ranch and its owners, local natural history and a small selection of books and gifts. Rangers can provide trail maps, event information and suggestions for combining the park with Red Rock Canyon. Because hours change with the season, check the Nevada State Parks website for the current schedule.

Camping and Lodging

There is no overnight camping in the park, so most visitors stay in the Las Vegas area. Summerlin, about 20 to 30 minutes away, has a wide range of hotels including Red Rock Casino Resort and Spa, and the Las Vegas Strip is about 30 minutes away with its large casino resorts. Closer to the park, the small community of Blue Diamond has limited services, and the Red Rock Canyon Campground, a few minutes east, offers tent and RV sites. About 45 minutes away, Pahrump has hotels and casinos such as the Pahrump Nugget, and Mount Charleston offers lodges and cabins in the cool mountains. Check each property's website for current availability.
