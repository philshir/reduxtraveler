How to Get There

From the Las Vegas Strip, head north on Las Vegas Blvd, turn right on Washington Avenue, and the park will be on your right at the corner of E Washington Ave and Las Vegas Blvd.

Overview

Old Las Vegas Mormon Fort State Historic Park preserves the remains of the first non-Native settlement in the Las Vegas Valley, built in 1855 by Mormon missionaries who established a way station along the Salt Lake to Los Angeles route. The fort, made of adobe bricks, sat beside the springs and creek that gave Las Vegas its name, which means the meadows. Though the mission lasted only a few years, the fort later served as a ranch, a mining headquarters and a railroad-era landmark, and a portion of the original adobe is among the oldest standing structures in Southern Nevada. Visitors come to learn the surprising pre-casino history of Las Vegas, to see the preserved walls and to take a quiet break from the Strip.

Access

The park is in downtown Las Vegas at Las Vegas Boulevard North and Washington Avenue, about 10 minutes from the Strip and about 5 minutes from Fremont Street. Most visitors arrive by car or rideshare, and parking is available at the park. It is also reachable by city bus routes that run along Las Vegas Boulevard. The park is small and has level paths, so it is a convenient stop to combine with other downtown attractions.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and unlike the desert around it, the experience is indoors and out. Summers in Las Vegas are extremely hot, so plan to visit in the morning and take advantage of the air-conditioned museum areas. Spring and fall are the most comfortable times for walking the grounds, and winter is mild, with cool mornings and pleasant afternoons. The park is closed on Sundays and Mondays, and it is typically closed on state holidays.

Things to Do

Discover the Origins of Las Vegas

Old Las Vegas Mormon Fort State Historic Park gives a rare look at the valley before the casinos, when it was a green oasis along a desert trail. Exhibits tell how the fort came to be and how the area grew into a modern city.

See the Preserved Adobe Walls

A section of the fort's original adobe wall remains, with reconstructed and interpreted sections showing how the stockade was laid out. Seeing the building materials up close helps visitors understand life on the frontier.

Tour the Museum and Exhibits

Inside, displays include artifacts, historic photographs and stories about the Mormon missionaries, Paiute people, ranchers and later settlers. Guided or self-guided tours explain the fort's changing roles.

Learn About Las Vegas Springs and Creek

The fort was built beside the water that made Las Vegas a stopping point for travelers. Interpretive information describes how springs, creeks and wetlands shaped the early community.

Pair With Downtown Attractions

The park is a short trip from the Mob Museum, the Neon Museum and Fremont Street, so it fits easily into a day of exploring downtown history and culture.

Top Trails

Mormon Fort Grounds Walk

Length: Less than 0.25 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short loop through the grounds passes the preserved adobe walls, interpretive signs and landscaped areas. It is flat and accessible and takes only a short time.

Downtown Heritage Walk to Fremont Street

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

An urban walk from the fort toward Fremont Street passes historic downtown buildings, murals and museums, and it connects old and new Las Vegas. It is best in the morning or evening, when temperatures are cooler.

Visitor Centers

The park's small visitor center and museum building is the best place to begin and is staffed during open hours. Exhibits cover the fort's construction and use, local Native history and the early settlement of the valley, and rangers or volunteers can offer tours and answer questions. A small shop offers books and souvenirs. Hours are limited, since the park is open Tuesday through Saturday, so check the Nevada State Parks website before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the park, but downtown Las Vegas is full of options. Within a short walk or ride are classic downtown hotels such as the Golden Nugget, Plaza Hotel and Casino, El Cortez, Circa Resort and Casino and The D, and just a few minutes south on Las Vegas Boulevard are the major Las Vegas Strip resorts such as Bellagio, Caesars Palace and Venetian. Budget and chain hotels are available throughout the city, and Henderson and Summerlin, about 20 to 30 minutes away, have additional options. Nearby outdoor areas such as Red Rock Canyon and Lake Mead have campgrounds for those who want to combine city and nature. Check each property's website for current availability.
