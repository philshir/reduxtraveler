Luxury hotel and casino is one of Las Vegas's largest and best known landmarks, giving guests a sense of life during the Roman Empire

General Information
Average Room Rates with Resort Fee:
Roughly $175–$300 per night on weeknights, including the resort fee of about $62.30 per night (with tax); rates vary widely by tower, season and events.
Average Room Rates Weekends and Holidays with Resort Fee:
Roughly $300–$500+ per night on Friday–Saturday nights, holidays and major events, including the resort fee of about $62.30 per night (with tax).
Parking Rates:
Self-parking is generally about $20–$25 per day and valet about $40–$50 per day, with fees varying by day and length of stay; parking is free for Caesars Rewards Platinum tier and higher. Confirm current rates with the resort.
Pet Policy:
Dogs only (no cats or other pets) in designated Pet Stay rooms, which must be arranged with the hotel in advance; up to two dogs of 50 pounds or less each, with a nightly pet fee (reported at $100 per night) and a refundable deposit. Pets are not allowed in the casino, restaurants, pools or spa. Service animals are welcome.
Smoking Policy:
Smoking is allowed on the casino floor, as Nevada law exempts casinos from its indoor smoking ban. Most hotel rooms are non-smoking, and smoking rooms are limited and being phased out at Caesars resorts; check when booking.
Budget:
Expensive
Location:
3570 S Las Vegas Blvd, Las Vegas, NV 89109

Overview

Caesars Palace is one of the most famous hotel-casinos in the world. It opened in 1966 and has stood in the middle of the Las Vegas Strip ever since, next to Bellagio and the LINQ. People come for the Roman Empire fantasy that set it apart from its neighbors, as well as for the large casino, headline concerts at the Colosseum, the Forum Shops, and a wide choice of restaurants, pools, and nightlife. Even visitors who do not stay here often stop to walk through the casino and the Forum Shops and to see the fountains out front.

Hotel Design and Rooms

The resort is themed around ancient Rome, with marble-style statues, columns, fountains, and staff once dressed in togas. Its several hotel towers, including the Roman, Augustus, Octavius, Julius, and Palace Towers and the Nobu Hotel, vary in style, and a rooms count of well over 3,000 makes it one of the larger Strip resorts. Rooms in the newer towers are modern and upscale, with contemporary decor and luxury suites, while older towers have been renovated over the years. Room quality and price vary by tower, so it is worth checking which one you are booking.

Special Attractions

The Roman fountains and statues along the Strip frontage, including the Fountain of the Gods and a replica of the Trevi Fountain, are free to see and make popular photo stops. Inside, the Forum Shops are known for their painted ceiling that changes from dawn to dusk, marble-style fountains, and statues, and the Garden of the Gods pool complex adds Roman-style columns and gardens.

Dining Options

Caesars Palace has one of the widest dining selections on the Strip, from celebrity-chef and fine dining restaurants to casual and affordable options. Well-known names have included Restaurant Guy Savoy, Nobu, Mr Chow, and Gordon Ramsay Hell's Kitchen, and the Bacchanal Buffet is among the best-known buffets in Las Vegas. The Forum Shops and the casino floor add many cafes, food courts, pizza and sandwich counters, and bars. Restaurants change often, so check current listings before you visit.

Las Vegas Shows

The Colosseum is the resort's main showroom and was built for long residencies by major recording artists. Celine Dion, Elton John, Rod Stewart, Shania Twain, Mariah Carey, and Adele have all headlined here. Absinthe, a raunchy, high-energy circus and variety show with acrobats, comedy, and adult humor, plays in an intimate tent-style theater outside the resort, with seating very close to the stage. Caesars Palace also has other venues for comedy, concerts, and boxing events, and its schedule varies, so check what is playing during your dates.

Pool Options

The Garden of the Gods Pool Oasis is a large pool complex with several themed pools surrounded by Roman-style columns, statues, and gardens, and it includes the adults-oriented Venus pool club and cabanas. Some pools are seasonal, while others, depending on the year, stay open in cooler months, so confirm which are operating if you visit in winter. Omnia Dayclub offers a party-style day club experience for guests who want DJs and bottle service.

Nightlife Options

Omnia is the resort's large nightclub, with top DJ performances and impressive lighting and sound. Cleopatra's Barge, a floating lounge on the casino floor, is a classic Caesars hangout, and there are bars and lounges throughout the casino and the Forum Shops for a quieter drink.

Shopping Options

The Forum Shops at Caesars is the resort's own shopping complex, a multilevel mall with luxury and designer brands, fashion and jewelry stores, specialty shops, and restaurants, all set under an artificial sky with fountains and statues. It is one of the most visited malls in the United States and is worth a visit even for window shopping.

Special Amenities

Guests can use Qua Baths & Spa, a large spa known for its Roman baths, treatment rooms, and relaxation areas, along with a salon and a fitness center. The resort also offers concierge services, private gaming salons for high rollers, and luxury suites and villas for those who want a more exclusive stay.

Nearby Attractions

Caesars Palace is within walking distance of the Fountains of Bellagio, the Bellagio Conservatory, the LINQ Promenade and High Roller observation wheel, and the Venetian and Palazzo. The Fabulous Las Vegas sign is at the south end of the Strip, and Sphere is a short ride north, both easily reached by taxi, rideshare, or bus.

The Forum Shop at Caesars
★ 5.0

The Forum Shops at Caesars Palace is one of Las Vegas’s premier retail destinations, offering over 160 specialty stores and luxury brands such as Gucci, Louis Vuitton, and Versace, all set within a lavish Roman-inspired environment complete with marble columns, fountains, and painted sky ceilings.
