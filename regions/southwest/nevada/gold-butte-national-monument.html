Gold Butte National Monument

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Nevada

Gold Butte National Monument, Nevada
RuggyBearLA, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Expansive desert wilderness featuring red sandstone formations, ancient petroglyphs, and rugged backcountry trails ideal for off-road exploration and solitude.

General Information

Hours:
Open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year
Fees:
No admission fees or permits required
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed
Seasons:
Best visited from late October through early spring (March–April)
Location:
Gold Butte National Monument, NV
Website:
blm.gov/visit/gold-butte-national-monument

How to Get There

From Las Vegas (90 mi): Take I-15 N to Exit 112 toward Riverside/Bunkerville, cross the Virgin River bridge, then turn right onto Gold Butte Road and follow it south into the monument.

Overview

Gold Butte National Monument is a remote expanse of Mojave Desert in southeastern Nevada, protecting nearly 300,000 acres of red sandstone formations, canyons, mountains and ancient cultural sites. It was designated a national monument in December 2016 to preserve its rock art, desert tortoise habitat, geology and historic mining and ranching remnants. Gold Butte is known for the sculpted sandstone fins of Little Finland, the colorful rock of Whitney Pocket, petroglyph-covered canyons and its wide-open solitude. Visitors come for backcountry adventure, photography, rock art, stargazing and a rare chance to explore a rugged desert landscape without crowds.

Access

Gold Butte lies about 90 miles northeast of Las Vegas and south of the town of Mesquite, on the edge of Lake Mead National Recreation Area. Most visitors reach it by driving I-15 to the Riverside and Bunkerville exit and then following Gold Butte Road south into the monument. The first stretch is paved, but many of the best sites require travel on unpaved roads, and routes such as the road to Little Finland are best handled by a high-clearance vehicle. There are no services, cell coverage is limited, and visitors should carry extra water, fuel, a spare tire and a map.

Seasons

Gold Butte is open year-round, but the desert climate strongly shapes when to visit. Late October through early spring offers the most comfortable temperatures for hiking and exploring, and spring can bring wildflowers after wet winters. Summers are extremely hot and unsafe for most hiking, and monsoon storms in late summer can cause flash floods and wash out dirt roads. Winter nights are cold and the roads can be muddy after storms, so check weather and road conditions before heading out.

Things to Do

Explore One of Nevada's Most Remote Desert Landscapes

Gold Butte National Monument is a vast backcountry of red and tan sandstone, narrow canyons and dramatic mountain ranges. Visitors can drive scenic backroads, stop at viewpoints and hike to formations that few people see.

Discover Ancient Petroglyphs and Cultural Sites

The monument contains thousands of years of human history, including petroglyphs, rock shelters and traditional Southern Paiute cultural sites. Sites such as Falling Man and Mud Wash are best visited with respect, staying on trails and never touching the rock art.

Visit Whitney Pocket

Whitney Pocket is a scenic area of colorful, wind-sculpted sandstone with a primitive campground and short trails. It is one of the easiest and most popular stops in the monument.

Drive the Gold Butte Backcountry Byway

This scenic backway travels through the heart of the monument, passing mountains, desert washes and viewpoints. Along the way are access points to hiking areas, historic mining remnants and the old Gold Butte townsite.

Camp Under Dark Desert Skies

With no city lights nearby, the night sky at Gold Butte is brilliant. Dispersed and primitive camping let visitors enjoy a quiet evening of stargazing far from the crowds.

Top Trails

Little Finland Loop

Length: Approximately 5.4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

Little Finland is one of Gold Butte's most iconic destinations. The trail leads visitors through an incredible landscape of intricately sculpted sandstone formations that resemble delicate fins, waves and natural sculptures shaped by thousands of years of erosion.

Whitney Pocket Trail

Length: Approximately 1.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail explores the colorful sandstone outcrops and desert scenery of Whitney Pocket. The route provides excellent opportunities for photography, wildlife viewing and discovering the area's unique geological features.

Whitney Pocket Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 3.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Visitors hiking to the overlook are rewarded with panoramic views of the monument's rugged terrain, colorful sandstone formations and expansive Mojave Desert landscapes.

Billy Goat Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 2.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 900 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This short but rewarding hike climbs to elevated viewpoints overlooking portions of Gold Butte National Monument. The trail offers expansive desert vistas and excellent opportunities for photography.

Visitor Centers

Gold Butte National Monument does not have a staffed visitor center within its boundaries. Maps, road conditions, safety information and cultural resource guidelines are available from the Bureau of Land Management through its Gold Butte website and its Southern Nevada offices in Las Vegas. Visitor information for the surrounding area is also available in nearby Mesquite and Overton. Download maps and directions in advance, since cell service is spotty.

Camping and Lodging

There is no lodging in the monument, but primitive and dispersed camping is available, with a small primitive campground at Whitney Pocket and other sites along the backcountry roads. There are no hookups, water, trash service or restrooms at most sites, so campers must be fully self-sufficient and pack out everything. The nearest hotels are in Mesquite, about an hour away, where casino resorts such as CasaBlanca Resort, Virgin River Hotel and Casino and Eureka Casino Resort offer rooms, dining and golf. Overton and Logandale also have limited lodging near Valley of Fire State Park, and Las Vegas is about 1.5 to 2 hours away with a full range of options. Check each property's website for current availability.
