How to Get There

From Escalante (28 mi): Drive northeast on UT-12 for 28 miles to Boulder; the museum is on the right side of the highway in the center of town.

Overview

Anasazi State Park Museum is a small Utah state park in the village of Boulder that preserves the Coombs Site, one of the largest Ancestral Puebloan communities west of the Colorado River. The village, which sits near the boundary of Ancestral Puebloan and Fremont territory and shows traits of both cultures, was occupied roughly from A.D. 1050 to 1200 according to Utah State Parks. The site was excavated by the University of Utah in 1958 and 1959 ahead of the Glen Canyon Dam project, the state acquired the six-acre property in 1960, and it opened to the public as a state park in 1970. Visitors come to see artifacts in the museum, walk among the village ruins and enter a life-size, six-room replica dwelling, making it a worthwhile stop on a drive along Highway 12.

Access

The park is in Boulder, Utah, at about 6,700 feet along scenic Highway 12 (UT-12), which links Escalante to the southwest with Torrey and Capitol Reef to the north. Drive about 28 miles northeast from Escalante, and the museum is on the highway in the center of town. UT-12 is paved and well maintained, but the stretch over Boulder Mountain north of town reaches high elevations and can be snowy in winter. Services in Boulder are limited, so plan fuel and supplies in Escalante or Torrey. Pets are allowed outdoors but not in the buildings.

Seasons

The museum and site are open year-round, and spring through fall is the best time to visit, when the weather is mild and Highway 12 is clear. Summer days can be hot but the village’s elevation keeps nights cool, and fall brings color to the aspens on nearby Boulder Mountain. Winters are cold at this elevation, with snow and occasional ice on the highway, and the park is a quiet stop with fewer visitors. The park closes on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year’s Day, and hours can change seasonally, so check with Utah State Parks before you go.

Things to Do

Tour the Museum

Exhibits display pottery, tools and other artifacts excavated from the Coombs Site and explain the daily life, farming and trade of the people who lived here. A gift shop sells books and local crafts.

Walk the Village Ruins

A short outdoor path leads through the excavated portion of the village, where low stone walls and pit structures show how the community was laid out. Most of the village remains unexcavated and protected below ground.

Enter the Replica Dwelling

A life-size, six-room replica of an Ancestral Puebloan home stands near the original ruins, giving visitors a sense of the rooms and construction methods of the time.

Visitor Centers

The park’s museum serves as its visitor center, with exhibits, staff who can answer questions about the site and the surrounding area, and a gift shop. A park brochure is available online from Utah State Parks, and the staff can also point visitors to nearby attractions along Highway 12, including trails and viewpoints in Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument. The park can be reached by phone at 435-335-7308.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping at the park. Boulder itself has a handful of lodges, inns and restaurants, which fill up quickly in the busy season, so book ahead. Escalante, about 28 miles southwest, has more motels, lodging and services, and Escalante Petrified Forest State Park there has a campground. Torrey, roughly an hour northwest near Capitol Reef National Park, offers a wider selection of motels, cabins and campgrounds, and Bryce Canyon City and Tropic are about two hours to the southwest.
