How to Get There

From St. George, take Bluff Street north, continue on Sunset Boulevard into Santa Clara, then follow Old Highway 91 and turn right onto Gunlock Drive to reach the park.

Overview

Gunlock State Park is a Utah state park built around Gunlock Reservoir on the Santa Clara River, about 15 miles northwest of St. George in southwestern Utah. The dam was completed in 1970 for irrigation and flood control, and the park opened to the public the same year. The reservoir sits in a red rock desert setting and is popular for boating, paddleboarding, kayaking, swimming and fishing for bass and catfish, with picnic areas, a beach and camping along the shore. The park is best known for Gunlock Falls, a seasonal cascade that forms over red sandstone below the dam when the reservoir overflows after wet winters, drawing photographers and waders from across the region.

Access

Gunlock State Park is reached by paved roads from St. George. From the city, follow Bluff Street north, continue through Santa Clara, then take Old Highway 91 and turn onto Gunlock Road, which leads to the park entrance. The drive takes about 25 minutes, and the small farming community of Gunlock lies roughly a mile north of the park. All areas of the park are accessible by passenger car, and the reservoir is well signed. Road work has occasionally affected the entrance road, so check the Utah State Parks website for current conditions.

Seasons

Gunlock is a year-round park, but spring is the headline season. In years with enough winter precipitation, the reservoir spills over its sandstone ledges and the waterfalls flow for a few weeks, typically in spring, drawing crowds, so go early and expect limited parking. The falls are not active every year, and the rocks around them are slick, with swift water. Summers are hot and dry, which is peak season for swimming, boating and fishing. Fall is mild and pleasant for hiking and camping, while winters are mild compared with northern Utah. The boat ramp operates on seasonal hours, and algae advisories sometimes apply to the reservoir, so check current conditions.

Things to Do

Visit the Famous Gunlock Falls

One of the park's most celebrated attractions is Gunlock Falls, a seasonal waterfall phenomenon that occurs when the reservoir spills over its sandstone ledges after periods of heavy runoff. During these events, visitors can witness cascading waterfalls flowing over smooth red-rock formations, creating one of Utah's most unique natural spectacles.

Explore the Red Rock Landscape

The park's surrounding desert terrain offers scenic viewpoints, sandstone formations, wildlife viewing, and excellent photography opportunities. Visitors can enjoy panoramic views of the surrounding mountains, lava fields, and desert ecosystems.

Top Trails

Gunlock Falls Exploration Route

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 167 feet
Difficulty: Easy

During waterfall season, visitors can follow informal routes along the sandstone formations to view the cascading water and unique geological features. The area provides exceptional photography opportunities and beautiful desert scenery.

Gunlock Reservoir Shoreline Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy shoreline walk offers scenic views of the reservoir, surrounding red cliffs, and opportunities for birdwatching and wildlife observation. The trail is suitable for visitors of all ages.

Visitor Centers

Gunlock State Park does not have a visitor center or museum. The park office and ranger station handle questions, permits and fee payment, and the entrance station and posted signs provide basic information and safety warnings about the reservoir and waterfall area. Restrooms, showers, picnic shelters and a boat ramp are available. For maps and current conditions, contact Utah State Parks or visit the park website before your trip.

Camping and Lodging

The park has campsites along the reservoir, including RV sites, tent sites and group camping, with picnic tables, fire pits and grills; reservations are made through Utah State Parks and fill quickly in spring and on summer weekends. Cottages are also listed among the lodging options. Hotels and motels are available in St. George, about 25 minutes away, with a wide range of chain hotels, resorts and vacation rentals, and in nearby Santa Clara and Ivins. Within about an hour, Hurricane, Springdale near Zion National Park and Cedar City offer additional lodging and campgrounds.
