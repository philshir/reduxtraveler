How to Get There

From Capitol Reef National Park Visitor Center: Follow the Scenic Byway for about 7.9 miles, turn left onto Capitol Gorge Road, and continue 2 miles to the trailhead.

Overview

The Capitol Gorge to Tanks Trail winds through a dramatic Navajo sandstone canyon where ancient petroglyphs, historic pioneer inscriptions, and naturally carved waterpockets reveal a layered story of human and geological time. As sunlight filters through the sheer canyon walls, hikers move through a landscape shaped by millennia of erosion, where the faded markings of the Fremont culture and the etched names of 19th‑century settlers offer rare glimpses into the lives of people who once navigated this desert corridor. The journey toward the “Tanks” leads into a quieter, more exposed basin, where these naturally formed rock basins collect precious rainwater and provide a striking contrast between sculpted stone and desert sky.

Reaching the trailhead requires following the Capitol Reef Scenic Drive from the visitor center for roughly eight miles until the pavement ends, then continuing two miles down the graded Capitol Gorge Road to the parking area at the canyon’s entrance. From there, hikers walk a mostly flat, sandy path through the gorge, keeping an eye out for posted signs marking key features such as the Pioneer Register at 0.6 miles and the spur to the Tanks at around 0.9 miles; the short climb to the Tanks is steeper and rockier but well‑traveled. Because summer heat in the canyon can be intense and shade limited, visitors are encouraged to carry ample water, wear sun protection, and start early in the day for the most comfortable experience.
