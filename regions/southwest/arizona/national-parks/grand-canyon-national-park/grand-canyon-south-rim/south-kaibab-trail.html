General Information

Length:
14 miles round trip to the Colorado River
Elevation Gain:
Approximately 4,800 ft
Difficulty:
Strenuous
Hours:
Open 24 hours a day, year-round, subject to weather, trail conditions, and National Park Service closures.
Fees:
Grand Canyon National Park entrance fee required.
No permit required for day hiking.
Backcountry permit required for overnight camping.
Pet Policy:
Pets are not allowed on the South Kaibab Trail.
Closest cities with hotels:
Tusayan, AZ (7 mi)
Seasons:
Best visited in fall, winter, and spring. Summer hiking can be extremely dangerous due to intense heat and the lack of shade and water along the trail.
Location:
South Kaibab Trailhead, Grand Canyon National Park, AZ
Website:
nps.gov

How to Get There

From Tusayan, AZ (7 mi): Take AZ-64 North into Grand Canyon National Park, continue to the Grand Canyon Visitor Center, park in the visitor area, then take the free Kaibab Route shuttle bus to the South Kaibab Trailhead.

Overview

South Kaibab Trail is a steep, exposed corridor trail from the South Rim to the Colorado River, famous for following a ridge that gives 360-degree views of the canyon. It starts near Yaki Point, reached by the free park shuttle or the Rim Trail (the road is closed to private vehicles), and passes Ooh Aah Point, Cedar Ridge and Skeleton Point before dropping to the Black Suspension Bridge and Phantom Ranch, about 7 miles in. Rangers maintain and patrol it.

Good to Know

  • Water: none along the trail on the south side of the river; treated water only at Bright Angel Campground and Phantom Ranch. Bring plenty and treat natural water.
  • Camping: only at Bright Angel Campground, by permit from the Backcountry Information Center; camp stoves only.
  • Difficulty: steep, with a final descent averaging 22%. Cedar Ridge has toilets and is a rest stop for hikers and mule trains.
