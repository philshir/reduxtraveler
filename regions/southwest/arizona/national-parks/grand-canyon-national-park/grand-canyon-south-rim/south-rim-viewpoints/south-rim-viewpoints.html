Overview

The South Rim of Grand Canyon National Park is the most visited part of the canyon, and its overlooks offer a wide variety of experiences. Near Grand Canyon Village, easily reached viewpoints such as Mather Point, Yavapai Point, and Yaki Point provide classic panoramas of the inner canyon, the Colorado River, and the temples and buttes that rise from its depths. They are connected by the Rim Trail and by the free Kaibab/Rim Route shuttle.

West of the village, seven-mile Hermit Road passes a string of overlooks including Maricopa, Powell, Hopi, Mohave, and Pima Point, which are famous for sunsets and long views down the canyon. The road is served by the Hermits Rest Route shuttle for most of the year, and private vehicles are allowed only in winter.

To the east, Desert View Drive follows the rim for about 25 miles through quieter country to the Desert View Watchtower. Along the way are Grandview, Moran, Lipan, and Navajo Points, along with the secluded Shoshone Point. A handful of lesser-known promontories, including Grandeur, Jicarilla, Pinal, Comanche, and Yuma Points, are undeveloped or reached on foot. Sunrise and sunset are the best times to visit, and visitors should always stay behind railings and well back from unfenced edges.
