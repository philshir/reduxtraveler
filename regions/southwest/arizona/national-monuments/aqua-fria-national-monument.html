How to Get There

From Phoenix, AZ (40 mi): Take I-17 N toward Flagstaff and exit at Badger Springs Road or Bloody Basin Road for access to trailheads and scenic areas within the monument.

Overview

Agua Fria National Monument protects about 71,000 acres of rolling semi-desert grassland, mesas and deep canyons along the Agua Fria River, about 40 miles north of Phoenix. Established in 2000 and managed by the Bureau of Land Management, the monument is significant for its extraordinary density of archaeological sites: it holds more than 450 prehistoric sites, including large pueblos and petroglyph panels built by ancestral peoples between about AD 1250 and 1450. The cottonwood- and willow-lined river canyon also supports rich wildlife, including mule deer, pronghorn, javelina, golden eagles and more than 140 bird species. People come for quiet hiking, petroglyph viewing, birdwatching and backcountry exploring so close to the Phoenix metro area.

Access

The monument lies immediately east of I-17, about 40 miles north of Phoenix and 5 miles from Black Canyon City, and is easy to reach by car. The most popular access is Badger Springs Road (I-17 exit 244), where a graded dirt road leads to the Badger Springs trailhead; Bloody Basin Road (exit 259) provides access to the northern part of the monument. Most roads are unpaved and can be rough or impassable after heavy rain, and high-clearance vehicles are recommended for backcountry routes. There are no developed facilities, services or drinking water in the monument.

Seasons

Late fall through spring is the best time to visit, when daytime temperatures are mild and wildflowers bloom in the grasslands, often peaking in March and April. Summers are extremely hot with little shade, and afternoon monsoon storms from July to September can flood washes and close dirt roads. Winter days are pleasant, and nights can be cold. The river flows more reliably in winter and spring, and some archaeological areas can be restricted to protect sensitive resources.

Things to Do

Explore One of Arizona's Most Significant Archaeological Landscapes

Aqua Fria National Monument protects more than 70,000 acres of high desert grasslands, mesas, canyons, and archaeological sites in central Arizona, north of Phoenix. The monument contains hundreds of ancient pueblo ruins, thousands of petroglyphs, and remarkably well-preserved evidence of Native American cultures that inhabited the region between approximately A.D. 1200 and 1450.

Discover Ancient Petroglyphs and Pueblo Sites

The monument is renowned for its extensive collection of cultural resources. Visitors can explore areas containing ancient rock art, masonry structures, terraces, and agricultural features created by Indigenous peoples who once lived along the Agua Fria River. Archaeologists consider the monument one of the most important prehistoric cultural landscapes in the Southwest.

Explore the Agua Fria River Canyon

The Agua Fria River cuts through the monument, creating a dramatic canyon system lined with mesas, cliffs, and diverse desert habitats. Visitors can enjoy scenic overlooks, photography, wildlife viewing, and opportunities to experience Arizona's rugged backcountry landscapes.

Enjoy Wildlife Viewing

The monument supports a wide variety of wildlife, including mule deer, javelina, coyotes, bobcats, golden eagles, hawks, reptiles, and numerous desert bird species. The mixture of grasslands, riparian corridors, and desert scrub habitats makes the area an excellent destination for nature enthusiasts.

Experience Arizona's Dark Skies

Due to its remote location and limited light pollution, Agua Fria National Monument is a rewarding destination for stargazing, night photography, and astronomy enthusiasts. Clear desert skies often provide exceptional viewing conditions throughout much of the year.

Enjoy Backcountry Exploration and Photography

Visitors can explore rugged roads, scenic overlooks, and remote desert landscapes featuring lava formations, mesas, wildflowers, and expansive views. The monument offers outstanding opportunities for landscape photography and solitude.

Top Trails

Badger Springs Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the monument's most popular hikes, this trail descends into Agua Fria Canyon and provides access to the river corridor. Visitors enjoy desert scenery, wildlife viewing opportunities, and seasonal water flows.

Pueblo La Plata Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This interpretive trail leads to one of the monument's best-known archaeological sites, featuring a large prehistoric pueblo and interpretive information about the people who once inhabited the region.

Black Mesa Overlook Route

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This route provides expansive views of the Agua Fria River Canyon, mesas, and surrounding desert landscapes. It is particularly popular among photographers and visitors seeking panoramic scenery.

Agua Fria Canyon Exploration Routes

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Significant
Difficulty: Moderate to Difficult

Numerous primitive routes and backcountry paths allow experienced hikers to explore the monument's canyon systems, archaeological resources, and remote desert environments while enjoying solitude and spectacular scenery.

Visitor Centers

There is no visitor center in the monument. Maps, rules and current road conditions are available from the Bureau of Land Management, including its Hassayampa Field Office in Phoenix, and from the BLM website. Black Canyon City, a few miles from the monument, has fuel, food and basic services. Bring plenty of water, a map and a full tank of gas.

Camping and Lodging

There are no developed campgrounds or lodging in the monument, though dispersed camping is allowed on BLM land in many areas. Nearby lodging, restaurants and RV parks are in Black Canyon City, with a wider selection in Anthem and Phoenix, about 45 minutes away, and Prescott, about an hour north. Lake Pleasant Regional Park, about 35 minutes away, offers developed camping. Check each location's website for current availability.
