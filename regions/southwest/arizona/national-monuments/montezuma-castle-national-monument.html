How to Get There

From Phoenix (94 mi) Get on I-17 N/US-60 W from W Washington St and W Adams St. Follow I-17 N to W Middle Verde Rd in Camp Verde. Take exit 289 from I-17 N. Drive to Montezuma Castle Rd.
From Flagstaff (54 mi) Follow I-17 S to W Middle Verde Rd in Yavapai County. Take exit 289 from I-17 S. Drive to Montezuma Castle Rd.
To Montezuma Well From Montezuma Castle (10.2 mi): Get on I-17 N from Montezuma Castle Rd.Take exit 293 toward McGuireville/Montezuma Well. Merge onto County Rd 77. Turn right.

Overview

Montezuma Castle National Monument in the Verde Valley protects one of the best-preserved cliff dwellings in North America, a five-story, roughly 20-room structure tucked into a limestone cliff about 90 feet above Beaver Creek. The Sinagua people built it in the 12th century and lived in the valley until about 1425. Early settlers mistakenly thought the Aztec emperor Montezuma had a connection to it, and the name stuck. Theodore Roosevelt designated it one of the first national monuments in 1906, and the monument also includes the detached Montezuma Well, a limestone sinkhole several miles away. Visitors come for the dramatic cliff dwelling, a short shady walk beneath sycamores and a window into the ancient farming culture of central Arizona.

Access

The monument is located just off I-17 near Camp Verde, about 50 miles south of Flagstaff, 90 miles north of Phoenix and 35 minutes from Sedona. Take exit 289 and follow Montezuma Castle Road to the visitor center and parking area. The roads are paved, and the main loop trail is short, paved and accessible. Montezuma Well is a separate site about 11 miles northeast, reached via exit 293 and County Road 77, and many visitors see both. Visitors cannot enter the cliff dwelling, which can be viewed from the trail.

Seasons

The monument is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons, with mild temperatures and fall color along the creek. Summers are hot, often above 100 degrees, so visit in the morning, though the sycamore shade helps. Winters are mild, with a chance of cool mornings and occasional frost. Hours are slightly shorter in winter, and afternoon storms are possible in July and August.

Things to Do

See a Remarkable Ancient Cliff Dwelling

Montezuma Castle National Monument gives visitors an up-close view of Sinagua architecture built into a natural alcove. The walls blend into the cliff and have survived for centuries.

Walk the Montezuma Castle Loop Trail

A short paved trail leads beneath sycamore trees to viewpoints of the dwelling and the remains of a larger complex at the base of the cliff. Interpretive signs describe the people and their way of life.

Visit Montezuma Well

Montezuma Well, a sinkhole filled with spring-fed water, features cliff dwellings along its rim and irrigation canals used by the Sinagua. It is free to visit and offers a short walk with views of the water.

Explore the Visitor Center Museum

The visitor center has exhibits of Sinagua artifacts, tools and pottery, along with explanations of the cliff dwelling and the people who built it.

Enjoy Beaver Creek and Local Wildlife

Beaver Creek and its cottonwood and sycamore trees attract birds and other wildlife. The picnic area near the entrance is a pleasant place to rest.

Top Trails

Montezuma Castle Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved, accessible trail winds beneath the cliff, with views of the dwelling and informational signs. It takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

Montezuma Well Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 0.4 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This short trail circles part of the sinkhole rim and passes ruins built into the cliff. A path also leads down to the outlet and a small Hohokam-style irrigation ditch.

Visitor Centers

The Montezuma Castle Visitor Center offers a museum, a bookstore, restrooms and rangers who answer questions and provide maps for both Montezuma Castle and Montezuma Well. A smaller contact station is at the Well. Hours vary by season, and the monument is closed on Christmas Day, so check the National Park Service website for current hours and fees.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the monument. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Camp Verde, just a few minutes away, and in Cottonwood, about 20 minutes away. Sedona, about 35 minutes north, and Flagstaff offer more resorts and lodges. Camping is available at Dead Horse Ranch State Park in Cottonwood and at RV parks in Camp Verde. Check each location's website for current availability.
