★5.0

How to Get There

From Phoenix, AZ (50 mi): Take I-10 E to AZ-387 S, then follow AZ-87 S to Coolidge and turn onto W Ruins Drive to reach the monument.

Overview

Casa Grande Ruins National Monument preserves the Great House of the Hohokam people, a four-story adobe tower built around 1350 in the Sonoran Desert of south-central Arizona, along with the remains of a large village and irrigation canals. The Hohokam were skilled desert farmers who dug hundreds of miles of canals to water crops, and Casa Grande is among their largest and most mysterious structures, possibly used for ceremonial purposes and astronomical observation. It was set aside as the first prehistoric and cultural reserve in the United States in 1892 and became a national monument in 1918. A protective roof built in 1932 shelters the Great House. Visitors come for the fascinating architecture, interpretive programs and a quick look at ancient desert engineering near Phoenix and Tucson.

Access

The monument is in the city of Coolidge, about 50 miles southeast of Phoenix and 65 miles northwest of Tucson, and is reached by car on paved roads. From I-10, take AZ-387 and AZ-87 to Coolidge and turn onto West Ruins Drive to the entrance. Parking is free, and the main walk to the Great House is short and accessible. The monument is a convenient stop between Phoenix and Tucson and can be combined with Picacho Peak State Park or Tonto National Monument.

Seasons

The monument is open daily, and the cooler months from October through April are the best time to visit, when the desert is pleasant for walking. Summer temperatures often exceed 100 degrees, so visit early in the morning, and bring water. Monsoon storms from July to September can bring dust and heavy rain. Winter days are mild, and spring brings wildflowers. Hours are limited, with last entry in late afternoon, so plan accordingly.

Things to Do

Explore the Largest Hohokam Great House

Casa Grande Ruins National Monument gives visitors a close-up look at a rare surviving Hohokam building and its surrounding compound. The structure is made of caliche earth, a hard material found in desert soils.

Walk Around the Great House

A short paved path leads around the four-story Great House beneath its protective roof. Interpretive signs describe how the tower was built and how it may have been used.

See the Compound and Ball Court

The walk continues to remains of surrounding walls, rooms and a ball court, showing the scale of the village. Rangers often offer talks about the Hohokam.

Learn About Hohokam Canals and Farming

Exhibits explain how the Hohokam dug canals to bring river water to desert fields, a system that influenced modern water management in the region.

Visit the Museum and Join Ranger Programs

The visitor center has artifacts and displays, and ranger-led talks and special programs are offered in the cooler season.

Top Trails

Casa Grande Ruins Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved loop leads from the visitor center to the Great House, the compound and a ball court. It is accessible and takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

Visitor Centers

The Casa Grande Ruins visitor center is the starting point, with a museum of Hohokam artifacts, exhibits on desert farming and archaeology, a bookstore and restrooms. Rangers lead talks and answer questions, and the Great House can be viewed from the path. Hours are daily with a late-afternoon last entry, and the center is closed on some holidays, so check the National Park Service website for current times.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the monument. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Coolidge and the larger city of Casa Grande, about 20 minutes away, with many chain hotels along I-10. Phoenix, Tucson and Florence offer more options within an hour. Camping is available at Picacho Peak State Park, about 35 minutes south, and at RV parks in Casa Grande. Check each property's website for current availability.
