Coronado National Memorial

How to Get There

From Tucson, AZ (90 mi): Take AZ-92 S through Sierra Vista, then turn east on E Montezuma Canyon Rd and follow signs to the memorial entrance.

Overview

Coronado National Memorial commemorates the 1540 to 1542 expedition of Spanish conquistador Francisco Vásquez de Coronado, the first major European exploration of the Southwest, and sits on the U.S.-Mexico border in the Huachuca Mountains of southeastern Arizona. Designated Coronado International Memorial in 1941 as a symbol of goodwill between the United States and Mexico, it became a national memorial in 1952. The memorial protects forested canyons, grasslands and Sky Island habitat where Sonoran and Chihuahuan Desert plants and animals mix with those of the Rocky Mountains and Mexico, making it a favorite for birders and hikers. Visitors come for the sweeping views from Montezuma Pass, short hikes, cave exploration and a chance to see wildlife near the border.

Access

The memorial is about 21 miles south of Sierra Vista and roughly 90 miles southeast of Tucson. From AZ-92, visitors turn onto Montezuma Canyon Road, a mostly paved road that leads to the visitor center. Beyond the visitor center, the 3-mile road to Montezuma Pass is unpaved, steep and narrow, and it is not recommended for vehicles over 24 feet or for trailers, but is passable for most passenger cars in dry weather. Services are very limited near the memorial, so fill up in Sierra Vista.

Seasons

The memorial is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking. Summer days are warm, with afternoon monsoon thunderstorms from July through September that make the landscape green but can create flash floods and slippery roads. Winter days are mild but nights can be cold, and snow occasionally closes the unpaved road to Montezuma Pass. Birdwatching is excellent in spring and summer, when migrating and nesting species arrive.

Things to Do

Walk in the Footsteps of Coronado

Coronado National Memorial offers exhibits and trails that trace the history of the expedition and its encounters with native peoples. The setting in the Huachuca foothills gives a sense of the terrain the explorers faced.

Enjoy the View From Montezuma Pass

At 6,575 feet, Montezuma Pass offers sweeping views over the San Pedro Valley and into Mexico. It is a favorite spot for sunset and for watching the border landscape.

Hike Coronado Peak

A short trail from the pass climbs to the summit of Coronado Peak, with 360-degree views, interpretive signs and a picnic area.

Explore Coronado Cave

Coronado Cave is a limestone cave reached by a short, steep trail. Visitors can explore with a flashlight and a free permit from the visitor center; the cave is closed seasonally to protect bats.

Watch Birds and Wildlife

The memorial is a hotspot for birds, including Mexican jays, hummingbirds and warblers, and for wildlife such as coatimundis, deer and javelina.

Top Trails

Coronado Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 0.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This short trail climbs from Montezuma Pass to the summit of Coronado Peak, with interpretive signs and big views of the valley and the border.

Coronado Cave Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs to the mouth of Coronado Cave, with views of the canyon along the way. A flashlight and a permit are required to enter the cave.

Joe's Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail crosses the ridge from Montezuma Pass toward the visitor center, with wide views of grasslands and mountains. It is best done with a shuttle or as a longer out-and-back.

Visitor Centers

The Coronado National Memorial Visitor Center is on Montezuma Canyon Road and has exhibits on the Coronado expedition, the natural history of the Huachuca Mountains and the border region, a bookstore and restrooms. Rangers provide trail maps, cave permits and current road conditions, and a picnic area is nearby. Hours vary, so check the National Park Service website. The memorial is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas, and there is no entrance fee.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the memorial, which has a picnic area only. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Sierra Vista, about 30 minutes away, and the historic towns of Bisbee and Tombstone offer inns and bed-and-breakfasts about an hour away. Camping is available at Coronado National Forest campgrounds such as Parker Canyon Lake and in Carr and Ramsey canyons in the Huachuca Mountains. Check each location's website for current availability.
