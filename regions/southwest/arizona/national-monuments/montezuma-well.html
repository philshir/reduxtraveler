Montezuma Well

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Arizona
  3. Montezuma Well

Monument protects a set of well-preserved dwellings built and used by the Sinagua people between approximately 1100 and 1425 AD. The main structure comprises five stories and about 20 rooms

Collapsed limestone sinkhole filled with a spring-fed pool and surrounded by ancient cliff dwellings, vegetation, and desert landscape.

How to Get There

From Camp Verde (6 mi): Drive east on AZ-260 E for about 5 miles, turn north onto Montezuma Well Road, and continue for approximately 1 mile to the parking area.

Overview

Montezuma Well is a detached unit of Montezuma Castle National Monument and one of the most unusual natural and cultural sites in central Arizona: a limestone sinkhole about 368 feet across, fed by springs that pour out roughly 1.4 million gallons of water a day. The collapse of an underground cavern created the Well, and its warm, carbonated water has supported farming and wildlife for centuries. The Sinagua people built cliff dwellings along the rim and irrigated crops with canals still visible today, and the water contains several species found nowhere else on earth. Montezuma Well was added to the national monument system in 1947. Visitors come for the dramatic sinkhole, a short walk to ruins and canals, and a quieter alternative to Montezuma Castle.

Access

The Well is about 11 miles northeast of Montezuma Castle and 6 miles from Camp Verde. Take I-17 to exit 293 toward McGuireville and follow County Road 77 and Montezuma Well Road to the parking area, which is a drive of roughly 1 mile from the highway. The roads are paved, and the parking area has restrooms and a picnic ground. Visitors reach the rim and the cliff dwellings on a short paved trail, though the trail down to the outlet is steeper.

Seasons

The site is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for walking. Summer afternoons are hot, but the water and shade make it relatively cool, and thunderstorms can pass through in July and August. Winters are mild, and the water stays the same temperature all year, so it can steam on cold mornings. The monument closes on Christmas Day and hours vary by season, so check the National Park Service website.

Things to Do

Peer Into a Natural Sinkhole Fed by Springs

Montezuma Well is a natural limestone sinkhole with a pool of spring-fed water in the bottom. The viewpoint on the rim gives a full view of the sinkhole and its green edges.

See Cliff Dwellings on the Rim

Ruins built into the walls above the water show how the Sinagua adapted the sinkhole for living space. Some small rooms can be seen from the trail.

Follow the Ancient Irrigation Canal

Part of a prehistoric canal, used for centuries to irrigate crops, is preserved near the picnic ground. It is an easy walk and an example of the engineering used by the early farmers.

Walk to the Outlet

A trail descends to the outlet where the spring water flows through a tunnel in the rock, giving a look at how the water leaves the Well.

Picnic and Enjoy the Quiet

A shaded picnic area with a stream makes a pleasant stop, and the site is typically much less crowded than Montezuma Castle.

Top Trails

Montezuma Well Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 0.4 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This short paved trail climbs to the rim of the sinkhole for views of the water and ruins. It takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

Outlet Trail

Length: Approximately 0.25 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail drops to the base of the sinkhole, where the spring water exits. The return is uphill, so allow time for the climb.

Visitor Centers

There is a small contact station at Montezuma Well with exhibits about the sinkhole, its water and the Sinagua, and rangers can answer questions and provide maps. The main visitor center, with a museum and bookstore, is at Montezuma Castle, about 11 miles away. Admission to the Well is included with Montezuma Castle admission or a valid park pass, and hours vary, so check the National Park Service website.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the Well. Hotels and restaurants are in Camp Verde and Cottonwood, about 20 to 30 minutes away, and Sedona and Flagstaff have the widest range of lodging, 40 minutes to an hour away. Camping is available at Dead Horse Ranch State Park in Cottonwood and at RV parks in Camp Verde. Check each location's website for current availability.
