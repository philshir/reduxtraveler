How to Get There

From Page, AZ (40 mi): Take US-89 S to House Rock Valley Road, then drive south on the unpaved road to access trailheads and scenic areas; high-clearance vehicles recommended.

Overview

Vermilion Cliffs National Monument is a remote, spectacular 294,000-acre landscape of colorful sandstone cliffs, buttes, canyons and plateaus on the Arizona Strip, just south of the Utah border. The monument was established in 2000 and is managed by the Bureau of Land Management as part of the National Conservation Lands. It is world-famous for The Wave, a swirling sandstone formation in Coyote Buttes North, and also protects White Pocket, Paria Canyon, Buckskin Gulch and the Paria Plateau, as well as a release site for endangered California condors. The monument holds hundreds of archaeological sites and extraordinary rock art. People come for some of the most photogenic landscapes in the West, remote hiking and solitude.

Access

The monument straddles US-89A between Page and Jacob Lake, about 40 miles from Page, and is reached through a mix of paved highway and rugged dirt roads. House Rock Valley Road and BLM roads to areas like White Pocket and Coyote Buttes are unpaved and sandy, so high-clearance, four-wheel-drive vehicles are required for some trailheads and the roads can be impassable after rain. The Wire Pass trailhead for Coyote Buttes North is reached from US-89 in Utah via House Rock Valley Road. There are no services in the monument, so bring water, food, fuel and navigation tools.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the best seasons to visit, with moderate temperatures for hiking. Summers are extremely hot, often over 100 degrees, and monsoon storms from July to September can cause deadly flash floods in slot canyons and make roads impassable. Winter brings cold temperatures and occasional snow, and high-clearance roads can become muddy. Permits for The Wave are limited and awarded by lottery, with applications months in advance, so planning ahead is essential. California condors can be spotted year-round near Navajo Bridge.

Things to Do

Hike Through One of the West's Most Colorful Landscapes

Vermilion Cliffs National Monument is a world of swirling sandstone, towering cliffs and deep canyons. The area rewards hikers and photographers with extraordinary scenery.

Hike to The Wave

The Wave in Coyote Buttes North is a sculpted sandstone formation known worldwide. Access requires a daily or advance permit awarded by lottery, and the trail is unmarked.

Explore White Pocket

White Pocket is a remote area of twisted, brain-like sandstone formations and is reached by a sandy 4x4 road. Many visitors join a guided tour from Page or Kanab.

See California Condors

The monument is a release site for California condors, and the birds are often seen soaring near the Vermilion Cliffs and Navajo Bridge in Marble Canyon.

Backpack Paria Canyon

Paria Canyon and Buckskin Gulch form one of the longest slot canyon systems in the world, with a multi-day hike that requires a permit.

Top Trails

The Wave (Coyote Buttes North)

Length: Approximately 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This unmarked route crosses slickrock and sand to The Wave, with navigation skills and a permit required. It is best done in cooler weather.

Wire Pass to Buckskin Gulch

Length: Approximately 3.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Moderate

This route leads through a narrow slot canyon into Buckskin Gulch, with towering walls and dramatic light. Check the weather forecast, since flash floods can occur.

White Pocket Loop

Length: Approximately 2 to 3 miles of exploration
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

White Pocket is explored on foot with no marked trail, as visitors wander through the swirled rock formations. A high-clearance 4x4 vehicle is required for access.

Visitor Centers

There is no visitor center within the monument. The BLM's Paria Contact Station on US-89 in Utah provides information, permits and weather updates for Coyote Buttes, Buckskin Gulch and Paria Canyon, and the Arizona Strip Interagency Information Center in St. George, Utah, is open on weekdays. The Condor viewing area near Navajo Bridge in Marble Canyon has a small visitor center operated by the National Park Service. Check the BLM website for current hours.

Camping and Lodging

Dispersed camping is allowed outside wilderness areas, and there are no developed campgrounds in the monument, though the Lees Ferry campground in Glen Canyon National Recreation Area and BLM sites near the Paria are nearby. Lodging and restaurants are in Marble Canyon, about 30 minutes away, Page, about 45 minutes away, and Kanab, Utah, about an hour away. Check each location's website for current availability.
