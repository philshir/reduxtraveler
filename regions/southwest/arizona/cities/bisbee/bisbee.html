How to Get There

From Tucson (94.5 miles): Get on I-10 E from W Congress St. Follow I-10 E and AZ-80 E to West Blvd in Bisbee. Take the West Blvd exit from AZ-80 E. Drive to Tombstone Canyon.

Overview

Bisbee is a charming former copper mining town in southeastern Arizona, about 92 miles southeast of Tucson and 11 miles from the Mexican border, that has reinvented itself as one of the Southwest’s most distinctive artsy, historic destinations. Its steep, walkable “Old Bisbee” is packed with Victorian houses, an Art Deco county courthouse, galleries, coffee shops and restaurants, tucked into narrow canyons of the Mule Mountains.

Bisbee is significant as one of the richest mining districts in the world, and as a rare Western boomtown that was not abandoned when the ore ran out. Visitors come for the mine tours, the quirky small-town atmosphere, the live theater and the historic lodging, including the Copper Queen Hotel, and they come to wander the stairways and hillside streets that make the town feel like a European village in the desert.

Bisbee was founded in 1880 as a copper, gold and silver mining town, named for Judge DeWitt Bisbee, a backer of the Copper Queen Mine, and it boomed in the early 1900s. It was also the source of the fine turquoise called Bisbee Blue. The mines shut down in 1975, and the town revived itself around heritage tourism, launching the Queen Mine Tour in 1976. Artists and free spirits moved in from the 1970s, and today Bisbee is known for its arts scene, live theater and compact, pedestrian-scale downtown.

Popular sights include the Copper Queen Mine Tour, the Lavender Pit, a huge open-pit mine whose terraced walls show the colors of the copper ore, the Copper Queen Hotel, the Cochise County Courthouse and Warren Ballpark, built in 1909 and possibly baseball’s oldest park still in use. The district’s many hillside stairways are a local landmark, and Lowell, a preserved Main Street frozen in the 1950s, is just down the road.

Bisbee’s culture centers on its mining past and its artist community. The Bisbee Mining & Historical Museum, a Smithsonian affiliate in the old Phelps Dodge general office building, tells the story of the district, while galleries, studios, the Central School art center and local theater keep the arts scene lively. Annual events such as the Bisbee 1000 Great Stair Climb in the fall draw visitors from around the region.

Bisbee has few formal parks within the town itself, but its stairways, canyon streets and mine sites serve as outdoor attractions. Within a short drive are Coronado National Memorial on the Mexican border, Ramsey Canyon Preserve, which is known for its hummingbirds, the Coronado National Forest and Chiricahua National Monument.

At about 5,300 feet, Bisbee is cooler than the Sonoran Desert cities, with summer highs usually in the 80s and low 90s°F and a summer monsoon from July through September that brings afternoon thunderstorms. Winters are mild, with highs in the 50s and 60s°F, cold nights and an occasional dusting of snow. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons to visit.
