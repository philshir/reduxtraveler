Birthing Cave

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Arizona
  3. Sedona
  4. Birthing Cave

Photo by Jimmy Conover on Unsplash; Image Size Adjusted

A shallow sandstone alcove with a distinctive curved opening that frames panoramic desert views and is accessed by a short hike with a brief scramble near the end.

General Information
Length:
Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain:
About 250–300 feet
Difficulty:
Easy to Moderate
Hours:
Open daily from sunrise to sunset
Fees:
Free admission (no Red Rock Pass required)
Pet Policy:
Dogs are allowed but must be kept on a leash
Closest cities with hotels:
Sedona (7 mi)
Seasons:
Fall, winter, and spring are best; summer is very hot with little shade
Location:
Long Canyon Trailhead, Sedona, AZ 86336
Website:
fs.usda.gov

How to Get There

From Sedona (7 mi): Take AZ-89A west, turn right onto Dry Creek Road, continue north to Long Canyon Road, and follow it to the Long Canyon Trailhead where the hike begins.

Overview

Set within the rugged terrain of northern Arizona’s red rock country, Birthing Cave is a striking natural alcove carved into sandstone cliffs, its sculpted contours forming a distinctive opening that frames the surrounding landscape. Reached by a relatively short trail, the journey offers a gradual passage through desert vegetation—juniper, cactus, and sunlit rock—before culminating in a brief but rewarding scramble up slickrock to the cave itself. Once inside, the interplay of light and shadow reveals the formation’s unique shape, creating a visual corridor that draws the eye outward to sweeping vistas of mesas and canyon walls.

Beyond its aesthetic appeal, the site carries cultural and spiritual significance, with traditions linking the formation’s distinctive, womb-like shape to themes of renewal and origin. Today, visitors come not only for its photogenic qualities but also for the contemplative atmosphere it imparts, where silence and expansive views encourage reflection. Though compact in scale, the experience captures the essence of the region—dramatic geology, layered cultural meaning, and a landscape that feels both intimate and vast.
