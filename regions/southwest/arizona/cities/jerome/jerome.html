Overview

Jerome is a quirky former copper mining town perched on Cleopatra Hill more than 5,000 feet above the Verde Valley, about 100 miles north of Phoenix on State Route 89A between Sedona and Prescott. Once home to more than 10,000 people, it is now a small artists’ community of about 450 people, known for its “ghost town” character, steep streets, art galleries, wine tasting rooms and views across the valley.

Jerome is significant as one of the best-preserved copper mining towns in the West, and it is popular because it combines history with a lively arts scene and a reputation for ghost stories. People come to wander its hillside streets, explore the old mining sites, taste Verde Valley wines, shop in the galleries and enjoy the views from a town that seems to cling to the mountain.

The town grew around some of the richest copper deposits ever found, including the United Verde Mine, and boomed in the 1920s. The mines closed for good in 1953 and the population dropped to fewer than 100 before residents turned to tourism. Jerome became a National Historic Landmark in 1967 and remains a top stop for history lovers.

Popular stops include the Sliding Jail, a leaning jail that slid down the hillside, the Gold King Mine and Ghost Town, with its collection of antique vehicles and mining equipment, the historic hotels and saloons along Main Street, and the art galleries, shops, coffee houses and wineries that fill the old commercial buildings.

Jerome’s museums tell the story of the mining era. Jerome State Historic Park is housed in the Douglas mansion, built by mine owner “Rawhide Jimmy” Douglas, and the Mine Museum run by the Jerome Historical Society displays photographs and artifacts. Annual events include the Paso de Casas home tour in May and the Festival of Lights in December.

Audrey Headframe Park preserves the headframe of the Audrey Shaft and offers a glimpse down a mine shaft. Jerome State Historic Park has picnic areas with views across the valley, and nearby outdoor options include Dead Horse Ranch State Park in Cottonwood, Tuzigoot National Monument and the surrounding national forests.

At more than 5,000 feet, Jerome is cooler than the Verde Valley below, with summer highs usually in the 80s and low 90s°F and monsoon storms in July and August. Winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and chilly nights, and snow is occasional but brief. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for strolling the town’s steep streets.

Featured Locations

Audrey Headframe Park
★ 5.0

Historic mining landmark featuring a towering headframe structure and a deep shaft viewing platform that reveals the scale and engineering of early 20th-century extraction operations.

Gold King Mine and Ghost Town
★ 5.0

Historic open-air attraction featuring preserved mining structures, antique vehicles, and interactive exhibits that showcase early industrial life and frontier-era operations.

Jerome Mining Museum
★ 5.0

Compact history museum featuring mining artifacts, photographs, and exhibits that illustrate daily life and industry in a once-booming copper town.

Jerome State Historic Park
★ 5.0

Historic hilltop museum complex housed in a 1916 mansion featuring mining exhibits, artifacts, and panoramic views overlooking a former copper boomtown landscape.

Jerome's Sliding Jail
★ 5.0

Historic early-20th-century jail structure known for its dramatic downhill displacement and preserved as a unique roadside landmark reflecting the instability of a mining-era landscape.
