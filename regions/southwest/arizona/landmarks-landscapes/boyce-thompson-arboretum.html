Boyce Thompson Arboretum

How to Get There

From Phoenix, AZ (60 mi): Take US-60 E toward Globe for about an hour, then turn right onto Arboretum Way just before entering Superior to reach the entrance.

Overview

Boyce Thompson Arboretum is Arizona's oldest and largest botanical garden, a 392-acre "living museum" of desert plants at the foot of Picketpost Mountain near Superior. Mining magnate William Boyce Thompson founded it in 1924 to study and showcase plants from the world's arid regions, and it is now a state park operated in partnership with the University of Arizona. Its trails wind through themed gardens of cacti, agaves, palms and trees from deserts on every continent, along a canyon with a stream, a small lake and a historic mansion. The arboretum is an Audubon Important Bird Area with some 270 recorded species, and visitors come to enjoy blooming cacti and wildflowers in spring, birdwatching, shady paths and a quiet escape just an hour from Phoenix.

Access

The arboretum is on US-60 about 3 miles west of Superior and about 60 miles east of Phoenix, reached by a paved road. Take US-60 east through Apache Junction and Florence Junction to the entrance on the south side of the highway, where a large parking lot serves the visitor center. Most paths are well-maintained and many are accessible, while some trails climb hills with uneven surfaces. The drive is a convenient day trip from the Phoenix area and pairs with the Apache Trail and Tonto National Monument.

Seasons

Fall through spring is the best time to visit, with March and April bringing peak blooming of wildflowers and cacti and mild temperatures. Summers are hot, so the arboretum opens early and visitors should walk in the morning. The stream and Ayer Lake are more active after winter and monsoon rains. The park is closed on Independence Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, and winter evenings may feature special events and holiday lights.

Things to Do

Wander a Desert Botanical Garden

Boyce Thompson Arboretum has gardens of desert plants from Arizona, Australia, Africa and South America, arranged along shaded paths. Labeled plants make it easy to learn.

Walk the Main Loop Trail

The 1.5-mile main loop passes the cactus garden, the Wallace Desert Garden, palm and eucalyptus groves, and exhibits from Australia and South America, plus Ayer Lake and Queen Creek.

See "Mr. Big" the Eucalyptus

One of the largest red gum eucalyptus trees in the United States, nicknamed "Mr. Big," towers over a grove of eucalyptus planted in the 1920s.

Go Birdwatching

The creek, lake and trees draw hummingbirds, warblers and raptors, and bobcats, javelinas and other animals can be seen in the riparian zone.

Tour the Picket Post House

The historic Picket Post House, Thompson's former mansion, overlooks the grounds and can be viewed from trails.

Top Trails

Main Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This well-maintained loop passes the main gardens, the lake and Queen Creek. It is the best introduction to the arboretum and suitable for most visitors.

Ayer Lake and Queen Creek Trails

Length: Approximately 1 mile of connected paths
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

These shady paths follow the stream and lake, with chances to see birds and other wildlife. They are popular in spring and fall.

High Trail

Length: Approximately 1 to 2 miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This higher route climbs above the gardens, offering views of the canyon and surrounding mountains.

Visitor Centers

The Smith Interpretive Center near the entrance has exhibits on desert plants and the arboretum's history, a garden shop with plants and gifts, a cafe, restrooms and staff who can recommend routes. Hours are daily with early opening in summer, and the arboretum is closed on Independence Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, so check the arboretum website for current times and fees. Children under 5 and members are free.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the arboretum. Hotels, inns and restaurants are in Superior, about 10 minutes away, and in Globe and Miami, about 30 to 40 minutes east. Apache Junction, Mesa and Phoenix offer many more hotels within about an hour. Camping is available at Lost Dutchman State Park near Apache Junction and in the Tonto National Forest. Check each location's website for current availability.
