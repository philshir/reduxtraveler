How to Get There

From Tubac: Travel through the historic village center to the intersection of Burruel Street and Tubac Road, then continue to 1 Burruel Street at the entrance to Tubac Presidio State Historic Park.

Overview

Tubac Presidio State Historic Park preserves the site of the Presidio of San Ignacio de Tubac, established in 1752 as the first permanent Spanish colonial settlement in what is now Arizona, in the Santa Cruz River Valley south of Tucson. The Spanish built the fort to protect the region after the O'odham revolt of 1751, and in 1775 Captain Juan Bautista de Anza led a colonizing expedition from here to found San Francisco. Tubac later became a Mexican and then American settlement, and the first newspaper printed in Arizona, the Weekly Arizonian, was published here in 1859. Arizona's oldest state park, it opened in 1958 and today includes a museum, a preserved underground section of the presidio and several historic buildings. Visitors come to walk through 270 years of border history in the heart of the Tubac arts village.

Access

The park is in the village of Tubac, about 45 minutes south of Tucson and 20 minutes north of Nogales, reached from I-19 exit 34. From the village center, follow Burruel Street to the park entrance. The roads are paved and parking is available at the park, which is walkable from Tubac's shops and galleries. The Juan Bautista de Anza National Historic Trail begins at the park, and many visitors combine it with a stop at Tumacácori National Historical Park, a few miles south.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with fall through spring the most comfortable seasons, when daytime temperatures are mild. Summers are hot, with monsoon storms from July to September, so mornings are best. Winter days are pleasant, and Tubac's art festival in early February and holiday events draw crowds. The park is closed on Mondays, Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day, and hours may be shorter in the summer.

Things to Do

Step Into Arizona's Spanish Colonial Past

Tubac Presidio State Historic Park is a compact site where visitors can see the foundation of the original fort, historic homes and exhibits that trace the region's history from the 1700s to the present.

See the Underground Presidio Ruins

A protected underground room reveals the foundations and adobe walls of the original presidio, giving a rare look at the building's construction.

Tour the Museum and Historic Buildings

The Otero Hall museum, the 1885 schoolhouse, the Rojas House and the Griffin Museum display artifacts, photographs and a historic printing press that produced the first newspaper in Arizona.

Walk the Anza Trail

The Juan Bautista de Anza National Historic Trail leaves from the park and follows the Santa Cruz River toward Tumacácori, tracing the route of the 1775 colonizing expedition.

Browse Tubac's Galleries and Cafes

The village around the park is known for its art galleries, craft shops and restaurants, which make it a popular day trip from Tucson.

Top Trails

Presidio Historic Walk

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short walk links the museum, the underground ruins and historic buildings, with signs explaining the park's history.

Juan Bautista de Anza Trail to Tumacácori

Length: Approximately 4.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This riverside trail follows the Santa Cruz River under cottonwoods to Tumacácori National Historical Park and is popular with walkers, birdwatchers and horseback riders.

Visitor Centers

The park museum and visitor center, housed in historic buildings, has exhibits on the presidio, the Anza expedition and early Arizona, a gift shop, restrooms and staff who can answer questions and provide maps. The park is open Tuesday through Sunday and closed on Mondays and major holidays, with discounts for educators, military members, veterans and first responders. Check the park website for current hours and fees.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. The village of Tubac has inns, a golf resort and restaurants within walking distance, and more hotels are in Rio Rico, Nogales and Green Valley. Tucson, about 45 minutes north, has the widest selection. Camping is available at Patagonia Lake State Park, about 35 minutes east, and in the Coronado National Forest. Check each location's website for current availability.
