How to Get There

From Winslow (2 mi): Travel north from Winslow on AZ-87, follow signs for Homolovi State Park, and continue to the visitor center and archaeological sites located along the Little Colorado River floodplain.

Overview

Homolovi State Park protects one of the most significant ancestral Hopi cultural landscapes in the Southwest, along the Little Colorado River near Winslow in northern Arizona. The name Homolovi means "Place of the Little Hills" in Hopi, and the park preserves several large pueblo villages, including Homolovi I, II, III and IV, that were home to ancestors of the Hopi people in the 1200s and 1300s before they migrated north to the Hopi mesas. The park was established in 1986, and the Hopi continue to regard the sites as sacred. Visitors come for the pueblo ruins, petroglyph panels, a visitor center museum and the wide desert landscape, as well as camping and access to Winslow, Meteor Crater and Petrified Forest National Park.

Access

The park is about 5 miles northeast of Winslow, reached by driving north on AZ-87 from I-40 exit 257 and following signs to the visitor center and campground. The main roads are paved, while some access roads to the more remote ruins are graded dirt and may be rough in wet weather. Flagstaff is about 1 hour west, and Petrified Forest National Park is about an hour east. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and the park is open daily except Christmas Day.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with spring and fall the most comfortable seasons for exploring the ruins, when daytime temperatures are mild. Summers are hot and sunny, with monsoon thunderstorms from July to September that can cause muddy roads and flash flooding in washes. Winters are cold and windy, with occasional snow, but the sites are quiet. Wildflowers appear after spring and summer rains, and the dark skies are good for stargazing.

Things to Do

Discover an Ancestral Hopi Homeland

Homolovi State Park offers a rare opportunity to walk among pueblo villages that are among the best-documented ancestral Hopi sites. Respectful visiting and staying on trails help protect the sacred areas.

Walk Through Homolovi II Pueblo

The largest ruin in the park once had hundreds of rooms around a central plaza. Trails and signs describe how people lived, farmed and traded here.

See Petroglyphs and Rock Art

Petroglyph panels near the trails show symbols and figures that are meaningful to the Hopi. They offer a glimpse into the beliefs and history of the people who lived here.

Visit the Museum and Visitor Center

Exhibits cover Hopi history and culture, archaeology and the park's natural setting, with pottery and tools found at the sites.

Camp, Bird and Stargaze

A campground in the park makes a good base for visiting Winslow and the region, and the Little Colorado River corridor attracts birds and wildlife.

Top Trails

Homolovi II Ruins Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail leads past the main pueblo mound and surrounding features, with interpretive signs about the village's layout and the people who lived there.

Homolovi I and Nusungvö Trails

Length: Varies; short loops of about 1 to 2 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

These trails visit other sites and scenic overlooks along the Little Colorado River, offering a quieter, more rugged experience than the main ruins.

Visitor Centers

The Homolovi Visitor Center and museum features exhibits on the Hopi, archaeology and the park's natural history, a gift shop with Hopi art, restrooms and staff who can recommend trails and explain visiting etiquette at sacred sites. Hours vary by season, and the park is closed on Christmas Day, so check the Arizona State Parks website for current times and fees. Children 6 and under are free.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground with tent and RV sites, restrooms and showers. Hotels and restaurants are in Winslow, only about 10 minutes away, including the historic La Posada Hotel, a restored Harvey House, and the town's Route 66 attractions such as Standin' on the Corner Park. Holbrook, with its Wigwam Motel, is about 35 minutes east, and Flagstaff offers the widest selection of lodging about an hour west. Check each location's website for current availability.
