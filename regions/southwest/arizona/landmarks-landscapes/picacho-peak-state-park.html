Picacho Peak State Park

★5.0

How to Get There

From Phoenix, AZ (75 mi): Take I-10 E toward Tucson for about 70 miles, then take Exit 219 and follow signs to the park entrance.

Overview

Picacho Peak State Park surrounds a 3,374-foot volcanic spire that rises abruptly from the Sonoran Desert between Phoenix and Tucson and is visible for miles along I-10. The peak is a remnant of ancient volcanic activity, and its name simply means "peak" in Spanish. The park is also the site of the westernmost Civil War battle, the skirmish at Picacho Pass in April 1862, which is reenacted every March. Visitors are drawn by the steep Hunter Trail to the summit, which uses cables and steps in sections, by a spectacular display of Mexican gold poppies and other wildflowers in late winter and early spring when conditions allow, and by camping and picnicking at the foot of the peak.

Access

The park is directly off I-10 at exit 219, about 45 miles northwest of Tucson, roughly 75 miles southeast of Phoenix and 20 miles from Eloy. It is one of the easiest state parks in Arizona to reach, with a paved entrance road, a visitor center and trailheads within a short distance of the highway. Visitors arrive by car, and the park makes a convenient stop on the drive between Phoenix and Tucson. Some trails involve steep cables, so they are not suitable for everyone.

Seasons

Spring is the signature season, when wildflowers carpet the base of the peak in March and April after wet winters, and the Civil War reenactment draws crowds in March. Fall and winter bring comfortable temperatures for hiking and camping. Summers are extremely hot, with temperatures often above 105 degrees, and hiking to the summit should be avoided or done only at dawn. The park closes on Christmas Day and early on Thanksgiving, and trail conditions can change after storms.

Things to Do

Climb a Desert Landmark

Picacho Peak State Park is best known for its dramatic spire and the steep, cable-assisted trail to the summit, which rewards hikers with panoramic views.

See Spring Wildflowers

In good years, Mexican gold poppies, lupine and other flowers blanket the desert floor, and the park becomes a popular destination for photography.

Attend the Civil War Reenactment

Each March the park hosts a reenactment of the Battle of Picacho Pass, with costumed participants, camps and demonstrations that tell the story of the far-western Civil War skirmish.

Hike the Desert Trails

Shorter trails at the base of the peak offer easier hikes through saguaro, ocotillo and cholla, with views of the surrounding desert.

Camp, Picnic and Stargaze

The campground and picnic areas at the foot of the peak make a convenient overnight stop, and the surrounding desert offers quiet evenings and night skies.

Top Trails

Hunter Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,500 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

This steep trail climbs to the summit with sections that use cables and steps, and some scrambling. It offers outstanding views but requires good shoes, plenty of water and cool weather.

Sunset Vista Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs through the desert to the base of the peak and connects with the summit trails. It is a good alternative for those who want views without the cable sections.

Calloway Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

A trail through the foothills with desert plants and wildflowers in spring, offering a quieter outing than the summit trail.

Visitor Centers

The park visitor center at the entrance has exhibits on the geology, plants and history of the area, including the Civil War battle, a small gift shop, restrooms and staff who can provide trail maps and conditions. Hours vary by season, and the park is closed on Christmas Day and closes early on Thanksgiving, so check the Arizona State Parks website. Entry is charged per vehicle, with a lower fee for individuals and bicycles.

Camping and Lodging

The park campground has tent and RV sites, many with electric hookups, restrooms and showers, and an off-leash dog area for registered campers. Hotels and restaurants are in Eloy, about 20 minutes away, and in Casa Grande, about 30 minutes away, with a wide selection along I-10. Tucson and Phoenix offer the largest range of lodging about 45 minutes to 1.25 hours away. Check each location's website for current availability.
