How to Get There

From Albuquerque (59 mi): Take US-550 N toward San Ysidro, then turn onto NM-4 and follow it through Jemez Springs until reaching the historic site just north of town along the scenic canyon route.

Overview

Jemez Historic Site, formerly Jemez State Monument, preserves the ruins of Giusewa Pueblo and the San José de los Jemez mission church in the red-rock canyon of the Jemez River. Giusewa, meaning "place at the boiling waters" in the Towa language, was a village of about 200 rooms occupied by ancestors of the people of Jemez Pueblo in the 1400s and 1500s. Spanish Franciscans built a massive stone mission church here in 1621 to 1622, and the walls of the church, rising several stories, still stand. The site is part of the New Mexico Historic Sites system. Visitors come to walk among the pueblo and mission ruins, learn about the Jemez people and Spanish colonization, and enjoy a short stop along the scenic Jemez Mountain Trail.

Access

The site is on NM-4 just north of the village of Jemez Springs, about 60 miles northwest of Albuquerque and roughly 1.5 hours from Santa Fe. Nearly all visitors arrive by car on paved roads, taking US-550 to San Ysidro and then NM-4 north through Jemez Pueblo and canyon scenery. Parking is at the visitor center, and the short trail is easy to walk. NM-4 continues north to Valles Caldera National Preserve and Bandelier, making the site a natural stop on a Jemez Mountain loop.

Seasons

The site is open year-round except for Mondays, Tuesdays and major holidays, and spring and fall are the most comfortable times to visit. Summer can be warm in the canyon, with afternoon thunderstorms. Fall brings golden cottonwoods and aspen along the road, and winter is quiet and cold, with snow occasionally closing parts of NM-4. Check the historic sites website for hours and special events before visiting.

Things to Do

Walk Through a 400-Year-Old Mission and Pueblo

Jemez Historic Site is a compact, well-interpreted site where visitors can see how Pueblo and Spanish worlds met. Signs along the trail explain the architecture, daily life and the Pueblo Revolt era.

See the San José de los Jemez Mission Church

The mission church's thick stone walls are the highlight of the site. Standing inside the ruins, visitors can imagine the 17th-century church and the labor of the Jemez people who built it.

Explore Giusewa Pueblo

Excavated rooms and a kiva show how the village was laid out. The site's canyon setting, with red cliffs and a nearby hot springs source, helps explain why it was settled.

Visit the Museum and Visitor Center

A small museum displays artifacts and explains the history of Jemez Pueblo, the Spanish mission and the later history of the canyon.

Enjoy the Jemez Springs Area

The village of Jemez Springs, just south, is known for its hot springs bathhouses, art galleries and cafes, and Soda Dam, Battleship Rock and Jemez Falls are all nearby.

Top Trails

Giusewa Pueblo and Mission Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This self-guided trail loops through the pueblo ruins and past the mission church, with interpretive signs along the way. It takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

Visitor Centers

The Jemez Historic Site Visitor Center and museum is the starting point for the trail and has exhibits on Jemez Pueblo, the Spanish mission and the history of the canyon, along with a small gift shop and restrooms. The site is open Wednesday through Sunday from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. and closed Monday, Tuesday and some holidays, with free admission for children and for New Mexico residents on the first Sunday of each month. For a broader introduction to the Jemez people, the Walatowa Visitor Center in Jemez Pueblo, south of the site on NM-4, provides information on Pueblo culture and events.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. The village of Jemez Springs is only minutes away and has inns, lodges, bed-and-breakfasts, hot springs bathhouses and restaurants. National forest campgrounds in the Jemez Mountains, including Jemez Falls and Redondo, are about 20 to 30 minutes north. Cuba, Rio Rancho and Bernalillo offer additional lodging about an hour away, and Albuquerque, Santa Fe and Los Alamos have the widest selections. Check each property's website for current availability.
