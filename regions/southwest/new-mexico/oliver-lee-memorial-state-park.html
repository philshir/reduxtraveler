How to Get There

From Las Cruces (90 mi): Take US-70 East to Alamogordo, then head south on US-54 for 8 miles, turn left onto Dog Canyon Road and continue 4 miles to the park entrance.

Overview

Oliver Lee Memorial State Park sits at the mouth of Dog Canyon on the western slope of the Sacramento Mountains, where springs create a green oasis against the surrounding Tularosa Basin desert. The canyon was long used by Native Americans, including Apaches, and by settlers and outlaws, and rancher Oliver Lee established a homestead here in the 1880s; his restored ranch house is now part of the park. The park combines frontier history, a campground, wildlife and a steep trail into the mountains. Visitors come to camp under the stars, tour the historic ranch house, hike the Riparian Nature Trail and tackle the Dog Canyon National Recreation Trail, with sweeping views of White Sands and the Tularosa Basin.

Access

The park is about 12 miles south of Alamogordo, reached by car on paved roads: take US-54 south for about 8 miles and turn onto Dog Canyon Road for another 4 miles to the entrance. Alamogordo is about 1 hour 15 minutes from Las Cruces and about 2.5 hours from Albuquerque. White Sands National Park is about 45 minutes away, so many visitors combine them. There is no public transportation, and the park is quiet with few services nearby, so bring supplies.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with October through April the most pleasant time to visit, when the desert is mild. Summers are hot in the lowlands, though the canyon's springs and shade provide relief. Fall is popular for camping and stargazing under the clear basin sky, and spring brings wildflowers. Winters are cool, and snow sometimes dusts the high Sacramento Mountains above. Monsoon storms in summer can cause flash floods in Dog Canyon, and the steep trail is best avoided in hot weather.

Things to Do

Visit a Desert Oasis at Dog Canyon

Oliver Lee Memorial State Park is a green canyon mouth with springs, cottonwoods and wildlife between desert and mountains. It offers camping, picnicking and short nature walks within sight of the Tularosa Basin.

Tour the Oliver Lee Ranch House

The restored ranch house offers a window into life in the late 1800s. Guided tours are offered on a limited schedule, so call ahead to confirm availability.

Hike the Dog Canyon National Recreation Trail

This steep trail climbs from the park high into the Sacramento Mountains, gaining more than 3,000 feet in about 5.5 miles. It is a serious hike with big views of the basin, best for experienced hikers in cooler seasons.

Walk the Riparian Nature Trail

A short interpretive trail follows the stream near the campground, with signs about the plants and wildlife of the canyon spring.

Camp and Stargaze

The campground and open basin skies make the park a good place for astronomy, and White Sands National Park is within a short drive.

Top Trails

Riparian Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy walk follows the spring-fed stream through cottonwoods and wetland plants. It is a pleasant option for families and birdwatchers.

Dog Canyon National Recreation Trail

Length: Approximately 5.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: More than 3,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

This demanding trail climbs steeply through the canyon to viewpoints high on the mountainside. Carry plenty of water, start early and turn around before conditions become hot.

Visitor Centers

The Oliver Lee Memorial State Park Visitor Center has exhibits on Dog Canyon's natural and cultural history, the Lee family and the ranch house, as well as maps, restrooms and information on trails and camping. Rangers can help schedule ranch house tours and recommend hikes. Hours vary, so call the park or check the New Mexico State Parks website for current times and tour availability.

Camping and Lodging

The park campground has 44 sites, some with water and electric hookups, as well as restrooms, showers and picnic shelters. Reservations are recommended on weekends and in the cooler seasons. There is no lodging in the park. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Alamogordo, about 15 to 20 minutes away, and the mountain village of Cloudcroft, about 45 minutes up the Sacramento Mountains, offers lodges and cabins, including a historic resort. Las Cruces is about 1.25 hours west. Check each property's website for current availability.
