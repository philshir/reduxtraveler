How to Get There

From El Paso, TX (45 mi): Take I-10 W from El Paso and continue for about 45 miles, then take the exits into the city center along Main St or Avenida de Mesilla.

Overview

Las Cruces is the second-largest city in New Mexico, set in the Mesilla Valley along the Rio Grande at the foot of the jagged Organ Mountains. It is known for its desert scenery, its New Mexican chile and farmland, its historic neighbor Mesilla, and as the home of New Mexico State University. With a warm, sunny climate and a relaxed pace, it is the principal city of southern New Mexico and a convenient base for exploring the region.

Visitors come to Las Cruces for its mix of Hispanic, Native American, and Anglo heritage, its farmers market, its wineries and green and red chile dishes, and the dramatic landscapes nearby. The Organ Mountains–Desert Peaks National Monument is on the city’s doorstep, White Sands National Park is about an hour’s drive east, and the growing community of Spaceport America lies to the north.

The Las Cruces area was a stop on the El Camino Real, the royal road connecting Mexico City with Santa Fe, and its name, "the crosses," is said to refer to crosses marking graves along the trail. The city was formally founded in 1849 after the Mexican–American War, and in 1853 the Gadsden Purchase placed the area firmly within the United States, with the nearby town of Mesilla serving as a key stagecoach stop and briefly as a Confederate territorial capital. The railroad arrived in 1881, and the founding of the agricultural college that became New Mexico State University in 1888 helped drive steady growth.

Historic Mesilla Plaza is the city’s signature landmark, an adobe-lined square with shops, restaurants, and the San Albino Basilica, where Billy the Kid was tried in 1881. Beyond town, the jagged peaks of the Organ Mountains, the trails of Dripping Springs Natural Area, and the sweeping views from the nearby overlooks draw photographers and hikers. The Organ Mountains–Desert Peaks National Monument protects nearly 500,000 acres of desert and mountain terrain.

Las Cruces has a strong collection of small museums. The Branigan Cultural Center and the Las Cruces Museum of Art present local history and the visual arts, the Museum of Nature & Science highlights regional natural history, and the Las Cruces Railroad Museum occupies the former Santa Fe depot. The New Mexico Farm & Ranch Heritage Museum traces the state’s agricultural past with living-history demonstrations and working animals.

The city has many parks and open spaces, from the cottonwood corridors of the Mesilla Valley Bosque State Park along the Rio Grande to neighborhood greens such as Young Park and La Llorona Park. The national monument adds a vast network of desert trails, and the nearby Rio Grande provides birdwatching and quiet walking paths just minutes from downtown.

Las Cruces has a warm, dry desert climate with abundant sunshine. Summers are hot, with highs often in the upper 90s °F and a monsoon season of brief afternoon storms from July to September. Winters are mild and sunny, with daytime highs in the 50s °F, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking and sightseeing. Annual rainfall is low, usually under 10 inches.
