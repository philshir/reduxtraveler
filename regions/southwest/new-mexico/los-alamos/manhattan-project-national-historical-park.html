How to Get There

Access varies by location: The park is spread across three separate regions—Los Alamos (New Mexico), Oak Ridge (Tennessee), and Hanford (Washington). Visitors typically begin at a local visitor center, then explore nearby historic sites, museums, and guided tour locations within each community.

Overview

Manhattan Project National Historical Park preserves places tied to the secret World War II effort to build the first atomic weapons, and is unusual in that it spans three communities: Los Alamos, New Mexico; Oak Ridge, Tennessee; and Hanford, Washington. At Los Alamos, scientists led by J. Robert Oppenheimer designed and built the bombs on a remote mesa; at Oak Ridge, workers enriched uranium; and at Hanford, the B Reactor produced plutonium. The park was established in 2015 as a partnership of the National Park Service and the Department of Energy to tell the scientific, military, social and ethical story of the project. Visitors come to see historic buildings and sites, learn about the secret wartime cities and reflect on the lasting impact of nuclear technology.

Access

The New Mexico portion of the park is in the town of Los Alamos, on a mesa in the Jemez Mountains about 35 miles northwest of Santa Fe and about 1.5 hours from Albuquerque. Visitors arrive by car, following US-84/285 north from Santa Fe and then NM-502 up the mesa. Most of the sites in Los Alamos are in or near the town's historic district and can be visited on foot, while some of the original Manhattan Project sites, such as V-Site and Gun Site, are on restricted land and are accessible only on scheduled tours. The Oak Ridge and Hanford units are separate and are reached on their own, so plan each as its own trip.

Seasons

Los Alamos can be visited year-round, and spring through fall is the easiest time for walking tours and day trips. Winter can bring snow and cold to the mesa, and NM-502 and the roads in the area occasionally see delays. Some special tours, including those of restricted sites, are offered on limited dates, so advance reservations are important. Hanford B Reactor tours run mostly from spring through fall and sell out quickly, while Oak Ridge sites are open much of the year.

Things to Do

Discover the Birthplace of the Atomic Age

The Los Alamos unit of the park brings together the places where the atomic bombs were designed and assembled. The town's historic district preserves buildings from the secret wartime laboratory, giving visitors a close look at the people and homes of the Manhattan Project.

Tour the Los Alamos Historic District

The district includes Fuller Lodge, Ashley Pond, the Bathtub Row homes where scientists lived, and the Los Alamos Ranch School buildings that were taken over by the project. A walking tour is the best way to see the key stops and learn about daily life on "the Hill."

Visit the Museums and Visitor Center

The Los Alamos Visitor Center, the Los Alamos History Museum and the Bradbury Science Museum help visitors understand the science, the secrecy and the legacy of the project. Exhibits include artifacts, photographs and replicas of the devices that were developed here.

Take a Guided Tour of Restricted Sites

Limited tours offered through park partners can include sites such as V-Site and the Gun Site, where bomb components were assembled and tested. These tours require advance registration and have limited availability.

Explore the Park's Other Units

Visitors who can travel beyond New Mexico can see the Graphite Reactor at Oak Ridge, Tennessee, and the B Reactor at Hanford, Washington, to complete the story of the Manhattan Project.

Top Trails

Los Alamos Historic District Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 to 2 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This self-guided walk links Fuller Lodge, Ashley Pond, Bathtub Row and other historic buildings in the center of Los Alamos. It is suitable for all ages and can be combined with a stop at the museums.

Visitor Centers

The park's Los Alamos Visitor Center is the starting point for the New Mexico unit and provides exhibits, maps, a bookstore and information on walking tours and special programs. The Oak Ridge and Hanford units have their own visitor facilities with separate schedules. Hours vary by location and season, so check the National Park Service website before planning a visit. Park access is free, though some partner museums and tours may charge separate fees.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the park. Hotels, restaurants and shops are in Los Alamos itself, and a wider range of lodging is available in Santa Fe, about 45 minutes away, and Española, about 30 minutes away. Camping is available at Bandelier National Monument's Juniper Campground and at national forest campgrounds in the Jemez Mountains, both within about an hour. Check each property's website for current availability.
