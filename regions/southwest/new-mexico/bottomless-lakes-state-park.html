How to Get There

From Roswell (15 mi): Take US-380 East for 12 miles, then turn south onto NM-409 and continue 3 miles to the park entrance.

Overview

Bottomless Lakes State Park was New Mexico's first state park, established in 1933, and protects a chain of deep, spring-fed sinkhole lakes along the Pecos River escarpment in the southeastern part of the state. The lakes formed when underground gypsum and limestone caverns dissolved and collapsed, and their deep blue-green water, rimmed by red bluffs, gave rise to the legend that they were bottomless, although they are actually 17 to 90 feet deep. Civilian Conservation Corps crews developed many of the park's early facilities. The park is popular as a cool, scenic refuge in the Chihuahuan Desert, with swimming at Lea Lake, camping, hiking, birdwatching and scuba diving close to Roswell.

Access

The park is about 15 miles southeast of Roswell, reached by car on paved roads: take US-380 east for about 12 miles and turn south on NM-409 for about 3 miles to the entrance. Roswell, with its small airport and highway connections, is the usual gateway, and Carlsbad is about 1.25 hours south. A park road connects the lakes, with pull-offs and trailheads at several of them, so visitors can drive between swimming, picnic and camping areas. There is no public transportation to the park.

Seasons

The park is open year-round and is busiest from late spring through summer, when Lea Lake is the main swimming destination and a swimming beach is staffed when conditions allow. Summer days are hot, but the lakes are cool and shady spots are welcome. Spring and fall bring comfortable temperatures for hiking and birdwatching, and migrating birds, including sandhill cranes nearby, make fall and winter good for wildlife viewing. Winters are mild but can be cold and windy, and some facilities, including the swimming areas, are closed in the off season.

Things to Do

Explore One of New Mexico's Most Unusual Natural Landscapes

Bottomless Lakes State Park combines turquoise sinkhole lakes, red bluffs and desert grassland in an area that otherwise appears flat and dry. The lakes are linked to the Pecos River valley's geology and attract an unusual mix of wildlife.

Swim and Paddle at Lea Lake

Lea Lake is the park's main recreation lake, with a swimming area, picnic shelters and a campground nearby. Swimming and paddling are the most popular summer activities, and the water stays cool even in the hottest weather.

Go Scuba Diving in a Desert Sinkhole

Certified divers can explore some of the deeper sinkholes in a rare landlocked scuba experience. Permits and rules apply, so check with the park before planning a dive.

Hike and Watch Wildlife

The park has more than four miles of trails through bluffs and wetlands with views over the lakes. Visitors may see roadrunners, deer, herons and the rare Pecos sunflower.

Picnic and Camp Under Dark Skies

Picnic shelters and campgrounds make the park a good base for a relaxed weekend, and the dark southeastern New Mexico sky is excellent for stargazing.

Top Trails

Bluff Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile
Elevation Gain: Minimal to moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This trail follows the bluff above the lakes and offers some of the best views of the turquoise water and surrounding desert. Interpretive signs describe the geology and wildlife.

Wetlands Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This flat trail passes through marshy areas and reeds near one of the lakes. It is a good choice for birdwatching and for visitors with children.

Visitor Centers

The Bottomless Lakes State Park Visitor Center has exhibits on the geology, history and wildlife of the lakes, as well as maps, restrooms and information on trails, camping and diving. Staff can advise on swimming conditions and seasonal closures. The park is open daily from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. with a last entry time in the afternoon, and visitor center hours vary by season, so check the New Mexico State Parks website for current times.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a developed campground at Lea Lake with sites that include water and electric hookups, as well as more primitive camping at the Lower Lakes. There is no lodging in the park. Hotels, motels, restaurants and shops are plentiful in Roswell, about 20 minutes away, and the city's UFO-themed attractions make it a popular overnight stop. Artesia and Carlsbad, about an hour to 1.25 hours south, offer more lodging, and Brantley Lake State Park near Carlsbad has additional camping. Check each location's website for current availability.
