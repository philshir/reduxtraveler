Eldorado Canyon State Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Colorado
  3. Eldorado Canyon State Park

Eldorado Canyon State Park
Jeffrey Beall, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Steep sandstone canyon walls rising above a rushing creek create a dramatic landscape known for world-class rock climbing, scenic hiking trails, and panoramic foothill views.

General Information
Hours:
Daily: Sunrise to sunset
Visitor Center: 9am to 4pm (closed Thursdays)
Timed entry reservations required for vehicles on weekends and holidays from May through early October
Fees:
Vehicle Pass: $10–$15
Individual (walk-in/bike-in): $4
Pet Policy:
Dogs allowed
Seasons:
All year; best in spring through fall for climbing and hiking, quieter in winter
Location:
9 Kneale Rd, Eldorado Springs, CO 80025
Website:
cpw.state.co.us/state-parks/eldorado-canyon-state-park

How to Get There

From Boulder (5 mi): Take CO-93 south from downtown, turn right onto CO-170 west, and follow it through Eldorado Springs to the end of the road where the park entrance is located.

Overview

Eldorado Canyon State Park is a dramatic gorge of golden sandstone cliffs that rise up to 850 feet above South Boulder Creek, just south of Boulder at the edge of the Front Range foothills. The canyon is one of the best rock climbing areas in the country, known for classic routes such as the Bastille and the Naked Edge, and it is also a beautiful place for hiking, picnicking, fishing and wildlife watching. Opened as a state park in 1978, it protects a landscape with a long history as a gateway to the high country, once home to a railroad and a resort. Visitors come for the cliffs, the creek and the easy escape from the city into a rugged mountain setting.

Access

The park is at the end of CO-170 in Eldorado Springs, about 5 miles south of Boulder and about 30 miles northwest of Denver. Most visitors drive from Boulder via CO-93 and CO-170, a paved route that takes about 15 minutes. The park road is narrow and parking is limited, and timed vehicle entry reservations are required on weekends and holidays from May through early October. Public transportation does not reach the park, and cyclists and walkers can enter for a lower fee.

Seasons

The park is open year-round. Spring and fall are the best times to visit, with mild temperatures that are ideal for climbing and hiking, and fall brings golden cottonwood leaves along the creek. Summer can be hot and busy, with afternoon thunderstorms, and the timed-entry system helps manage crowds. Winter is quieter and usually mild enough for hiking, though ice can make trails and cliffs hazardous and the road may be slippery. Visit midweek or early in the day to avoid peak crowds.

Things to Do

Gaze Up at Towering Sandstone Cliffs

Eldorado Canyon State Park is framed by walls of tan and orange rock that tower over the creek. Even visitors who do not climb enjoy watching climbers on the faces.

Watch or Try Rock Climbing

The canyon is famous for world-class traditional climbing on the Redgarden Wall, Bastille and Naked Edge. Climbers of all levels come, and the park has information on routes and safety.

Hike the Canyon Trails

Trails lead from the canyon floor to overlooks, historic sites and the high country. Options range from easy riverside strolls to steep climbs.

Picnic and Fish Along South Boulder Creek

Picnic areas along the creek offer shade and the sound of running water. Anglers can fish for trout in season.

Look for Wildlife and Wildflowers

Deer, raptors, foxes and birds live in the canyon, and spring and summer bring wildflowers. Peregrine falcons may nest on the cliffs, and some areas close seasonally to protect them.

Top Trails

Fowler Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This wide, mostly level trail hugs the canyon wall above the creek and offers close-up views of climbers and cliffs. It is a good choice for families and for those who want scenic views without a big climb.

Rattlesnake Gulch Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 800 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs past the ruins of an old hotel to views over the canyon and the plains. It is steeper than the Fowler Trail and rewards effort with sweeping views.

Streamside Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path follows South Boulder Creek through the heart of the canyon. It is a quiet, shaded stroll.

Visitor Centers

The Eldorado Canyon State Park Visitor Center, near the park entrance, has exhibits on the canyon's geology, history and climbing heritage, restrooms, maps and rangers who can answer questions and provide current conditions. The center is typically open daily during the day but may be closed one day a week, so check the Colorado Parks and Wildlife website before going. Staff can also explain the timed-entry reservation system.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Camping is available at nearby Golden Gate Canyon State Park, about an hour away, and in Roosevelt National Forest campgrounds in the foothills. Boulder, 15 minutes away, has a wide range of hotels, including the historic Hotel Boulderado, the St Julien Hotel and Spa and many chain hotels. Louisville and Superior are about 20 minutes away with additional options, and Denver, about 45 minutes away, has the widest selection, including the Brown Palace and the Crawford Hotel at Union Station. Check each property's website for current availability.
