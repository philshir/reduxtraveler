Hartman Rocks Recreation Area

★5.0

How to Get There

From Montrose (65 mi): Take US-50 E toward Gunnison, turn right onto Gold Basin Road just before entering town, and follow signs to the Hartman Rocks Base Area.

Overview

Hartman Rocks Recreation Area is a Bureau of Land Management area just south of Gunnison, known for its granite domes, boulder fields and more than 70 miles of trails across rolling sagebrush hills. The terrain is a classic Gunnison Valley landscape, with views of the Sawatch and West Elk ranges, and it has become one of Colorado's top mountain biking destinations. The area also draws hikers, trail runners, rock climbers, horseback riders and campers. Visitors come for a free, uncrowded outdoor playground that is open much of the year, thanks to the valley's sunshine and low snowfall.

Access

The recreation area is about 3 miles southwest of Gunnison, reached by driving through town to Gold Basin Road and following signs to the Hartman Rocks base area. Most visitors arrive by car or bike, and Gunnison is about an hour and a half from Montrose and about 3.5 hours from Denver. Trailheads are along Gold Basin Road, some of which is unpaved but generally passable. Mountain bikers often ride directly from town on connecting trails.

Seasons

The area is open year-round and is among the best in the Gunnison Valley for shoulder-season riding. Late spring through fall is the prime season, with warm days and cool nights. Summer afternoons bring thunderstorms, and trails can become slick when wet. Winter brings snow and cold temperatures, and many people switch to fat biking, snowshoeing or cross-country skiing. Muddy trails in spring should be avoided to prevent damage.

Things to Do

Ride Mountain Bike Trails

Hartman Rocks has trails for every level, from smooth beginner loops to technical rocky routes. The mix of granite slabs and flowing singletrack is a draw for riders.

Hike and Trail Run Among the Boulders

The area has many hiking routes that wind through granite domes and sagebrush with views of the surrounding mountains. They are quiet and good for sunset walks.

Climb the Granite

Boulders and cliffs offer bouldering and climbing opportunities for different levels. Climbers should bring their own gear and follow local ethics.

Camp for Free in the Hills

Dispersed camping is available with a stay limit and a Leave No Trace approach. The setting under open skies is excellent for stargazing.

Visit Gunnison

The town of Gunnison has restaurants, shops, the Western State Colorado University campus and nearby Curecanti National Recreation Area. It is a convenient place to resupply.

Top Trails

Hartman Rocks Beginner and Intermediate Loops

Length: Varies; loops of about 3 to 10 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Several loops near the main trailhead offer flowing singletrack and views of the valley. They are good for riders and hikers who want to explore without a big climb.

Hartman Rocks Granite Dome Routes

Length: Varies; routes of about 4 to 12 miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

Trails in the upper part of the area climb across granite domes and boulder fields with far-reaching views. They are more technical and best for experienced riders and fit hikers.

Visitor Centers

Hartman Rocks Recreation Area does not have a visitor center. Information kiosks at the main trailhead and along Gold Basin Road provide maps, rules and trail descriptions, and the Bureau of Land Management website lists current conditions. Local bike shops and the Gunnison visitor center in town can offer maps, advice on trails and rentals.

Camping and Lodging

Dispersed camping is allowed in designated areas within the recreation area, with no fees, no hookups and a stay limit, so bring water and pack out everything. There is no lodging on site. Gunnison, only 3 miles away, has chain hotels, local motels and inns, and Crested Butte, about 45 minutes north, offers lodges, condos and bed-and-breakfasts. Montrose, about an hour and a half west, has a wide range of hotels, and Lake City has cabins and small inns. Developed campgrounds are available at Curecanti National Recreation Area and nearby national forests. Check each property's website for current availability.
