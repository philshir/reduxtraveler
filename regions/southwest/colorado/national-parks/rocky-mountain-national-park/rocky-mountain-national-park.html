How to Get There

From Estes Park (4 mi): Follow US-36 W through Estes Park to the Beaver Meadows entrance, the main gateway to the park's east side and Trail Ridge Road; the west side is entered near the town of Grand Lake via US-34 from Granby.

Overview

Rocky Mountain National Park in north-central Colorado protects 415 square miles of the Front Range, with soaring peaks, glacial valleys, alpine lakes, and some of the most accessible high-country scenery in the country. The park has more than 60 peaks over 12,000 feet, including Longs Peak at 14,259 feet, and the Continental Divide runs through its center. Its signature attraction is Trail Ridge Road, one of the highest continuous paved roads in the United States, which climbs above the treeline to 12,183 feet and crosses the park from Estes Park to Grand Lake.

The park was established in 1915, thanks in large part to the efforts of naturalist Enos Mills, and it is one of the most visited national parks. People come for the alpine tundra drive, the lakes and waterfalls reached by short trails, the chance to see elk, moose, and bighorn sheep, and challenging climbs such as Longs Peak. The park is only about 1.5 to 2 hours from Denver, which makes it popular for both day trips and longer vacations.

Park Sections

The park is usually divided into an east side and a west side, connected by Trail Ridge Road. On the east side, entered through Estes Park by the Beaver Meadows and Fall River entrances, are Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park, Sheep Lakes, and the busy Bear Lake Road corridor leading to Bear Lake, Glacier Gorge, and Sprague Lake. The Wild Basin area in the southeast and the Longs Peak area are reached from separate entrances off Highway 7. On the west side, the Grand Lake entrance leads to the Kawuneeche Valley, a quieter area along the Colorado River headwaters, near the town of Grand Lake.

Sightseeing and Exploring the Park

Trail Ridge Road is the park's great scenic drive, 48 miles from Estes Park to Grand Lake, with about 11 miles above treeline. Popular pull-offs include Many Parks Curve, Rainbow Curve, Forest Canyon Overlook, Rock Cut, and the Alpine Visitor Center at 11,796 feet. Open in summer, the one-way gravel Old Fall River Road climbs to the Alpine Visitor Center along a narrow, winding route. Other scenic areas include Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park, and the historic Holzwarth Historic Site on the west side.

Hiking

Rocky Mountain has about 355 miles of trails for every level. Easy options include the Bear Lake loop (0.6 miles), Sprague Lake, and the short walk to Lily Lake. The moderate hike from Bear Lake to Alberta Falls and on to Emerald Lake is the most popular, about 3.6 miles round trip to Emerald Lake. Other well-known trails lead to Calypso Cascades and Bluebird Lake in Wild Basin and to Chasm Lake on Longs Peak. The Glacier Gorge Trail reaches several lakes. The climb of Longs Peak is a long, strenuous 14.5-mile round trip that requires an alpine start, and it is dangerous in bad weather. Altitude and afternoon thunderstorms are the main hazards.

Other Activities

Beyond sightseeing and hiking, the park is known for wildlife viewing, fly fishing in its streams and lakes, horseback riding from concession stables, and rock and mountain climbing, including the East Face of Longs Peak and the cliffs of Lumpy Ridge. Ranger programs, guided hikes, and evening talks are offered in summer. In winter, snowshoeing and cross-country skiing are popular on the east and west sides. Estes Park outfitters offer guided climbing, fishing, and jeep tours, and the Stanley Hotel and the town's shops and restaurants are a short drive away. Backpacking and backcountry camping require a permit.

Flora and Wildlife

The park is a favorite for wildlife viewing. Elk are common in Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park, especially during the fall rut from September to October, when bulls bugle. Moose live in the Kawuneeche Valley on the west side, and bighorn sheep are often seen at Sheep Lakes and along Trail Ridge Road. Yellow-bellied marmots, pikas, mule deer, and black bears also live in the park. The alpine tundra shows wildflowers in July and August, and the aspens turn gold in late September. Always keep your distance from wildlife; viewing is on your own, with ranger programs in season.

Access

The park is reached by car, with the east side entrances at Estes Park, about 70 miles northwest of Denver (roughly 1.5 to 2 hours) and the west side entrance at Grand Lake, on US-34. Denver International Airport is the closest major airport, about 75 miles from Estes Park, and Boulder is about 35 miles away. Trail Ridge Road connects the two sides but is closed in winter, which means that driving from one side to the other takes about 2.5 hours in winter by way of I-70. A timed-entry reservation system may be required in peak season, so check the National Park Service website. Free summer shuttles also serve the Bear Lake corridor.

Seasons

Summer is the busiest season, with Trail Ridge Road usually open from late May through mid-October, wildflowers in the high country, and frequent afternoon thunderstorms. Fall brings golden aspens and the elk rut, and is considered one of the best times to visit. Winter is cold and snowy: Trail Ridge Road closes, Bear Lake Road stays open for snowshoeing and skiing, and winds can be strong. Spring is a time of mud and snow, with the high country still closed and elk calving in May and June. Old Fall River Road usually opens in July and closes in early fall.

Camping and Lodging

The park has five campgrounds, including Moraine Park, Glacier Basin, Aspenglen, and Longs Peak on the east side and Timber Creek on the west, and most require reservations in summer. Backcountry camping requires a permit. There is no hotel inside the park. Estes Park, just outside the east entrance, has the most lodging, including the historic Stanley Hotel, the Estes Park Resort, and the YMCA of the Rockies, as well as many cabins, motels, and vacation rentals. Grand Lake and Granby serve the west side, and Boulder and Denver, each about 1.5 to 2 hours away, offer a wide range of hotels.

Visitor Centers

The park has several visitor centers. The Beaver Meadows Visitor Center on the east side is the main visitor center, with exhibits, a bookstore, and trip planning. The Fall River Visitor Center has a gift shop and exhibits, and the Alpine Visitor Center on Trail Ridge Road is open in summer. On the west side, the Kawuneeche Visitor Center near Grand Lake serves that entrance, and the Moraine Park Discovery Center is open seasonally. Hours vary by season, so check the National Park Service website before you go.

Shuttle System

Rocky Mountain National Park runs a free shuttle in summer to reduce traffic on busy Bear Lake Road. The Bear Lake Road Shuttle connects the Park & Ride lot with Bear Lake and nearby trailheads, and the Moraine Park Shuttle serves the Moraine Park area. A separate Hiker Shuttle, run from Estes Park, takes visitors from the Estes Park Visitor Center to the park. Using these shuttles avoids the parking problems at the most popular trailheads. Check the National Park Service website for the current routes, schedule, and any reservation requirements.

Highlights

  • Trail Ridge Road and the Alpine Visitor Center
  • Bear Lake and Hallett Peak
  • Grand Lake: the west-side town
  • Wildlife: elk (especially in the fall rut), moose in Kawuneeche Valley, bighorn sheep, and marmots

Good to Know

  • Timed entry: a reservation is required in peak season (late May to mid-October), so check the park website
  • Weather: afternoon thunderstorms are common above treeline; start early
