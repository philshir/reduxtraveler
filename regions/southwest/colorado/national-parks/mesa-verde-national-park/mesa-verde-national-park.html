How to Get There

From Page, AZ (225 mi): Follow AZ-98 E to US-160 E in Navajo County. Follow US-160 E to Mesa Top Ruins Rd in Montezuma County. Take the exit toward Mesa Verde National Park from US-160 E. Turn right onto Mesa Top Ruins Rd.

Overview

Mesa Verde National Park in southwestern Colorado protects the largest and best-preserved collection of Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings in the United States. More than 5,000 archaeological sites, including about 600 cliff dwellings, are found on the green mesa tops and in the canyon alcoves. The Ancestral Puebloan people lived here for more than 700 years, from about 550 to 1300 AD, building pit houses, pueblos, and the stone villages tucked beneath the cliffs. The park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, designated in 1978, and is the only national park created to preserve the works of people.

Mesa Verde, Spanish for "green table," was established as a national park in 1906 by President Theodore Roosevelt. Rancher Richard Wetherill and his brother-in-law Charlie Mason found Cliff Palace in 1888, which drew attention to the area. People visit to tour the dwellings with rangers, climb ladders into Balcony House, look into Spruce Tree House, drive the mesa-top loops, and learn about the people who lived here and the descendants who still live in the Southwest.

Park Sections

The park's main road leaves US-160, about 10 miles east of Cortez and 7 miles from Mancos, and climbs the mesa to the Far View area, about 15 miles in. From there, the road splits. Chapin Mesa, about 6 miles farther, is the park's most visited area and has Spruce Tree House, the Chapin Mesa Archeological Museum, Cliff Palace, Balcony House, and the Mesa Top and Cliff Palace loop roads. Wetherill Mesa, 12 miles to the west of Far View on a narrow road, is quieter and includes Long House and Step House, and is open only in summer. Allow about 45 minutes to drive from the entrance to Chapin Mesa.

Sightseeing and Exploring the Park

The best way to see the dwellings is on the two loop drives on Chapin Mesa. The 6-mile Mesa Top Loop Road passes about a dozen overlooks and sites showing the development of the Ancestral Puebloan culture, from pit houses to cliff dwellings, including Square Tower House and Sun Point Overlook. The Cliff Palace Loop Road gives views of Cliff Palace and Balcony House from across the canyon. The Far View Sites Complex, near the lodge, includes mesa-top villages. Several overlooks along the entrance road have views across the Montezuma Valley and toward the La Plata and San Juan Mountains. The roads are paved, but RVs and trailers are restricted on some stretches.

Hiking

Mesa Verde has fewer trails than most national parks, because of the fragile archaeological sites, but they are rewarding. The Petroglyph Point Trail (2.4 miles round trip) leads to one of the largest rock art panels in the park, and the Spruce Canyon Trail is a similar length. The Soda Canyon Overlook Trail (about 1.2 miles round trip) is an easy walk to an overlook of Balcony House. The Prater Ridge Trail, the Knife Edge Trail, and the Point Lookout Trail near Morefield Campground offer longer walks with views. Most trails require registration, and leaving marked trails or entering sites without permission is not allowed.

Other Activities

The main activities in the park are the ranger-led cliff dwelling tours. Cliff Palace, Balcony House, and Long House are open by guided tour only, usually from spring to fall, with tickets reserved on Recreation.gov, and you should plan ahead. The tours involve ladders and tunnels in places. Rangers also give evening programs at Morefield Campground, and the park offers stargazing, bird walks, and winter snowshoe walks. Nearby Hovenweep, Canyons of the Ancients, and the Cortez Cultural Center are good additions. There are no commercial outfitters inside the park, though the Far View Lodge offers a restaurant and a lodge.

Flora and Wildlife

Mesa Verde is not known for a single plant or animal, but its mesa tops hold a pinyon-juniper woodland mixed with Gambel oak, Utah serviceberry, and sagebrush, and the canyons have yucca, mountain mahogany, and wildflowers in spring and early summer. Fires have burned large parts of the park in the last decades, and the burned areas are recovering with grasses and wildflowers. Wildlife includes mule deer, wild turkeys, coyotes, black bears, mountain lions, and a number of birds such as peregrine falcons and ravens. Deer and turkeys are often seen near the roads and Morefield Campground. Viewing is on your own.

Access

Mesa Verde is in the Four Corners region and is reached by car on US-160. Cortez is about 10 miles from the entrance (15 minutes), Mancos about 7 miles, and Durango about 35 miles (roughly 45 minutes to 1 hour). Farmington, New Mexico, is about 1 hour away, Albuquerque about 5 hours, and Denver about 7 hours. The closest commercial airports are Durango-La Plata County Airport and Cortez Municipal Airport. There is no public transportation to the park. The park road is long and winding, so allow plenty of time to reach the tour meeting points.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, but the busiest season is late spring through early fall, when all tours are operating. Summer is warm and busy, with afternoon thunderstorms. Spring and fall are milder, and fall has fewer crowds. Cliff dwelling tours usually run from about April through October, depending on the site, and Wetherill Mesa opens in late May and closes in the fall. In winter, the park receives snow, and many facilities and tours are closed, though Chapin Mesa and its museum remain open and the cliff dwellings look dramatic under snow. Far View Lodge and Morefield Campground are seasonal.

Camping and Lodging

Morefield Campground, about 4 miles inside the entrance, has several hundred tent and RV sites, a camper store, showers, and a gas station, and is open from spring to fall. Far View Lodge, near the Far View area, offers rooms with views across the valley and is open seasonally. Outside the park, Cortez has the most hotels and motels, including chain hotels, and Mancos has smaller inns, cabins, and a few campgrounds. Durango, about 45 minutes away, offers more choices, from the historic Strater Hotel to many chain properties, and has the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad.

Visitor Centers

The Mesa Verde Visitor and Research Center, near the park entrance, is the starting point for most visitors and is where you buy tour tickets, pick up information, and see exhibits. The Far View Visitor Center, open seasonally on the mesa, provides information. On Chapin Mesa, the Chapin Mesa Archeological Museum is open year-round and shows artifacts and exhibits about Ancestral Puebloan life, and nearby are the trailhead for Spruce Tree House and a gift shop. Hours vary by season, so check the National Park Service website.

Highlights

  • Cliff Palace: the largest cliff dwelling in North America, with about 150 rooms (ranger-guided tour only)
  • Balcony House and Spruce Tree House: tours and, at times, self-guided visits; Balcony House requires ladders
  • Chapin Mesa: the Mesa Top Loop Road with overlooks and the archaeological museum
  • Wetherill Mesa: a quieter area, including Long House, open in summer
  • Tickets: buy tour tickets in advance on Recreation.gov, and allow time for the winding 45-minute drive to the mesa

Reservation Requirements

Question Answer
Are reservations required? Yes, for all ranger-led cliff dwelling tours except Step House.
Where are reservations made? Recreation.gov or by phone through the National Park Service reservation system.
When do reservations open? 14 days in advance at 8:00 AM MDT.
Do tours sell out? Frequently, especially during summer and holiday periods.

Reservations are highly recommended because tour capacities are limited and popular tours often sell out shortly after becoming available. Visitors receive digital confirmation and must present their reservation before joining a tour.

Cliff Palace Tour

The Cliff Palace Tour is the most popular ranger-led experience in the park. Built between 1190 and 1280 CE, the site was once home to more than 100 people and remains one of the most impressive examples of Ancestral Puebloan architecture. During the approximately 45-minute guided tour, visitors descend uneven stone steps, climb four ladders, and explore portions of the dwelling while learning about its history and construction.

  • Duration: Approximately 45 minutes
  • Walking Distance: About 0.25 mile
  • Elevation Change: Approximately 100 feet
  • Ladders: Four ladders must be climbed

Planning Tips

  • Reserve as soon as tickets become available.
  • Allow extra travel time inside the park. Tour sites may require 55 to 90 minutes of driving from the park entrance.
  • Carry water, sun protection, and sturdy footwear.
  • Download or screenshot reservations before arrival. Cellular service is extremely limited.
  • Leave sufficient time between multiple tours.
