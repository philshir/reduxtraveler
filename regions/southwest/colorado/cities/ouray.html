How to Get There

From Grand Junction (96 miles): Follow I-70BL E and 29 Rd to US-50 E in Orchard Mesa. Use any lane to turn left onto US-50 E. Continue on G50 Rd. Take G Rd to US-50 E/S Main St in Delta. Follow US-50 E to US-550 S in Ouray.

Overview

Ouray, nicknamed "the Switzerland of America," is a small town in a narrow valley ringed by peaks of the San Juan Mountains, at 7,792 feet. It is known for its hot springs, its Victorian Main Street, ice climbing, jeep roads, and the dramatic drive over the Million Dollar Highway.

Visitors come to soak in the hot springs pools, hike to waterfalls, take jeep tours into the high country, and climb frozen waterfalls in winter. Ouray is also a base for exploring Telluride, Silverton, and the Uncompahgre Gorge, and it has a lively dining and lodging scene for a town of about 1,000 residents.

Ouray was founded in 1876 when prospectors discovered silver and gold in the surrounding mountains, and the town is named for Chief Ouray of the Uncompahgre Ute. At one point, it had more horses and mules than people. Mining brought rail and wealth, including the Camp Bird Mine, once among the top gold producers in the state, and Main Street was lined with hotels and saloons in the 1880s. When mining declined, the town turned to tourism, and Main Street is now a National Historic District.

The Million Dollar Highway, US 550 between Ouray and Silverton, is one of the most dramatic mountain drives in the country, with steep drop-offs and no guardrails along cliffs. Box Canyon Falls, a waterfall in a narrow slot canyon in town, and the Perimeter Trail loop around the valley are popular walks, and the 4WD routes over Imogene Pass and Yankee Boy Basin reach alpine meadows.

The Ouray County Museum, in the former 1887 hospital, covers the area's mining, ranching, and Ute history. The Ouray Hot Springs Pool, built in the 1920s and now a large complex of soaking pools, and the Beaumont Hotel and other Victorian buildings on Main Street preserve the town's past, and the Ouray Ice Festival each January celebrates its status as an ice-climbing capital.

The Ouray Ice Park, the world's first public ice climbing park, fills the Uncompahgre Gorge with frozen waterfalls each winter, and in summer the Ouray Perimeter Trail, Box Canyon Falls Park, and the Bear Creek and Blue Lakes trails offer outings. Mount Sneffels Wilderness, with its 14,000-foot peak, lies just to the west.

Ouray has a mild summer and a cold winter. Summer highs are in the 70s and 80s °F with cool nights and afternoon thunderstorms, autumn brings brilliant aspen color in late September, and winters are cold but sunny, with heavy snow in the surrounding peaks. Avalanche danger can close the Million Dollar Highway in winter.

Things to Do

Experience the "Switzerland of America"

Nestled in a dramatic box canyon surrounded by towering peaks, Ouray is one of Colorado's most scenic mountain towns. Known as the "Switzerland of America," Ouray offers spectacular alpine scenery, historic charm, natural hot springs, and abundant outdoor recreation opportunities throughout the year.

Relax in the Ouray Hot Springs

The Ouray Hot Springs Pool is one of the town's most popular attractions, featuring naturally heated mineral water pools surrounded by stunning mountain views. Visitors can soak year-round while enjoying the scenic beauty of the San Juan Mountains.

Walk the Ouray Perimeter Trail

This unique trail system encircles the town and showcases waterfalls, cliffs, historic sites, mountain views, and beautiful canyon scenery. The route provides an excellent introduction to Ouray's natural beauty and is considered one of the area's signature hiking experiences.

Visit Box Canyon Falls Park

A short distance from downtown, Box Canyon Falls features a powerful waterfall plunging through a narrow quartzite canyon. Elevated walkways and viewing platforms allow visitors to experience the dramatic sights and sounds of the rushing water from within the canyon.

Explore Jeep Roads and Scenic Mountain Passes

Ouray is a renowned destination for four-wheel-drive adventures. Historic mining roads such as Engineer Pass, Cinnamon Pass, and Imogene Pass provide access to ghost towns, alpine basins, wildflower meadows, and breathtaking mountain scenery throughout the San Juan Mountains.

Discover Historic Downtown Ouray

The town's historic district features beautifully preserved Victorian-era buildings, local shops, restaurants, museums, and galleries. Visitors can learn about Ouray's mining heritage while enjoying the charm of one of Colorado's most picturesque mountain communities.

Top Trails

Ouray Perimeter Trail

Length: Approximately 6 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

The premier hiking trail in Ouray, the Perimeter Trail circles the town while passing waterfalls, overlooks, historic tunnels, and scenic mountain viewpoints. Hikers enjoy panoramic views of the surrounding San Juan Mountains and the town below.

Blue Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 8.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,500 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

One of Colorado's most spectacular hikes, the Blue Lakes Trail leads visitors to brilliant turquoise alpine lakes surrounded by rugged peaks, waterfalls, and wildflower-filled basins. The scenery is especially stunning during summer months.

Box Canyon Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short walk provides access to viewing platforms overlooking Box Canyon Falls. It is an excellent option for visitors seeking dramatic scenery without a strenuous hike.

Baby Bathtubs Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A family-friendly trail leading to a series of natural pools and cascades formed by mountain streams. The route offers scenic forest surroundings and beautiful views close to downtown Ouray.
