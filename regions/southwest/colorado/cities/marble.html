How to Get There

From Carbondale (30 mi): Take CO-133 south from Carbondale, turn onto County Road 3 at Redstone, and follow the road along the Crystal River into the town.

Overview

Marble is a very small mountain town in the Crystal River Valley of the Elk Mountains, at nearly 8,000 feet, famous for the Yule marble quarry on the slopes above. The pure white stone from this quarry was used for the Lincoln Memorial and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, and the town today is a quiet outpost of about 100 residents surrounded by mountains.

Visitors come to see the quarry and its history, to drive the scenic Crystal River road from Carbondale, and to use Marble as a base for exploring the high country, including the ghost town of Crystal and Crystal Mill. Marble offers a more remote and less crowded Colorado mountain experience than the better-known resort towns of Aspen and Vail.

Marble was founded in the 1880s as a mining camp, and it took off when the Colorado Yule Marble Company opened the quarry in 1905 and built a mill and a railroad to carry the stone. Marble from the quarry went into the Lincoln Memorial, the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, and the Colorado State Capitol. The quarry closed in 1941 and reopened in later decades, and the town survived through quarrying and tourism, with a revival that continues today.

The Yule Marble Quarry, high above the town, is the main attraction and can be seen on guided tours, and the ruins of the old marble mill, with its huge stone arches, sit near the center of town. The drive to Crystal Mill, a photogenic old mill perched over the Crystal River, is one of the best-known 4WD routes in Colorado, and the ghost town of Crystal lies nearby.

The Marble Historical Society Museum preserves photographs, tools, and stories from the quarry era, and the Marble Art Park and local sculptors continue the town's stonework tradition. Because the town is so small, most culture centers on local history and the arts and crafts of its residents.

Marble is surrounded by public land. The Raggeds and Maroon Bells–Snowmass Wilderness areas and the Gunnison and White River National Forests offer trails, and Beaver Lake, Lead King Basin, and the Crystal River are popular spots for fishing, camping, and photography. Jeep roads reach old mining camps and high basins.

Marble has a cold mountain climate. Winters are snowy and quiet, and some roads close, while summers are warm and sunny, with highs in the 70s and 80s °F and afternoon storms. Autumn is among the best times to visit, with aspen color throughout the valley.

Things to Do

Discover One of Colorado's Most Scenic Mountain Communities

Nestled in the Crystal River Valley beneath dramatic mountain peaks, Marble is a small historic town known for its natural beauty, rich quarrying heritage, and access to outdoor recreation. The town's remote setting and stunning alpine surroundings make it a popular destination for visitors seeking a quieter Colorado mountain experience.

Visit the Historic Marble Quarry

Marble is home to the famous Yule Marble Quarry, the source of marble used in notable structures including the Lincoln Memorial, the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, and portions of the Colorado State Capitol. Guided tours provide insight into the quarry's history, geology, and importance to American architecture.

Explore the Mill Site Park and Marble Museum

Visitors can learn about the area's mining and quarrying history at Mill Site Park and the Marble Museum. Exhibits showcase the town's development during the marble boom, historic photographs, machinery, and stories of the workers who helped build the community.

Drive to Crystal Mill

One of Colorado's most photographed landmarks, Crystal Mill is located a short distance from Marble along a rugged mountain road. Built in 1892 and perched above the Crystal River, the historic structure offers spectacular scenery and exceptional photography opportunities.

Experience Outdoor Adventure in the Crystal River Valley

The surrounding White River National Forest offers hiking, mountain biking, fishing, camping, wildlife viewing, jeep touring, and photography opportunities. Visitors can explore alpine forests, wildflower-filled meadows, mountain streams, and high-country landscapes throughout the region.

Enjoy Scenic Drives and Fall Colors

The roads surrounding Marble provide access to some of Colorado's most beautiful mountain scenery. During autumn, the area becomes especially popular for viewing golden aspen groves, rugged peaks, and colorful landscapes throughout the Crystal River Valley.

Top Trails

Crystal Mill Road and Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip from Marble
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following the route toward Crystal Mill, this scenic journey offers spectacular views of the Crystal River, mountain cliffs, waterfalls, and historic mining sites. Visitors can hike, bike, or utilize high-clearance vehicles depending on conditions.

Lead King Basin Loop

Length: Approximately 7 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This beautiful alpine route travels through historic mining areas, dense forests, wildflower meadows, and mountain passes. The trail provides excellent views of the Elk Mountains and is especially popular during summer and fall.

Beaver Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,200 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic hike climbs through aspen groves and evergreen forests to a peaceful alpine lake surrounded by mountain scenery. The trail is known for wildflowers, wildlife sightings, and beautiful fall colors.

Yule Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 9 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

A challenging high-country hike that rewards visitors with pristine alpine lakes, wildflower meadows, and panoramic views of the surrounding peaks. The trail passes near the historic Yule Marble Quarry and showcases the rugged beauty of the area.
