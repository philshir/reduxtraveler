Places to See in Telluride

Bear Creek Falls Trail
★ 5.0

Scenic out-and-back hiking trail winding through forested canyon terrain to a dramatic multi-tiered waterfall with steady elevation gain and mountain views.

Bridal Veil Falls
★ 5.0

Towering alpine waterfall cascading over a sheer cliff face along a rugged mountain trail with sweeping canyon views and a historic structure perched above.

Gondola
★ 5.0

Free scenic aerial transit system providing a smooth mountain ride between two alpine communities with sweeping panoramic views of forested peaks and valleys.

How to Get There

From Grand Junction (126 mi): Follow I-70BL E and 29 Rd to US-50 E in Orchard Mesa. Continue on US-50 E. Take US-550 S to CO-62 W/State Hwy 62/Sherman St in Ridgway. Follow CO-62 W/State Hwy 62 and CO-145 S to W Colorado Ave in Telluride

From Ouray (50 mi): Head north on US-550 N/3rd St/Main St toward 7th Ave. Turn left onto CO-62 W/State Hwy 62/Sherman St. Turn left onto CO-145 S. At the traffic circle, take the 2nd exit onto W 145 Spur Hwy N. Continue straight onto W 145 Spur Hwy. At the traffic circle, continue straight onto W Colorado Ave.

Overview

Telluride is a former silver mining camp turned ski and festival town in a box canyon of the San Juan Mountains, at about 8,750 feet. The town is a National Historic Landmark District, with well-preserved Victorian buildings against a backdrop of towering peaks and waterfalls, and it is known for its film, music, and food festivals.

Visitors come to ski the steep terrain at Telluride Ski Resort, to ride the free gondola between the town and Mountain Village, and to attend the Telluride Film Festival, the Telluride Bluegrass Festival, and other events. In summer, the box canyon is a base for hiking, mountain biking, and four-wheel-drive trips to alpine basins, and the town’s remote location gives it a quieter, more intimate feel than many resorts.

Prospectors found silver in the area in the 1870s, and the town was founded in 1878 as Columbia and renamed Telluride in 1887. In 1889 Butch Cassidy committed his first bank robbery here, taking the San Miguel Valley Bank. Telluride played a role in electrical history when the Ames power plant, built in 1891, produced some of the first commercial alternating current in the country. After mining declined, the town nearly emptied, then the ski area opened in 1972, and the Telluride Film Festival, begun in 1974, helped the town reinvent itself.

Main Street is lined with Victorian buildings, including the Sheridan Hotel and Opera House, in the middle of the box canyon. Bridal Veil Falls, at 365 feet the tallest free-falling waterfall in Colorado, drops at the head of the canyon, and the free gondola links the town with Mountain Village. Mount Sneffels, a 14,000-foot peak, and Ouray are within a short drive.

The Telluride Historical Museum, in an 1896 hospital, tells the story of the town’s mining and pioneer era, and the Pinhead Institute offers science programs and talks. The Telluride Film Festival, held each Labor Day weekend, and the Bluegrass Festival in June are the best-known events, with jazz, mushroom, and wine festivals filling the summer.

The San Miguel River and the open Valley Floor beneath the town offer walking and wildlife viewing, and trails such as the Bear Creek Falls Trail and Jud Wiebe Trail climb the canyon walls with views of the town. Lizard Head Wilderness and Mount Sneffels Wilderness lie nearby, and the Telluride Ski Resort operates as a mountain park in summer.

Telluride has snowy winters and mild summers. The ski season typically runs from late November through early April, with about 300 inches of snow, and winter highs are in the 30s °F. Summers have highs in the 70s °F, with cool nights and afternoon thunderstorms, and autumn brings golden aspens in late September.
