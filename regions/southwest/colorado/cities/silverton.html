How to Get There

From Durango (48 mi): Head north on Main Ave toward E 11th St. Continue straight to stay on Main Ave. Main Ave turns left and becomes E 14th St. Turn right onto US-550 N/N Main Ave. Continue straight onto State Hwy 110. Turn left onto W 12th St.

From Ouray (23 mi): Head south on US-550 S/3rd St/Main St toward 3rd St/Main St. Slight left onto Co Rd 2. Turn left onto Greene St. Turn left onto W 12th St.

Overview

Silverton is a former silver mining camp at 9,318 feet in the San Juan Mountains, and most of the town is a National Historic Landmark District. Surrounded by peaks over 13,000 feet, with dirt side streets and saloons on Blair Street that still feel like the Old West, it is one of the most atmospheric mining towns in Colorado.

Visitors come to ride the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, to drive the Alpine Loop and the Million Dollar Highway, and to tour mines and museums. Silverton has a quiet, remote feel, and in winter it is a center for backcountry skiing and snowmobiling, including guided skiing at nearby Silverton Mountain.

Prospectors found placer gold in the area in 1860, but real development followed treaties with the Ute and the discovery of silver veins in the 1870s. Silverton was established in 1874 and became the county seat of San Juan County, and the narrow-gauge railroad arrived from Durango in 1882 to haul ore. Mining carried on for more than a century, until the Sunnyside Mine closed in 1991, and the railroad now carries tourists instead of ore.

The Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad arrives in the middle of town after a scenic ride along the Animas River, and Blair Street still holds historic saloons and brothels turned into shops. The Alpine Loop leads to Animas Forks and ghost towns high in the mountains, and the Million Dollar Highway leads north to Ouray.

The San Juan County Historical Society runs a museum in the 1902 county jail, and the Mining Heritage Center and the Old Hundred Gold Mine tour show visitors how miners worked underground. The town's annual events include Hardrockers Holiday, and several galleries and old hotels preserve the 19th-century character.

Silverton is surrounded by the San Juan National Forest. Trails and 4WD roads lead to alpine lakes such as Ice Lake Basin, Molas Lake Park offers camping and fishing, and the Kendall Mountain Recreation Area has a ski hill above town. The Animas River runs right through the valley.

Silverton has a cold, high-alpine climate. Winters are long and snowy, with low temperatures well below zero, and summers are short and cool, with highs in the 70s °F and frequent afternoon thunderstorms. The railroad runs from late spring through autumn, and the best months for sightseeing are June through September.

Things to Do

Explore a Historic Mining Town in the San Juan Mountains

Silverton is a National Historic Landmark District located high in the San Juan Mountains of southwestern Colorado. Originally founded during the Colorado mining boom, the town retains its Old West character with historic buildings, mountain scenery, museums, and outdoor recreation opportunities that attract visitors throughout the year.

Ride the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad

One of Colorado's most famous attractions, the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad takes passengers through spectacular mountain canyons, along the Animas River, and deep into the San Juan Mountains. The historic steam-powered train provides a memorable journey and a unique look at the region's mining heritage.

Discover Alpine Scenic Drives and Jeep Adventures

Silverton is a gateway to many of Colorado's most scenic mountain roads, including the legendary Alpine Loop Scenic Byway. Visitors can explore historic mining districts, ghost towns, high mountain passes, alpine lakes, and breathtaking views by four-wheel-drive vehicle or guided tour.

Visit Historic Downtown Silverton

Downtown Silverton features well-preserved Victorian-era buildings, restaurants, shops, museums, and historic sites. Visitors can learn about the area's mining history while strolling along Greene Street and enjoying the authentic atmosphere of this mountain community.

Explore Ghost Towns and Mining History

The mountains surrounding Silverton are dotted with abandoned mines, historic structures, and ghost towns such as Animas Forks. These sites offer a fascinating glimpse into the lives of Colorado's early miners and settlers during the late nineteenth century.

Enjoy Outdoor Recreation Year-Round

From hiking, mountain biking, fishing, and camping in summer to snowmobiling, backcountry skiing, and winter photography, Silverton provides endless opportunities for outdoor adventure. The surrounding San Juan Mountains are known for their rugged beauty, abundant wildlife, and dramatic alpine landscapes.

Top Trails

Ice Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,500 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

One of Colorado's most famous hikes, Ice Lakes Trail climbs through forests, waterfalls, and wildflower-filled meadows before reaching brilliant turquoise alpine lakes surrounded by towering mountain peaks. The scenery is among the most spectacular in the state.

Highland Mary Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 7 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,800 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This beautiful alpine trail leads to a collection of pristine mountain lakes nestled among rolling tundra and rugged peaks. Wildflowers, wildlife viewing, and expansive mountain vistas make this one of the area's most popular hikes.

Colorado Trail to Molas Pass Overlook

Length: Approximately 3 to 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Accessible from nearby Molas Pass, this section of the Colorado Trail offers stunning views of the Weminuche Wilderness, alpine meadows, and the San Juan Mountains. The route is popular with hikers, trail runners, and backpackers.

South Mineral Creek Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short and family-friendly trail leads to scenic waterfalls surrounded by dramatic mountain scenery. The area also provides access to nearby alpine lakes and popular camping destinations.
