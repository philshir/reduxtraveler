How to Get There

From Denver (35 mi): Take I-70 west from Denver into the mountains and use Exit 240, then follow signs directly into downtown along Colorado Blvd.

Overview

Idaho Springs is a historic gold rush town in Clear Creek Canyon, about 30 miles west of Denver on I-70, at 7,526 feet. It is known for its old-West main street, its mining heritage, and the natural hot springs that gave the town its name, and it is the gateway to the Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway, the highest paved road in North America.

Visitors come for a quick escape from Denver, with gold panning, mine tours, soaks in the hot springs, and rafting on Clear Creek among the favorite activities. The town's restaurants and breweries are a popular stop on the way to the ski resorts of Summit County, and the road to Mount Blue Sky and St. Mary's Glacier leads to alpine scenery within an hour of the city.

Prospector George Jackson found placer gold at the confluence of Chicago Creek and Clear Creek in January 1859, setting off the Colorado Gold Rush. The town grew around the hot springs and nearby mines, and the Argo Tunnel, completed in 1910, was built to drain and connect the area's mines. The Argo Mill processed gold ore until the 1940s, and the town later became a tourist stop on the early highways to the mountains.

Historic downtown Idaho Springs has well-preserved brick buildings, and the Argo Gold Mine and Mill is an iconic landmark on the hillside. The Phoenix Gold Mine offers underground tours and gold panning, and the Indian Hot Springs has been used for centuries by Native Americans and travelers. The Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway, formerly the Mount Evans Scenic Byway, climbs to 14,130 feet, and Saint Mary's Glacier is a short drive away.

The Heritage Museum and Visitor Center and the Underhill Museum cover the mining history of the area, with displays of tools, photographs, and mineral specimens. The town's art galleries, restored buildings, and live music venues add to the Clear Creek County cultural scene.

Idaho Springs sits within the Arapaho National Forest, and the high-country trails and campgrounds of the Mount Blue Sky area, including Mount Blue Sky itself, are a short drive away. Echo Lake Park, Bridal Veil Falls, and the trail to St. Mary's Glacier offer easy outings, and Clear Creek provides fishing and whitewater rafting right through town.

Idaho Springs has a mild mountain climate with plenty of sunshine. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F and thunderstorms in the afternoon, autumn is crisp and colorful, and winters are cold but often sunny, with less snow in town than in the high country. The Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway is open only from about Memorial Day to early fall.

Things to Do

Relax in Historic Colorado Hot Springs

Idaho Springs is best known for its rich mining history and natural hot springs. Visitors can unwind in geothermal mineral pools at Indian Hot Springs, a popular attraction featuring indoor and outdoor soaking options in a tropical-style setting. The town has welcomed travelers seeking relaxation since the late 1800s.

Tour the Argo Mill and Tunnel

One of Colorado's most important mining landmarks, the Argo Mill and Tunnel offers guided tours through a historic gold-processing mill and the engineering marvel that connected many of the area's silver and gold mines. Visitors can learn about the Colorado Gold Rush and even try gold panning.

Experience Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway

Formerly known as the Mount Evans Scenic Byway, this spectacular mountain road climbs to elevations exceeding 14,000 feet and provides access to alpine tundra, mountain lakes, wildlife viewing, and breathtaking views. It is among the highest paved roads in North America.

Explore Downtown Idaho Springs

The historic downtown district features Victorian-era buildings, local restaurants, breweries, coffee shops, and unique boutiques. Visitors can stroll along Miner Street and discover the town's mining heritage while enjoying its vibrant mountain-town atmosphere.

Go Whitewater Rafting and Outdoor Adventure

Clear Creek flows directly through Idaho Springs and is one of Colorado's premier destinations for whitewater rafting. The surrounding mountains also offer opportunities for hiking, rock climbing, off-road touring, mountain biking, fishing, and scenic photography.

Visit Echo Lake

Located a short drive from town, Echo Lake sits at the base of Mount Blue Sky and offers beautiful mountain scenery, picnicking, fishing, hiking, wildlife viewing, and year-round sightseeing opportunities.

Top Trails

St. Mary's Glacier Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 400 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

One of the most popular hikes near Idaho Springs, this trail leads to a scenic alpine lake and permanent snowfield often referred to as St. Mary's Glacier. Visitors enjoy spectacular mountain scenery and opportunities for photography throughout the year.

Chief Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This rewarding summit hike offers panoramic views of the Continental Divide, Mount Blue Sky, and surrounding mountain ranges. The trail is known for its accessibility compared to many higher-elevation peak hikes.

Chicago Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 10 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

This challenging trail traverses forests, waterfalls, and alpine terrain before reaching beautiful high-elevation lakes below Mount Blue Sky. It is a favorite among experienced hikers seeking exceptional mountain scenery.

Echo Lake Trail System

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

The trails around Echo Lake offer accessible hiking opportunities with scenic lake views, wildflowers, forests, and opportunities to observe wildlife in a beautiful alpine setting.
