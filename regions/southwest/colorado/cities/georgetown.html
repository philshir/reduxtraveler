How to Get There

From Denver (45 mi): Take I-70 West toward the mountains, exit at 228 for Georgetown, and follow signs into the historic downtown district.

Overview

Georgetown is a historic mining town in the Front Range, about 45 miles west of Denver on I-70, at 8,530 feet in a narrow valley along Clear Creek. Known as the "Silver Queen of the Rockies," it is famous for its beautifully preserved Victorian architecture, the narrow-gauge Georgetown Loop Railroad, and the bighorn sheep that often graze nearby.

Visitors come to ride the steam train, to tour historic homes and a Victorian hotel, to hike and drive on the Guanella Pass Scenic Byway, and to enjoy a walkable downtown of antique shops and cafes. The town is an easy day trip from Denver, with a famous Christmas Market in December and a quiet, small-town feel in every season.

Gold was found in the area in 1859 during the Pikes Peak Gold Rush, and the discovery of rich silver veins in the 1860s turned Georgetown into one of the biggest towns in Colorado. At its peak it was one of the largest towns in Colorado, and the Colorado Central Railroad built the spectacular Georgetown Loop, completed in 1884, to climb the steep grade to Silver Plume. The silver crash of 1893 ended the boom, and the town was largely preserved as a result. The Georgetown–Silver Plume Historic District was named a National Historic Landmark District in 1966.

The Georgetown Loop Railroad, which crosses the high Devil's Gate Bridge between Georgetown and Silver Plume, is the town's signature attraction. In town, the Hotel de Paris and Hamill House are well-preserved examples of the town's silver-era wealth, and the Guanella Pass Scenic Byway climbs south from town to a high mountain pass with sweeping views and access to Mount Bierstadt, one of the most popular 14,000-foot peaks in Colorado.

The Hotel de Paris Museum, a French-style hotel built by Louis Dupuy in the 1870s, and the Hamill House Museum, the home of a silver-mining magnate, show how wealthy residents lived in the 1800s. The Georgetown Energy Museum covers the history of hydroelectric power, and the town's historic fire stations, church, and shops add a living-history feel, along with seasonal events such as the Christmas Market.

Georgetown Lake and the Clear Creek corridor provide fishing, picnicking, and views, and the surrounding Arapaho National Forest has trails for hiking and mountain biking. Guanella Pass has campgrounds and alpine lakes, and the Mount Bierstadt trailhead is a short drive from town. Bighorn sheep are often seen near the Georgetown Lake exit in the winter and spring.

Georgetown has a cool mountain climate with four seasons. Winters are cold with regular snow, summers are mild, with highs in the 70s °F and cool nights, and autumn brings golden aspens along Guanella Pass. The Christmas Market in early December is among the best times to see the town.

Things to Do

Step Back in Time in a Historic Mining Town

Nestled in the Rocky Mountains along Interstate 70, Georgetown is one of Colorado's best-preserved Victorian mining communities. Founded during the Colorado Silver Boom, the town features historic architecture, mountain scenery, museums, and a charming downtown district that transports visitors back to the late 1800s.

Ride the Georgetown Loop Railroad

One of the town's most popular attractions, the Georgetown Loop Railroad is a historic narrow-gauge steam train that travels through mountain terrain between Georgetown and Silver Plume. The ride crosses high bridges, passes through scenic valleys, and offers a glimpse into Colorado's rich railroad and mining history.

Explore Historic Downtown Georgetown

Visitors can stroll along Sixth Street and explore beautifully preserved Victorian buildings, local shops, cafés, galleries, and historic landmarks. The downtown area showcases the character of Colorado's mining era while offering a relaxing mountain-town atmosphere.

Visit the Hamill House Museum

Built in 1867, the Hamill House Museum is one of Georgetown's finest historic homes. Guided tours allow visitors to experience period furnishings, elegant Victorian décor, and stories about the influential families who contributed to the town's development during the mining boom.

Discover Georgetown Lake

Located just outside downtown, Georgetown Lake provides opportunities for fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, wildlife viewing, and walking. During winter, the lake is also popular for ice fishing and scenic mountain photography.

Experience Guanella Pass Scenic Byway

The nearby Guanella Pass Scenic Byway connects Georgetown with Grant and traverses spectacular alpine landscapes. Visitors can enjoy scenic drives, fall foliage viewing, hiking trails, wildlife watching, and breathtaking mountain panoramas along the route.

Top Trails

Silver Dollar Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 4.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 900 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

Located along Guanella Pass, this popular hike leads visitors to a beautiful alpine lake surrounded by rugged mountain scenery. Adventurous hikers can continue farther to Murray Lake for additional views and high-country exploration.

Bierstadt Lake and Mount Bierstadt Area Trails

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Strenuous
Difficulty: Moderate to Difficult

The Guanella Pass area provides access to numerous alpine hikes, including routes near Mount Bierstadt, one of Colorado's famous fourteeners. Hikers can enjoy expansive views, high-altitude lakes, wildflowers, and mountain wildlife.

Georgetown Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic path circles Georgetown Lake and offers beautiful lake and mountain views. The trail is suitable for walking, jogging, biking, birdwatching, and enjoying the area's peaceful surroundings.

Argentine Central Railroad Grade Trail

Length: Approximately 4 to 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following portions of a historic railroad route, this trail provides excellent views of Georgetown Valley and surrounding peaks. Visitors can experience remnants of Colorado's mining and railroad heritage while enjoying alpine scenery.
