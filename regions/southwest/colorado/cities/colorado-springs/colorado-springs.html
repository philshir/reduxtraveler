How to Get There

From Denver (71 mi): Get on I-25 S from W Colfax Ave. Follow I-25 S to W Bijou St in Colorado Springs. Take exit 142 from I-25 S. Continue on W Bijou St to destination.

Overview

Colorado Springs is the second-largest city in Colorado, set at about 6,000 feet on the eastern edge of the Front Range, about 70 miles south of Denver. It stands at the foot of Pikes Peak and is known for its dramatic red rock scenery, its military and Olympic institutions, and its dry, sunny climate.

Visitors come to see Pikes Peak, to walk among the sandstone formations of Garden of the Gods, to ride the Manitou Incline, and to tour attractions such as Seven Falls and the Cave of the Winds. The city is home to the U.S. Air Force Academy, the U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Training Center, and the Broadmoor resort, and its neighbor Manitou Springs adds a quirky historic downtown with mineral springs.

Colorado Springs was founded in 1871 by General William Jackson Palmer, a railroad builder who designed it as a resort town for wealthy health seekers and English visitors, earning it the nickname "Little London." The Cripple Creek gold rush of the 1890s made fortunes in the city, and the gold millionaire Spencer Penrose built the Broadmoor in 1918. The military arrived with Camp Carson in 1942, and Peterson Air Force Base, NORAD’s Cheyenne Mountain complex, and the Air Force Academy followed, making the military a mainstay of the local economy.

Pikes Peak, at 14,115 feet, is the city’s defining landmark and one of the most famous mountains in North America. The Ute called it Tava, "Sun," and the peak was named for Lt. Zebulon Pike, who saw it in 1806 but never climbed it. Edwin James made the first recorded ascent in 1820, and "Pikes Peak or Bust" became the slogan of the 1859 gold rush. In 1893 Katharine Lee Bates was inspired by the view from the summit to write "America the Beautiful." Today visitors can reach the top by the Pikes Peak Highway, the Pikes Peak Cog Railway, which opened in 1891, or the strenuous Barr Trail, and the annual Pikes Peak International Hill Climb, "the Race to the Clouds," has run since 1916.

Beyond Pikes Peak, Colorado Springs is full of natural and man-made landmarks. Garden of the Gods is a free city park of towering red sandstone fins, the Manitou Incline is a steep climb on an old railway bed, and Seven Falls and Cave of the Winds are classic family attractions. The Air Force Academy Cadet Chapel, with its soaring aluminum spires, the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, and the Broadmoor round out the list.

The city has a strong museum scene. The U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum, which opened in 2020, honors American athletes, and the Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum tells the local story from the old county courthouse. The Western Museum of Mining & Industry, the National Museum of World War II Aviation, the Peterson Air and Space Museum, and the Space Foundation Discovery Center cover the city’s industry and military heritage, and the Fine Arts Center and Taylor Museum focus on regional art.

Colorado Springs has a large network of parks and open spaces. Garden of the Gods Park and Palmer Park offer trails with mountain views, Cheyenne Mountain State Park and Red Rock Canyon Open Space are popular for hiking and biking, and the Seven Bridges Trail climbs through a forested canyon. America the Beautiful Park sits downtown, and Crystal Creek Reservoir lies in the mountains west of the city.

The Colorado Springs Switchbacks FC, who play in the USL Championship, are the city’s professional team. They play at Weidner Field downtown, which opened in 2021. The Air Force Falcons, who compete in Division I football, hockey, and other sports, and the Colorado College Tigers, whose hockey team is particularly popular, are the city’s best-known college teams.

Colorado Springs enjoys a sunny, semi-arid climate with about 250 to 300 days of sunshine a year. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F and afternoon thunderstorms, and winters are cold but sunny, with highs in the 40s °F and snow that usually melts quickly. Spring can be snowy and windy, and fall is dry and mild.
