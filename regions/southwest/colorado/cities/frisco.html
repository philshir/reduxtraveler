How to Get There

From Denver (72 mi): Take I-70 west from Denver through the mountains, exit at Frisco (Exit 201 or 203), and follow signs into downtown and Main Street.

Overview

Frisco is a town in Summit County on the shore of Dillon Reservoir, at about 9,100 feet, and a central hub for mountain recreation in the Colorado Rockies. It is known for its walkable Main Street, easy access to four ski resorts, a marina on the lake, and a friendly, small-town atmosphere in the middle of a busy resort region.

Visitors come to ski at nearby Copper Mountain, Breckenridge, Keystone, and Arapahoe Basin, to sail and paddle on Dillon Reservoir, and to bike the paved paths that radiate from town. Frisco is also a popular, less expensive base for lodging and dining, with a strong collection of local restaurants, breweries, and festivals.

Frisco was founded in 1873 and incorporated in 1880, during the Colorado Silver Boom, and was named for San Francisco. It served as a supply and transportation center for the mining camps of Summit County, with a railroad connection to Leadville and Denver. After mining declined, the town shrank to a few hundred residents until Dillon Reservoir, completed in 1963 to supply water to Denver, and the opening of nearby ski areas turned Frisco into a recreation center. Its Main Street is now a historic district of restored buildings.

Main Street, with its historic buildings, shops, and restaurants, is the center of Frisco, and the Dillon Reservoir shoreline provides views across the water to the Tenmile Range. The Frisco Bay Marina offers sailing, paddleboarding, and kayaking, and the Frisco Adventure Park has a tubing hill, disc golf, and a skate park. Scenic drives to Fremont Pass, Loveland Pass, and Vail Pass are all within an hour.

The Frisco Historic Park and Museum preserves 19th-century buildings, including a schoolhouse, a chapel, and a log jail, and tells the story of the town's mining era. Main Street also hosts the annual BBQ Challenge and summer concerts, with galleries, local music, and art walks adding to the town's cultural life.

The Frisco Peninsula Recreation Area and the Frisco Bay Marina give access to the lakeshore, the Frisco Nordic Center offers cross-country skiing, and the Rainbow Lake and Mount Royal trails climb the hills above town. A network of paved recreation paths runs through town to Breckenridge, Copper Mountain, and Dillon, and the White River National Forest covers the high country.

Frisco has a cool mountain climate. Winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s °F, and summers are mild, with highs in the 70s °F, cool nights, and afternoon thunderstorms. Fall brings golden aspens along the lake, and spring is a quiet season of lingering snow.

Things to Do

Experience Summit County's Mountain Adventure Hub

Frisco is located in the heart of Colorado's Summit County and offers easy access to world-class outdoor recreation in every season. Surrounded by the Rocky Mountains and situated along the shores of Dillon Reservoir, the town serves as an ideal base for exploring nearby ski resorts, hiking trails, lakes, and scenic mountain passes.

Stroll Historic Main Street

Frisco's charming Main Street features historic buildings, locally owned shops, restaurants, breweries, art galleries, and cafés. The walkable downtown preserves the town's mining heritage while offering a welcoming mountain-town atmosphere for visitors year-round.

Visit Frisco Bay Marina and Dillon Reservoir

During the summer months, the Frisco Bay Marina provides opportunities for boating, kayaking, paddleboarding, canoeing, sailing, fishing, and lakeside relaxation. Dillon Reservoir's mountain backdrop creates one of the most scenic recreation areas in Colorado.

Explore the Frisco Adventure Park

Located on the western edge of town, Frisco Adventure Park offers year-round activities. Winter visitors can enjoy tubing, beginner skiing, snowboarding, and Nordic skiing, while summer brings hiking, biking, disc golf, and family-friendly outdoor recreation.

Discover Local History

The Frisco Historic Park and Museum showcases the town's mining and railroad history through preserved structures, exhibits, artifacts, and educational programs. Visitors can gain insight into the lives of early settlers who helped shape Summit County.

Enjoy Easy Access to Colorado's Top Ski Resorts

Frisco is centrally located near several of Colorado's most popular ski destinations, including Breckenridge, Copper Mountain, Keystone, and Arapahoe Basin. This convenient location makes Frisco a favorite destination for winter sports enthusiasts.

Top Trails

Mount Royal Trail

Length: Approximately 3.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,400 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

One of Frisco's most popular hikes, the Mount Royal Trail climbs steeply above town to spectacular overlooks of Frisco, Dillon Reservoir, and the surrounding mountain ranges. The panoramic summit views are among the best in Summit County.

Rainbow Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 450 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This family-friendly trail climbs through forests to scenic Rainbow Lake. The route offers beautiful views, seasonal wildflowers, and opportunities for wildlife viewing close to town.

Lily Pad Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A local favorite, this trail passes through aspen groves and evergreen forests before reaching a picturesque mountain lake. The hike is especially popular during summer wildflower season and autumn foliage season.

Frisco Peninsula Recreation Path

Length: Approximately 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved and natural-surface trail system follows the shoreline of Dillon Reservoir, providing excellent opportunities for walking, biking, photography, birdwatching, and enjoying scenic mountain and lake views.
