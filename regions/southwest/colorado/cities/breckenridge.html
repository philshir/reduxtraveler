How to Get There

From Denver (81 mi): Take N Broadway and W 8th Ave to W 6th Ave/6th Ave. Freeway. Follow US-6 W and I-70 W to CO-9/N Summit Blvd in Summit County. Take exit 203 from I-70 W. Continue on CO-9. Drive to N Pine St in Breckenridge.

Overview

Breckenridge is a former gold mining town in Summit County, set at about 9,600 feet at the base of the Tenmile Range along the Blue River. It is one of the most visited mountain towns in Colorado, known for the well-preserved Victorian buildings of its National Historic District, a lively Main Street, and Breckenridge Ski Resort, which spreads over five peaks above town.

People come to ski and snowboard in winter, to hike, bike, and fish in summer, and to enjoy a walkable town of breweries, restaurants, and festivals. Breckenridge is about 80 miles from Denver, which makes it popular for weekend trips, and its year-round events, including the International Snow Sculpture Championships in January, give it a festive character in every season.

Gold was discovered along the Blue River in 1859, and the camp grew quickly during the Colorado Gold Rush. It was named for U.S. Vice President John C. Breckinridge, and the spelling was changed after he joined the Confederacy. Placer mining gave way to dredging and hard-rock mining, and in 1887 miners found "Tom's Baby," the largest gold nugget ever found in Colorado. The town declined in the 20th century until the ski area opened in 1961 and brought a new boom, and the preserved Victorian buildings that remain today were a key part of its revival.

Main Street is lined with 19th-century buildings that now hold shops, restaurants, and galleries, and the Breckenridge Historic District includes more than 250 historic structures. The BreckConnect Gondola links downtown to the base of the ski mountain, and the Blue River Recreation Trail runs along the river through town. In the surrounding mountains, Quandary Peak, one of the most popular 14,000-foot peaks in Colorado, is a short drive to the south.

The Breckenridge Heritage Alliance runs several museums that tell the mining and community story, including the Edwin Carter Discovery Center, the Barney Ford House Museum, and the Washington Mine and Reiling Dredge. The Breckenridge Arts District has galleries and studios in restored historic buildings, and the Riverwalk Center hosts concerts and the Breckenridge Music Festival in summer.

Breckenridge sits within the White River National Forest, and trails start right from town. Carter Park and the Blue River Recreation Trail are in the center, the Illinois Gulch Open Space and Cucumber Gulch Preserve offer wildlife viewing and quiet walks, and hikers can reach alpine lakes and wildflower meadows within a short drive. Dillon Reservoir and the other Summit County lakes are nearby.

Breckenridge has snowy winters and mild, sunny summers. Winter highs are in the 30s °F, with about 300 inches of snow on the ski mountain. Summer highs are in the 70s °F, with cool nights and afternoon thunderstorms, and fall brings golden aspens and quiet trails. Spring is the least predictable season, with late snows common.

Things to Do

Discover Colorado's Historic Mining Town Turned Mountain Adventure Hub

Breckenridge is a vibrant mountain town located in Colorado's Summit County at an elevation of approximately 9,600 feet. Founded during the Colorado Gold Rush in 1859, Breckenridge combines rich mining history with world-class outdoor recreation, a lively downtown district, scenic alpine landscapes, and year-round activities. Today, it is one of Colorado's most popular destinations for skiing, hiking, biking, festivals, and mountain adventures.

Explore Historic Main Street

Breckenridge's charming downtown features beautifully preserved Victorian-era buildings that house restaurants, breweries, art galleries, boutiques, museums, and specialty shops. Visitors can stroll along Main Street and experience the town's blend of historic character and modern mountain culture.

Visit Breckenridge Ski Resort

Breckenridge Ski Resort is one of the most visited ski resorts in North America, offering access to five mountain peaks, extensive ski and snowboard terrain, scenic chairlift rides, alpine hiking, mountain biking, and breathtaking views of the Tenmile Range.

Experience Breckenridge's Mining Heritage

The town's history is showcased through attractions such as the Breckenridge Welcome Center Museum, the Barney Ford House Museum, and historic mining sites throughout the area. Visitors can learn about the gold rush era and the pioneers who helped establish the community.

Enjoy Year-Round Outdoor Recreation

Breckenridge offers hiking, mountain biking, fishing, rafting, golf, horseback riding, skiing, snowboarding, snowshoeing, and wildlife viewing. Its location within the Rocky Mountains provides easy access to alpine lakes, mountain passes, and scenic wilderness areas.

Top Trails

McCullough Gulch Trail

Length: Approximately 6.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: About 1,400 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the area's most popular hikes, this trail leads to waterfalls, alpine lakes, meadows, and stunning mountain scenery. Wildflowers and panoramic views make it a favorite during summer months.

Sawmill Reservoir Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Beginning near downtown, this family-friendly trail climbs through forests to a peaceful mountain reservoir with scenic views overlooking Breckenridge.

Mohawk Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 7 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: About 1,700 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This spectacular alpine hike passes waterfalls, historic mining remains, and multiple high-elevation lakes surrounded by rugged mountain peaks. It is considered one of Summit County's premier hiking destinations.

Boreas Pass Trail

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Following a historic railroad route, this trail offers beautiful views of the Tenmile Range, aspen groves, wildflowers, and remnants of Colorado's mining and railroad history.
