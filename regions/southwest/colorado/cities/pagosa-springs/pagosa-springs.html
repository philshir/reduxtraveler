Overview

Pagosa Springs is the main community in Archuleta County in southwestern Colorado, set at about 7,100 feet along the San Juan River with the San Juan National Forest all around. It is known for its hot springs, including the Great Pagosa Spring, one of the deepest geothermal springs in the world, and for the heavy snowfall of the nearby Wolf Creek Pass.

Visitors come to soak in the riverside hot springs, to ski or snowshoe at Wolf Creek Ski Area, and to hike, fish, and raft in the surrounding wilderness. Pagosa Springs is a small town of about 2,000 people with a relaxed pace, and it is a popular base for exploring Chimney Rock, the San Juan Mountains, and Durango, which is about an hour away.

The Ute people considered the hot spring sacred and called it "Pagosah," or "healing waters." The U.S. Army built Fort Lewis near the spring in 1878 to keep the peace, and a town grew around the spring after the fort moved west in 1880. Pagosa Springs developed as a center for ranching, logging, and tourism, and the nearby Chimney Rock National Monument preserves Ancestral Puebloan buildings on a high ridge.

The Great Pagosa Spring, measured at more than 1,000 feet deep, steams beside the San Juan River downtown, and the Springs Resort has dozens of pools along the river. Treasure Falls, a 105-foot waterfall right off US 160, and Wolf Creek Pass, which crosses the Continental Divide at 10,857 feet, offer mountain scenery. Chimney Rock National Monument is about 17 miles west of town.

The Mother Spring and Nathan’s Hippy Dip Hot Spring preserve the area’s springs, and the Fred Harman Art Museum shows work by the Western illustrator who created Red Ryder, a popular comic-strip hero. The Pagosa Springs Visitor Center and local galleries celebrate the area’s Ute, Hispanic, and ranching heritage, and local festivals add to the town’s cultural life.

The San Juan River Walk runs through the center of town with picnic areas and a tubing run, and the San Juan National Forest and Weminuche Wilderness offer hiking, camping, and fishing. Echo Canyon Reservoir and Piedra Falls are popular outings, and Treasure Falls and Wolf Creek Pass are easy roadside stops.

Pagosa Springs has four seasons with sunny days. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F and monsoon thunderstorms in July and August, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and low temperatures often below zero. Wolf Creek Ski Area, east of town, receives more than 400 inches of snow in an average year.

Things to Do

Relax in Pagosa Springs' Famous Hot Springs

Pagosa Springs is renowned for its geothermal waters and is home to the Great Pagosa Spring, widely recognized as the world's deepest geothermal hot spring. Visitors can enjoy numerous soaking pools at The Springs Resort, Overlook Hot Springs, and other facilities located along the San Juan River in the heart of downtown.

Explore the San Juan River

The San Juan River flows directly through Pagosa Springs and provides opportunities for fishing, tubing, rafting, kayaking, and riverside walks. The popular River Walk trail follows the water through town and offers scenic views, picnic areas, and access to local shops and restaurants.

Visit Wolf Creek Ski Area and Wolf Creek Pass

Located east of town in the San Juan Mountains, Wolf Creek Ski Area is known for receiving some of the highest annual snowfall totals in Colorado. During warmer months, Wolf Creek Pass offers spectacular mountain scenery, scenic overlooks, wildlife viewing opportunities, and access to hiking trails throughout the national forest.

Discover Waterfalls and Mountain Scenery

The Pagosa Springs area is surrounded by the San Juan National Forest and features numerous waterfalls, rivers, alpine lakes, and wilderness areas. Popular destinations include Treasure Falls, Piedra Falls, and scenic drives through some of the most beautiful mountain terrain in southern Colorado.

Experience Wildlife and Nature

The Rocky Mountain Wildlife Park allows visitors to observe rescued native Colorado wildlife, including bears, elk, wolves, mountain lions, and other animals. The area is also an excellent destination for birdwatching, photography, and outdoor recreation.

Explore Ancient History at Chimney Rock National Monument

Just west of Pagosa Springs, Chimney Rock National Monument preserves important ancestral Puebloan archaeological sites. Visitors can learn about the area's cultural heritage while enjoying panoramic views of the surrounding mountains and valleys.

Top Trails

Piedra River Trail

Length: Up to 8 miles round trip (various turnaround options)
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

One of the area's most popular hikes, the Piedra River Trail follows a scenic canyon with breathtaking overlooks, forested sections, and views of the Piedra River below. The trail is suitable for a wide range of hiking abilities.

Treasure Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 0.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 200 feet
Difficulty: Easy

This short hike leads to one of the most photographed waterfalls in southwestern Colorado. Treasure Falls plunges more than 100 feet down a mountainside and is easily accessible from U.S. Highway 160 near Wolf Creek Pass.

Fourmile Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic forest trail leads hikers to a beautiful waterfall surrounded by lush mountain terrain. Wildflowers, forests, and seasonal wildlife sightings make this a favorite summer hiking destination.

San Juan River Walk

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located in downtown Pagosa Springs, this paved riverside path follows the San Juan River and connects parks, hot springs, shops, and restaurants. It is perfect for a leisurely walk, biking, or enjoying views of the river.
