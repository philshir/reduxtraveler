How to Get There

From Frisco (8 mi): Take I-70 west from Frisco, use exit 195 for Copper Mountain, then follow Copper Mountain Dr into the resort village and parking areas.

Overview

Copper Mountain is a ski resort in Summit County, about 75 miles west of Denver on I-70 and eight miles south of Frisco, at a base elevation of about 9,700 feet. It is known for naturally separated terrain, with beginner runs on one side, intermediates in the middle, and experts on the other, and for a compact, pedestrian-friendly village at its base.

Visitors come to ski, snowboard, and snow tube in winter and to hike, bike, and attend concerts and events in summer. The resort is popular with families and with skiers who want to avoid the crowds of larger resorts, and its easy access from Denver makes it a favorite for day trips and weekend stays. It is also a long-time training venue for the U.S. Ski Team.

The area was prospected for copper in the 1800s, which gave the mountain its name, but little of the mining boom that reached nearby Breckenridge and Leadville took hold here. The ski resort opened in December 1972 and was designed from the start around the idea that terrain should be laid out by ability level. Over the years it has added lifts, base villages, and summer activities, and it has become one of Summit County's four main ski resorts.

Copper Mountain's three villages (East Village, Center Village, and West Village) hold lodging, dining, and shops, with the ski mountain rising directly above. The Tenmile Range and Ten Mile Canyon form a dramatic backdrop, and in summer scenic chairlift rides, hiking, and mountain biking offer views of the surrounding peaks. The Climax Molybdenum Mine and Fremont Pass are a short drive south.

Copper Mountain's culture is centered on the resort life of its villages. Outdoor concerts, festivals, and food events in summer, and live music and après-ski gatherings in winter, give the base area a lively atmosphere, while nearby Frisco and Breckenridge offer museums and historic downtowns.

The Eagles Nest Wilderness lies to the north, with hiking on the Gore Range Trail and Wheeler Lakes Trail across the highway, and the Tenmile Canyon Recreation Path, a paved bike trail, runs from the resort to Frisco and onward to Breckenridge. The surrounding White River National Forest offers fishing, camping, and wildflower hikes through the summer.

Copper Mountain has a snowy alpine climate. Winters bring cold temperatures and about 300 inches of snow, and the ski season typically runs from early November into April. Summers are mild, with highs in the 60s and 70s °F, cool evenings, and afternoon thunderstorms, and autumn brings aspen color to the surrounding hills.
