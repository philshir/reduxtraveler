How to Get There

From Denver (198 mi): Take N Broadway and W 8th Ave to W 6th Ave/6th Ave. Freeway. Follow I-70 W to CO-82 in Glenwood Springs. Take exit 116 from I-70 W. Follow CO-82 to N Galena St in Aspen.

Overview

Aspen is a mountain town in the Elk Mountains along the Roaring Fork River, at nearly 8,000 feet, and one of the most famous ski and resort towns in the world. It is known for four neighboring ski mountains, a glamorous year-round social scene, a dense calendar of music and ideas festivals, and a compact downtown of Victorian brick buildings. Few places combine such dramatic alpine scenery with this level of culture and luxury.

Visitors come to ski or snowboard in winter, to hike beneath the peaks in summer, and to see the Maroon Bells, often called the most photographed mountains in North America. Aspen is also a destination for art, food, and wellness, and its summer festivals, galleries, and restaurants draw guests who may never touch a ski pole. Housing prices are among the highest in the country, and the town draws well-known visitors and residents.

Prospectors reached the area in 1879, when the settlement was first known as Ute City, and it was renamed Aspen in 1880 for the groves of aspen trees. The silver boom made it one of the richest mining towns in Colorado, but the 1893 silver crash left it nearly empty. Aspen was revived after World War II by Walter and Elizabeth Paepcke, who founded the Aspen Institute and Music Festival, and by skiers who opened Aspen Mountain's first lifts in 1946. The 1950 FIS World Championships put the town on the international ski map, and today it also hosts the X Games at Buttermilk.

Aspen's downtown has many sights within a short walk, from the 1889 Wheeler Opera House to the shops and galleries of the pedestrian malls. The Silver Queen Gondola carries visitors to the 11,200-foot summit of Aspen Mountain for views across the Elk Mountains, and the drive to the Maroon Bells and Maroon Lake, about 10 miles from town, is the signature outing. In summer, Independence Pass, which crests at 12,095 feet, offers one of the highest scenic drives in the state.

Aspen has a cultural life unusual for a town of its size. The Aspen Art Museum, in a striking building opened in 2014, shows contemporary art, the Aspen Music Festival and School brings classical performers each summer, and the Wheeler Opera House hosts concerts, theater, and film. The Aspen Institute and Aspen Ideas Festival make the town a gathering place for writers, scientists, and political leaders, and the Aspen Historical Society preserves the story of the mining and skiing eras.

Parks and trails start at the edge of town. The Maroon Lake Scenic Trail and Crater Lake Trail lead into the Maroon Bells–Snowmass Wilderness, and Hunter Creek and Smuggler Mountain offer hikes with views right above town. The White River National Forest surrounds Aspen, and in town Rio Grande Park and the John Denver Sanctuary provide peaceful riverside green space.

Aspen has a mountain climate with warm, sunny summers and heavy winter snow. Summer highs are generally in the 70s °F with cool nights and afternoon thunderstorms, autumn brings golden aspen leaves in late September, and winter highs are in the 30s °F, with more than 300 inches of snow a year on the ski mountains. Spring is slow and muddy, often called the quiet season.

Things to Do

Experience the Luxury and Natural Beauty of the Rocky Mountains

Aspen is one of Colorado's most iconic mountain towns, renowned for its world-class skiing, stunning alpine scenery, vibrant arts and culture scene, and year-round outdoor recreation. Located in the heart of the Elk Mountains at over 7,900 feet in elevation, Aspen combines historic charm with luxury amenities and serves as a gateway to some of the most spectacular landscapes in the Rocky Mountains.

Explore Downtown Aspen

The historic downtown district features beautifully preserved Victorian-era buildings, upscale shopping, art galleries, restaurants, museums, and cultural venues. Visitors can stroll along tree-lined streets, visit local boutiques, and experience the lively atmosphere that has made Aspen a premier mountain destination.

Ride the Silver Queen Gondola

One of Aspen's most popular attractions is the Silver Queen Gondola on Aspen Mountain. The ride carries visitors to over 11,000 feet above sea level, offering breathtaking views of surrounding peaks, alpine meadows, and the Roaring Fork Valley. During summer, visitors can enjoy hiking, dining, and live events at the summit.

Enjoy World-Class Arts and Culture

Aspen is home to the Aspen Music Festival and School, the Aspen Art Museum, and numerous cultural events throughout the year. Concerts, film festivals, lectures, and performances attract visitors from around the world.

Visit Maroon Bells

Just outside Aspen lie the Maroon Bells, often considered the most photographed mountains in North America. These twin peaks rise above Maroon Lake and offer extraordinary opportunities for sightseeing, hiking, wildlife viewing, and photography, especially during fall when golden aspens blanket the surrounding slopes.

Top Trails

Maroon Lake Scenic Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This accessible trail circles Maroon Lake and provides iconic views of the Maroon Bells. It is one of the most popular and photographed walks in Colorado.

Crater Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Beginning at Maroon Lake, this trail climbs through forests and rocky terrain to Crater Lake, offering spectacular views of the Maroon Bells and surrounding wilderness.

Hunter Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Starting near downtown Aspen, this popular hike follows Hunter Creek through forests and meadows while providing panoramic views of Aspen Mountain and the Roaring Fork Valley.

Smuggler Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 2.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: About 700 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A favorite local hike, this trail climbs to a scenic overlook above Aspen, offering outstanding views of the town, surrounding mountains, and Independence Pass region.

Geography

The city sits along the southeast (upper) end of the Roaring Fork Valley, along the Roaring Fork River, a tributary of the Colorado River about 40 miles (64 km) south of Glenwood Springs, Colorado. It is surrounded by mountain and wilderness areas on three sides: Red Mountain to the north, Smuggler Mountain to the east, and Aspen Mountain to the south.
