Overview

Durango is a Victorian railroad town on the Animas River in southwestern Colorado, at about 6,500 feet. It is known for its narrow-gauge steam train, its historic Main Avenue, its mountain-biking culture, and its position as a gateway to the San Juan Mountains and Mesa Verde.

Visitors come to ride the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, to raft and tube the Animas River, to ski at Purgatory Resort, and to enjoy a walkable downtown of restaurants, breweries, and shops. Fort Lewis College adds a youthful energy, and Durango is a base for trips to Mesa Verde National Park, the Million Dollar Highway, and the hot springs of Pagosa Springs.

Durango was founded in 1880 by the Denver & Rio Grande Railroad as a smelting and supply town for the nearby silver mines of the San Juan Mountains. The narrow-gauge line to Silverton opened in 1882 and has run continuously ever since. The town grew with the railroad and with ranching and farming, and the opening of the ski area, the college, and tourism helped it thrive after mining declined. Fort Lewis College, originally a military post in Pagosa Springs, moved to Durango in the 1950s.

The Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad runs from the historic depot along the Animas River to Silverton, climbing through spectacular canyon scenery. Historic Main Avenue includes the Strater Hotel and the Diamond Belle Saloon, and nearby Mesa Verde National Park, with its famous cliff dwellings, is about 35 miles west. The Million Dollar Highway leads north toward Silverton and Ouray.

The Animas Museum in the old Animas City school covers local history, and the Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad Museum displays locomotives and railroad artifacts. The Center of Southwest Studies at Fort Lewis College and local galleries and theaters add to the town’s cultural life, with the Durango Cowboy Gathering and the Iron Horse Bicycle Classic among the major annual events.

The Animas River Trail follows the river through the center of town, and Oxbow Park and Preserve protects riverside wildlife habitat. Chapman Hill, Overend Mountain Park, and Lake Nighthorse offer hiking and biking close to town, and the San Juan National Forest surrounds the area. Purgatory Resort, 25 miles north, offers skiing in winter and mountain activities in summer.

Durango has four distinct seasons with plenty of sunshine. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F and afternoon monsoon storms in July and August, autumn brings golden aspens, and winters are cold, with highs in the 40s °F in town and heavy snow in the mountains. Spring is mild and the river runs high with snowmelt.
