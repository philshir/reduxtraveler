Overview

Steamboat Springs is a Western mountain town in the Yampa Valley of northwestern Colorado, at about 6,700 feet. It is known for its cowboy heritage, its light "Champagne Powder" snow, its natural hot springs, and its long tradition of producing Olympic skiers, which has earned it the nickname "Ski Town USA."

Visitors come to ski at Steamboat Resort, to soak in hot springs such as Old Town Hot Springs and Strawberry Park, and to hike, bike, fish, and tube on the Yampa River in summer. The mix of a real working ranching town with a major ski resort, along with a walkable downtown and a year-round events calendar, gives Steamboat a friendly, down-to-earth atmosphere.

The area was home to the Ute before French trappers named Steamboat Springs after a chugging mineral spring that they mistook for a steamboat. Settlers arrived in the 1870s and ranching and coal mining supported the town, and in 1909 the railroad arrived. In 1913 Norwegian ski jumper Carl Howelsen introduced ski jumping, which led to the Winter Carnival and a century of skiing. Steamboat Resort opened in 1963 on Mount Werner, and the town has since produced dozens of Winter Olympians.

Steamboat Resort rises above the town with a gondola from the base area. Downtown, Lincoln Avenue is lined with shops and Western storefronts, and Fish Creek Falls, a 280-foot waterfall within a few miles of town, is a popular short hike. The Yampa River Core Trail runs through the center of the town, and hot springs pools are scattered around the valley.

The Tread of Pioneers Museum preserves the history of the valley from Ute times through ranching and skiing, and the Colorado Ski & Snowboard Hall of Fame honors the sport's history. Strings Music Festival brings classical and jazz performers each summer, and the Steamboat Springs Arts Council, galleries, and rodeo events such as Cowboys' Roundup Days add to the town's culture.

Howelsen Hill, one of the oldest continuously operating ski areas in North America, rises just across the river from downtown and has jumping hills, a tubing hill, and trails. Emerald Mountain offers hiking and mountain biking right at the edge of town, Steamboat Lake State Park lies to the north, and the Routt National Forest surrounds the area with trails and campgrounds.

Steamboat has a cold, snowy winter and a warm, dry summer. Winters bring about 300 inches of light, dry snow on the mountain, and summer highs are in the 80s °F with cool nights. Autumn brings golden aspens, and spring is a quiet season with the Yampa River running high.

Things to Do

Experience a Legendary Colorado Ski Town

Steamboat Springs is a renowned mountain destination in northwestern Colorado, famous for its world-class skiing, natural hot springs, ranching heritage, and outdoor recreation opportunities. Surrounded by the Yampa Valley and the Routt National Forest, the town offers year-round adventures and a welcoming Western atmosphere.

Enjoy Steamboat Ski Resort

Steamboat Ski Resort is internationally recognized for its abundant snowfall and legendary "Champagne Powder®" snow. In winter, visitors can enjoy skiing and snowboarding across expansive terrain, while summer activities include scenic gondola rides, mountain biking, hiking, and family-friendly mountain adventures.

Relax in Natural Hot Springs

Steamboat Springs offers several geothermal attractions, including Old Town Hot Springs in downtown and Strawberry Park Hot Springs located in a scenic mountain setting. Visitors can soak in naturally heated mineral waters while enjoying beautiful views of the surrounding landscape.

Explore Historic Downtown Steamboat Springs

Lincoln Avenue serves as the heart of downtown and features locally owned shops, galleries, restaurants, breweries, and historic buildings. The downtown district reflects the town's ranching roots and vibrant mountain culture.

Experience the Yampa River

Flowing through the center of town, the Yampa River provides opportunities for tubing, kayaking, paddleboarding, fishing, and riverside recreation. The Yampa River Core Trail offers a scenic pathway for walking and biking along the water.

Discover Western Heritage and Outdoor Adventure

Steamboat Springs is known as "Ski Town, U.S.A." and has produced more Winter Olympians than any other town in North America. Visitors can enjoy horseback riding, fly fishing, hiking, biking, wildlife viewing, and scenic drives throughout the surrounding mountains and valleys.

Top Trails

Fish Creek Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 to 5 miles round trip depending on route
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

One of the area's most popular hikes, Fish Creek Falls features a spectacular waterfall that drops more than 280 feet. The trail offers excellent photography opportunities and can be extended into the surrounding wilderness for a longer hiking experience.

Rabbit Ears Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail leads to one of the most recognizable landmarks near Steamboat Springs. Hikers enjoy panoramic views of the Yampa Valley, volcanic rock formations, and surrounding mountain ranges.

Emerald Mountain Trail System

Length: Various routes from 2 to 10+ miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Strenuous
Difficulty: Easy to Difficult

Located adjacent to downtown, Emerald Mountain offers an extensive network of trails popular with hikers, runners, and mountain bikers. The routes provide outstanding views of Steamboat Springs and the surrounding valleys.

Yampa River Core Trail

Length: Approximately 7.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved multi-use trail follows the Yampa River through town and connects parks, recreational areas, neighborhoods, and downtown attractions. It is ideal for walking, jogging, cycling, and sightseeing.
