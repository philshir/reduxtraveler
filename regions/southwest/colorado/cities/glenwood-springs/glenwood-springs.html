How to Get There

From Aspen (41 mi): Take CO-82 west through the Roaring Fork Valley, follow the highway to its junction with I-70, and continue into Glenwood Springs at the meeting point of the Colorado and Roaring Fork rivers.

Overview

Glenwood Springs is a small city at the confluence of the Colorado and Roaring Fork Rivers in western Colorado, at about 5,800 feet, in a canyon surrounded by towering cliffs. It is famous for its geothermal hot springs, which have drawn visitors for well over a century, and for the nearby scenery of Glenwood Canyon and Hanging Lake.

Visitors come to soak in the world-famous Glenwood Hot Springs Pool, to explore the Yampah Vapor Caves, to visit Glenwood Caverns Adventure Park, and to raft, bike, and hike in the Colorado River corridor. The city is also a convenient stop on I-70 between Denver and Utah and a gateway to Aspen, Carbondale, and the White River National Forest.

The Ute people had long used the hot springs, which they called "Yampah," or "Big Medicine." The city was founded in 1883 by Isaac Cooper, who named it after his wife’s hometown of Glenwood, Iowa, and it grew as a resort when the railroad arrived. The Hotel Colorado opened in 1893 and hosted President Theodore Roosevelt during a bear hunt in 1905. The gunfighter Doc Holliday spent his last months in Glenwood Springs and is buried at Linwood Cemetery, and the completion of the Glenwood Canyon section of I-70 in 1992 was one of the great engineering feats of the interstate system.

The Glenwood Hot Springs Pool, one of the largest mineral hot springs pools in the world, is the heart of the city, and the Hotel Colorado, a grand 1893 landmark, stands nearby. The Hanging Lake trail in Glenwood Canyon leads to a turquoise lake beneath a waterfall, and the Doc Holliday’s Grave Trail climbs to the historic Linwood Cemetery. Glenwood Caverns Adventure Park sits above town on Iron Mountain, with a gondola and cave tours.

The Frontier Historical Museum, in a Victorian house, preserves the history of the city and the region, and the Glenwood Springs Center for the Arts offers exhibits and classes. The Hotel Colorado and the Glenwood Hot Springs Lodge keep the resort tradition alive, and the Vaudeville Revue and local festivals add to the town’s entertainment.

The Glenwood Canyon Recreation Path is a 16-mile paved trail along the Colorado River through one of the most scenic canyons in the state, and Two Rivers Park lies at the confluence downtown. The Red Mountain Trail and Iron Mountain Hot Springs offer riverside and hillside outings, and Sunlight Mountain Resort and the White River National Forest are nearby. Rifle Falls State Park, with its triple waterfall, is a short drive west.

Glenwood Springs has a mild semi-arid climate with four seasons. Summers are hot, with highs in the 80s and 90s °F, and winters are cool, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and occasional snow. Spring and fall are comfortable, and the hot springs are popular year-round.

Things to Do

Soak in Glenwood Springs' Famous Hot Springs

Glenwood Springs is best known for its geothermal attractions. Visitors can relax at the historic Glenwood Hot Springs Pool, one of the largest mineral hot springs pools in the world, or enjoy the smaller riverside soaking pools at Iron Mountain Hot Springs. For a unique wellness experience, the Yampah Vapor Caves offer naturally occurring underground steam baths created by geothermal activity.

Visit Glenwood Caverns Adventure Park

Perched atop Iron Mountain, Glenwood Caverns Adventure Park combines family-friendly attractions, thrill rides, cave tours, and panoramic views of the surrounding Rocky Mountains and Glenwood Canyon. Guests can ride the scenic gondola to the summit, explore impressive limestone caverns, and enjoy attractions such as the Alpine Coaster and Giant Canyon Swing.

Hike to Hanging Lake

One of Colorado's most iconic natural attractions, Hanging Lake features crystal-clear turquoise water, cascading waterfalls, and lush vegetation suspended above Glenwood Canyon. The moderately challenging hike rewards visitors with spectacular scenery and is considered a must-see destination in the area.

Experience Outdoor Adventure

Glenwood Springs offers abundant outdoor recreation opportunities including whitewater rafting on the Colorado River, hiking, mountain biking, fishing, kayaking, and wildlife viewing. The city's location at the confluence of the Colorado and Roaring Fork Rivers makes it an ideal base for exploring Colorado's mountain landscapes.

Discover Local History

History enthusiasts can visit the gravesite of famed Western figure Doc Holliday at Linwood Cemetery, tour the historic Hotel Colorado, or explore exhibits at the Frontier Historical Museum to learn about the area's mining, railroad, and frontier heritage.

Explore Glenwood Canyon and the Roaring Fork Valley

The surrounding region features some of Colorado's most scenic landscapes. Visitors can take a drive through Glenwood Canyon, enjoy riverside viewpoints, hike canyon trails, or use Glenwood Springs as a gateway to destinations such as Aspen and the Roaring Fork Valley.

Top Trails

Hanging Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This nationally recognized trail climbs through Glenwood Canyon to the spectacular Hanging Lake. Along the route, visitors pass scenic creek crossings and waterfalls before reaching the lake's emerald waters and the nearby Spouting Rock waterfall.

Grizzly Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 7 miles round trip (varies by turnaround point)
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following Grizzly Creek through Glenwood Canyon, this scenic trail offers mountain views, creek access, wildflowers, and shaded forest sections. It is popular with hikers seeking a less crowded alternative to Hanging Lake.

Doc Holliday Grave Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This short but steep trail leads to Linwood Cemetery, where legendary gunslinger Doc Holliday is believed to be buried. The route also provides excellent views overlooking Glenwood Springs and the surrounding valleys.
