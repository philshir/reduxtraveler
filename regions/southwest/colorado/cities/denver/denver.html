Overview

Denver is the capital and largest city of Colorado, set in the South Platte River Valley on the western edge of the Great Plains at exactly one mile above sea level, which earns it the nickname the "Mile High City." With the Rocky Mountains on the western horizon, it is known for its sunny weather, its craft beer, its outdoor culture, and its role as the economic and cultural center of the Rocky Mountain region.

Visitors come to enjoy the city’s museums, restaurants, and live music, to catch a game, and to use Denver as a gateway to the Rockies. A revived downtown, the Union Station area, and the LoDo and RiNo districts give it a lively urban feel, while Red Rocks Amphitheatre and the foothills offer an immediate escape into nature. The 16th Street Mall and Larimer Square are popular walking areas.

Denver began in 1858 as a gold rush camp at the junction of Cherry Creek and the South Platte River and was named for Kansas Territorial governor James W. Denver. The city beat its rivals when it built a rail spur to the transcontinental line in 1870, and it became the territorial capital in 1867, nine years before Colorado became a state in 1876. Silver fortunes, cattle, and later oil, aerospace, and technology kept it growing, and the city has experienced waves of immigration and urban renewal in the decades since.

The Colorado State Capitol, whose gold-plated dome is a city landmark, has a step marked at exactly 5,280 feet above sea level. Nearby, the Cathedral Basilica, Civic Center Park, and Denver Union Station are central landmarks, and Larimer Square, the city’s oldest block, is lined with Victorian buildings. West of town, Red Rocks Park and Amphitheatre, one of the world’s great outdoor concert venues, sits among towering red sandstone formations.

Denver is a major museum city. The Denver Art Museum and its Hamilton Building, the History Colorado Center, the Denver Museum of Nature & Science, the Clyfford Still Museum, the Museum of Contemporary Art Denver, and the Kirkland Museum cover art, history, and science, and the Molly Brown House Museum and the Denver Botanic Gardens are other favorites.

Denver has more than 200 parks within the city and a large system of mountain parks in the foothills. City Park, with the zoo and the Museum of Nature & Science, and Washington Park are the largest urban parks, Confluence Park marks the city’s founding site on the South Platte, and Chatfield State Park and Mount Falcon Park offer lakes and trails at the edge of the metro area. The Denver Mountain Parks, including Red Rocks, extend into the foothills.

Denver is a major-league sports city. The Denver Broncos (NFL) play at Empower Field at Mile High, the Colorado Rockies (MLB) at Coors Field, the Denver Nuggets (NBA) and the Colorado Avalanche (NHL) at Ball Arena, and the Colorado Rapids (MLS) at Dick’s Sporting Goods Park in nearby Commerce City. The Colorado Mammoth of the National Lacrosse League also play at Ball Arena, and the Nuggets and Avalanche have both won recent championships.

Denver has a semi-arid continental climate with about 300 days of sunshine a year. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 80s °F and afternoon thunderstorms, and winters are cold but sunny, with highs in the 40s °F and snow that tends to melt quickly. Spring is the snowiest and most unpredictable season, and autumn is dry and pleasant.
