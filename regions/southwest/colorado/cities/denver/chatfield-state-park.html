Chatfield State Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Colorado
  3. Denver

Expansive natural area featuring a large reservoir, multi-use trails, and diverse recreational opportunities for outdoor enthusiasts.

How to Get There

Take US-85 south from Denver, turn right onto C-470 west, then exit onto Wadsworth Boulevard and head south to the park entrance.

Overview

Chatfield State Park is a 5,600-acre recreation area at the southwestern edge of the Denver metro area, centered on Chatfield Reservoir and the South Platte River. The reservoir was built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in the 1970s for flood control, and the surrounding land became a state park that now offers boating, swimming, camping, fishing, hiking, biking and horseback riding. It is a major wildlife area, with hundreds of bird species and prairie dogs, and it hosts a hot air balloon launch, an off-leash dog area, a botanical garden and a nature center. People come for easy access to the outdoors just 25 minutes from downtown Denver.

Access

The park is in Littleton, about 15 miles southwest of downtown Denver, and is reached by C-470 and Wadsworth Boulevard, with additional entrances from Roxborough Park Road. Most visitors drive, and several gates serve different areas of the park, including the marina, the swim beach, the campground and the trailheads. The Platte River Trail and the Highline Canal Trail also connect to the park for cyclists. Parking is limited at busy times, and the park may reach capacity on summer weekends.

Seasons

The park is open year-round. Summer is the busiest season, with boating, swimming and camping, while spring and fall are excellent for birdwatching, with migrants arriving in large numbers. Winter is quiet and often mild enough for hiking and biking, and the reservoir can freeze for ice fishing in cold years. Thunderstorms and strong winds can affect the water, and swim beaches and marinas have seasonal hours. Wildlife viewing is best in the early morning.

Things to Do

Boat, Swim and Paddle on Chatfield Reservoir

Chatfield State Park has a marina, boat ramps and a sandy swim beach on the reservoir. Visitors can sail, paddleboard, kayak, waterski or relax on the shore.

Watch Birds and Wildlife

The park is a haven for waterfowl, herons, raptors and songbirds, along with deer, coyotes and prairie dogs. The Audubon Society's nature center and wetlands add to the experience.

Hike and Bike the Park's Trails

More than 25 miles of trails wind through prairie, wetlands and shoreline, linking to the Platte River Trail and regional trails. They suit walkers, runners and cyclists.

Visit Chatfield Farms

The Denver Botanic Gardens at Chatfield Farms includes historic farm buildings, gardens and a pumpkin festival in autumn. It is a good family stop.

Let Dogs Run in the Off-Leash Area

A 69-acre fenced off-leash area gives dogs room to roam and swim, with a daily or annual pass. It is one of the largest in the state.

Top Trails

Chatfield Reservoir Perimeter Trail

Length: Approximately 10 miles loop (portions)
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Paved and gravel trails follow much of the reservoir shoreline, with views of the water and the foothills. It is flat and popular with cyclists and walkers.

Platte River Trail Segment

Length: Approximately 4 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved section of the Platte River Trail connects the park to the river and the greenways to Denver. It is a good route for biking and birdwatching.

Audubon Nature Center Wetlands Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short loop through wetlands and trees offers chances to see birds and wildlife. It starts near the nature center and is accessible and relaxed.

Visitor Centers

Chatfield State Park has a visitor center near the main entrance, which offers maps, information on activities and passes, restrooms and exhibits on the park's history and wildlife. Park rangers can answer questions about fishing, boating and trails. The Audubon Society of Greater Denver's Nature Center at Chatfield and the Denver Botanic Gardens at Chatfield Farms also have visitor facilities and programs. Hours vary by season, so check the Colorado Parks and Wildlife website.

Camping and Lodging

Chatfield has a large campground with sites for tents and RVs, some with electrical hookups, plus picnic areas, restrooms and showers, and reservations are recommended in summer. There is no lodging inside the park. Littleton, Lakewood and the Denver Tech Center, 10 to 25 minutes away, have many chain hotels, and Downtown Denver, about 25 to 30 minutes away, offers historic and luxury stays such as the Brown Palace Hotel, the Oxford Hotel and the Crawford Hotel at Union Station. Castle Rock, about 25 minutes south, has additional options. Check each property's website for current availability.
