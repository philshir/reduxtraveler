How to Get There

From Denver (66 mi): Take N Broadway to W Colfax Ave. Take US-36 W to MacGregor Ave in Estes Park. Continue on MacGregor Ave. Drive to Park Ln.

Overview

Estes Park is a mountain resort town in Larimer County, set at 7,522 feet along the Big Thompson River and the eastern gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park. It is known for its mountain setting, a walkable downtown of shops and restaurants, the Riverwalk, the historic Stanley Hotel, and elk that wander freely through town, especially during the fall rut.

Most visitors come for Rocky Mountain National Park, which offers alpine lakes, wildlife, and Trail Ridge Road, the highest continuous paved road in the country. Estes Park is also a classic family vacation spot and a quiet base for hiking, fishing, horseback riding, and scenery drives, and it is about 70 miles from Denver, making it an easy trip for Front Range travelers.

The Estes Valley was a summer hunting ground for the Arapaho and Ute people before Joel Estes settled there in 1859. The area began attracting tourists in the 1870s, including Isabella Bird, whose travel writing made it famous, and Enos Mills, a naturalist who led the campaign that created Rocky Mountain National Park in 1915. In 1909 F. O. Stanley, inventor of the Stanley Steamer car, opened the Stanley Hotel, and the town grew as a resort around the park and the hotel.

Rocky Mountain National Park covers more than 415 square miles of the Front Range, and Trail Ridge Road, which climbs above 12,000 feet, is its most famous drive. In town, The Stanley Hotel, which inspired Stephen King's novel The Shining, is a historic landmark, and the Estes Park Aerial Tramway climbs Prospect Mountain for views of the valley.

The Estes Park Museum tells the story of the valley from its earliest inhabitants through the national park era, and the Enos Mills Cabin Museum preserves the homestead of the park's founder. The MacGregor Ranch Museum shows a working historic ranch, and the town hosts music events, art galleries, and the Rocky Mountain Elk Fest in the fall.

Beyond the national park, Estes Park has its own scenic open spaces. Lake Estes and the Lake Estes Trail provide a paved loop near downtown, the Riverwalk follows the Big Thompson through the center of town, and nearby Lily Lake, Sprague Lake, and the trails of Moraine Park offer easy walks with mountain views.

Estes Park has a cool mountain climate. Summer highs are in the 70s °F, with cooler nights and afternoon thunderstorms, and the high country in the national park can be much colder. Fall brings elk and golden aspens, winters are cold with moderate snowfall and strong winds, and Trail Ridge Road is usually closed from about late October until Memorial Day weekend.

Things to Do

Explore Rocky Mountain National Park

Estes Park serves as the primary gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park, one of the most visited national parks in the United States. Visitors can enjoy spectacular mountain scenery, alpine lakes, abundant wildlife, scenic drives, hiking trails, and breathtaking viewpoints throughout the park.

Drive the Scenic Trail Ridge Road

Known as the highest continuous paved road in the United States, Trail Ridge Road crosses Rocky Mountain National Park and reaches elevations above 12,000 feet. The route offers panoramic mountain vistas, tundra landscapes, wildlife viewing opportunities, and numerous scenic overlooks.

Visit Historic Downtown Estes Park

Downtown Estes Park features charming mountain-town atmosphere with local shops, galleries, restaurants, breweries, and riverwalk paths. Visitors can stroll along Elkhorn Avenue and enjoy scenic views of the surrounding peaks while exploring the town's unique character.

Tour the Stanley Hotel

The iconic Stanley Hotel is one of Colorado's most famous landmarks. Opened in 1909, the historic hotel is known for its architecture, mountain views, guided tours, and connection to Stephen King's novel The Shining. The property remains one of the area's most popular attractions.

Watch Wildlife Throughout the Valley

Estes Park is renowned for wildlife viewing, particularly elk, mule deer, bighorn sheep, and moose. During the fall elk rut, visitors often observe large herds throughout town and nearby meadows as bull elk engage in their annual mating displays.

Enjoy Outdoor Recreation Year-Round

In addition to hiking, visitors can enjoy horseback riding, fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, rock climbing, camping, snowshoeing, cross-country skiing, and scenic photography. The surrounding national forest and mountain terrain provide endless opportunities for outdoor adventure.

Top Trails

Bear Lake Loop

Length: Approximately 0.6 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

One of Rocky Mountain National Park's most popular trails, Bear Lake Loop offers stunning views of surrounding peaks reflected in a beautiful alpine lake. The accessible trail is suitable for visitors of all ages and serves as a starting point for several longer hikes.

Emerald Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 700 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This classic Rocky Mountain National Park hike passes Nymph Lake and Dream Lake before reaching Emerald Lake beneath dramatic mountain cliffs. The trail is known for exceptional scenery and excellent wildlife viewing opportunities.

Gem Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3.4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

Located near Estes Park, this trail climbs through unique rock formations and ponderosa pine forests before reaching Gem Lake, a scenic mountain pool nestled among granite outcrops with panoramic views of Estes Valley.

Alberta Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1.6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 200 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A favorite family-friendly hike, this trail leads to Alberta Falls, one of Rocky Mountain National Park's most photographed waterfalls. Visitors enjoy rushing water, forest scenery, and easy access from the Bear Lake corridor.
