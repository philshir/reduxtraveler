How to Get There

From Montrose (49 mi): Take US-50 E toward Gunnison for approximately 49 miles, then follow signs to the Elk Creek Visitor Center or designated recreation areas.

Overview

Curecanti National Recreation Area is a string of three reservoirs on the Gunnison River in western Colorado: Blue Mesa, Morrow Point and Crystal. Created in 1965, the area protects about 43,000 acres of canyons, sagebrush hills and clear mountain water, including Blue Mesa Reservoir, the largest body of water in Colorado. It is known for boating, fishing, camping and dramatic scenery at the edge of the Black Canyon of the Gunnison National Park. People come for kokanee salmon and trout fishing, sailing, paddling, hiking, bird watching and quiet camping beneath huge skies.

Access

Curecanti stretches along US-50 between Gunnison and Montrose, with entrances and recreation sites along the highway and on side roads. The Elk Creek Visitor Center, the main hub, is about 16 miles west of Gunnison, while the Morrow Point and Crystal areas are reached from the west near the Black Canyon. Most visitors arrive by car, and the roads are paved. The drive from Denver takes about 4.5 hours, and from Gunnison to the Pine Creek area about 45 minutes.

Seasons

Curecanti is open year-round, with summer the busiest season for boating and camping. Spring and fall are quieter and good for fishing and hiking, with cooler temperatures and golden cottonwoods in autumn. Winters are cold and snowy, and ice fishing, snowshoeing and cross-country skiing replace boating. Some campgrounds, boat tours and visitor facilities operate seasonally, and roads to certain sites close in winter, so check conditions.

Things to Do

Experience Colorado's Largest Reservoirs

Curecanti National Recreation Area is built around three lakes carved into deep canyons. The scenery ranges from open sagebrush slopes to narrow, dramatic gorges.

Boat and Fish on Blue Mesa Reservoir

Blue Mesa is a favorite for sailing, powerboating, paddling and fishing, with trout, kokanee salmon and lake trout. Marinas provide rentals and boat ramps, and permits are required for boats.

Take a Boat Tour on Morrow Point

Seasonal ranger-led boat tours explore the narrow canyon of Morrow Point Reservoir, where sheer walls rise from the water. Tours depart from the Pine Creek Trail area and require advance tickets.

Watch Wildlife and Enjoy the Scenery

Elk, mule deer, bald eagles, ospreys and bighorn sheep live in the area, and the sky is dark at night. Early morning and evening are the best times for wildlife.

Camp and Stargaze

Campgrounds around the lakes offer lakeside sites, and the area's dark sky is superb for stargazing. The remote setting makes it a quiet place to relax.

Top Trails

Dillon Pinnacles Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs through sagebrush and rock to views of the Dillon Pinnacles and Blue Mesa Reservoir. It is a good choice for a moderate half-day hike.

Neversink Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail follows the Gunnison River through a shaded canyon, with chances to see birds and wildlife. It is a good family-friendly route.

Hermit's Rest Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A short trail leads to a scenic overlook and a small mesa with views of the surrounding landscape. It is short and rewarding.

Pine Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Strenuous
Difficulty: Strenuous

This trail descends the canyon wall to the boat dock for Morrow Point, including a long staircase. The climb back is tiring, so allow plenty of time.

Visitor Centers

The Elk Creek Visitor Center, on US-50 near Gunnison, is the main visitor center and is typically open Thursday through Monday in the main season. It has exhibits on the area's history and ecology, a bookstore, restrooms and rangers who provide maps, boat permit information and advice on current conditions. Additional ranger stations and contact points operate at Lake Fork and the Cimarron area, and hours change seasonally, so check the National Park Service website.

Camping and Lodging

Curecanti has several campgrounds, including Elk Creek, Lake Fork, Stevens Creek and Ponderosa, offering tent and RV sites, many with views of Blue Mesa Reservoir, and boat-in sites on the water. There is no lodging inside the recreation area. Gunnison, about 20 minutes east, has chain hotels and local inns, and Crested Butte, about 45 minutes north, offers lodges and condos. Montrose, about 45 minutes west, has a wide range of hotels, and Lake City provides cabins and small inns. Check each property's website for current availability.
