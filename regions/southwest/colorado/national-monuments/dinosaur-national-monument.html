Dinosaur National Monument

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Colorado
  3. Dinosaur National Monument

Expansive protected area featuring fossil beds, ancient petroglyphs, rugged canyons, and scenic rivers ideal for hiking, rafting, and exploration.

How to Get There

From Vernal, UT (20 mi): Take US-40 E toward Dinosaur, CO, and follow signs to the Quarry Visitor Center entrance.

Overview

Dinosaur National Monument straddles the Colorado-Utah border and protects more than 200,000 acres of rugged canyons, rivers and fossil-rich rock. It is best known for the Dinosaur Quarry, in Utah, where thousands of bones from Jurassic dinosaurs are exposed in a rock wall, and for the spectacular canyons of the Green and Yampa Rivers on the Colorado side. The monument was first set aside in 1915 to protect the fossils and later expanded to include the river canyons. People come to see real dinosaur bones, to float the rivers, to drive the scenic Harpers Corner Road and to hike in a remote landscape.

Access

The monument has two main areas. The Quarry area, with its exhibit hall, is near Jensen, Utah, about 20 miles from Vernal, while the Canyon area is reached from Dinosaur, Colorado, about 2 miles from the town along US-40. Most visitors arrive by car, and the roads to both areas are paved. The area is remote, with the nearest large towns being Vernal, Utah and Craig, Colorado. Rafters usually arrive on guided or permitted trips, which launch at river access points.

Seasons

Dinosaur National Monument is open year-round, but roads and services are seasonal. Summer is hot and busy, with rafting on the Yampa and Green Rivers at its peak in late spring and early summer. Spring and fall offer cooler weather, ideal for hiking and driving. Winters are cold and snowy, and Harpers Corner Road and other backcountry roads can close, but the Quarry Exhibit Hall remains open. Visitor center hours change by season, so check ahead.

Things to Do

See Real Dinosaur Bones at the Quarry Exhibit Hall

The Quarry Exhibit Hall in Utah houses a wall of about 1,500 dinosaur fossils left in place in the rock. Visitors can look at the bones and touch real fossils at the site.

Drive Harpers Corner Scenic Drive

This 31-mile road in Colorado climbs along the rim of the Canyons, passing overlooks and ending at a trailhead with views of the Green and Yampa Rivers. It is a highlight of the Colorado side.

Raft the Yampa and Green Rivers

The rivers offer some of the country's best whitewater and quiet floating, passing Echo Park and Split Mountain. Trips require permits or a commercial guide.

Hike and Look for Petroglyphs

Trails in the monument lead to canyons, rock art and fossil sites. Ancient petroglyphs on canyon walls show the area's long human history.

Stargaze in a Remote Landscape

With few lights nearby, the night sky is dark and clear. Camping inside the monument is a good way to enjoy it.

Top Trails

Fossil Discovery Trail

Length: Approximately 1.2 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail leads from the Quarry Visitor Center up a ridge past exposed fossil bones, including dinosaur fossils still in the rock. The route is exposed and best done in the cooler part of the day.

Sound of Silence Trail

Length: Approximately 3.2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This loop explores a quiet, otherworldly canyon of rocky badlands. It is a good choice for solitude.

Harpers Corner Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail follows a ridge to a viewpoint overlooking the river canyons from high above. It is a short and rewarding walk.

River Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A relaxed route follows the river from the Split Mountain area, with chances to see wildlife and rock formations.

Visitor Centers

Dinosaur National Monument has several visitor centers. The Quarry Visitor Center and Quarry Exhibit Hall, in Utah, display the famous fossil wall and other exhibits, and the Canyon Visitor Center near Dinosaur, Colorado, has exhibits about the river canyons and the Colorado side of the monument. Both have rangers, restrooms, bookstores and maps, and hours change seasonally. Closures occur on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day, so check the National Park Service website.

Camping and Lodging

The monument has several campgrounds, including Green River, Rainbow Park, Echo Park and Deerlodge Park, as well as backcountry and river camping with permits, and there is no lodging inside the monument. The closest hotels are in Vernal, Utah, about 20 miles from the Quarry, which has chain hotels such as the Holiday Inn Express and local motels. On the Colorado side, Dinosaur and Rangely have a few small motels, and Craig, about 90 minutes east, offers a larger selection. Check each property's website for current availability.
