Bent’s Old Fort National Historic Site

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Colorado
  3. Bent’s Old Fort National Historic Site

Reconstructed adobe fur trading post featuring historic exhibits, cultural demonstrations, and outdoor grounds that interpret life and commerce on a 19th-century frontier trade route.

How to Get There

From La Junta (8 mi): Take US-50 to CO-109 north, then turn east onto CO-194 and follow it directly to the historic site entrance and parking area.

Overview

Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site is a reconstructed adobe trading post on the Arkansas River in southeastern Colorado that once stood as the most important commercial center on the southern Great Plains. Built by the Bent brothers and Ceran St. Vrain in the early 1830s, the fort traded with Cheyenne, Arapaho and other Plains peoples, Mexican traders and American trappers, and it became a key stop on the Santa Fe Trail. It served as a hub of the buffalo robe and fur trade until it was abandoned in 1849. Today, rangers in period dress and carefully rebuilt rooms bring the fort's busy, multicultural world to life.

Access

The fort is about 8 miles east of La Junta along the Arkansas River, in Otero County, reached by taking US-50 to CO-109 and then CO-194 east. The drive from Pueblo takes about an hour and 15 minutes, and from Colorado Springs about 2 hours. Most visitors arrive by car, and a paved lot sits near the visitor entrance. From the lot, a short walk across the grounds leads to the fort itself.

Seasons

The site is open year-round, but the seasons shape the experience. Summer is hot and sunny, with the best living-history programs, and heat is strong on the open grounds. Spring and fall are more comfortable, and winter days can be cold, though usually dry, with fewer programs and reduced hours. Special events, such as demonstrations and Santa Fe Trail celebrations, take place at certain times of the year, so check the National Park Service calendar.

Things to Do

Step Inside a Santa Fe Trail Trading Post

Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site lets visitors explore a full-scale reconstruction of the adobe fort, with rooms furnished as they might have been in the 1840s. The courtyard, rooms and rooftop views make it an immersive experience.

See Living History Demonstrations

Rangers and costumed interpreters demonstrate blacksmithing, cooking, trade and other skills of the 1840s. Programs are often offered in the summer season.

Learn About a Cultural Crossroads

Exhibits describe the Cheyenne, Arapaho, Mexican and American traders who met at the fort, and the cultural exchange that took place. The story shows the fort as more than a military post.

Watch the Santa Fe Trail Story Unfold

The fort was a vital waypoint on the Santa Fe National Historic Trail, and displays explain the trade goods, wagons and routes that connected Missouri to New Mexico.

Enjoy the Arkansas River Setting

The cottonwood-lined river valley around the fort offers a peaceful setting and chances to see birds and wildlife.

Top Trails

Bent's Old Fort Approach Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short, level path leads from the parking lot across the grounds to the fort entrance. It is accessible for most visitors and gives a view of the fort from a distance.

Fort Grounds and River Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A casual walk around the fort and nearby river bottoms offers views of the adobe walls, cottonwoods and the Arkansas River. It is best in the cooler parts of the day.

Visitor Centers

The visitor entrance and fort offer the main information for the site, including exhibits, a short film, a bookstore, restrooms and rangers who can give tours and answer questions. Admission fees are currently suspended, and hours may change seasonally and with scheduled access to the fort interior, so check the National Park Service website before going. Guided and self-guided tours are available.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the fort. La Junta, about 15 minutes west, has the closest hotels and motels along with restaurants and shops, and Las Animas, about 25 minutes east, has a few additional options. Rocky Ford, about 25 minutes west, is known for its melons and offers small-town accommodations. Pueblo, about an hour and 15 minutes away, has a broader range of chain hotels, and Colorado Springs has a wide variety about 2 hours away. Camping is available at John Martin Reservoir State Park near Hasty, about 40 minutes east, and at Lake Pueblo State Park. Check each property's website for current availability.
