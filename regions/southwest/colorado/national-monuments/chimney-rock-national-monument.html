Chimney Rock National Monument

How to Get There

From Durango (47 mi): Take W 11th St to US-550 S/Camino Del Rio. Take US-160 E to CO-151 W in Archuleta County. Continue on CO-151 W. Drive to Forest Rd 617A.

Overview

Chimney Rock National Monument in the San Juan National Forest protects a remarkable Ancestral Puebloan site perched on a high ridge, thought to be an outlier of the great Chacoan culture centered at Chaco Canyon. Built around 1076 AD at 7,600 feet, the Great House Pueblo sits between two natural spires, Chimney Rock and Companion Rock, which line up with the rising moon during the major lunar standstill that happens about every 18.6 years. The 4,726-acre monument was designated in 2012 and combines archaeology, astronomy and mountain scenery. Visitors come for the guided tours of the ruins and the sweeping views over the Piedra River valley.

Access

The monument is on CO-151, about 17 miles west of Pagosa Springs and 20 miles from Pagosa's center, in Archuleta County. Visitors reach it by driving US-160 and then CO-151 south, with a paved road leading up to the entry gate and parking. Most people arrive by car, and the drive from Durango takes about an hour and a half. Access beyond the visitor center is by guided tour or self-guided walk depending on the season.

Seasons

The monument is open seasonally, usually from mid-May through mid-September, when the Forest Service and Chimney Rock Interpretive Association run guided tours and programs. Summer is the main season, with warm days and thunderstorms in the afternoons. Late spring and early fall are cooler and quieter, and the area is closed in winter because of snow. Special night programs around the lunar standstill and full moons are sometimes offered, so check the schedule before visiting.

Things to Do

Tour a Chacoan Great House on a Mountaintop

Chimney Rock National Monument's Great House Pueblo stands on a narrow ridge with views in all directions. Guided tours explain the construction, the culture and the connection to Chaco Canyon.

Learn About Lunar Alignments

The spires frame the rising moon during the lunar standstill, and archaeologists believe the Chacoan builders timed construction to the event. Rangers share the astronomy behind the site.

Visit the Visitor Center and Exhibits

The visitor center has exhibits on Ancestral Puebloan life, archaeology and the monument's natural history. It is the best first stop before a tour.

Walk the Mesa Village Trail

A short loop leads to the remains of a small village with stone foundations. It gives a closer look at everyday Ancestral Puebloan homes.

Enjoy the Views of the San Juan Mountains

From the ridge, the view stretches across the Piedra River valley to the San Juan Mountains. The scenery makes the site a favorite for photography.

Top Trails

Great House Trail

Length: Approximately 0.6 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Strenuous
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs to the Great House Pueblo atop the ridge, offering views of the spires and the valley. It is steep in places, and guided tours include the walk.

Mesa Village Trail

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short, easy loop leads to a small village site on the mesa. It is suitable for most visitors and includes interpretive signs.

Chimney Rock Overlook Route

Length: Included with guided site access
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

The tour route climbs to an overlook of the spires and surrounding country, and is led by trained guides who explain the archaeology.

Visitor Centers

The Chimney Rock visitor center near the entrance is the main stop and is open during the summer season. It has exhibits, a small bookstore, restrooms and staff or volunteers from the Chimney Rock Interpretive Association who lead tours and answer questions. Guided tours typically run from mid-May through mid-September, and reservations may be recommended, so check the Forest Service or interpretive association websites for schedules and times.

Camping and Lodging

There is no lodging at the monument, and camping is limited to nearby national forest sites and private campgrounds. Pagosa Springs, about 20 minutes east, has the nearest hotels and inns, including The Springs Resort and Spa with its hot springs, as well as many motels, lodges, cabins and vacation rentals. Campers can find sites at Pagosa Riverside Campground and in the San Juan National Forest. Durango, about an hour and a half west, offers a wider range of lodging, including the Strater Hotel. Check each property's website for current availability.
