Camp Hale National Monument

★5.0

How to Get There

From Leadville (16 mi): Take US-24 North toward Tennessee Pass; the monument entrance is on the left just before the summit.

Overview

Camp Hale – Continental Divide National Monument in central Colorado protects the site of a World War II training camp where the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division learned to fight on skis and in mountain terrain. Built in 1942 at about 9,200 feet in the Pando Valley, the camp trained thousands of soldiers who later fought in the Italian Alps, and many veterans later helped found Colorado's ski industry. The monument was designated in 2022 and also covers high-elevation lands along the Continental Divide, with wetlands, forests and alpine peaks. Visitors come to see the camp's remains, to learn about the soldiers' history and to hike, ski and camp in a spectacular mountain setting.

Access

The monument is on US-24 near Tennessee Pass, about 16 miles north of Leadville and about 25 miles south of Minturn and Vail. Most visitors arrive by car, and a paved highway leads to the main camp area with graded forest roads to trailheads and campgrounds. Leadville is roughly 1 hour from Colorado Springs and 2 hours from Denver, while Vail is about 30 to 40 minutes away. Snow can close some forest roads in winter.

Seasons

The monument is open year-round, but conditions change a lot. Summer offers wildflowers, green meadows and easy access for hiking, mountain biking and camping, though afternoon thunderstorms are common. Fall brings golden aspens, and winter turns the valley into a snowy playground for cross-country skiing and snowshoeing. Snow can make some roads inaccessible, and spring is a muddy season. Temperatures at this elevation are cool even in summer, so bring layers.

Things to Do

Explore the Remains of a WWII Mountain Training Camp

Camp Hale – Continental Divide National Monument has the foundations of barracks, bunkers and other buildings spread across the Pando Valley. Interpretive signs tell how soldiers trained for high-altitude warfare.

Learn the Story of the 10th Mountain Division

The division's ski troops were famed for their role in fighting in Italy, and many veterans returned to build ski areas such as Vail and Aspen. Exhibits and memorials help connect the camp to Colorado's later history.

Hike, Bike and Ski the Valley

Trails and forest roads cross the valley and climb into the surrounding mountains, with options for walking, mountain biking and, in winter, Nordic skiing and snowshoeing.

Watch for Wildlife and Wetlands

The Eagle River headwaters and restored wetlands support birds, elk and other wildlife. Early morning and evening are good times to look for animals.

Visit Nearby Ski Cooper and Leadville

Ski Cooper, where the 10th Mountain troops also trained, and the historic mining town of Leadville are close. They round out a day of history and mountain scenery.

Top Trails

Camp Hale Historic Loop

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A relaxed walk or ski route passes foundations and interpretive signs in the valley floor. It is a good introduction to the camp's layout and history.

Continental Divide Trail near Tennessee Pass

Length: Varies; sections of several miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Sections of the Continental Divide Trail near the pass provide forested climbs and high mountain views. They are popular with hikers who want a taste of the divide.

Visitor Centers

Camp Hale – Continental Divide National Monument does not have a large visitor center, and services at the camp are limited. Interpretive signs and kiosks explain the camp's history and rules, and the U.S. Forest Service provides additional information at its offices in Leadville and Minturn and on its White River National Forest website. The Colorado Ski and Snowboard Museum in Vail and the 10th Mountain Division Hut Association also tell parts of the story.

Camping and Lodging

Camping is available in the Camp Hale area at developed Forest Service campgrounds and dispersed sites, with rules about stays and fire restrictions, and there is no lodging at the monument. Leadville, about 20 minutes south, has the historic Delaware Hotel, several inns and motels and a hostel, and Minturn and Vail, about 25 to 40 minutes north, offer a wide range of lodges and hotels, including the Lodge at Vail. Copper Mountain and Frisco are also within about 45 minutes with condo and hotel lodging. Check each property's website for current availability.
