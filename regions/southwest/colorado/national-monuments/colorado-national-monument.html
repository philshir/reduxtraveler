How to Get There

From Grand Junction: Head north on S 13th St toward Ute Ave. Turn left onto Ute Ave. Ute Ave turns slightly right and becomes S 1st St. Turn left onto W Grand Ave. Continue onto Broadway. Turn left onto Monument Rd.

Overview

Colorado National Monument is a landscape of sheer red sandstone canyons, towering monoliths and sweeping mesas on the edge of the Colorado Plateau near Grand Junction. Local advocate John Otto lobbied for the area's protection, and it became a national monument in 1911. Its 23-mile Rim Rock Drive, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps, winds along the canyon rims with tunnels and viewpoints, and trails lead to formations such as Independence Monument and Coke Ovens. People come for the scenic drive, hiking, road cycling, desert bighorn sheep and views that rival those of better-known national parks.

Access

The monument has two entrances: the west entrance near Fruita and the east entrance near Grand Junction, both just a few miles from downtown. Visitors drive Rim Rock Drive, which connects the two, and the road is paved and well maintained though steep and winding. Grand Junction is about 4 hours from Denver and about 5 hours from Salt Lake City, and the regional airport is only minutes from the monument. Most visitors arrive by car, and many cyclists tackle the drive as a challenge.

Seasons

The monument is open year-round. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking, with warm days and pleasant temperatures. Summers are hot, often over 100°F, so early morning or evening hikes are best, and thunderstorms can occur. Winter is mild with only light snow, though ice can occasionally affect the road. The Independence Monument climb is a July 4 tradition, and the visitor center has extended summer hours.

Things to Do

Drive Rim Rock Drive

This 23-mile scenic road climbs from the valley to the plateau, passing viewpoints, tunnels and overlooks of canyons and red rock towers. It is one of Colorado's great scenic drives and a favorite for road cyclists.

See Independence Monument and Other Landmarks

Independence Monument is a free-standing sandstone tower that rises over 400 feet. Other highlights include Balanced Rock, Coke Ovens and Monument Canyon.

Hike Canyon and Rim Trails

Trails range from short overlook walks to long canyon hikes. Many offer expansive views and a close look at colorful rock formations.

Look for Desert Bighorn Sheep

The monument is home to desert bighorn sheep, which are often seen on cliffs and slopes. Visitors may also see eagles, lizards and mule deer.

Camp at Saddlehorn Campground

The campground near the visitor center lets guests stay among the rocks, with starry skies and sunrise views.

Top Trails

Serpents Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This restored historic road climbs a series of tight switchbacks from the valley to the rim. It is steep and exposed, and it offers views of the canyon and surrounding mesas.

Devils Kitchen Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail leads through a canyon to a rock formation known as Devils Kitchen, which is a series of boulders forming a cave-like space. It is a popular half-day hike.

Monument Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 6 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Strenuous

This long trail passes Independence Monument and other towering formations in the canyon. It is best as a shuttle hike or an out-and-back to a chosen point.

Otto's Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk leads to a rim viewpoint of Independence Monument, a good place for photography. It is easy and suits most visitors.

Visitor Centers

The Colorado National Monument Visitor Center, at Saddlehorn, is the main information point. It has exhibits about geology, wildlife and history, a bookstore, a short film, restrooms and rangers who provide maps and current conditions. Hours are typically 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., with extended summer hours, and the center is closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. A smaller contact station may operate at the east entrance in some seasons.

Camping and Lodging

Saddlehorn Campground offers about 80 sites for tents and RVs near the visitor center and is typically reservable through Recreation.gov, and there is no lodging within the monument. Fruita and Grand Junction, 5 to 15 minutes away, have a wide choice of hotels and motels, including the Grand Vista Hotel, Hotel Maverick and many chain hotels. Palisade, about 25 minutes east, offers bed-and-breakfasts and wine country stays, and the James M. Robb Colorado River State Park has camping nearby. Check each property's website for current availability.
