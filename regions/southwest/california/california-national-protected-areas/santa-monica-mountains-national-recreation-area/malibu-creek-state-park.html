How to Get There

From Los Angeles (34 mi): Take US-101 N to Las Virgenes Rd in Calabasas, then head south on Las Virgenes Rd for 4 miles to reach Malibu Creek State Park.

Overview

Malibu Creek State Park is a California state park of more than 8,000 acres in the Santa Monica Mountains, with volcanic cliffs, oak woodlands, a perennial creek and the Rock Pool. For decades the land served as a movie ranch, owned in turn by Paramount, 20th Century Fox and others, and it is best known as the setting for the film and television series M*A*S*H. Much of the land was donated by entertainer Bob Hope, and California State Parks acquired it in the early 1970s, establishing the state park in 1974 and opening it to the public in 1976. It is part of the Santa Monica Mountains National Recreation Area. Visitors come to hike to the M*A*S*H site, Century Lake and the Rock Pool, to mountain bike and to camp.

Access

The park entrance is on Las Virgenes Road in Calabasas, about four miles south of US-101 and roughly 34 miles from downtown Los Angeles. Most visitors drive and pay for day-use parking at the main lot, from which the popular trails start. There is no regular public transit to the park gate. The main trail to the M*A*S*H site and Century Lake is a wide, mostly flat fire road, and the visitor center is about a mile up it. Dogs are not allowed on the trails, and the park gates close at 10:00 PM.

Seasons

Spring is the most popular season, when the hills turn green and wildflowers bloom and the creek and Rock Pool are at their fullest after winter rains. Summer is hot and dry in the canyons and the creek can shrink, so hikers should start early and carry water. Fall brings clear skies and fewer crowds, and winter is mild but storms can make trails muddy or cause closures. The park has also been affected by wildfire, including the 2018 Woolsey Fire, and can close during fire danger, so check conditions before visiting.

Things to Do

Visit the Famous M*A*S*H Filming Site

One of the park's most popular attractions is the historic filming location used for the television series M*A*S*H. Visitors can hike to the site and view remnants of the set while learning about the park's long history as a major Hollywood filming location for movies and television shows, including Planet of the Apes and numerous westerns.

Enjoy Hiking and Outdoor Recreation

With more than 35 miles of trails, Malibu Creek State Park is a favorite destination for hiking, mountain biking, horseback riding, rock climbing, trail running, and nature photography. The varied terrain provides options for visitors of all experience levels.

Visit the Visitor Center

The Malibu Creek State Park Visitor Center features exhibits highlighting the area's Native American history, wildlife, ecology, and Hollywood heritage. Interactive displays and interpretive materials help visitors gain a deeper appreciation for the park's natural and cultural significance.

Top Trails

Malibu Creek Trail to the M*A*S*H Site

Length: Approximately 4.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail follows Malibu Creek through scenic canyon landscapes and leads to the historic M*A*S*H filming site. The route is family-friendly and offers excellent opportunities for photography, wildlife viewing, and exploring Hollywood history.

Rock Pool Trail

Length: Approximately 2.4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A favorite destination for visitors, this trail leads to a picturesque rock pool surrounded by dramatic cliffs and volcanic formations. The area is popular for sightseeing, photography, and relaxing in a beautiful natural setting.

Bulldog Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 15 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Difficult

This challenging trail traverses ridgelines and mountain terrain with sweeping views of the Santa Monica Mountains, Malibu coastline, and surrounding valleys. It is popular among experienced hikers, mountain bikers, and trail runners.

Ann Skager Accessible Trail

Length: Approximately 1.16 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Designed for accessibility, this paved and compacted trail offers views of Las Virgenes Creek, Stokes Canyon, and the surrounding mountains. Interpretive exhibits and accessible facilities make it suitable for visitors of varying mobility levels.

The park was also a key filming location for the film M*A*S*H (1970) and the subsequent television series (1972-83). The landscape was particularly seen in the opening credits for the show as helicopters carrying wounded approach the hospital with the recognizable Goat Buttes in the background.

The park is still used for occasional filming.

Visitor Centers

The park's visitor center is the Hunt House, a former club member's weekend home on the main trail about a mile from the trailhead parking lot. It is staffed on weekends from about noon to 4:00 PM, and it is the best place to ask about trail and filming-site information. A separate Santa Monica Mountains National Recreation Area visitor center operates at nearby King Gillette Ranch in Calabasas.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground near the entrance, with sites that can be reserved through ReserveCalifornia and that are popular on spring and fall weekends. For motels and hotels, the nearest options are in Calabasas, about four miles away, with more choices in Agoura Hills, Thousand Oaks and Malibu, all within about 30 minutes to an hour. The larger selection of hotels in Santa Monica and the San Fernando Valley is about an hour away.
