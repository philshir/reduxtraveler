How to Get There

From Fort Bragg (6–7 mi): Drive south on CA-1, turn right onto Point Cabrillo Drive, then turn onto South Caspar Drive and continue to Headlands Drive where parking is located outside the reserve.

Overview

The Caspar Headlands State Natural Reserve preserves a small but striking section of the Mendocino coastline about four miles north of the village of Mendocino, where windswept bluffs and wave-sculpted rocks create a dramatic meeting of land and sea. State natural reserves are units of the California state park system set aside to protect areas with outstanding or unusual natural or scenic values, and this one is managed with conservation as its primary goal, which means access is more limited than at a typical state beach. Perched above the Pacific, the reserve offers sweeping vistas, tide pools below teeming with marine life, and coastal grasses and wildflowers that carpet the headlands in spring. Its quiet, undeveloped character makes it a favorite for photographers, nature lovers and anyone seeking solitude along a rugged shoreline, and a residential community in Caspar South shares the reserve area, so visitors should respect private property.

Access

The reserve is on the Mendocino coast in Mendocino County, off Point Cabrillo Drive between Caspar and Mendocino. To reach it from Highway 1, take Point Cabrillo Drive south past the Caspar Beach campground area, turn right onto Caspar Drive, and continue to Headlands Drive. Parking is outside the reserve on Caspar Drive. Access to the reserve is arranged ahead of time with an entry permit from the Mendocino Sector Office of California State Parks, so contact the park or check its website before your visit. Drones are not allowed. There is no public transportation, and the closest town for gas, food and lodging is Fort Bragg, about 6 to 7 miles north.

Seasons

The reserve can be visited year-round, and the weather on the coast can change quickly, so bring layers. Spring brings wildflowers on the headlands, summer is the warmest and busiest season with frequent coastal fog, and fall often offers clear skies. Winter is the best time for dramatic waves and for spotting migrating gray whales offshore, typically from December through April, though storms can create hazardous surf and slippery bluffs. Low tides are the best time for exploring tide pools, so check tide tables before you go.

Things to Do

Explore a Hidden Gem on the Mendocino Coast

Caspar Headlands State Natural Reserve protects approximately 424 acres of rugged coastline, forested headlands, and marine habitats between the communities of Caspar and Mendocino. The reserve offers visitors a peaceful coastal experience featuring dramatic ocean views, scenic trails, native plant communities, and opportunities to observe the natural beauty of California's North Coast.

Enjoy Spectacular Coastal Views

The reserve's bluff-top locations provide sweeping views of the Pacific Ocean, rocky shorelines, sea stacks, and nearby Caspar Bay. Visitors can enjoy photography, sightseeing, and scenic walks while experiencing one of the less-crowded stretches of the Mendocino coastline.

Observe Marine Wildlife and Seabirds

Caspar Headlands is an excellent location for wildlife viewing. Visitors may spot migrating gray whales, harbor seals, sea lions, seabirds, and shorebirds throughout the year. Offshore rocks and coastal habitats support a diverse array of marine life and bird species.

Explore Forests and Coastal Habitats

Beyond its oceanfront scenery, the reserve contains forests of Bishop pine, coastal scrub habitats, and native plant communities. These environments provide important habitat for wildlife and create a unique blend of coastal and woodland landscapes.

Experience Tide Pools and Rocky Shorelines

During favorable tide conditions, visitors can explore portions of the rocky shoreline and observe intertidal ecosystems. Tide pools may reveal sea stars, anemones, crabs, mussels, and other marine organisms that thrive along the Mendocino Coast.

Enjoy Photography and Sunset Viewing

The reserve's dramatic cliffs, ocean vistas, crashing waves, and changing light conditions make it a favorite destination for landscape photographers. Sunset views over the Pacific Ocean are especially spectacular from the coastal overlooks.

Top Trails

Caspar Headlands Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic trail traverses coastal bluffs and headlands while providing beautiful views of the Pacific Ocean, Caspar Bay, and surrounding shoreline. It is one of the best ways to experience the reserve's dramatic coastal scenery.

Point Cabrillo Connector Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail connects portions of the reserve with nearby coastal attractions and offers opportunities for wildlife viewing, photography, and enjoying the region's natural beauty.

Coastal Bluff Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk leading to scenic overlooks where visitors can enjoy panoramic ocean views, whale watching opportunities, and spectacular perspectives of the rugged Mendocino coastline.

Forest and Headlands Loop

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This route combines coastal viewpoints with inland forested sections, showcasing the reserve's diverse ecosystems while providing opportunities for birdwatching, nature observation, and photography.

Visitor Centers

There is no visitor center at the reserve. Entry permits and information are handled through the Mendocino Sector Office of California State Parks, and the park can be reached at (707) 937-5804. The towns of Mendocino and Fort Bragg also have visitor information, and the nearby Point Cabrillo Light, about a mile south of Caspar Headlands State Beach, is another place to learn about the coast.

Camping and Lodging

Camping is not offered in the reserve itself. A private campground and RV park lies just north of the reserve at Caspar Beach, and nearby state parks, including Russian Gulch State Park and MacKerricher State Park, have campgrounds that are reserved through ReserveCalifornia. For inns, motels and restaurants, the closest towns are Mendocino, about four miles south, and Fort Bragg, 6 to 7 miles north. Willits and Ukiah, within about an hour or two, also have lodging.
