Empire Mine State Historic Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest

Empire Mine State Historic Park

Former gold mine complex featuring historic mining structures, landscaped gardens, underground mining exhibits, and stories from one of California's richest hard-rock gold discoveries.

How to Get There

From Grass Valley: Travel east from downtown on East Main Street, continue onto East Empire Street, and follow the road to 10791 East Empire Street at the entrance to Empire Mine State Historic Park.

Overview

Empire Mine State Historic Park in Grass Valley preserves one of the oldest, deepest and richest hard-rock gold mines in California. Gold was discovered on Ophir Hill in 1850, and the mine operated for about a century, producing roughly 5.8 million ounces of gold before closing in 1956. California State Parks bought the surface property in 1974 and established the park in 1975, and the site is now listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Visitors come to see the surviving mine buildings and machinery, the entrance to the flooded underground workings that once ran for hundreds of miles beneath the Sierra Nevada foothills, and the former home of mine owner William Bourn Jr. with its restored gardens.

Access

The park is on the eastern edge of Grass Valley in Nevada County, in the Sierra Nevada foothills about 24 miles north of Auburn on Highway 49. From the Empire Street exit, follow East Empire Street to the main entrance at 10791 East Empire Street, where there is parking beside the visitor center. A second trailhead, the Penn Gate parking lot, gives direct access to the park’s trail system and is open sunrise to sunset. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and there is no entrance fee for the trails.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and the mine yard, visitor center and tours run in every season. Hours are shorter in winter (November through February) than in summer, and the grounds close on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year’s Day. Tour schedules change with the season, with garden tours offered only from May through October and the mineyard tour sometimes replaced by an estate tour on very hot days. Summers are warm and dry, spring and fall are the most comfortable for walking the trails, and winters are cool and occasionally wet or snowy.

Things to Do

Tour the Mine Yard and Visitor Center

The visitor center houses “The Secret Room,” a scale model of the underground workings, and is the starting point for guided tours. Volunteer-led highlight tours are included with admission, and audio tour boxes are available when a guide is not.

Visit the Bourn Cottage and Gardens

The estate tour covers the former home of mine owner William Bourn Jr., the clubhouse and the surrounding grounds. In season, separate garden tours show the restored plantings around the cottage.

Hike, Bike or Ride the Park Trails

About 14 miles of trails wander through forest and past old mining sites, and are open to hikers, mountain bikers and horseback riders. The trails are free to use and can be reached from the Penn Gate parking lot as well as from the main entrance.

Picnic and Explore the Historic Grounds

Shaded lawns and the open mine yard are pleasant places to stop for a picnic between tours. Leashed pets are allowed in outdoor areas but not inside buildings.

Top Trails

Hardrock Trail

Length: Approximately 1.6 miles
Elevation Gain: Gentle, rolling terrain
Difficulty: Easy

This trail runs through the forest near the park’s Penn Gate parking area and connects with other trails in the park, including the Osborne Hill Loop. It is a good choice for a short walk past mining relics.

Osborne Hill Loop

Length: Approximately 2.4 miles
Elevation Gain: Gentle to moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This loop circles Osborne Hill through the backcountry portion of the park, passing old mine workings and stands of pine and oak. Side trips to mining sites can easily add distance.

Visitor Centers

The Empire Mine State Historic Park Visitor Center is the main stop for orientation, tickets, tour sign-up and exhibits, including The Secret Room model of the underground workings. It keeps shorter hours from November through February (10am to 4pm) than from March through October (10am to 5pm), and is closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year’s Day. Guided tours leave from the mine yard, and the staff and volunteers can recommend trails and provide maps.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. The closest hotels, inns, restaurants and services are in Grass Valley and neighboring Nevada City, both within a short drive, with Auburn about 24 miles to the south. For camping, Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park has a reservation-only campground roughly an hour away, and campgrounds can also be found elsewhere in the Sierra foothills. Because Grass Valley and Nevada City are popular in summer and during fall color, book ahead for weekends.
