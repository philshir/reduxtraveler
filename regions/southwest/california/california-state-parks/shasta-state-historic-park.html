Shasta State Historic Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. California
  3. Northern California
  4. Shasta State Historic Park

Shasta State Historic Park
Ian Poellet, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Preserved Gold Rush ghost town featuring historic brick buildings, mining-era ruins, museums, and interpretive exhibits that recount the rise and decline of a once-thriving frontier settlement.

General Information
Hours:
Park grounds: Daily, sunrise to sunset
Museum and Visitor Center: Daily, 10am to 4pm
Fees:
Vehicle day-use fee required
Additional fees may apply for special tours and events
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed on leash in outdoor areas but are not permitted inside buildings
Closest cities with hotels:
Redding, California (approximately 10 mi)
Seasons:
All year, with spring and fall offering the most comfortable temperatures for exploring the historic townsite
Location:
15312 CA-299, Redding, CA 96003
Website:
parks.ca.gov/?page_id=456

How to Get There

From Redding (10 mi): Take CA-299 E from Redding, continue through the foothills northeast of the city, and follow signs to 15312 CA-299 at Shasta State Historic Park.

Overview

Shasta State Historic Park preserves the ruins of Shasta City, a Gold Rush town about six miles west of Redding. Gold was discovered in the area in the late 1840s, and the town became a commercial center and a shipping point for mule trains and stagecoaches, growing to about 3,500 residents and becoming the largest settlement in Shasta County at its peak. It declined after the Central Pacific Railroad bypassed it for Redding in 1873, and the county seat moved to Redding in 1888. The site was named a California Historical Landmark in 1932 and was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1971. Visitors come to walk among the brick ruins, tour the restored 1861 courthouse museum and imagine life in a frontier boomtown.

Access

The park is on Highway 299 West in the community of Shasta, about 6 to 10 miles west of downtown Redding depending on the route, and it is easily reached by car in under 15 minutes from the city. Parking is available at the historic townsite, and the courthouse and the old brick ruins are within a short walk of each other. Surfaces are a mix of paved paths, dirt trails and uneven historic ground, and some areas have been affected by fire damage and repairs, so wear sturdy shoes. Pets are limited in this park, with dogs allowed only in the parking lot and on paved roadways according to California State Parks, so check before bringing a dog.

Seasons

Shasta is open year-round, and the most comfortable times for walking the townsite are spring and fall, when temperatures are mild and the surrounding hills are green or golden. Summers in the Redding area are hot, so plan outdoor visits for the morning and use the shaded buildings and museum during the afternoon. Winters are cool and can be rainy, with fewer visitors. The Courthouse Museum has limited days and hours, and the Litsch Store across the street is open for guided tours during summer and fall, so check current schedules before you go. The 2018 Carr Fire damaged parts of the park, and some trails and buildings may be closed for repairs.

Things to Do

Tour the Courthouse Museum

The 1861 Shasta County Courthouse has been restored and holds exhibits on the town’s history along with a collection of historic California artwork. A gift shop is in the building, and a small admission fee is charged for the museum.

Walk Through the Ghost Town

The brick ruins of former stores, hotels and public buildings line the old main street, and interpretive signs explain what each building was used for. Other remaining structures include the post office, a church and a Masonic lodge, along with the Litsch Store, which is open for tours in the summer and fall.

Visit Nearby Whiskeytown Lake

Whiskeytown National Recreation Area is only a few miles west and offers swimming, boating, waterfalls and hiking, making a good pairing with the historic park.

Visitor Centers

The park does not have a separate visitor center. The Courthouse Museum and its gift shop serve as the main information point, with exhibits, staff and volunteers, and the park also has restrooms, drinking water and picnic areas. Hours for the museum are limited to certain days of the week, so confirm them on the California State Parks website or by calling the park at (530) 243-8194 before your visit. A small cafe operates in the historic townsite.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Redding, about 6 to 10 miles east, has the area’s widest selection of hotels, motels and restaurants. Campgrounds are available at nearby Whiskeytown National Recreation Area, a short drive to the west, and around Shasta Lake, about 20 to 30 minutes north. Lodging is easy to find outside of holiday weekends, but summer is the busiest season on the lakes, so book ahead.
