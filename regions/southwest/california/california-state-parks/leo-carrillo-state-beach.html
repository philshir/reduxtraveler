Leo Carrillo State Beach

Coastal state beach known for its sea caves, rugged cliffs, tide pools, and scenic shoreline set against the Santa Monica Mountains.

How to Get There

From Santa Monica (28 mi): Take CA-1 N (Pacific Coast Highway) along the coastline for about 28 miles into Malibu, where the park entrance will be clearly marked along the ocean side of the highway.

Overview

Leo Carrillo State Park (often called Leo Carrillo State Beach) is a state park on the Malibu coast on the Los Angeles and Ventura County line. It was established in 1953 and named for Leo Carrillo, an actor and conservationist who served on the State Parks commission. The park covers about 2,500 acres, with roughly 1.5 miles of beach and a backcountry that stretches into the Santa Monica Mountains. It is best known for its tide pools, sea caves and reefs, and the coastline has appeared in many films and television shows, including Gidget, Grease and Cast Away. Visitors come to swim, surf, beachcomb and explore the rocky shore at low tide, and to camp in the park’s campground.

Access

The park is on Pacific Coast Highway (Highway 1) about 28 miles northwest of Santa Monica, at 35000 West Pacific Coast Highway in Malibu. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and parking is available in the main lots with a day-use fee. The state parks website lists day-use parking hours of 8am to 10pm. Dogs on leash are allowed in the day-use areas, the campground and on North Beach, but not on backcountry trails or South Beach. Traffic on Pacific Coast Highway can be heavy on summer weekends, so arriving early is wise.

Seasons

Summer is the busiest season, with warm sun, surf and swimming, and the beach and campground fill on weekends. The tide pools and sea caves are best explored at low tide, so check a tide table before you go. Spring brings wildflowers to the backcountry, and winter storms bring higher surf and occasional closures. In the dry season fire danger is high, and State Parks has posted coordinated closure protocols for the park during Red Flag Warnings in the Santa Monica Mountains. The 2018 Woolsey Fire burned through almost the entire park, which has since recovered and reopened.

Things to Do

Explore the Tide Pools and Sea Caves

At low tide, the rocky shoreline reveals tide pools where visitors can look for sea stars, anemones and crabs, and sea caves can be explored along the shore. Take care with slippery rocks and incoming tides.

Swim, Surf and Relax on the Beach

The park has long stretches of sand and is a favorite for swimming, surfing, windsurfing, surf fishing and beachcombing. Snorkeling and scuba diving are also popular along the reefs.

Camp Near the Beach

The campground is a popular base for families and beachgoers. Reservations are made through ReserveCalifornia.

Hike in the Santa Monica Mountains

Trails climb from the park into the Santa Monica Mountains, with ocean views and wildflowers in spring. Dogs are not allowed on the backcountry trails.

Top Trails

Nicholas Flat Trail

Length: Approximately 3.2 miles
Elevation Gain: About 1,680 feet
Difficulty: Difficult

This trail climbs steadily from the coast to a ridgeline and the meadow at Nicholas Flat, with views over the Pacific and the mountains. It is best hiked in the cooler months and early in the day.

Willow Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 0.9 miles
Elevation Gain: About 560 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

Willow Creek Trail climbs through the canyon and forms the eastern part of the Leo Carrillo scenic loop with the Nicholas Flat Trail. It is a shorter option for a taste of the backcountry.

Visitor Centers

The park lists an Environmental Learning Center and visitor center with interpretive exhibits, along with rangers and information at the entrance. Hours are not posted online, so call the park at (310) 457-8144 for current details. Information on tide pools, trails and fire closures is also available from California State Parks.

Camping and Lodging

The park’s campground can be reserved through ReserveCalifornia at 1-800-444-7275, and same-day bookings are possible when sites are available. Other lodging is in Malibu, Oxnard, Camarillo and Thousand Oaks, and Santa Monica is about 28 miles to the southeast. For more camping, Point Mugu State Park lies a short drive west along the coast.
