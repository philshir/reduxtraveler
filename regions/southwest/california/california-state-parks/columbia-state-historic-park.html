How to Get There

From Sonora (3 mi): Take CA-49 north from Sonora, continue a short distance to Columbia, and follow signs to Jackson Street in the heart of Columbia State Historic Park.

Overview

Columbia State Historic Park preserves one of the most complete Gold Rush towns in California, a restored mining community in the Sierra Nevada foothills of Tuolumne County. Gold was discovered here in March 1850, and Columbia grew so quickly that it was one of the state’s largest cities by 1853, earning the nickname “Gem of the Southern Mines.” Unlike many mining camps that vanished after the boom, it survived, and the state proclaimed it a state historic park in 1945. The downtown is also a National Historic Landmark District, with almost 30 Gold Rush-era buildings, many of the brick structures dating from rebuilding after fires in the 1850s. Visitors come to walk streets closed to through traffic, ride a stagecoach, watch costumed staff at candy and soap shops, pan for gold, and see the museum, saloons, theatre and restaurants, many of which operate in original buildings. Surrounded by the rolling foothills of the Sierra Nevada, the park is a popular destination for families and school groups.

Access

Columbia lies about 3 miles north of Sonora off Highway 49, the Gold Country road that runs through the Sierra Nevada foothills. Most visitors drive, and there is no public transportation to the park. Park admission and parking are free, and the historic district is easy to explore on foot, though some attractions, tours and special events charge fees. Dogs are allowed on leash outdoors but not inside historic buildings.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and spring and fall offer the most comfortable weather for exploring on foot. Summers in the foothills are hot, so plan on shade and water, while winter is cooler and quieter with occasional rain. Park exhibits are open from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM from October through April and until 5:00 PM from May through September, and the park is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day. Some attractions, such as stagecoach rides, run seasonally and depend on weather, and the town hosts special events throughout the year, including Gold Rush Days on the second Saturday of each month.

Things to Do

Stroll the Historic District

The main streets are lined with original brick and wooden buildings from the 1850s, many of which now hold shops, a candy kitchen, saloons and restaurants staffed by people in period dress.

Ride a Stagecoach

Stagecoach rides run through the park when season and weather permit, giving a taste of 19th-century travel.

Pan for Gold and Tour a Mine

Visitors can try gold panning and take mine tours to learn how miners worked the Mother Lode.

Visit Museums and Historic Buildings

The Columbia Museum, a two-story brick schoolhouse and a cemetery overlooking the town bring the Gold Rush era to life, and live theatre performances are offered at the Fallon Theatre.

Top Trails

Columbia Nature Trail

Length: About 1 mile
Elevation Gain: Varies
Difficulty: Easy

This one-mile nature trail near the historic town passes through foothill brushland where mule deer and wildflowers may be seen. It is best avoided during summer heat.

Visitor Centers

The park’s visitor center and museums are in the historic district and are typically open daily from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM, with longer hours in summer. The Columbia Museum, in the former Knapp building, tells the story of the town, and staff can provide maps, tour information and event schedules. For questions before you go, call the park at (209) 588-9128.

Camping and Lodging

Overnight stays inside the park are in two restored Gold Rush-era hotels, the City Hotel and the Fallon Hotel, which offer a small number of historic rooms and are reserved through ReserveCalifornia. There is no campground in the park itself. Sonora, about 3 miles away, has the closest selection of hotels, motels and restaurants, along with the nearby communities of Jamestown and Twain Harte. Within about 1 to 2 hours, Angels Camp, Murphys and Oakdale offer additional lodging, and campgrounds can be found in the surrounding Stanislaus National Forest.
