How to Get There

From Mammoth Lakes: Get on US-395 N from CA-203 E. Merge onto US-395 N. Follow CA-270 E to destination

Overview

Bodie State Historic Park is California’s best-known ghost town, preserved at 8,379 feet in the Bodie Hills east of the Sierra Nevada near Bridgeport, about 75 miles southeast of Lake Tahoe. Gold was discovered here in 1859, and after the Standard Company struck a rich ore deposit in the 1870s the town boomed, reaching a peak of an estimated 7,000 to 10,000 people by 1879. Mining declined from the 1880s, fires in 1892 and 1932 destroyed much of the town, and mining officially ended in 1942. Bodie was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1961 and a California state historic park in 1962, and it is maintained in a state of “arrested decay,” meaning the buildings are kept as they are rather than restored. About 100 weathered buildings still stand, many with furnishings and goods inside, and roughly 200,000 people visit each year to wander the dusty streets and see the Old West as it was left.

Access

Bodie is in the Bodie Hills of Mono County, reached from US-395 about seven miles south of Bridgeport. From there, State Route 270 leads east for 10 miles to the end of the pavement, and the last three miles are an unpaved road that can be rough, washboarded and slow. Drive slowly and call the park about current road conditions, especially after storms. There is no public transportation, and no food, gas or lodging in the park, so fill your tank and bring water and snacks. Restrooms are at the parking lot and picnic area, with outhouses elsewhere in the park, and it is a good idea to bring cash as well as a credit card.

Seasons

Summer is the main season, when the road is clear and the park keeps its longest hours, from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM in summer and 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM in winter. Fall is crisp and quieter, and at this elevation the weather can change quickly. Bodie is open year-round, but in winter the road is generally closed by heavy snow, and the park can be reached only by skis, snowshoes or snowmobiles, with sub-zero temperatures, strong winds and whiteouts common. Winter visitors should plan carefully, and winter museum and gift shop hours depend on staffing. Stamp Mill tours and history talks are seasonal or depend on staffing and weather, so check the park website before you go.

Things to Do

Walk Through the Ghost Town

Roam the streets on a self-guided walk past the saloon, hotel, church, school and houses, many with dusty furnishings still inside. A self-guided tour book is sold at the museum, with proceeds going to the park’s non-profit.

Tour the Standard Mill

When tours are offered, the Standard Mill, which housed a 20-stamp gold mill, can be visited with a guide. Ask at the museum about the daily tour schedule.

Visit the Museum

The museum, housed in the former Miners’ Union Hall, displays artifacts from the town’s mining and everyday life and has a bookstore.

Explore the Cemetery and Methodist Church

The 1882 Methodist church still stands, and the cemetery on the edge of town holds graves of miners and other early residents. Visitors should not collect or remove anything from the park.

Visitor Centers

The park’s visitor services are centered on the Bodie Museum in the former Miners’ Union Hall, which has exhibits, a bookstore and staff who can answer questions and give details on daily tours. There is no separate visitor center, and winter hours for the museum and gift shop depend on staffing. Information is also available from the California State Parks website.

Camping and Lodging

There is no lodging or camping in the park itself. California State Parks refers campers to the U.S. Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management for camping in the surrounding area. The closest town with motels, restaurants and services is Bridgeport, roughly 17 miles by road. Farther south, Lee Vining near Mono Lake, June Lake and Mammoth Lakes offer a wider selection of hotels, cabins and campgrounds within about 1 to 1.5 hours.
