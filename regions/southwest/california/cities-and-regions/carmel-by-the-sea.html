How to Get There

From San Francisco (122mi) Get on US-101 S. Continue on US-101 S to Monterey County. Continue on Ocean Ave. Take 8th Ave to Scenic Rd in Carmel-by-the-Sea.

Overview

Carmel-by-the-Sea is a storybook seaside village of about 3,200 residents on the Monterey Peninsula, roughly 120 miles south of San Francisco. It is known for its white-sand beach, fairy-tale cottages, art galleries, boutiques, tasting rooms and restaurants, and for strict rules that keep the town small, such as the lack of street numbers, neon signs and chain stores.

Visitors come to Carmel for its European-style charm, its walkable downtown on Ocean Avenue, the beach at the end of the street, and its pet-friendly hotels and shops. It is also the gateway to Point Lobos, the Carmel Valley and Big Sur, and it is close to Monterey and the golf courses of Pebble Beach.

History

The Spanish priest Junipero Serra moved Mission San Carlos Borromeo del Rio Carmelo to its present site in 1771, and the mission became his headquarters and burial place. The village was laid out in 1902 and became an artists' and writers' colony after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake drew artists south. The town incorporated in 1916, and the actor and director Clint Eastwood served as its mayor from 1986 to 1988.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The 17-Mile Drive, Carmel Beach, Point Lobos State Natural Reserve and Carmel Mission are the main sights, while Ocean Avenue is lined with courtyards and cottages. Tor House, built by poet Robinson Jeffers from stones of the shore, and the Hansel-and-Gretel-style cottages by Hugh Comstock are well-known architectural landmarks.

Culture and Museums

Carmel has more than 100 art galleries and a long tradition of theater and music, including the Forest Theater (1910), the Sunset Center, and the Carmel Bach Festival each July. The Carmel Mission Basilica and Museum displays the restored mission church, and the Carmel Art Association, founded in 1927, shows work by local artists.

Parks

Carmel Beach is a wide white-sand beach where dogs can run off-leash, and Mission Trail Nature Preserve has trails through coastal forest. Carmel River State Beach, Point Lobos State Natural Reserve and the Garrapata and Big Sur coast are nearby, offering tide pools, sea otters, harbor seals and migrating whales.

Seasons and Weather

Carmel has a cool, mild coastal climate, with highs in the 60s°F most of the year and summer fog that often lingers until the afternoon. Fall is usually the warmest and sunniest season, winter is wetter, with about 20 inches of rain, and the evenings are cool all year, so layers are useful.

Things to Do

Experience One of California's Most Charming Coastal Villages

Carmel-by-the-Sea is a picturesque seaside community on California's Monterey Peninsula known for its fairy-tale cottages, art galleries, white-sand beaches, and stunning ocean scenery. The town's unique architecture, walkable streets, and artistic heritage have made it one of the state's most beloved coastal destinations.

Relax at Carmel Beach

Carmel Beach is one of the most beautiful beaches on the West Coast, featuring soft white sand, scenic cypress trees, and panoramic views of Carmel Bay. Visitors can enjoy beach walks, photography, picnicking, sunset viewing, and watching surfers and wildlife along the shoreline.

Explore the Village and Art Galleries

Carmel-by-the-Sea is renowned for its vibrant arts community. The downtown area features numerous art galleries, boutique shops, wine tasting rooms, cafés, and restaurants. Visitors can wander through picturesque courtyards and hidden passageways while discovering local art and unique architecture.

Visit Mission San Carlos Borromeo de Carmelo

Founded in 1771 by Saint Junípero Serra, Carmel Mission is one of California's most significant historic landmarks. Visitors can explore the beautifully restored mission, museum exhibits, gardens, and historic structures while learning about California's Spanish colonial history.

Drive the Scenic 17-Mile Drive

Located adjacent to Carmel, the famous 17-Mile Drive passes through Pebble Beach and offers spectacular coastal scenery, golf courses, cypress groves, dramatic rocky shorelines, and iconic landmarks such as the Lone Cypress.

Visit Point Lobos State Natural Reserve

Just south of town, Point Lobos State Natural Reserve is often called the "Crown Jewel" of California's state park system. Visitors can enjoy hiking, wildlife viewing, photography, tide pooling, and some of the most dramatic coastal scenery found anywhere in California.

Top Trails

Carmel Beach Scenic Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This relaxing coastal walk follows the shoreline of Carmel Beach and provides spectacular views of Carmel Bay, Pebble Beach, offshore kelp forests, and the Pacific Ocean. It is especially popular at sunset.

Carmel River State Beach Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located at the southern edge of town, this scenic walk offers opportunities for birdwatching, wildlife viewing, and exploring the estuary where the Carmel River meets the Pacific Ocean.

Mission Trail Nature Preserve

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This nearby preserve contains woodland trails, native plant communities, and scenic overlooks with views of Carmel Valley, the Monterey Peninsula, and the surrounding coastal landscape.

Point Lobos Cypress Grove Trail

Length: Approximately 0.8 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short drive from Carmel-by-the-Sea, this iconic trail winds through one of the finest Monterey cypress groves in the world and offers spectacular views of rocky coves, turquoise waters, and coastal wildlife.
