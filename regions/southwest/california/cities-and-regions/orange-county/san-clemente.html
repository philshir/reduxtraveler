General Information

Pet Policy:
San Clemente is pet-friendly, offering dog parks, pet-friendly lodging, outdoor dining patios, and walking trails. Leashed dogs are permitted in many outdoor public areas, while individual beaches, parks, and businesses may have specific restrictions.
Seasons:
All year. Summer is popular for beaches and surfing, while spring and fall offer pleasant weather with fewer crowds. Winter provides mild coastal temperatures and scenic ocean views.

How to Get There

From Irvine, CA (25 mi): Take I-5 South through southern Orange County, continue to San Clemente, and use Avenida Palizada, Avenida Del Mar, or Camino De Estrella exits to access downtown, the pier, beaches, and coastal attractions.

Overview

San Clemente is a laid-back beach city on the Orange County coast, halfway between Los Angeles and San Diego, known as the “Spanish Village by the Sea” for its red-tile-roofed architecture, and for its surfing at Trestles and San Onofre, a pier, and the Casa Romantica Cultural Center and Gardens. The city has a walkable downtown, a coastal rail trail and the Richard Nixon “Western White House” history.

Things to Do

Enjoy Southern California's Spanish Village by the Sea

San Clemente is a charming coastal city located between Los Angeles and San Diego, celebrated for its Spanish Colonial Revival architecture, scenic beaches, surfing culture, and oceanfront recreation. Visitors can enjoy a relaxed beach-town atmosphere while exploring the city's historic landmarks, coastal trails, and beautiful Pacific shoreline.

Visit the San Clemente Pier

The historic San Clemente Pier is one of the city's most popular attractions. Visitors can enjoy fishing, dining, sightseeing, and panoramic ocean views while watching surfers and marine wildlife along the coastline. The pier also serves as a focal point for many community events and festivals.

Explore Downtown and Avenida Del Mar

Downtown San Clemente features boutique shops, art galleries, cafés, restaurants, and Spanish-style architecture that reflects the vision of the city's founder, Ole Hanson. Strolling along Avenida Del Mar provides opportunities for shopping, dining, and enjoying the city's unique coastal character.

Experience World-Class Surfing

San Clemente is renowned for its surfing conditions and is home to several famous surf breaks, including Trestles, San Onofre State Beach, and San Clemente State Beach. The city attracts surfers from around the world and has played an important role in Southern California's surf culture.

Relax at San Clemente State Beach

San Clemente State Beach offers sandy beaches, ocean views, camping facilities, picnicking areas, swimming, surfing, and beachcombing opportunities. The bluff-top setting provides spectacular coastal scenery and easy access to the shoreline.

Explore Casa Romantica Cultural Center and Gardens

Perched above the ocean near the pier, Casa Romantica is a historic Spanish Colonial Revival home built in 1927 by city founder Ole Hanson. Today, it serves as a cultural center featuring art exhibits, educational programs, beautiful gardens, and stunning coastal views.

Top Trails

San Clemente Beach Trail

Length: Approximately 2.3 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular oceanfront trail stretches along the beach between North Beach and Calafia Beach. The paved path provides excellent views of the Pacific Ocean, coastal bluffs, surfers, and the San Clemente Pier, making it ideal for walking, jogging, and cycling.

Sea Summit Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic bluff-top trail system offers panoramic ocean views, landscaped pathways, interpretive signage, and access to several overlooks. It is a popular destination for photography, sunset viewing, and coastal walks.

San Onofre Bluff Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located near San Clemente, this trail follows coastal bluffs overlooking world-famous surf breaks and provides access to beaches, scenic overlooks, and unique coastal habitats.

Forster Ranch Ridgeline Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This inland trail offers elevated views of San Clemente, surrounding hills, and the Pacific Ocean. It is popular among hikers, trail runners, and mountain bikers seeking a less crowded outdoor experience.

Architectural style

Known as the “Spanish Village by the Sea”, San Clemente has long been known for its Spanish style architecture. Downtown San Clemente restaurants and shops are adorned with red tile roofs, cream stucco walls, and dark wood doors and windows. The historic "North Beach" area is home to San Clemente's Casino Building and Ole Hanson Beach Club, which were renovated in 2010 and 2016. The homes in the area range in style, but stick to the Spanish theme for the most part. The area's oldest homes are in Southwest San Clemente, directly south of downtown and "North Beach" area, directly north of downtown. The homes in the Southwest Riviera neighborhood include several new constructions in the Cape Cod style, as well as new modern residences. More traditional, older homes sit in the Lasuen "boot" district; the neighborhood surrounding Lasuens or "Lost Winds" beach is characterized by a variety of styles in both single and double story fashion, with Hansons’s traditional Spanish style sprinkled throughout, crafting an eclectic atmosphere. The renovations to historic buildings in North Beach have sparked a revival in the area, attracting new residents and business owners.

Surfing

San Clemente is known for its many surfing locations, which include Trestles, Lowers, Middles & Uppers, Cotton's Point, Calafia Beach Park, Riviera, Lasuens (most often called Lost Winds), The Hole, T-Street, The Pier, Linda Lane, 204, North Beach and Poche Beach.
