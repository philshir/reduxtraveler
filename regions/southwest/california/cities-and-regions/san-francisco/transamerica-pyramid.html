Overview

The Transamerica Pyramid is a pyramid-shaped 48-story modernist skyscraper in San Francisco, California, and the second tallest building in the San Francisco skyline. Located at 600 Montgomery Street between Clay and Washington Streets in the city's Financial District, it was the tallest building in San Francisco from its completion in 1972 until 2018 when the newly-constructed Salesforce Tower surpassed its height. The building no longer houses the headquarters of the Transamerica Corporation, which moved its U.S. headquarters to Baltimore, Maryland. The building is still associated with the company by being depicted on the company's logo. Designed by architect William Pereira and built by Hathaway Dinwiddie Construction Company, the building stands at 853 feet (260 m). On completion in 1972 it was the eighth-tallest building in the world. It is also a popular tourist site. In 2020, the building was sold to NYC investor Michael Shvo, who in 2022 hired Norman Foster to redesign the interiors and renovate the building.

The building is a tall, four-sided pyramid with two "wings" to accommodate an elevator shaft on the east and a stairwell and a smoke tower on the west. The top 212 feet (65 m) of the building is the spire.

The top of the Transamerica Pyramid is covered with aluminum panels. During the Christmas holiday season, on Independence Day, and during the anniversary of 9/11, a brightly twinkling beacon called the "Crown Jewel" is lit at the top of the pyramid.

The Transamerica Pyramid is one of San Francisco's most recognizable landmarks and a defining feature of the city's skyline. Completed in 1972 and rising 853 feet above the Financial District, the distinctive pyramid-shaped skyscraper was designed by architect William Pereira and served as the tallest building in San Francisco for many years. Its unmistakable silhouette has become an enduring symbol of the city and remains one of the most photographed buildings in California.

While the building's office floors are generally not open to the public, visitors can still enjoy a rewarding experience at the Transamerica Pyramid Center, which includes the beautifully restored Transamerica Redwood Park, public art exhibitions, architectural displays, dining options, and outdoor gathering spaces. Following a major renovation, the property has evolved into a destination that combines architecture, culture, art, and nature in the heart of San Francisco.

Transamerica Redwood Park

One of the most unique aspects of the Transamerica Pyramid Center is the Transamerica Redwood Park. Located directly at the base of the tower, the half-acre park contains nearly 50 mature Coast Redwood trees that were transplanted from the Santa Cruz Mountains in the early 1970s. The park offers a peaceful contrast to the surrounding Financial District and has become a favorite place for visitors and locals to relax, photograph the Pyramid, and enjoy public art installations.

  • Redwood Grove with mature coastal redwoods.
  • Reflecting Pool and Fountain surrounded by landscaped seating areas.
  • Public Art Installations that rotate throughout the year.
  • Excellent Photography Opportunities of the Pyramid rising above the trees.

Best Experience for First-Time Visitors

For most visitors, the best experience is to combine a visit to the Transamerica Pyramid Center with time spent in the Redwood Park. Walking among the towering redwoods while looking up at the Pyramid provides one of the most iconic and uniquely San Francisco photo opportunities in the city. Pairing the visit with nearby Chinatown, North Beach, or the Ferry Building makes for an excellent half-day itinerary in downtown San Francisco.

The Transamerica Pyramid stands as a defining feature of San Francisco’s skyline, a late modernist architectural marvel completed in 1972. Designed by William Pereira, the 48-story structure rises to 853 feet and was once the tallest building in the city. Its unique pyramid shape, clad in crushed quartz, was initially controversial but has since become a beloved symbol of the city. The building is surrounded by Transamerica Redwood Park, a tranquil green space featuring sculptures and seasonal exhibitions, offering a serene escape in the heart of the Financial District.

Today, the Transamerica Pyramid Center is more than just an office tower—it’s a cultural hub. Visitors can explore art installations, including works by Max Ernst, and exhibitions like “Past as Prologue” by the Eames Institute. The revitalized center includes cafes, retail shops, and immersive experiences that blend architecture, nature, and history. Though the observation deck is closed, the lobby offers a live feed from the top floor and historical displays. Whether admiring its form from nearby Coit Tower or strolling through its redwood park, the Pyramid invites tourists to discover a new perspective on urban design and community space.
