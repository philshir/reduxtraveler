Overview

The Sierra Nevada is a mountain range in the Western United States, between the Central Valley of California and the Great Basin, and it is home to some of the country’s most famous scenery. Visitors come for Yosemite Valley, giant sequoias, alpine lakes, and granite peaks, as well as skiing, hiking, climbing, and backpacking.

The range includes Yosemite, Sequoia, and Kings Canyon national parks, as well as Lake Tahoe, Mount Whitney, historic Gold Rush towns, and the scenic drive along U.S. 395.

Geography

The Sierra runs about 400 miles north to south and is 50 to 80 miles wide. The western slope rises gradually from the foothills, while the eastern side drops steeply in a high escarpment above the Owens Valley and Great Basin. Mount Whitney, at 14,505 feet, is the highest peak in the contiguous United States. The range is mostly granite that formed deep underground more than 100 million years ago, then was lifted and carved by glaciers into valleys, domes, and cliffs.

History

The Miwok, Paiute, Washoe, Mono, and Yokuts peoples lived in and around the mountains for thousands of years. The Donner Party was trapped by snow in the Sierra in the winter of 1846–47, and gold discovered at Coloma in 1848 started the California Gold Rush in the western foothills. The transcontinental railroad crossed the range over Donner Summit in 1869. Naturalist John Muir helped make the Sierra famous and founded the Sierra Club in 1892, and Yosemite Valley was first protected by the federal government in 1864.

Cities and Towns

Gateway towns include Truckee and South Lake Tahoe in the north, Mariposa and Oakhurst near Yosemite, Three Rivers at the entrance to Sequoia, and Mammoth Lakes, Bishop, and Lone Pine on the east side. Gold Rush towns such as Nevada City, Placerville, and Sonora preserve historic downtowns.

National Protected Areas

Yosemite National Park is known for Yosemite Valley, El Capitan, Half Dome, and its waterfalls. Sequoia National Park and Kings Canyon National Park protect giant sequoias such as the General Sherman Tree and deep glacial canyons. Devils Postpile National Monument has a wall of basalt columns, Manzanar National Historic Site preserves a World War II incarceration site, and the Pacific Crest National Scenic Trail and John Muir Trail run along the crest.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Calaveras Big Trees State Park protects giant sequoia groves, Emerald Bay and Donner Memorial state parks sit near Lake Tahoe, and Mono Lake Tufa State Natural Reserve has the lake’s tufa towers. History is preserved at Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park in Coloma, Columbia State Historic Park, Empire Mine State Historic Park, and the ghost town of Bodie State Historic Park.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

Summer is the main season for hiking, camping, and climbing, with high mountain roads such as Tioga Road in Yosemite usually open from late May or June until the first big snow in the fall. Spring is the time for peak waterfalls in Yosemite and wildflowers in the foothills, and fall brings golden aspens along the eastern Sierra in October. Winter is ski season at Tahoe, Mammoth, and other resorts, and chains are often required on mountain roads. Many high-elevation roads and trails close from November until early summer.
