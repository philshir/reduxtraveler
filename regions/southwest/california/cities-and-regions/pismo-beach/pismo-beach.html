How to Get There

From San Luis Obispo (12 mi): Take US-101 S toward the coast, exit onto Price Street, and continue west toward the oceanfront and downtown area near the pier and main beach access points.

Overview

Pismo Beach is a laid-back beach town of about 8,000 people on the Central Coast, about 12 miles south of San Luis Obispo. Known as the "Clam Capital of the World," it is famed for its wide sandy beach, its wooden pier, its bluff-top hotels and its relaxed, family-friendly feel.

Visitors come to Pismo Beach to surf, swim, fish from the pier, stroll the boardwalk and watch sunsets, and to drive on the sand at nearby Oceano Dunes. The town is also close to the Edna Valley and Arroyo Grande wine regions, to Avila Beach and to San Luis Obispo, and it is known for clam chowder and fresh seafood.

History

The Chumash lived here for thousands of years, and the name Pismo comes from their word for tar, which seeped from the ground. The Spanish gave the land to Mission San Luis Obispo, and in the 1880s John Michael Price began developing a resort after a railroad arrived. Pismo clams, once dug by the thousands, made the town famous, and Pismo Beach incorporated as a city in 1946.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Pismo Beach Pier, built in 1924 and extended in later decades, is the town's symbol. Pismo State Beach includes the Monarch Butterfly Grove, which hosts thousands of monarchs from October to February, Shell Beach has bluff-top tide pools, and Oceano Dunes State Vehicular Recreation Area is the only California state park where cars can drive on the beach.

Culture and Museums

Pismo Beach is a casual beach town, but its cultural calendar includes the Pismo Beach Clam Festival each October, and the Classic Car Show. The nearby Oceano Depot Railroad Museum and the Dunes Center in Oceano explain local history and ecology, and the town's tasting rooms showcase central coast wines.

Parks

Pismo State Beach has about five miles of sand, camping and the Monarch Butterfly Grove, while Dinosaur Caves Park offers bluff-top views and a playground. Shell Beach and Margo Dodd Park have tide pools, and the Oceano Dunes and Oceano Lagoon provide birding and off-road recreation.

Seasons and Weather

Pismo Beach has a mild, coastal climate, with highs in the 60s to low 70s°F year-round. Summers are cool and often foggy, early fall is typically the warmest and sunniest season, and winter brings most of the 16 inches of rain. The water stays cold, so wetsuits are common for surfers and swimmers.
