Overview

Southeastern California is a land of extremes and contrasts, where vast desert basins meet rugged mountain ranges under an expansive sky. The region centers on the Sonoran (Colorado) Desert and includes the resort city of Palm Springs, Joshua Tree National Park, and the Salton Sea. It also reaches parts of the Mojave Desert.

Visitors come for mid-century modern architecture, golf and spa resorts, music festivals, desert hiking, rock climbing, and stargazing under some of the darkest skies in Southern California. The area’s isolation and stark beauty have long attracted adventurers, artists, and those seeking solitude.

Geography

The region lies in the low-elevation Colorado Desert, a part of the Sonoran Desert, where rainfall often measures less than 10 inches a year. Plants such as creosote bush and ocotillo thrive, and the Joshua tree grows in the higher Mojave section of Joshua Tree National Park. The Salton Sea sits in a basin about 230 feet below sea level, the Colorado River forms the eastern border, and the San Jacinto and Santa Rosa mountains rise above Palm Springs. The Imperial Sand Dunes are among the largest dune fields in North America.

History

The Cahuilla, Chemehuevi, Quechan, and other peoples have long lived in the desert, and the Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla still manage the springs and canyons at Palm Springs. Spanish explorer Juan Bautista de Anza crossed the desert in the 1770s. The Salton Sea was formed in 1905, when the Colorado River broke through irrigation works and flooded the basin for about two years. Irrigation turned the Imperial Valley into a major farming region, and Palm Springs became a Hollywood retreat in the 1930s through the 1960s, leaving a legacy of mid-century modern design.

Cities and Towns

Palm Springs is the best-known city, with its aerial tramway, mid-century architecture, and resorts, and nearby Cathedral City, Rancho Mirage, Palm Desert, La Quinta, and Indio form the Coachella Valley. Indio hosts the Coachella and Stagecoach music festivals. Twentynine Palms and Yucca Valley are the main gateways to Joshua Tree, and El Centro and Blythe are the larger communities in the Imperial and Colorado River valleys.

National Protected Areas

Joshua Tree National Park is the area’s most visited protected area, with Joshua tree forests, boulder fields, and desert palm oases. The Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains National Monument protects the mountains above Palm Springs. The Imperial Sand Dunes Recreation Area is managed by the Bureau of Land Management for off-road recreation, and the Sonny Bono Salton Sea National Wildlife Refuge is a key stop for migrating birds.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Anza-Borrego Desert State Park is California’s largest state park, with badlands, palm canyons, and spectacular wildflower blooms in wet springs. Salton Sea State Recreation Area offers camping and birding, and Mount San Jacinto State Park is reached by the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway. Nearby, the Indian Canyons, managed by the Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla, preserve palm-lined canyons with hiking trails.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

The best time to visit is from October through April, when daytime temperatures are in the 60s to 80s, and winter is peak season in Palm Springs and Joshua Tree. Spring brings wildflowers after wet winters, often in February and March at Anza-Borrego, and the Coachella festival in April. Summers are extremely hot, with temperatures often above 110°F, so most outdoor activity should be done in the early morning. Cooler months are best for hiking, climbing, stargazing, and birdwatching at the Salton Sea.
