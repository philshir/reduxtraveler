Andrew Molera State Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. California

Relatively undeveloped state park on the Big Sur coast situated at the mouth of the Big Sur River. Activities at the park include hiking, fishing and beachcombing, with miles of trails winding through meadows, beaches and hilltops

How to Get There

From Carmel-by-the-Sea, CA (23 mi): Take CA-1 south for approximately 23 miles along the scenic Big Sur coast to reach Andrew Molera State Park.

Overview

Andrew Molera State Park is the largest state park on the Big Sur coast, a relatively undeveloped area at the mouth of the Big River with a long beach, a walk-in campground, meadows and trails to coastal bluffs. Horseback rides, surfing and hiking are offered, and it is the location of the Big Sur Ornithology Lab and a trail to Molera Point. It was named for Andrew Molera, who popularized the artichoke in California.

Things to Do

Experience the Largest State Park on the Big Sur Coast

Andrew Molera State Park encompasses thousands of acres along California's iconic Big Sur coastline and offers a unique combination of coastal bluffs, sandy beaches, river valleys, redwood groves, and rugged mountain landscapes. The park's relatively undeveloped character makes it a favorite destination for visitors seeking a more natural and less crowded Big Sur experience.

Explore the Big Sur River Estuary

One of the park's most distinctive features is the Big Sur River estuary, where the river meets the Pacific Ocean. This ecologically important habitat supports a variety of bird species, marine life, and native vegetation, making it a popular area for wildlife observation and photography.

Relax at Andrew Molera Beach

Visitors can hike to a scenic beach that offers dramatic coastal views, ocean breezes, and opportunities for beachcombing, photography, birdwatching, and enjoying the rugged beauty of the Big Sur coastline. Due to strong currents and rough surf, the beach is generally better suited for sightseeing than swimming.

Enjoy Wildlife Viewing and Birdwatching

The park's diverse habitats support deer, sea otters, harbor seals, California condors, hawks, and numerous migratory bird species. The river estuary and coastal bluffs are particularly popular areas for observing wildlife throughout the year.

Explore Scenic Meadows and Redwood Groves

Andrew Molera State Park contains a variety of ecosystems, including open grasslands, oak woodlands, chaparral-covered hillsides, and magnificent redwood groves. These diverse landscapes provide visitors with a broad sampling of Big Sur's natural beauty.

Experience Horseback Riding and Backcountry Exploration

The park's extensive trail network offers opportunities for hiking, horseback riding, trail running, and exploring remote sections of the Big Sur coast. Many trails provide spectacular views of the Pacific Ocean and surrounding mountains.

Top Trails

Beach Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail crosses the Big Sur River and leads to Andrew Molera Beach. Visitors enjoy views of the river estuary, surrounding mountains, and dramatic coastline before arriving at the beach.

Bluffs Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Following coastal terraces above the ocean, this scenic trail offers panoramic views of the Pacific, rugged shoreline, and offshore rock formations. It is one of the park's most accessible and rewarding hikes.

Ridge Trail

Length: Approximately 5.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Moderate to Difficult

This trail climbs into the Santa Lucia Mountains and rewards hikers with spectacular views of Big Sur, the Pacific Ocean, and the surrounding wilderness. Seasonal wildflowers often bloom along portions of the route.

Creamery Meadow Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A peaceful trail that travels through open meadows, river habitats, and oak woodlands. It is particularly popular for birdwatching, wildlife photography, and enjoying the park's quieter natural settings.

Features

The Cooper Cabin: Andrew Molera State Park features the historic Cooper Cabin, built in 1861 or 1862. It is the oldest structure in Big Sur. Fur trader Juan Bautista Rogers Cooper was Andrew Molera's grandfather.

The Ventana Wildlife Society has established a Discovery Center within the park. The Discovery Center includes exhibits on local wildlife, including the California condor, and a bird banding laboratory. Scientists and other laboratory employees give regular tours of Andrew Molera State Park, explaining the flora and fauna that are unique to the area.

The park also features a year-round waterfall, 40-foot (12 m) Highbridge Falls. Other nearby waterfalls include Limekiln Falls, Salmon Creek Falls, McWay Falls in Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park, and Pfeiffer Falls in Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park.

Recreation

Andrew Molera State Park has over 20 miles (32 km) of hiking trails. Some run along the shore, others along the Big Sur River, while still others climb to high ridges with views of the entire Big Sur coast.

The only camping available in the park is in a 24-site walk-in campground. Registration is on a first-come, first-served basis. No dogs are allowed on the trails or campground.

Marine protected areas

Point Sur State Marine Reserve and Marine Conservation Area are marine protected areas offshore from Andrew Molera State Park. Like underwater parks, these marine protected areas help conserve ocean wildlife and marine ecosystems.
