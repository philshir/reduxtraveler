Golden Canyon, Gower Gulch, and Badlands Loop

How to Get There

From Furnace Creek (3.3 mi): Drive south on CA-190 and turn right onto Badwater Road; continue for 2 miles to reach the Golden Canyon parking area.

Overview

Golden Canyon, Gower Gulch, and the Badlands Loop form one of the most popular and scenic hiking areas in Death Valley National Park, winding through golden-hued badlands, narrow canyons, and colorful eroded hills near Furnace Creek. Highlights include the towering Red Cathedral, the sweeping views from Zabriskie Point, and the dramatic Manly Beacon. Hikers can choose from routes of about 3 to 8 miles, from a simple out-and-back in Golden Canyon to longer loops through Gower Gulch and the badlands. No permit is required.

Getting to the Trailhead

Death Valley National Park is crossed by CA-190, which connects the main hub of Furnace Creek with Stovepipe Wells and the park's east and west entrances. The main roads are paved and accessible to all vehicles, and an entrance fee is required. Services are far apart, so fill up on gas and water before heading out. There are two trailheads: the Golden Canyon Trailhead, 2 miles south of CA-190 on Badwater Road, and Zabriskie Point on CA-190, 3.5 miles east of Badwater Road. Both have paved parking lots with spaces for RVs and buses.

Trail Description

From the Golden Canyon Trailhead, the trail heads up the canyon between golden walls of eroded mudstone, past numbered interpretive stops. After about a mile, a spur leads to the base of the Red Cathedral, a towering wall of red rock, about 3 miles round trip. Hikers can continue up Golden Canyon toward Zabriskie Point, passing Manly Beacon, or make a loop of about 4.3 miles by returning through Gower Gulch, a wide canyon with colorful mineral deposits, and walking along the base of the hills back to the trailhead. The Badlands Loop, about 2.7 miles, starts from Zabriskie Point and winds through the eroded hills. Junctions are signed, but the badlands are a maze of similar-looking ridges and washes, so carry a map and follow the trail markers.

Trail Conditions and Difficulty

The National Park Service rates these hikes as moderate to strenuous, with about 535 to 834 feet of elevation gain depending on the route. The Gower Gulch Loop includes a short section of easy scrambling down a dry fall. Summer temperatures on the valley floor regularly exceed 110 degrees, and the National Park Service advises against hiking at low elevations after 10 a.m. in summer; hike from late fall through early spring, start early, and carry plenty of water.
