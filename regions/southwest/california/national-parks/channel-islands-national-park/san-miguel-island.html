How to Get There

From Los Angeles (70 mi to Ventura Harbor): Take US-101 N to Victoria Ave in Ventura, then follow signs to Ventura Harbor Village where Island Packers ferry departs to San Miguel Island.

Overview

San Miguel Island is the westernmost island in Channel Islands National Park, a remote, windswept plateau of about 14 square miles that sits directly in the path of the strong northwest winds and swells of the open Pacific. Lying about 55 miles from Ventura, it is one of the most isolated and least-visited places in the national park system, with long white sand beaches, sand dunes, rugged cliffs and a landscape shaped by constant wind.

Visitors come to San Miguel for its wild, otherworldly scenery and its extraordinary wildlife. Point Bennett, at the island’s western tip, hosts one of the largest gatherings of seals and sea lions in the world, with tens of thousands of California sea lions, northern elephant seals, northern fur seals and harbor seals at peak season. Inland, the eerie “caliche forest” preserves ghostly mineral casts of ancient plant roots and trunks. The island also holds a rich human history: the Chumash lived here for thousands of years, explorer Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo visited in 1542 and is commemorated by a monument above Cuyler Harbor, and from 1930 to 1942 caretaker Herbert Lester and his family lived on the island, earning him the nickname “King of San Miguel.” The island is owned by the U.S. Navy and managed by the National Park Service.

Sightseeing and Exploring the Island

There are no roads on San Miguel, and visitors explore on foot from the beach at Cuyler Harbor, a long crescent of white sand framed by cliffs, where boats land by skiff. From the beach, the Nidever Canyon Trail climbs to the ranger station and campground, passing the Cabrillo Monument with sweeping views over the harbor, and the ruins of the Lester Ranch. Because of the island’s fragile resources and its past use by the military, visitors beyond the beach, campground and main trail must stay on designated trails, and destinations such as the caliche forest and Point Bennett can be visited only with a ranger or authorized guide.

Hiking

Hiking on San Miguel ranges from moderate to very strenuous. The trail from Cuyler Harbor up Nidever Canyon to the ranger station and campground is about 1 mile with a steep climb of about 500 feet. From there, ranger-led hikes visit the caliche forest, about 5 miles round trip, and the summit areas of San Miguel Hill and Green Mountain, with views over the island. The longest and most famous hike is the ranger-led trek to Point Bennett, about 15 to 16 miles round trip, which rewards hikers with views of the massive pinniped rookeries. The island is extremely windy and often foggy, so hikers should bring layers, wind protection and plenty of water.

Other Activities

Wildlife viewing is the island’s top activity, with seals and sea lions at Point Bennett and along the shoreline, the endemic island fox, and seabirds such as Brandt’s cormorants and western gulls. The beach at Cuyler Harbor is a beautiful spot for beachcombing and picnicking, though the water is cold and often rough. The crossing itself is a highlight, with frequent sightings of dolphins and whales. The island’s remote location and lack of light pollution also make it a spectacular place for stargazing on clear nights.

Getting to San Miguel Island

San Miguel Island is reached by boat with Island Packers, the park’s official concessionaire, which runs a limited number of day and camping trips from Ventura Harbor, typically from spring through fall. The crossing takes about 3.5 to 4 hours each way and often stops at Santa Rosa Island along the way. Landing is by skiff onto the beach at Cuyler Harbor, which may involve getting wet. Because of the island’s exposure to wind and swell, trips are frequently canceled or delayed, so visitors, especially campers, should be prepared for schedule changes and extra days on the island.

Seasons

San Miguel Island is open year-round, but boat trips are limited and mostly run from spring through fall. Spring brings wildflowers and the start of the seabird nesting season, while summer is the peak breeding season for California sea lions and northern fur seals at Point Bennett. Northern elephant seals gather in large numbers from about December through March for breeding and pupping, and again in spring and summer for molting. Wind is constant throughout the year, fog is common in late spring and summer, and fall often brings the calmest weather and clearest skies.

Camping and Lodging

There is no lodging on San Miguel Island. A small primitive campground located about a mile up the trail from Cuyler Harbor offers about nine sites with windbreaks, picnic tables, food storage boxes and a pit toilet, but no water, so campers must bring all water and supplies. Reservations are required through Recreation.gov, along with boat transportation. Most visitors stay on the mainland in Ventura or Oxnard near the harbors, with additional lodging in Camarillo and Santa Barbara.

Visitor Centers

There is no visitor center on San Miguel Island, but a ranger station near the campground is typically staffed during the visitor season, and rangers lead hikes and provide information. On the mainland, the Robert J. Lagomarsino Visitor Center at Ventura Harbor is the park’s main visitor center, with exhibits on the islands, a film and trip-planning information, and the Outdoors Santa Barbara Visitor Center at Santa Barbara Harbor offers additional information.

Top Trails

Cuyler Harbor to Lester Ranch

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

From the landing beach, the trail climbs to the site of the old Lester Ranch and a memorial to explorer Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo.

Caliche Forest Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A ranger-led hike to a ghostly field of calcium carbonate casts of ancient plants.

Point Bennett Trail

Length: Approximately 16 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

A long ranger-led hike across the island to Point Bennett, one of the largest gatherings of seals and sea lions in the world.
