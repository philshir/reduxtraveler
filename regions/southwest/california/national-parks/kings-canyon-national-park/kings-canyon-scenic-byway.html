How to Get There

From Grant Grove Village (36 mi to Roads End): Drive north on CA-180 past the Hume Lake Road junction, then follow the highway as it descends into Kings Canyon to Cedar Grove Village and Roads End.

Overview

The Kings Canyon Scenic Byway is the section of Highway 180 that leads from Grant Grove deep into Kings Canyon, one of the deepest canyons in North America, ending at Roads End beyond Cedar Grove. Over about 36 miles, the road winds through Sequoia National Forest and Giant Sequoia National Monument, drops thousands of feet to the bottom of the canyon along the South Fork of the Kings River, and then follows the river between towering granite walls into Kings Canyon National Park. It is widely considered one of the most spectacular drives in the Sierra Nevada.

People drive the byway for its dramatic scenery, from sweeping overlooks of the rugged canyon to roadside waterfalls, marble cliffs and the glacier-carved valley of Cedar Grove, often compared to Yosemite Valley. The road was built in the 1930s, partly with convict labor, and opened to Cedar Grove in 1939. Today, it is the only road into the heart of Kings Canyon, giving visitors access to riverside trails, meadows, waterfalls and the trailheads at Roads End that lead into the park’s vast wilderness.

Sightseeing and Scenic Drives

The byway is packed with pull-offs and viewpoints. Heading north from Grant Grove, the road passes the turnoff to Hume Lake and then descends past Junction View, an overlook above the meeting of the Middle and South Forks of the Kings River, and Yucca Point, where a steep trail drops to the river. At the bottom of the canyon, the road passes the marble cliffs near Boyden Cavern, which has offered guided cave tours, and Grizzly Falls, a roadside waterfall with a picnic area that is best in spring and early summer. After entering Kings Canyon National Park, the road follows the river into Cedar Grove, with stops at Canyon View, Knapp’s Cabin, Roaring River Falls, Zumwalt Meadow and, at the end of the road, Muir Rock and Roads End.

Hiking

The byway provides access to trails ranging from short walks to long wilderness treks. Easy hikes include the short paved walk to Roaring River Falls and the Zumwalt Meadow loop, about 1.5 miles through a meadow beneath North Dome and Grand Sentinel. The River Trail connects Roaring River Falls, Zumwalt Meadow and Roads End along the South Fork. Moderate to strenuous hikes include Mist Falls, about 8 miles round trip from Roads End, the steep Yucca Point Trail, and the Hotel Creek and Don Cecil Trails, which climb the canyon walls to sweeping overlooks. From Roads End, backpackers set out on famous routes such as the Rae Lakes Loop and trails into Paradise Valley.

Other Activities

Beyond sightseeing and hiking, the byway is popular for picnicking at riverside and roadside areas, wildlife viewing and photography, especially in the early morning and late afternoon light. In summer, swimming at Muir Rock and other calm spots along the river is a favorite activity once water levels drop, and fishing for trout is popular along the South Fork with a California fishing license. Horseback rides have been offered from the Cedar Grove pack station, and ranger programs are held in Cedar Grove in summer. Just off the byway, Hume Lake offers swimming, paddling and fishing.

Getting to the Kings Canyon Scenic Byway

The byway begins near Grant Grove, about 55 miles (roughly 1 to 1.5 hours) east of Fresno via Highway 180, and visitors from Sequoia National Park can reach it via the Generals Highway, about an hour from Giant Forest. The drive from Grant Grove to Roads End is about 36 miles and takes about 1 to 1.5 hours without stops, though most visitors spend the whole day. The road is a dead end, so visitors must return the same way. There are limited services along the route, with fuel generally available only at Hume Lake and seasonally at Cedar Grove, so travelers should fill up in advance. Fresno Yosemite International Airport is the nearest major airport.

Seasons

The section of Highway 180 beyond the Hume Lake Road junction is open seasonally, typically from late April through mid-November, and closes in winter due to snow and rockfall. Spring and early summer bring roaring waterfalls, high river flows and wildflowers, but the river is dangerously cold and swift during snowmelt. Summer is hot in the canyon bottom, often in the 90s °F, making early morning and evening the best times for hiking. Fall brings cooler temperatures, golden cottonwoods and black oaks, calmer river flows and fewer visitors, until the road closes for the season.

Camping and Lodging

At the end of the byway, Cedar Grove offers in-park lodging at the seasonal Cedar Grove Lodge, along with several campgrounds, including Sentinel, Sheep Creek and Moraine, which offer riverside sites in summer. Along the byway in Sequoia National Forest, campgrounds around Hume Lake and in the canyon offer additional camping, and Grant Grove, at the start of the byway, has the John Muir Lodge, Grant Grove Cabins and several campgrounds. The nearest city with a wide selection of hotels and services is Fresno, about 1 to 1.5 hours west of Grant Grove.

Visitor Centers

The Kings Canyon Visitor Center in Grant Grove Village, at the start of the byway, is the main visitor center for the park, with exhibits, a film, a bookstore and ranger information, and it is a good place to check road and trail conditions before driving into the canyon. In summer, the Cedar Grove Visitor Center near Cedar Grove Village offers information and exhibits about the canyon, and the Roads End Permit Station issues wilderness permits for backpackers heading into the backcountry.
