How to Get There

From Foothills Visitor Center (2 mi): Drive east on Mineral King Road from Highway 198 in Three Rivers. Follow the steep, winding road for approximately 25 miles to reach the Eagle Lake Trailhead. Hike 3.6 miles with 2,200 ft elevation gain to reach Eagle Sinkhole.

Overview

The Eagle Sinkhole is a striking geological formation located along the Eagle Lake Trail in the Mineral King region of Sequoia National Park. Nestled at over 9,000 feet elevation, this natural depression is carved into the granite landscape and serves as a dramatic waypoint en route to Eagle Lake. The sinkhole is surrounded by alpine meadows, rugged cliffs, and dense pine forests, offering hikers a glimpse into the dynamic forces that shaped the Sierra Nevada. The trail to the sinkhole is steep and challenging, rewarding adventurers with panoramic views and encounters with wildlife such as marmots and deer.

Accessing the Eagle Sinkhole requires navigating Mineral King Road, a narrow and winding mountain route known for marmot activity that can damage vehicles. Visitors are advised to wrap their cars in tarps and carry bear-proof containers for food storage. The area is remote, with limited services and no cell reception, making preparation essential. The sinkhole itself is a popular stop for hikers continuing to Eagle Lake or Mosquito Lakes, and is best visited in summer or early fall when snow has melted and trails are clear. Its unique geological character and secluded setting make it a hidden gem for seasoned hikers and nature lovers.
