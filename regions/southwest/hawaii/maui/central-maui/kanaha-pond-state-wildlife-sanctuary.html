Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Hawaii
  3. Maui
  4. Central Maui
  5. Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary

Forest & Kim Starr, CC BY 3.0 US, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Protected coastal wetland and bird sanctuary home to endangered Hawaiian waterbirds and over 90 migratory species.

General Information

Hours:
Daily: Sunrise to sunset
East-side trails open August 31 to March 31 only
Fees:
Free admission
Access restricted to marked trails and viewing areas
Pet Policy:
Pets are not allowed
Closest cities with hotels:
Kahului (1 mi), Wailuku (3 mi)
Seasons:
Best from September to March (birdwatching season)
Location:
Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary, Kahului, HI
Website:
dlnr.hawaii.gov/wildlife/sanctuaries/kanaha

How to Get There

From Kahului, HI (1 mi): Head east on Keolani Place, turn right onto Amala Place, and follow signs to the sanctuary parking area near the bird viewing platform.

Overview

Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary is a coastal wetland on the edge of Kahului, right beside Kahului Airport, that protects important habitat for native and migratory waterbirds. The pond's shallow water and mudflats are home to the endangered Hawaiian stilt, or aeʻo, and the Hawaiian coot, or ʻalae keʻokeʻo, as well as black-crowned night herons and many visiting shorebirds. Once a royal fishpond, it is now managed as a sanctuary and offers a chance to watch wildlife just minutes from the island's main town. Visitors come for birdwatching, photography and a quiet break near the airport.

Access

The sanctuary is in Kahului along Hāna Highway (Highway 36), just east of Kahului Airport and about 1 mile from the main town center. A small parking area and viewing platform are reached from the highway, and the site is easy to find. Most visitors arrive by car, and the location makes it a quick stop on the way to or from the airport. Bus service in Kahului also serves the general area.

Seasons

The sanctuary is open year-round during daylight hours, but the birds change with the seasons. Fall through early spring, from about September to March, is the best time to see migratory shorebirds and ducks, as well as resident waterbirds. Summer is hot and drier, with fewer migrants but continued sightings of stilts and coots. Visitors should stay on marked trails and viewing areas, and in some areas of the sanctuary, the east-side trail is open only part of the year, from late August through March, to protect nesting birds.

Things to Do

Watch Native Hawaiian Waterbirds

Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary is one of the best places on Maui to see the endangered aeʻo and ʻalae keʻokeʻo. Both nest and feed in the shallow pond, and observers can often see them at close range from the viewing deck.

Look for Migratory Birds

Seasonal visitors include shorebirds such as the Pacific golden plover, wandering tattler and ruddy turnstone, along with ducks arriving from far north. A spotting scope or binoculars helps with identification.

Visit the Observation Pavilion

A viewing pavilion has interpretive signs about the birds and the history of the wetland. It is a simple, accessible way to enjoy the sanctuary.

Learn About a Former Hawaiian Fishpond

Interpretive information describes how the pond was used in ancient times and how it evolved into a sanctuary. It adds cultural context to the wildlife experience.

Add Kanaha Beach Park

Right next to the sanctuary, Kanaha Beach Park is a famous windsurfing and kitesurfing beach. It is a good spot to watch athletes on windy afternoons.

Top Trails

Kanaha Pond Viewing Walk

Length: Less than 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk from the parking area leads to the viewing platform and the pond edge. The route is flat and easy, with chances to see birds at close range.

East Side Wetland Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This seasonal trail follows the east side of the pond and is open only from late August through March. It offers a closer look at nesting and migratory birds, and visitors must stay on the path.

Visitor Centers

Kanaha Pond State Wildlife Sanctuary does not have a visitor center, but the observation pavilion and its signs provide information about the wildlife, the history of the pond and the rules of the sanctuary. Information about trail closures and bird sightings can be found on the Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife website, and local birding groups sometimes lead outings.

Camping and Lodging

Camping is not permitted at the sanctuary, and there is no lodging on site. The closest hotels are in Kahului, only minutes away, including the Courtyard by Marriott Maui Kahului Airport, Maui Seaside Hotel and Maui Beach Hotel. Wailuku, about 10 minutes away, has additional small hotels and inns, and Pāʻia, about 15 minutes east, offers boutique stays near the beaches. About 25 to 30 minutes away, Kīhei and Wailea have a large selection of condos and luxury resorts, and Kāʻanapali resorts are about 45 minutes away. Check each property's website for current availability.
