Pua'a Ka'a State Wayside Park

★5.0

Lush rainforest rest stop featuring short trails to waterfalls, picnic areas, and pools ideal for a refreshing dip during a scenic drive.

General Information

Hours:
Daily: 6am to 6pm
Fees:
Free entry and parking
Pet Policy:
Pets are not allowed
Closest cities with hotels:
Hāna (13 mi), Haiku (25 mi)
Seasons:
All year; waterfalls most active during rainy season (winter)
Location:
Pua'a Ka'a State Wayside Park, Hāna, HI
Website:
dlnr.hawaii.gov

How to Get There

From Kahului, HI (40 mi): Drive east on Hana Highway (HI-36 E) for about 1 hour and 30 minutes; the park is located just past mile marker 22 with parking on the left and waterfalls on the right.

Overview

Puaʻa Kaʻa State Wayside Park is a shady, waterfall-filled roadside park along the Hāna Highway, about halfway between Pāʻia and Hāna. A small stream tumbles over lava ledges into pools, and the park has picnic tables, restrooms and an easy path to the cascades. The name means rolling pig, and it comes from a traditional story about the area. The park is a favorite stop for travelers on the Road to Hāna because it offers a cool place to rest, to listen to waterfalls and to enjoy a picnic in the rainforest.

Access

The park is along the Hāna Highway at about mile marker 22, roughly 40 miles from Kahului and about 12 to 13 miles from Hāna. Most visitors arrive by rental car or guided tour, and the park has a small parking lot on the road with the waterfall on the opposite side. The drive from Kahului takes about 1.5 hours or more with stops. The highway is narrow and winding, so drive carefully, and the lot can be crowded in the middle of the day.

Seasons

The park is open year-round during the day. Rain is common on this side of Maui, and the waterfalls are strongest in winter and after storms, though they can run in any season. Summer and early fall tend to be drier, and mornings are the best time for quieter visits. Flash floods are a real danger after heavy rain, so stay out of the stream if it rises or becomes muddy, and watch for slippery rocks along the paths.

Things to Do

Visit a Rainforest Waterfall Stop

Puaʻa Kaʻa State Wayside Park has a small waterfall and pools just steps from the parking area. The setting is green, cool and beautiful and is among the most relaxing stops on the Hāna Highway.

Picnic Beside the Stream

Picnic tables and shaded lawns give a comfortable spot for lunch. Restrooms make it a practical rest stop on the long drive.

Take Photos of the Cascades

The lava ledges and ferns around the falls make a lovely photo subject, especially in soft morning light. Bring a rain jacket and keep gear dry.

Learn the Story of the Place

The name of the park refers to a legend, and interpretive information tells about the stream and the surrounding landscape. It adds cultural context to the stop.

Combine With Nearby Attractions

The park is close to Waikani Falls, Wailua Valley and the Hāna Highway's other waterfalls. It is a good midway point between Pāʻia and Hāna.

Top Trails

Puaʻa Kaʻa Waterfall Path

Length: Less than 0.25 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path from the parking area leads to the falls and the stream. The walk is easy, though the ground may be wet and slippery.

Stream and Picnic Area Walk

Length: Less than 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A casual stroll around the park grounds follows the stream, passes picnic areas and offers several views of small cascades. It is suitable for families.

Visitor Centers

Puaʻa Kaʻa State Wayside Park does not have a visitor center. The park has restrooms, picnic tables and signage about the stream and the history of the area. Visitors should bring water and food, and for more information about road conditions and attractions along the Hāna Highway, ask in Pāʻia or Hāna or check the Hawaiʻi State Parks website.

Camping and Lodging

Camping and lodging are not available at the wayside park. The nearest accommodations are in Hāna, about 25 minutes away, including the Hotel Hāna-Maui, Hāna Kai Maui and several bed-and-breakfasts and vacation rentals. Nāhiku and Keʻanae have a few small stays along the route. Back along the highway, Haʻikū and Pāʻia offer inns and boutique rentals about 1 to 1.5 hours away, and Kahului, Kīhei and Wailea offer chain hotels and resorts about 2 to 3 hours away. Many travelers choose to stay overnight in Hāna to avoid driving the road twice in one day. Check each property's website for current availability.
