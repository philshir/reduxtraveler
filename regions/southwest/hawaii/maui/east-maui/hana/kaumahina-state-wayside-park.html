How to Get There

From Kahului, HI (28 mi): Take Hana Highway (HI-36 E) for about 50 minutes; the park is located just past mile marker 12 on the mauka (mountain) side of the road.

Overview

Kaumahina State Wayside Park is a small roadside park along the Hāna Highway on Maui's northeast coast, perched on a hillside with sweeping views of the shoreline and the rugged Keʻanae Peninsula. The park is a popular rest stop for travelers on the Road to Hāna, offering picnic tables, restrooms and a pleasant lawn amid native and introduced trees. A short path through the park's landscaped grounds includes Hawaiian plants and lookouts toward the coast and Honomanū Bay. Visitors come to stretch their legs, enjoy the views and have a break from the winding drive.

Access

Kaumahina is on the Hāna Highway (Highway 360) at about mile marker 12, roughly 28 miles from Kahului and about 50 minutes from Pāʻia. The park is on the mountain side of the road with a small, paved parking area and a signed entrance. Nearly all visitors arrive by car as part of a Road to Hāna drive or a guided tour. The road is narrow and winding, so plan to drive slowly and take breaks at pullouts and parks like this one.

Seasons

The park is open year-round during daylight hours. The Hāna Highway corridor is rainy, particularly in winter, but the wayside park is a good place to wait out passing showers. Views are usually clearest in the morning, and spring and summer tend to be the driest periods. Heavy rain can cause landslides and road closures on the highway, so check conditions before the drive, and watch for slippery paths and muddy spots on the grounds.

Things to Do

Take a Break on the Road to Hāna

Kaumahina State Wayside Park is a convenient stop with restrooms, picnic tables and shady lawns. It is a good place to eat lunch, rest and plan the rest of the drive.

Enjoy Views of the Coastline

From the lookouts, visitors can see rocky shores, sea cliffs and surf along the northeast coast. On clear days the view stretches across the bays and the Keʻanae Peninsula.

See Native Hawaiian Plants

Signs and plantings in the park introduce native and Polynesian-introduced plants, such as koa, hala and kukui. A short walk through the garden helps visitors understand the island's forest.

Look Out Toward Honomanū Bay

The views to the east include the large bay with its dark boulder beach, which is hard to see from the road. It is a good photo stop, but there is no access from the park.

Combine With Other Road to Hāna Stops

The park is close to Keʻanae Arboretum, the Keʻanae Peninsula and other roadside attractions. It makes a good first or middle stop for a day on the highway.

Top Trails

Kaumahina Native Plant Path

Length: Less than 0.25 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short, gentle path winds through the landscaped grounds past native plants and lookouts. It can be muddy after rain, but it is easy for most visitors.

Lookout Terrace Walk

Length: Less than 0.25 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A brief walk to the lookout terrace offers views of the coast and bays below. It is a good spot to take photos and watch the surf.

Visitor Centers

Kaumahina State Wayside Park does not have a visitor center. The park has restrooms, picnic tables and interpretive signs, and visitors will find limited services, so bring food and water. For information on current road conditions and attractions along the Hāna Highway, check with visitor resources in Pāʻia or Hāna or the Hawaiʻi State Parks website.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the wayside park. Hāna, about 22 miles away or about an hour's drive, has the nearest hotels and inns, including the Hotel Hāna-Maui, plus bed-and-breakfasts and vacation rentals. Haʻikū and Pāʻia are about 30 to 45 minutes back along the highway and offer inns, vacation rentals and boutique stays, and Kahului and Wailuku have chain hotels about an hour away. The resorts of Kāʻanapali, Kīhei and Wailea are about 1.5 to 2 hours away. Check each property's website for current availability.
