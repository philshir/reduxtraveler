How to Get There

From Kahului, HI (17 mi): Take HI-311 S to HI-31 S (Piilani Hwy), then turn right onto Wailea Ike Drive and follow signs to the resort area.

Overview

Wailea is a master-planned luxury resort community on the sunny southwest coast of Maui, covering about 1,500 acres between Kihei and Makena. It has crescent beaches, upscale hotels and condominiums, golf courses, spas and shopping, and its low-density design keeps it feeling open and landscaped rather than crowded.

Visitors come to Wailea for its calm beaches and clear water, snorkeling, whale watching in winter, fine dining and golf, and as a comfortable base for exploring the rest of Maui. It is a favorite for honeymoons and family vacations, close to Makena's quieter beaches and Molokini Crater.

History

The area was a dry, rocky shoreline once used by Hawaiians for fishing and later for ranching and sugar. Alexander and Baldwin, together with Northwestern Mutual, developed Wailea starting in 1971 and opened the first resort in the mid-1970s. The area has since grown with additional resorts, and the master plan has protected its beachfront and views.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Wailea Beach Path is a coastal walk of about 1.5 miles that links the resorts, beaches and shops. Wailea Beach, Ulua Beach and Polo Beach are the main swimming areas, and nearby Makena has Big Beach and Little Beach. Maui Ocean Center in Maalaea displays Hawaiian marine life.

Culture and Museums

Wailea's cultural offerings include luau and hula shows at the resorts, the Shops at Wailea, and the Maui Ocean Center. The resorts display Hawaiian art and offer cultural programs. Nearby Kihei hosts local events and farmers markets.

Parks

The Wailea Beach Path and the beach parks provide public access to the shoreline, and the Wailea Golf Club runs three championship courses. Kealia Pond National Wildlife Refuge and Haleakala National Park are a short drive away, and Makena State Park has a famous beach and trails.

Seasons and Weather

Wailea is one of the driest and sunniest places on Maui, with about 14 inches of rain a year and highs in the mid 80s°F year-round. Summer is slightly warmer, winter brings humpback whales offshore from December to April, and the south shore receives its largest surf in summer from southern swells.

Things to Do

  • Beaches
  • Championship golf courses and tennis facilities
  • Restaurants and shopping

History

Wailea began as a fishing settlement, and its name means “water of Lea,” the goddess of canoe-builders. Legend says she turned the area into a beautiful forest for birds. Early residents lived toward the mountains growing sweet potatoes (ʻuala) and came down to the shore to fish. After the Great Māhele, cattle ranching dominated and eventually formed Ulupalakua Ranch.
