Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area

★5.0

  2. Hawaii
  3. Maui
  4. East Maui

High-elevation forest reserve offering misty trails, redwood groves, panoramic overlooks, and rustic camping in a cool, alpine-like setting.

How to Get There

From Kahului, HI (25 mi): Take HI-37 S to Kula; turn right onto Waipoli Road and follow the winding road uphill to the recreation area (4WD recommended).

Overview

Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area is a high-elevation forest park on the western slopes of Haleakalā, where cool, misty air and towering trees feel a world away from Maui's beaches. At roughly 6,200 feet, the area is covered by conifers, including redwoods, cypress, pine and eucalyptus, many of them planted by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s to restore watershed and forest cover. The park offers peaceful hiking and mountain biking trails, a campground and a rustic cabin, and in clear weather it has far-reaching views. Visitors come for quiet, for forest walks and for a different side of Maui.

Access

The park is reached from the Upcountry community of Kula by driving up Waipoli Road, a long, narrow and winding road with steep switchbacks that climbs about 10 miles from Kula Highway. The upper section is rough and often unpaved or patched, and four-wheel drive or a high-clearance vehicle is recommended. The drive can take 45 minutes to an hour. Fog, rain and rough conditions can make the road challenging, so check the weather and conditions first.

Seasons

The recreation area is open year-round, but weather is often cool, damp and misty at this elevation. Summer, particularly May through September, offers the clearest skies and driest trails. Winter brings more rain and fog, and temperatures can drop into the 40s, so warm and waterproof layers are important. The road can be muddy or washed out after storms, and fallen trees may block trails, so check conditions before heading out.

Things to Do

Escape Into a Misty Upcountry Forest

Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area is a quiet forest of redwoods, cypress and pines high on Haleakalā. The setting is cool and calm and offers a dramatic contrast to Maui's beaches.

Hike Forest Trails

A network of trails winds through the forest, including loops from the campground and longer routes linking to other parts of the Kula Forest Reserve. Trails vary from easy walks to challenging all-day hikes.

Mountain Bike the Singletrack

The area is one of Maui's best-known mountain bike destinations, with trails that descend through the forest. Riders should be experienced and prepared for steep, slippery terrain.

See Views on Clear Days

When the clouds clear, the upper trails offer views across the isthmus to West Maui and out to the ocean. Mornings are the best time to catch open skies.

Camp or Stay in the Cabin

The park has a campground and a rustic cabin that can be reserved. Nights are cold, and the dark sky makes it a special place for stargazing on clear nights.

Top Trails

Redwood Trail

Length: Approximately 1.7 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail runs through a grove of redwoods and other conifers and connects the campground area with other parts of the forest. It is shady and pleasant but can be muddy and slippery.

Plum Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This loop travels through the forest and across the slopes with changing vegetation and occasional views. It is a longer outing that suits hikers who want to see more of the forest.

Boundary Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Significant
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

A long forest trail follows the boundary of the reserve and reaches open slopes with views. It is mostly for experienced hikers and bikers and requires good preparation.

Visitor Centers

Polipoli Spring State Recreation Area does not have a visitor center. The campground area has restrooms, picnic tables and information boards, and the Hawaiʻi State Parks website provides current conditions, camping permits and cabin reservations. Because there are no stores or services nearby, visitors should stock up in Kula or Makawao before heading up.

Camping and Lodging

The recreation area offers a campground and a rustic cabin, with restrooms and basic facilities, and both require reservations through the state parks system. Nights are cold, and visitors should bring warm gear and supplies. Nearby, Kula has the Kula Lodge and several bed-and-breakfasts and cottages, and Makawao, about 30 minutes away, has small inns and vacation rentals. Beach resorts are plentiful in Kīhei and Wailea, about 45 minutes to an hour away, including the Fairmont Kea Lani, Grand Wailea and Four Seasons Resort Maui. Kahului and Wailuku have chain hotels about 40 minutes away. Check each property's website for current availability.
