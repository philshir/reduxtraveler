Pāpalaua State Wayside Park

Beachfront park offering tent camping, surfing, and kayaking along a shaded shoreline with limited facilities and scenic coastal views.

Website:

How to Get There

From Wailea (26 mi): Take HI-31 north to HI-30 (Honoapiʻilani Hwy), continue past Olowalu to mile marker 11; the park is on the ocean side of the highway.

Overview

Pāpalaua State Wayside Park is a roadside beach park on Maui's leeward coast near Olowalu, along Honoapiʻilani Highway between Lahaina and Maʻalaea. The park has a long sandy shoreline lined with shady trees, picnic tables and a calm, shallow ocean that is often good for swimming, snorkeling and paddling. It sits next to the Olowalu reef, which is known for its coral and sea turtles, and the setting offers views toward Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi. People come for a relaxed beach day, a quick swim and the chance to camp at the edge of the sea.

Access

The park is on the ocean side of Honoapiʻilani Highway (Highway 30) near mile marker 11, about 10 miles south of Lahaina and about 18 miles from Wailuku. Most visitors arrive by car, either from the Kāʻanapali and Lahaina area or from Maʻalaea and Central Maui, and the paved parking area is just off the highway. The drive from Kāʻanapali takes about 25 minutes, and from Kahului about 30 minutes. Public transportation is limited, and visitors should follow current guidance on roads and conditions in West Maui.

Seasons

The park is open year-round and the leeward coast is one of the sunniest and driest parts of Maui. Summer, with calm seas, is a good time for swimming and snorkeling, while winter brings larger swell and occasional strong currents, but also the chance to see whales offshore. Mornings are the best time for clear water and cooler temperatures. Camping is available by permit and closes on certain days, and the park has little shade in places, so bring sun protection.

Things to Do

Relax at a Calm Leeward Beach

Pāpalaua State Wayside Park has a long strip of sand and gentle water that makes it a laid-back place to swim and sunbathe. The beach is less developed than the resort areas nearby.

Snorkel the Olowalu Reef

The nearby Olowalu reef is one of Maui's most important coral reefs, home to turtles and many reef fish. Visitors should snorkel carefully, follow reef-safe practices and avoid touching coral.

Enjoy a Picnic Under the Trees

Shady trees and picnic tables give a pleasant place to stop for lunch. The park is also a good place to watch the sun dip behind Lānaʻi.

Watch for Whales in Winter

From December through April, humpback whales pass offshore, and visitors can often see them from the beach. Binoculars help spot breaches and spouts.

Camp at the Edge of the Sea

Permitted camping lets visitors enjoy sunsets and starry skies right on the shoreline. Permits must be arranged in advance through the county system.

Top Trails

Pāpalaua Beach Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk along the shoreline follows the curving beach and offers views of Lānaʻi and the West Maui Mountains. It is flat and best in the morning or evening.

Olowalu Coastal Walk

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A casual walk along the highway-side shoreline and beaches leads toward the Olowalu area and its historic sites and reef. Visitors should walk carefully, take care near the roadway and respect private property.

Visitor Centers

Pāpalaua State Wayside Park does not have a visitor center. The park has basic facilities such as restrooms, picnic tables and showers, and signs with rules and safety information. For camping permits, current conditions and information about the Lahaina and West Maui area, check Maui County resources and the Hawaiʻi State Parks website.

Camping and Lodging

The park allows camping by permit through the county, and the campsites are close to the sea. There is no lodging in the park. Nearby, the resorts of Kāʻanapali, about 25 minutes away, include the Sheraton Maui, Westin Maui and Hyatt Regency Maui, and Kīhei and Wailea, about 35 to 45 minutes away, offer condos and large resorts such as the Grand Wailea and Fairmont Kea Lani. Wailuku and Kahului have chain hotels and are about 30 minutes away. Lahaina is still recovering from the 2023 wildfire, so check current conditions and availability before planning to stay there. Check each property's website for current availability.
