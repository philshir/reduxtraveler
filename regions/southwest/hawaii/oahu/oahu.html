Overview

Oahu, “The Gathering Place,” is the most visited and most populated of the Hawaiian Islands, home to roughly one million people, about two-thirds of the state, and to the capital, Honolulu. It offers a blend few places can match: the world-famous beaches and nightlife of Waikīkī, the historic sites of Pearl Harbor, the volcanic crater of Diamond Head, the big-wave surf of the North Shore, and the lush windward coast with beaches such as Lanikai that regularly rank among the world’s best.

The island is 44 miles long and 30 miles across, formed by two eroded volcanoes, the Waiʻanae and Koʻolau ranges, with a broad plain between them. Ancient tradition says it was named by the navigator Hawaiʻiloa for his daughter. Honolulu is the state’s largest city and main deep-water port, and Kamehameha the Great’s conquest of the island in 1795 helped unite the Hawaiian Islands.

Top attractions include Waikīkī Beach, Pearl Harbor National Memorial and the USS Arizona Memorial, Diamond Head, ʻIolani Palace, Hanauma Bay, the Polynesian Cultural Center, Kualoa Ranch, Bishop Museum and the North Shore. The University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa’s Rainbow Warriors compete in Honolulu.
