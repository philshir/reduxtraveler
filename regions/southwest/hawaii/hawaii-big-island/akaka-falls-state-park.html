'Akaka Falls State Park

★5.0
  1. Southwest
  2. Hawaii
  3. Hawaii (Big Island)
  4. 'Akaka Falls State Park

Scenic rainforest park featuring a paved loop trail with panoramic views of two waterfalls, including a 442-foot free-falling cascade.

How to Get There

From Hilo, HI (15 mi): Drive north on HI-19 for about 11 miles, turn left onto Highway 220 (ʻAkaka Falls Road), and continue 3.6 miles to the park entrance at the end of the road.

Overview

ʻAkaka Falls State Park is a lush rainforest park on the Hāmākua Coast of the Big Island, famous for ʻAkaka Falls, a 442-foot waterfall that plunges straight down into a deep, fern-draped gorge. A short loop trail winds through tropical vegetation, including bamboo, orchids, ginger and tree ferns, with viewpoints of the main falls and the smaller Kāhūnā Falls. The park protects one of the most dramatic and easily seen waterfalls in the Hawaiian Islands, and the setting along Kolekole Stream also holds cultural stories and native wildlife. It is popular because the falls are easy to reach, the walk is short and the scenery is as lush and tropical as visitors imagine Hawaiʻi to be.

Access

The park is about 11 miles north of Hilo and a few miles from the town of Honomū, at the end of Highway 220 (ʻAkaka Falls Road) off the Hawaiʻi Belt Road (Highway 19). Nearly all visitors arrive by rental car or tour, and the drive from Hilo takes about 20 to 30 minutes. A paved parking area sits beside the park entrance, and because the lot is small, it can fill up quickly in the middle of the day. Non-resident visitors should check current entry and parking requirements and may need to pay or reserve online in advance.

Seasons

The park is open year-round and the Hāmākua Coast is one of the wetter parts of the island, so rain is possible in any season. Winter, from November through March, tends to be wettest, and heavy rain can make ʻAkaka Falls thunder and the trail slippery, with the falls at their most powerful. Summer is typically drier and sunnier, though the lush vegetation stays green all year. Early morning often brings the clearest views before clouds or mist gather, and the gates close promptly in the late afternoon.

Things to Do

Discover One of Hawaiʻi's Most Beautiful Waterfalls

ʻAkaka Falls State Park centers on a gorge where Kolekole Stream drops 442 feet over a lava cliff. The falls can be seen from well-placed viewpoints along a short paved loop, making this one of the island's easiest waterfall visits.

Walk Through a Tropical Rainforest

The loop trail passes bamboo, banyan, orchids, hibiscus, ginger and giant ferns that create a green tunnel above the path. The walk is a good introduction to Hawaiian rainforest plants and the sounds of the forest.

See Kāhūnā Falls and Smaller Cascades

In addition to the main falls, the trail offers views of Kāhūnā Falls and other smaller cascades in the same gorge. These add variety and give a sense of the stream's course through the valley.

Learn the Stories Behind the Falls

The falls are named for a chief, and local tradition links the area to legends and spiritual beliefs. Interpretive information and local guides help visitors appreciate the cultural significance of the site.

Visit Honomū and the Hāmākua Coast

The nearby village of Honomū has small shops, galleries and cafés in plantation-era buildings. The coast's scenic drive passes more waterfalls, gulches and ocean views, making it easy to turn the park into a half-day outing.

Top Trails

ʻAkaka Falls Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 0.4 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved loop descends gently through tropical forest to viewpoints of ʻAkaka Falls and Kāhūnā Falls before climbing back to the parking area. It includes some steps and can be slippery after rain, but it is suitable for most visitors and takes about 30 to 45 minutes.

Visitor Centers

ʻAkaka Falls State Park does not have a visitor center. An entrance area and parking lot have restrooms, interpretive signs and sometimes park staff or attendants who can answer questions. For more information, the Hawaiʻi State Parks website provides current fees, hours and reservation details, and the Hilo area visitor resources can help with road conditions and nearby attractions.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the park, so most visitors stay in nearby towns. Hilo, about 20 to 30 minutes south, has the closest range of hotels, including the Grand Naniloa Hotel, the Hilo Hawaiian Hotel, Dolphin Bay Hotel and other small inns, along with a wide choice of restaurants. Along the Hāmākua Coast, bed-and-breakfasts and vacation rentals offer a quiet, scenic stay. About an hour south, Volcano Village has lodges and inns near Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park, including Volcano House, and the Kona and Kohala coasts, about 1.5 to 2 hours away, have large resort areas. Check each property's website for current availability.
