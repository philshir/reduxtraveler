How to Get There

From Hilo (55 mi): Take Highway 19 north through Honokaʻa and continue west to reach Waimea town center.

Overview

Waimea, also called Kamuela, is a ranching town of about 10,000 people on the northern Big Island, at about 2,700 feet in the Kohala uplands between Mauna Kea and the Kohala Mountains. Its cool, green pastures look more like New Zealand or Wyoming than the tropics, and it is the center of Hawaii's paniolo (cowboy) culture.

Visitors come to Waimea for Parker Ranch, one of the largest privately owned cattle ranches in the country, for the Kohala coast beaches and resorts a short drive downslope, for farm-to-table restaurants, and as the gateway to Waipio Valley and the Mauna Kea summit road.

History

The Hawaiians settled the area long ago, and Kamehameha I was reportedly born in North Kohala. In 1793, British captain George Vancouver brought cattle as a gift to Kamehameha I, and the animals multiplied until John Palmer Parker, a sailor who married into the royal family, founded Parker Ranch in 1847. Hawaiian cowboys, called paniolo, learned their skills from Mexican and Spanish vaqueros brought in the 1830s, and the ranch shaped the town's identity.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Parker Ranch, with its visitor center, the Mana Hale and Pu'uopelu historic homes and cattle drives, is the main sight in town. Nearby, Puukohola Heiau National Historic Site, built by Kamehameha I in 1791, and the coastal resorts of Waikoloa and Hapuna Beach are within a 30-minute drive.

Culture and Museums

The Kahilu Theatre stages concerts, plays and dance, the Isaacs Art Center shows work by Hawaii artists in a historic schoolhouse, and the Parker Ranch Historic Homes show the lives of the ranching family. The headquarters of the Keck and Subaru observatories on Mauna Kea are also in Waimea, which reflects the area's mix of ranching and science.

Parks

Waimea's open green space is mostly the rolling pasture of Parker Ranch, and Waimea Park has sports fields and a playground. The Kohala Mountain Road leads north to North Kohala and the Pololu Valley lookout, and the road up Mauna Kea starts a short drive south.

Seasons and Weather

Waimea has a cooler, breezier climate than the coast, with highs in the 70s°F and lows in the 50s at night. It gets about 40 inches of rain a year, often in light mists and showers, and fog and wind are common, so a jacket is useful even in summer.

Things to Do

Hawaiian art is on display in the center of town at:

  • Isaacs Art Center
  • Wishard Gallery
  • Paniolo Heritage Center at Pukalani Stables
  • Gallery of Great Things

Getting Around

  • Route 19 leads 56 miles southeast to Hilo, and 43 miles southwest to Kailua-Kona along the coast.
  • Route 190 offers a shorter, 39-mile inland route to Kailua-Kona.
