How to Get There

From Hilo (67 mi): Take Highway 19 north along the coast, continue past Waimea, and turn left onto Old Puako Road to reach the beach entrance.

Overview

Hapuna Beach State Recreation Area protects one of the largest and most beautiful white-sand beaches in Hawaiʻi, a broad half-mile crescent of golden sand on the sunny Kohala Coast of the Big Island. Hapuna is frequently ranked among the best beaches in the United States, with clear turquoise water, gentle sandy slopes and a backdrop of lava fields and distant Mauna Kea. The state recreation area offers lifeguards, picnic pavilions, restrooms, showers and food service, which makes it easy to spend an entire day. People come to swim, bodyboard, snorkel, sunbathe and watch the sun set over the Pacific.

Access

The beach is off Highway 19 on the Kohala Coast, about 35 miles north of Kailua-Kona and about 13 miles from Waimea, a short drive from the Mauna Kea and Hapuna resort areas. A paved entrance road leads to a large parking lot near the beach, and the area is easy to find with signs along the highway. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and non-resident visitors may need to pay parking or entry fees. The drive from Kona takes about 45 minutes, and from Hilo it takes around 1.5 hours.

Seasons

Hapuna is open year-round, with hours that close in the evening. Summer, from May through September, brings the calmest water and is ideal for swimming and snorkeling. Winter, from November through March, often brings high surf, strong shore break and dangerous currents, so visitors should follow lifeguard flags and warnings. The Kohala Coast is one of the driest areas of the island, with sunshine most days all year, so plan for strong sun and bring shade. Weekends and holidays can be busy, and mornings are quieter.

Things to Do

Spend the Day at One of Hawaiʻi's Best Beaches

Hapuna Beach State Recreation Area has a wide stretch of soft sand and clear water that makes it a favorite for families, couples and visitors who want an easy day by the ocean. The setting is open and scenic, with views of the coast and Maui's Haleakalā on clear days.

Swim, Snorkel and Bodyboard

In calm summer conditions, the water is clear and gentle for swimming and snorkeling near the rocky edges of the bay. In winter, bodyboarders and skimboarders enjoy the waves, but conditions can be hazardous and should be approached with caution.

Relax at the Picnic Areas

Picnic pavilions, tables and shaded areas sit behind the beach, so groups can enjoy lunch or a barbecue between swims. Showers and restrooms are nearby, and a snack stand offers food and drinks.

Watch the Sunset

Hapuna faces west, so the sunset over the Pacific is a highlight, often with whales visible offshore in winter. The sky colors and open horizon make it a great spot for photographs.

Stay Overnight in an A-Frame Shelter

The recreation area offers reservable A-frame shelters near the beach for overnight use, which is a unique way to experience the coast. Permits are required, so reserve through the state parks system in advance.

Top Trails

Hapuna Beach Shoreline Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk along the sand and the beach edge shows off the length of the beach and its views of the coast. It is flat and relaxed, and the best times are early morning and evening.

Ala Kahakai Trail Segment to Mauna Kea Beach

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A coastal path over lava and sand links Hapuna with the neighboring Kauna‘oa (Mauna Kea) Beach and follows part of the Ala Kahakai National Historic Trail. The route is exposed and rocky in places, so wear sturdy footwear and bring water.

Visitor Centers

Hapuna Beach State Recreation Area does not have a visitor center, but the entrance area and pavilions have lifeguard towers, restrooms, showers and a snack stand, and staff can give basic information. For rules, permits for A-frame shelters and current conditions, check the Hawaiʻi State Parks website or ask at the pavilion. Nearby resort concierge desks can also help with activities and ocean safety information.

Camping and Lodging

The recreation area offers reservable A-frame shelters for overnight stays, which require a state parks permit, and there are no traditional campsites. The Kohala Coast has some of the island's best resorts, including the Hapuna Beach Prince Hotel, Mauna Kea Beach Hotel, Mauna Lani Bay Hotel and Bungalows, Fairmont Orchid and the Hilton Waikoloa Village, all within about 15 to 25 minutes. Waimea, about 20 minutes inland, has a smaller choice of inns and vacation rentals, and Kailua-Kona, about 45 minutes south, has a wide range of hotels and condos. Resort lodging is often expensive and can be booked far in advance. Check each property's website for current availability.
