Kohala Historical Sites State Monument

Cultural heritage site featuring ancient Hawaiian temples and the birthplace of King Kamehameha I, accessible via rugged coastal trails and scenic viewpoints.

How to Get There

From Hilo (75 mi): Take Highway 19 north to Waimea, then Highway 250 north to Hawi, and follow signs to Upolu Point Road for access to the monument.

Overview

Kohala Historical Sites State Monument protects two of the most sacred places in Hawaiian history on the remote northern tip of the Big Island: the birthplace of Kamehameha I and Moʻokini Heiau, one of the oldest and most important temples in Hawaiʻi. Moʻokini is a massive luakini heiau, a temple used for major ceremonies, traditionally said to date back many centuries, and the nearby site marks where Kamehameha the Great, who would unite the Hawaiian Islands, was born in about 1758. The monument sits at windswept Upolu Point and is a National Historic Landmark. Visitors come to experience a place of deep spiritual and historical importance and to enjoy the dramatic coastline and open space of North Kohala.

Access

The monument is at the far northern end of the island, near Upolu Point, about 20 minutes past the town of Hāwī. Visitors take Highway 270 to Hāwī and follow Upolu Point Road toward the coast, and the last stretch is rough and unpaved, so a high-clearance or four-wheel-drive vehicle is recommended, especially after rain. The area is remote, with few services, and visitors should be prepared with water, fuel and supplies. From Hilo, the drive takes around 2 hours, and from Waimea it is about 45 minutes.

Seasons

The monument is open year-round during daylight hours, but conditions change with the weather. The area is exposed to strong trade winds, and the unpaved road can be muddy or impassable after heavy rain, so dry weather is best. Summer tends to be drier and calmer, while winter can bring high surf along the coast and stronger winds. Visitors should treat the site with respect as a sacred place, staying on paths and not climbing on or moving stones, and should check access conditions before driving out.

Things to Do

Visit the Birthplace of Kamehameha the Great

The monument includes the traditional birth site of Kamehameha I, the leader who united the Hawaiian Islands. The simple site and its quiet setting are part of what makes it so moving.

See Moʻokini Heiau

Moʻokini is one of the oldest known heiau in Hawaiʻi, a large stone platform with walls that still stand. Walking its grounds, visitors can sense the importance it held for ancient Hawaiians.

Learn About Hawaiian History and Culture

Interpretive information, together with local cultural practitioners, helps explain the role of the site in the rise of Kamehameha. It is a good place to reflect on the cultural heritage of the island.

Take in the Coastline of Upolu Point

The point faces the ʻAlenuihāhā Channel, with views toward Maui's Haleakalā on clear days. Coastal cliffs, wind-shaped vegetation and open skies make it a scenic and peaceful place.

Combine With North Kohala Sights

The monument fits well with a day exploring Hāwī, Kapaʻau and the Pololū Valley Lookout. The scenic Kohala district drive is among the most beautiful on the island.

Top Trails

Moʻokini Heiau Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk leads from the parking area to the heiau and its surrounding grounds. The terrain is open, windswept and uneven in places, so wear sturdy shoes and be respectful of the sacred site.

Kamehameha Birth Site Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A brief path leads to the birthplace area, which is marked with a stone enclosure and interpretive information. It is best visited with quiet reflection.

Visitor Centers

Kohala Historical Sites State Monument does not have a visitor center or staffed facilities. Interpretive signs at the site provide information about the heiau and Kamehameha's birthplace, and cultural practitioners from the community sometimes care for the grounds. For current access, road conditions and cultural protocols, check the Hawaiʻi State Parks website or ask in Hāwī or Kapaʻau before heading out.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the monument. The nearest services are in Hāwī and Kapaʻau, about 20 to 30 minutes away, which have small inns, bed-and-breakfasts, vacation rentals and a handful of cafés and shops. Waimea, about 45 minutes away, has a wider range of lodging, and the Kohala Coast resorts, including the Hapuna Beach Prince Hotel, Mauna Lani Bay Hotel and Fairmont Orchid, are about 45 minutes to an hour south. Beach camping may be available at county parks in the region, with a county permit. Check each property's website for current availability.
