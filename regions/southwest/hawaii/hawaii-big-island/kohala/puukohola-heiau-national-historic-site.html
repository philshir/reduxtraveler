How to Get There

From Hilo (67 mi): Take Highway 19 north to Waimea, then west on Highway 270 to Kawaihae; the park entrance is on the left before Spencer Beach Park.

Overview

Puʻukoholā Heiau National Historic Site preserves the last major ancient Hawaiian temple, built in 1790 and 1791 by Kamehameha the Great on a hill overlooking Kawaihae Bay. A prophecy told that if Kamehameha built a heiau to the war god Kū on the Hill of the Whale, he would rule all the islands, and the temple was completed soon before he did unite the Hawaiian Islands in 1810. The massive stone platform, nearly 225 feet long, is surrounded by related sites, including Maʻilekini Heiau, the John Young homestead and the submerged Hale o Kapuni, a shark temple in the bay. People come for the history, the cultural significance and the views of the coast and Mauna Kea.

Access

The site is on the Kohala Coast at Kawaihae, just off Highway 270 and close to the junction with Highway 19, about 30 minutes north of Waikoloa and about 25 minutes from Waimea. Most visitors reach it by car, and the paved entrance road leads to a parking lot and the visitor center. The site is adjacent to Spencer Beach Park and a short walk from the shoreline of Pelekane Bay. Parking is free, and the drive from Kona takes about 1 hour and from Hilo about 1.5 hours.

Seasons

The park is open year-round and the dry, sunny Kohala Coast is a good choice in nearly any season. Summers are hot and breezy, with little shade on the trails, so morning visits are the most comfortable. Winter brings occasional rain and strong surf, although the area remains drier than much of the island. Whales can sometimes be seen offshore from December through April, and the visitor center and park hours are limited to the daytime, with an early afternoon gate closure.

Things to Do

Explore the Last Great Hawaiian Temple

Puʻukoholā Heiau National Historic Site centers on a huge stone temple built to fulfill a prophecy of unifying the islands. Walking around the outside of the heiau, visitors can appreciate the scale and the skill of the stonework.

See Maʻilekini Heiau and Related Sites

Below the main temple, Maʻilekini Heiau was later converted into a fort by Kamehameha. Nearby, the remains of the John Young homestead and the underwater Hale o Kapuni add to the story.

Visit the Visitor Center

The center explains the historical events, the prophecy and the people involved, with exhibits, a film and rangers who can answer questions. It sets the context for the walk to the heiau.

Look for Marine Life and Whales

From the shoreline, visitors can look for sea turtles and, in winter, humpback whales offshore. The calm waters of Pelekane Bay are part of the cultural landscape.

Pair With Beaches and Hiking

Spencer Beach Park is right next door, and Hapuna Beach and Kauna‘oa Beach are a short drive away. The Ala Kahakai National Historic Trail passes nearby for longer walks.

Top Trails

Puʻukoholā Heiau Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short, paved and gravel path climbs to the heiau and its viewpoints. It is accessible to most visitors, and the views of the coast and Mauna Kea are excellent.

Pelekane Bay and Maʻilekini Heiau Loop

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This longer loop descends toward the bay and the historic features by the shore, then returns to the visitor center. The terrain is open and sunny, so bring water and sun protection.

Visitor Centers

The Puʻukoholā Heiau National Historic Site Visitor Center is open daily and is the best first stop. It has exhibits on the building of the heiau and the life of Kamehameha, a short film, a bookstore, restrooms and rangers who can provide maps and answer questions. Ranger talks and cultural programs are offered at times, and the center is typically open in the daytime with a mid-afternoon closing, so check the National Park Service website for current hours.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the national historic site, but the adjacent Spencer Beach Park, a county park, has beachside camping with a permit. Nearby resorts and hotels on the Kohala Coast include the Hapuna Beach Prince Hotel, Mauna Kea Beach Hotel, Mauna Lani Bay Hotel and Bungalows, Fairmont Orchid and the Hilton Waikoloa Village, all within about 15 to 30 minutes. Waimea, about 25 minutes inland, has a smaller selection of inns and rentals, and Kailua-Kona offers a wide choice of hotels and condos about an hour south. Check each property's website for current availability.
