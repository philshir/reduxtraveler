How to Get There

From Kīlauea Visitor Center, drive south on Chain of Craters Road to the Puʻuloa Petroglyphs trailhead, then hike 6.6 miles along the Puna Coast Trail to reach ʻĀpua Point.

Overview

The ʻĀpua Point campsite lies along the remote southern coast of Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park, offering a rugged and unforgettable backcountry experience. Reached only by foot via the Puna Coast Trail, this 6.6-mile trek from the Puʻuloa Petroglyphs trailhead traverses vast fields of pāhoehoe and ʻaʻā lava, sandy dunes, and windswept coastal terrain. The site itself is a sparse oasis, marked by a few coconut palms and low stone walls that provide minimal shelter from the elements. With no drinking water available, hikers must carry in all supplies, including at least four liters of water per person per day. A composting toilet is available, but all trash must be packed out, in accordance with Leave No Trace principles.

Despite its isolation, ʻĀpua Point rewards the prepared and adventurous with stunning ocean views, dramatic lava formations, and a sense of solitude rarely found elsewhere. The beach is not safe for swimming due to strong rip currents, but shallow tide pools offer a place to cool off. The area is also a nesting site for endangered sea turtles, and visitors are urged to respect posted boundaries. Camping is allowed with a permit, and the site accommodates up to 16 hikers per night. For those seeking a raw, immersive encounter with the Big Island’s volcanic coastline, ʻĀpua Point is a destination that promises both challenge and beauty.
