Gauley River National Recreation Area

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. West Virginia
  3. Gauley River National Recreation Area

Protected river corridor offering whitewater rafting, rugged hiking, and scenic views through steep Appalachian gorges.

How to Get There

From Beckley, WV (45 mi): Take US-19 N to WV-129 W toward Summersville Lake; follow signs to Gauley River access points near Mason Branch or Woods Ferry.

Overview

Gauley River National Recreation Area is a National Park Service unit in southern West Virginia that protects about 25 miles of the Gauley River and a section of the Meadow River, established by Congress in 1988. The Gauley runs through a deep, forested gorge below Summersville Lake and is renowned among paddlers for its Class III to V rapids. Each fall, scheduled water releases from Summersville Dam create one of the best whitewater seasons in the eastern United States, drawing rafters and kayakers from around the world. Most of the area is undeveloped, so people come mainly for whitewater, fishing, and wild river scenery.

Access

The recreation area is in Nicholas County, a short drive from the town of Summersville and not far from New River Gorge National Park and Preserve. From Beckley, US-19 north leads toward Summersville, and local roads then lead to river access points such as Woods Ferry and Mason Branch. Much of the gorge can be reached only by river or on rough, steep roads, so many visitors join guided rafting trips from commercial outfitters. Check conditions and access with the National Park Service before heading to a river put-in.

Seasons

Fall is the headline season, when scheduled dam releases (generally on weekends from September into early November) deliver big rapids and the surrounding hardwoods turn color. Outside the release period, river flows are much lower, and the area is quiet, suited to fishing, hiking, and sightseeing. Spring rain can raise the river levels, and summers are warm and humid, with nearby Summersville Lake busy with swimmers and boaters. Winter is cold and the river is largely empty, and some access roads may be icy.

Things to Do

Raft the Gauley
Fall releases run the biggest rapids; outfitters run guided trips.

Visit Summersville Lake
Clear blue water, sandstone cliffs, swimming, and boating are nearby.

Explore Carnifex Ferry Battlefield State Park
Exhibits, overlooks, and walking paths mark an important Civil War battle.

Visitor Centers

The Gauley River National Recreation Area has no dedicated visitor center of its own and is managed together with other National Park Service units in the region. Information and maps are available at the visitor centers of New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, and commercial outfitters near Summersville and Fayetteville provide trip information in season. Check with the National Park Service for current contact points and release schedules.

Camping and Lodging

Developed lodging inside the recreation area is minimal, but camping is available at Summersville Lake, which is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and many rafting outfitters run their own campgrounds and cabins. Hotels, motels, and cabin rentals can be found in Summersville, about 10 miles away, and in Fayetteville, which caters to rafters and climbers. Beckley and Charleston, within about an hour, provide the widest choice of chain hotels. Fall release weekends are busy, so book early.
