Cedar Creek and Belle Grove National Historical Park

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. Virginia
  3. Cedar Creek and Belle Grove National Historical Park

Doug Coldwell, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Preserved Civil War battlefield featuring historic farms, scenic valley landscapes, museums, and interpretive trails commemorating a pivotal Union victory in the Shenandoah Valley Campaign.

General Information
Hours:
Park grounds open daily from sunrise to sunset
Visitor facilities and museums generally operate daily from 9am to 5pm, with seasonal variations
Fees:
Park grounds: Free
Belle Grove Plantation House tours: Admission fees may apply
Special programs and events may have separate fees
Pet Policy:
Pets are permitted in outdoor park areas and trails when leashed; pets are not allowed inside historic buildings and visitor facilities
Closest cities with hotels:
Winchester, Virginia (approximately 18 mi)
Seasons:
Spring and fall are the most popular seasons due to mild weather, wildflowers, and autumn foliage, though the park is open year-round
Location:
7712 Main Street, Middletown, VA 22645
Website:
nps.gov

How to Get There

From Winchester (18 mi): Take I-81 south to Exit 302, follow VA-37 and US-11 south toward Middletown, then continue to the visitor contact station and historic sites along Main Street.

Overview

Cedar Creek and Belle Grove National Historical Park in the Shenandoah Valley preserves the battlefield of the Battle of Cedar Creek, fought on October 19, 1864. Union forces under Philip Sheridan turned a surprise Confederate attack into a decisive victory that gave the Union control of the valley. The park also includes Belle Grove Plantation, a late-18th-century manor home built by Isaac Hite Jr., which tells the stories of the families, laborers and enslaved people who shaped the region. Rolling farmland and ridges are open through driving tours, walking trails and exhibits.

Access

The park is in Middletown, Virginia, about 18 miles south of Winchester. Visitors take I-81 to Exit 302 and follow VA-37 and US-11 south to the visitor contact station on Main Street and the historic sites nearby. Parking is free. There is no public transportation to the park.

Seasons

The park grounds are open from sunrise to sunset. Spring and fall bring the most comfortable weather, wildflowers and fall color, and October is the anniversary of the battle with the largest living history event. Summers are warm and green. Winters are cold with occasional snow. Belle Grove is open seasonally.

Things to Do

Tour Belle Grove Plantation

Guided tours show the manor, grounds and the lives of the Hite family and the enslaved workers.

Drive the Battlefield

A self-guided tour with signs covers key sites of the Battle of Cedar Creek.

Visit the Heater House

The restored home was in the middle of the fighting and is open for special events.

Attend the Reenactment

Each October, Cedar Creek Battlefield Foundation hosts living history events.

Explore the Shenandoah Valley

Winchester, New Market and Skyline Drive are all nearby.

Top Trails

Cedar Creek Battlefield Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy

A walk over fields and ridgelines with views of the battlefield and signs about troop movements.

Belle Grove Plantation Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 50 feet
Difficulty: Easy

A flat path around the manor, farm buildings and orchards.

Visitor Centers

The visitor contact station on Main Street in Middletown has exhibits, a bookstore and staff who provide maps and tour information. Belle Grove Plantation has its own visitor center and tour office, operated by the Belle Grove nonprofit partner, and the Cedar Creek Battlefield Foundation operates a visitor center and museum nearby.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Camping and cabins are at Shenandoah River State Park, about 40 minutes south, and in Shenandoah National Park and the George Washington National Forest. Hotels and inns are in Middletown, Strasburg and Winchester, about 20 minutes north, with bed-and-breakfasts in the valley.
