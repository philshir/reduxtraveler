Petersburg National Battlefield

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. Virginia

Extensive Civil War battlefield preserving siege lines, fortifications, historic roads, and landscapes that witnessed the decisive campaigns leading to the fall of Confederate defenses in Virginia.

Fees:

No entrance fee required
Pet Policy:

Closest cities with hotels:
Petersburg, Virginia
Seasons:
Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons to visit, though the battlefield is open year-round
Location:
5001 Siege Rd, Petersburg, VA 23803
Website:
nps.gov/pete

How to Get There

From Petersburg (3 mi): Take VA-36 east from downtown Petersburg, follow signs for the Eastern Front Visitor Center, and continue to 5001 Siege Road at the main battlefield entrance.

Overview

Petersburg National Battlefield preserves the network of trenches, forts and defensive lines around Petersburg, Virginia, where Union and Confederate forces fought the longest campaign of the Civil War, from June 1864 to April 1865. The siege cut off Richmond's supply lines and ended with the Confederate evacuation of both cities. The area was first protected as a national military park in the 1920s and was later redesignated a national battlefield. Driving routes and trails past preserved earthworks show the trench warfare that foreshadowed the 20th century, and visitors come to see the Crater, the forts and the remnants of the siege lines.

Access

The park has several units in and around Petersburg, about 25 miles south of Richmond along I-95. The Eastern Front unit, which has the main visitor center and tour road, is just east of the city, while City Point is in nearby Hopewell and Five Forks is southwest of Petersburg. Because the units are separate, a car is needed to see them all. Allow several hours for the Eastern Front and extra time to visit City Point and Five Forks. Check the National Park Service website for current hours and road or unit closures.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the best seasons, with mild temperatures, spring bloom and fall color along the tour road and trails. Summers are hot and humid with little shade on the open fields, so plan visits for the morning or evening and bring water. Winters are generally mild but can be cold and damp, and the bare trees open views of the earthworks. Anniversary programs and living history events are held at different times of the year, often around the dates of key battles.

Park Units

  • Eastern Front: the main battlefield with forts and earthworks
  • City Point: the enormous Union supply base that supported General Grant
  • Five Forks: the Confederate defeat that triggered the evacuation of Petersburg and Richmond

Visitor Centers

The Eastern Front Visitor Center is the main stop, with exhibits, a film and ranger information to help plan a visit and a tour of the park road. City Point has a visitor center near Appomattox Manor, which served as Grant's headquarters, and Five Forks has a small visitor facility that may be open seasonally. Hours vary by season and unit, so check with the park before your visit.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the park. Petersburg, Colonial Heights and Hopewell have motels and chain hotels within a short drive, and Richmond, about 25 miles north, offers a wide range of hotels and restaurants. For camping, Pocahontas State Park near Chesterfield is within about 30 minutes, and there are private campgrounds in the Richmond-Petersburg area.
