Fort Fisher State Historic Site

★5.0

Preserved Civil War fortification featuring restored earthworks, interpretive exhibits, historic artifacts, scenic coastal landscapes, and a visitor center exploring the fall of Wilmington during the final months of the war.

How to Get There

From Wilmington, NC (18 mi): Take US-421 south through Carolina Beach and Kure Beach, continue along Fort Fisher Boulevard South, and follow signs directly to the historic site visitor center and fort grounds.

Overview

Fort Fisher State Historic Site protects the earthen fort that guarded the last open Confederate seaport of Wilmington during the Civil War. Its massive defenses were among the strongest in the South until a huge Union amphibious attack captured the fort in January 1865, sealing the fate of Wilmington and hastening the end of the war. Today a visitor center, restored earthworks and trails interpret the soldiers, sailors and civilians, including enslaved people, who were caught up in the fight. The site sits between the Atlantic Ocean and the Cape Fear River.

Access

The site is at 1610 Fort Fisher Boulevard South in Kure Beach, about 18 miles south of Wilmington on US-421. Visitors can drive from I-40 and US-421, and parking is free. The Fort Fisher ferry to Southport leaves from the nearby Fort Fisher ferry terminal. There is no public transportation.

Seasons

The visitor center is open Tuesday through Saturday and closed on Sundays, Mondays and state holidays. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for walking the earthworks. Summers are hot and humid, with beach crowds on US-421. Winters are mild, with nor'easters at times. Hurricane season runs from June through November.

Things to Do

Tour the Visitor Center

Exhibits and a film explain the fort, the blockade runners and the capture of Fort Fisher.

Walk the Earthworks

Remnants of the huge land face show how the fort was built to resist attack.

Take a Guided Tour

Standard and Above the Scenes tours go deeper into the history and the site.

Visit the Shipwreck Overlook

Offshore wrecks of blockade runners are part of the site's story.

Enjoy Fort Fisher State Recreation Area

A nearby state park with beach and dunes is a short drive south.

Top Trails

Fort Fisher Civil War Trail

Length: Approximately 1.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk along the surviving land face and the sea face with signs about the fort and the battle.

Basin Trail

Length: Approximately 1.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A path in the Fort Fisher State Recreation Area through coastal forest, marsh and the Basin.

Visitor Centers

The Fort Fisher visitor center has exhibits, a theater, a gift shop and staff who give out maps and tour information. General admission is free, and guided tours charge a small fee. The site is operated by the North Carolina Division of State Historic Sites.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. Camping is available at Carolina Beach State Park, about 20 minutes north, which also has a marina. Hotels, motels and vacation rentals are in Kure Beach and Carolina Beach, and Wilmington has a large selection, including historic inns. Southport is reached by ferry.
