200-mile (320 km) string of barrier islands off the coast of North Carolina and a major tourist destination known for their wide expanse of open beachfront and the Cape Hatteras National Seashore

Bodie Island Lighthouse

★ 5.0

156 feet (48 m) tall lighthouse on Bodie Island on the Outer Banks built in 1872

Cape Hatteras Lighthouse

★ 5.0

Lighthouse located on Hatteras Island in the Outer Banks and is part of the Cape Hatteras National Seashore

Cape Hatteras National Seashore

★ 5.0

National seashore which preserves the portion of the Outer Banks of North Carolina from Bodie Island to Ocracoke Island, stretching over 70 miles

State park located in Nags Head, North Carolina, covering a 427-acre (1.73 km2) area, and includes the tallest active sand dune system in the eastern United State

Overview

The Outer Banks ("OBX") are a 200-mile string of barrier islands off the North Carolina coast, separating the Atlantic Ocean from the sounds behind them. They are one of the East Coast's top beach destinations, known for wide open beaches, lighthouses, wild coastal scenery, and Cape Hatteras National Seashore.

The Outer Banks are steeped in history. The lost English colony at Roanoke Island (1587) is where Virginia Dare became the first English child born in the Americas; hundreds of shipwrecks gave the surrounding waters the name "Graveyard of the Atlantic"; and on December 17, 1903, the Wright brothers made the first powered flight at Kill Devil Hills.

Highlights include the Wright Brothers National Memorial, Jockey's Ridge State Park, Fort Raleigh, Pea Island National Wildlife Refuge, and Cape Hatteras National Seashore. Six lighthouses dot the islands, including Bodie Island, Cape Hatteras, and Currituck Beach in Corolla, and small communities like Rodanthe offer beach stays. Getting around includes NC Highway 12 and free and paid state ferries to Ocracoke and beyond.
