Pea Island National Wildlife Refuge

How to Get There

From Rodanthe (near major hotel and vacation rental accommodations, about 8 mi): Take NC-12 North toward Oregon Inlet, continue through the refuge, and follow signs to the Pea Island National Wildlife Refuge Visitor Center at 14500 NC Highway 12, approximately 4 miles south of the Basnight Bridge.

Overview

Pea Island National Wildlife Refuge covers 5,834 acres of a barrier island on Hatteras Island, next to Cape Hatteras National Seashore, and is managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. It was established in the 1930s as a refuge for migratory birds, particularly wintering waterfowl, and is known as a "birder's paradise" with more than 365 bird species recorded. Habitats include ocean beach, dunes, freshwater and brackish ponds, salt flats and salt marsh. Visitors come to watch birds, walk the wildlife trails, paddle, fish and beachcomb, and the refuge draws more than two million visitors a year.

Access

The refuge stretches along NC 12 south of Oregon Inlet, about 10 miles south of Nags Head, with its visitor center near the middle of the refuge on NC 12. Most visitors drive from Nags Head, Manteo or Rodanthe, and parking areas along the highway serve the beach and the wildlife trails. There is no public transportation. NC 12 is subject to flooding and overwash during storms, so check road conditions in winter and during hurricane season.

Seasons

Fall and winter are the best times for waterfowl, with snow geese, ducks, swans and other birds gathering on the ponds, while spring and fall bring migrating shorebirds and songbirds. Summer is warm and humid, good for beach walks and sea turtle nesting, though mosquitoes and biting flies can be bothersome near the marsh. Winter can be windy and cold but quiet. Storms and high tides can close roads and trails, and some areas are closed seasonally to protect nesting birds.

Things to Do

Watch Birds and Wildlife

Observation platforms, ponds and wildlife trails make for excellent birding, and the refuge also supports mammals, reptiles and threatened species such as loggerhead sea turtles and piping plovers.

Walk, Paddle and Beachcomb

Trails and boardwalks lead to ponds and marsh, canoes and kayaks can be used on the sound side, and the ocean beach is open for walking and shelling.

Fish and Learn

Surf and sound fishing are popular, and the visitor center and interpretive programs introduce the refuge's ecology and history, including the African American crew of the Pea Island Life-Saving Station.

Visitor Centers

The Pea Island visitor center on NC 12 has exhibits on the refuge's wildlife and habitats, a bookstore, restrooms and staff who can point out current bird sightings and trail conditions. It is open daily during limited daytime hours, and hours may change seasonally. Cape Hatteras National Seashore has more visitor facilities nearby on Bodie Island and in Buxton.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging inside the refuge. The nearest lodging is in Rodanthe, Waves, Salvo and Avon on Hatteras Island, which offer cottages, vacation rentals and a few motels. Hotels are also plentiful in Nags Head, Kill Devil Hills and Manteo, within about an hour. The National Park Service operates the Oregon Inlet Campground in Cape Hatteras National Seashore just to the north, and other private campgrounds are found on the island.
