How to Get There

From Asheville, NC (≈65 mi): Take I‑40 E toward Marion, exit onto US‑221 N toward Linville Falls, then follow NC‑183 and NC‑181 to Forest Service Road 210 (Gingercake Road), which provides access to the Linville Gorge Wilderness Area trailheads.

Overview

Linville Gorge Wilderness, nicknamed "The Grand Canyon of the East," is the third-largest wilderness area in North Carolina and one of only two wilderness gorges in the Southern United States. Managed by the U.S. Forest Service within Pisgah National Forest, its nearly 12,000 acres surround the Linville River, which runs roughly 1,400 feet below the rim, so hiking in or out is a serious, rewarding workout. The gorge is protected as federal wilderness to preserve its rugged character, with no roads, buildings or motorized access, and it is known for steep cliffs, old forests and sweeping views. Dense hardwood-pine forest shelters black bear, deer, wild turkey and peregrine falcons, along with copperheads and timber rattlesnakes, so stay alert on the trail.

Don't miss:

  • Linville Falls — the gorge's signature waterfall, with multiple overlooks and trails of varying difficulty.
  • Wiseman's View — a rock outcrop near the center of the gorge with sweeping views into the canyon.
  • Linville Caverns — a privately run cave attraction reachable by car from US-221.

Access

The gorge is reached by Forest Service roads and trailheads off NC 181 and NC 183 on its east and west rims, with the Linville Falls area on the Blue Ridge Parkway at its northern end. Some rim roads, including the route to Wiseman's View, are gravel and rough, and conditions vary, so a car with good clearance helps. From Asheville, the drive takes roughly an hour and a half. Permits may be required for some uses, especially overnight camping, so check with the Forest Service before going, and note that trails are steep, rocky and not heavily signed.

Seasons

Spring brings wildflowers and, in late spring, blooming rhododendron along the rim and river, and summer is warm on the rim but hot and humid in the gorge, with frequent afternoon thunderstorms. Fall is the most popular season, when the foliage across the gorge is at its peak and the overlooks are busiest. Winter brings snow and ice, and rim roads and trailheads can be closed or difficult to reach. Some cliffs are closed seasonally to protect nesting peregrine falcons.

Things to Do

Hike or Backpack the Gorge Floor
Remote trails, river crossings, and steep descents make this some of the most demanding — and rewarding — backcountry hiking in the Southeast. Wilderness permits may be required for overnight camping during peak periods.

Rock Climb the Cliffs
Climbing is allowed throughout the gorge, with popular routes at Table Rock, The Amphitheater, The North Carolina Wall, Shortoff Mountain, and Hawksbill Mountain; the largely unclimbed Gold Coast Cliffs await more adventurous climbers. Some areas close seasonally to protect nesting peregrine falcons.

Take in the Overlooks
Wiseman's View and Table Rock deliver some of the most dramatic panoramas in the Blue Ridge, with sheer cliffs, dense forest, and mountain ridges stretching to the horizon.

Visitor Centers

There is no visitor center inside the wilderness. The nearest is the seasonal Linville Falls Visitor Center on the Blue Ridge Parkway, which has exhibits, maps and rangers and is the gateway to the Linville Falls trails. The Forest Service ranger district office near Marion can also provide permits and information on current conditions.

Camping and Lodging

Backcountry camping is allowed in the wilderness with the required permits, and there are no developed facilities inside. The Linville Falls Campground, operated by the Blue Ridge Parkway, sits near the northern end of the gorge, and other national forest campgrounds are within an hour or two. Lodging is available in the town of Linville Falls and in Spruce Pine, Marion, Boone, Blowing Rock and Banner Elk, all within about an hour, and Asheville is about an hour and a half away.
