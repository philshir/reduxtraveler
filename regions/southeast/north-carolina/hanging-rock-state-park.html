How to Get There

From Winston-Salem, NC (30 mi): Take NC-8 N to Danbury, then turn onto Hanging Rock Park Road and follow signs to the main entrance.

Overview

Hanging Rock State Park in the Sauratown Mountains north of Winston-Salem packs cliffs, waterfalls, and 20-plus miles of trails into one of the most scenic parks in the Piedmont. The Sauratown Mountains are an isolated range that stands well above the surrounding countryside, giving the park views across the rolling plains. The signature hike climbs to the Hanging Rock overlook, a quartzite formation with sweeping Piedmont views. Built up by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, the park became a state park in the mid-1930s and has a lake, a family campground, and cabins for a day trip or a weekend. Visitors come to hike to the waterfalls and overlooks, swim in summer, and climb the cliffs.

Access

The park is in Stokes County near the town of Danbury, about 30 miles north of Winston-Salem and roughly an hour from Greensboro. From Winston-Salem, take NC-8 north to Danbury, then turn onto Hanging Rock Park Road and follow signs to the main entrance. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, and there is no public transportation. Parking areas fill on sunny weekends, especially in spring and fall, and the park may close its gate temporarily when it reaches capacity.

Seasons

Fall and spring are the busiest times, with fall color on the ridges usually peaking in October and spring bringing wildflowers and strong waterfall flow. Summer is hot and humid, but the lake's swim beach and shady trails make it the time for family outings. Winter days can be crisp and clear, with the best long views through bare trees, though ice can make rocky trails slippery. The park gate hours change with the seasons, so check the schedule before you go, and the park is closed on Christmas Day.

Things to Do

Hike to Hanging Rock
A relatively short, popular trail leads to the overlook.

Chase the Waterfalls
Lower Cascades, Hidden Falls, Window Falls, Upper Cascades, and Tory's Falls are reached on forest trails.

Paddle, Swim, and Climb
The lake offers kayaking, canoeing, and summer swimming, and designated rock faces draw climbers.

Visitor Centers

The Visitor Center, open daily from 9am to 5pm, has exhibits on the park's natural and cultural history, trail maps, and information from the park staff. Rangers lead interpretive programs and guided hikes during the busy seasons. The lake area has seasonal facilities, including the swim beach and boat rentals, which follow seasonal hours.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a family campground, backcountry sites for backpackers, group camping, and vacation cabins, and fees and reservations apply. Outside the park, the nearest hotels are in Winston-Salem, about 30 miles away, with lodging and restaurants also in Danbury, King, and Mount Airy. Pilot Mountain State Park, with its own campground, is about 40 minutes away, and Stone Mountain State Park is a little over an hour away.
