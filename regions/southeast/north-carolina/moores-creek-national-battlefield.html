Moores Creek National Battlefield

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. North Carolina

Preserved Revolutionary War battlefield featuring historic earthworks, walking trails, exhibits, and natural landscapes commemorating a decisive Patriot victory that helped secure North Carolina from British control.

How to Get There

From Wilmington (25 mi): Take I-40 west from Wilmington to Exit 398, follow NC-210 west toward Currie, then continue to Patriot Hall Drive and the battlefield visitor center entrance.

Overview

Moores Creek National Battlefield preserves the site of a brief but decisive Revolutionary War battle fought on February 27, 1776, when Patriot militia defeated a larger Loyalist force trying to reach the coast and link up with British operations. The Patriot victory crushed Loyalist strength in North Carolina, discouraged British plans for the region, and helped push the colony toward independence. The site was first protected in the 1920s as a national military park and is now a unit of the National Park Service with the status of national battlefield. Visitors come for the quiet, wooded setting, the reconstructed earthworks and bridge site, and the exhibits that explain a battle that is little known but historically important.

Access

The battlefield is in the small community of Currie in Pender County, about 25 miles northwest of Wilmington. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, following I-40 and NC 210 to Patriot Hall Drive and the visitor center. There is no public transportation to the site, and parking at the visitor center is generally easy. Admission is free, and the grounds are open from sunrise to sunset, with the visitor center open daily except on a few winter holidays.

Seasons

Spring and fall bring the most comfortable weather for walking the trails, with mild temperatures and fewer insects. Summers are hot and humid, with mosquitoes, ticks and biting flies along the wooded, low-lying trails, so repellent and water are advisable. Winters are generally mild, and the park is quiet then, though low sections of boardwalk and trail can be wet after heavy rain. Special programs and living-history events are often centered around the anniversary of the battle in late February, and hurricane season from June through November can occasionally affect the area.

Things to Do

Walk the Battlefield

Interpretive trails and boardwalks lead through pine forest and wetlands along the creek, passing the historic bridge site, reconstructed earthworks and monuments that tell the story of the battle.

Explore the Visitor Center

Exhibits, artifacts and a short film in the Patriot Hall visitor center explain the events of 1776 and the Loyalist and Patriot forces involved.

Picnicking and Wildlife Watching

The grounds have picnic areas, and the creek-side forests and wetlands offer opportunities to see birds and other wildlife. Leashed pets are welcome outdoors but not inside buildings.

Visitor Centers

The Patriot Hall visitor center is the park's main facility, with exhibits, artifacts, a film, restrooms, a bookstore and rangers who can suggest walks and answer questions. It is the best place to start, as the exhibits give context for the earthworks and bridge site outside. The visitor center is open daily during the day, with a few winter-holiday closures, and the outdoor grounds stay open from sunrise to sunset.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the battlefield. The nearest full range of hotels, motels and restaurants is in Wilmington, about 25 miles away, with additional options in Burgaw and the beach communities near Wilmington, roughly an hour or less by car. Camping is available at Carolina Beach State Park, about an hour south, and at several private campgrounds and RV parks in the Wilmington area. Visitors often combine the battlefield with a stay in Wilmington's historic downtown.
