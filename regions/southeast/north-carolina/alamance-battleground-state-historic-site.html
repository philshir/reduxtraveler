Alamance Battleground State Historic Site

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. North Carolina
  3. Alamance Battleground State Historic Site

Dallas Epperson, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Preserved pre-Revolutionary War battlefield featuring historic grounds, interpretive exhibits, a reconstructed frontier-era homestead, and memorials commemorating a pivotal conflict between colonial officials and backcountry settlers.

General Information
Hours:
Tuesday to Saturday: 9am to 5pm
Guided tours: 11am, 1pm, and 3pm
Closed Sunday, Monday, and designated state holidays
Fees:
Site admission: Free
Guided tours: Adults (18-64) $2 + tax
Seniors (65+) and Military: $1 + tax
Youth (3-17): $1 + tax
Pet Policy:
Leashed pets are permitted on the outdoor grounds; pets are not allowed inside historic buildings except where permitted by site regulations
Closest cities with hotels:
Burlington, North Carolina (approximately 6 mi)
Seasons:
Spring and fall provide the most comfortable weather for exploring the grounds, though the site is open year-round
Location:
5803 NC-62, Burlington, NC 27215
Website:
historicsites.nc.gov/all-sites/alamance-battleground

How to Get There

From Burlington (6 mi): Take NC-62 South from Burlington, continue for approximately six miles through rural Alamance County, and follow signs to the historic site entrance at 5803 NC-62.

Overview

Alamance Battleground State Historic Site preserves the site of the Battle of Alamance, fought May 16, 1771, the culmination of the Regulator Movement, in which backcountry farmers protested corruption and unfair taxation. Armed Regulators confronted militia under Royal Governor William Tryon near Great Alamance Creek and were defeated, but their grievances foreshadowed the drive for independence. The site is operated by North Carolina's state historic sites program and interprets the battle and the conditions of life in the colonial backcountry. Visitors come for the open battlefield, monuments, the visitor center exhibits and the Allen House, a restored frontier log dwelling.

Access

The site is in Alamance County in the North Carolina Piedmont, about 6 miles south of Burlington on NC-62. It is easy to reach from I-40/85, and Greensboro is about 25 miles west, with Durham, Chapel Hill and Raleigh within roughly an hour. Most visitors arrive by car, and parking is free at the site. The grounds are open and largely flat, so walking the battlefield is easy for most visitors. The site is closed Sundays, Mondays and state holidays, so confirm hours before making the drive.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the most comfortable times to walk the battlefield, with mild temperatures and blooming or colorful trees. Summers in the Piedmont are hot and humid, and winters are cool with occasional frost or ice that can limit outdoor exploration. Living-history demonstrations, guided tours and commemorative events are scheduled throughout the year, often around the May anniversary of the battle, so check the site's calendar if you want to catch one. The site is open year-round except for Sundays, Mondays and holidays.

What to See

  • The battlefield and interpretive exhibits
  • Allen House: a restored 18th-century log dwelling showing frontier life in the Piedmont
  • Living-history demonstrations, guided tours, and commemorative events

Visitor Centers

The visitor center at the site has exhibits on the Regulator Movement, the Battle of Alamance and daily life in the colonial backcountry, and staff can help with guided tours. Guided tours are offered at 11am, 1pm and 3pm, for a small additional fee. The center is a good first stop before walking the battlefield and visiting the Allen House.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. Hotels, motels and restaurants are available in Burlington, about 6 miles away, as well as in nearby Graham and Mebane. Greensboro, about 25 miles west, and Durham, Chapel Hill and Raleigh, within about an hour, offer much larger selections. For camping, Eno River State Park near Durham and Jordan Lake State Recreation Area have campgrounds within about an hour's drive.
