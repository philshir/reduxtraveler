National Preserve located in South Florida, about 45 miles (72 kilometers) west of Miami on the Atlantic coastal plain

General Information

Hours:
Big Cypress National Preserve is open for public use 24 hours a day year round. The Nathaniel P. Reed Visitor Center is open seven days per week from 9:00 am to 4:30 pm. The Oasis Visitor Center is open Friday-Monday from 9:00 am to 4:30 pm. Both facilities are closed December 25th.
Fees:
No fees
Pet Policy:
Pets may accompany visitors in the campgrounds, parking lot grassy areas, picnic areas, and at pullouts along the paved scenic drives. Pets NOT allowed on any boardwalks or back country hiking trails or in the visitor centers.
Closest cities with hotels:
Fort Lauderdale

All year

How to Get There

Overview

Big Cypress National Preserve is a unit of the National Park System in South Florida that protects more than 720,000 acres of cypress swamp, prairie, pineland, hardwood hammock and mangrove next to Everglades National Park. Congress established it in 1974, and it was one of the first two national preserves in the park system, set aside largely to protect the fresh water that flows through the Big Cypress Swamp into the Everglades. As a preserve rather than a national park, it still allows activities such as hunting and permitted off-road vehicle use. It is home to alligators, black bears and the endangered Florida panther, and the southern end of the Florida National Scenic Trail is here. The Miccosukee and Seminole Tribes have lived in and used this land for generations and retain rights to live on and use it traditionally. Most visitors come for the Loop Road scenic drive, boardwalk walks, paddling, camping and wildlife watching.

Access

The preserve lies about 45 miles west of Miami, and the Tamiami Trail (US-41) crosses it, so most visitors arrive by car from Miami to the east or Naples to the west. The nearest town is Everglades City. Loop Road (State Road 94) leaves the Tamiami Trail as a scenic drive that is partly unpaved, and some campgrounds, such as Bear Island, sit at the end of long gravel roads. Backcountry areas and off-road vehicle trails are reached by hiking or by permit, and trails may be flooded. Services are limited, so fill the gas tank and bring water, food and insect repellent.

Seasons

The cooler, drier winter season, roughly November through April, is the best time to visit: water levels drop, wildlife concentrates near remaining water, mosquitoes are fewer, and the Florida Trail and ranger-led swamp walks are at their best. Summers are hot, humid and wet, with heavy afternoon storms, flooded trails and intense mosquitoes, and hurricane season runs from June through November. Hunting seasons in fall and winter, including archery, muzzle-loading and general gun hunts, affect some areas, and some campgrounds open only seasonally, so check the National Park Service for current dates and closures.

Things to Do

Drive the Loop Road Scenic Drive

Drive the Loop Road Scenic Drive through remote cypress swamp with frequent alligator and wading-bird sightings.

Walk a Boardwalk

Walk a boardwalk into a cypress swamp, and join ranger-led swamp walks and canoe trips in season.

Camp

12 campgrounds for RVs and vehicles, and backcountry hiking, paddling, and biking.

See Wildlife

See wildlife including roseate spoonbills and wood storks.

Winter's cooler, drier weather is the best time to hike, and regulated deer, turkey, and hog hunting seasons have long helped protect the area.

Top Trails

Kirby Storter Boardwalk

Length: About 1 mile round trip
Difficulty: Easy

Highlights include cypress swamp and prairie marsh overlook.

Tree Snail Hammock Trail

Length: About 0.3 miles round trip
Difficulty: Easy

Highlights include tropical hardwood hammock and rare Florida tree snails.

Gator Hook Trail

Length: About 8 miles round trip
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

Highlights include remote wetlands and cypress; often wet.

Florida Trail - Big Cypress Section

Length: Varies
Difficulty: Moderate

Highlights include wetlands and forests on the statewide scenic trail.

Visitor Centers

The Nathaniel P. Reed Visitor Center is open daily from 9:00 am to 4:30 pm, and the Oasis Visitor Center, which has a boardwalk and an alligator viewing area, is open Friday through Monday from 9:00 am to 4:30 pm. Both are closed December 25. Staff can provide maps, road and trail conditions, and information on ranger programs and campgrounds.

Camping and Lodging

There are no lodges inside the preserve, but it has 12 campgrounds for tents, RVs and vehicles, and reservations are required through Recreation.gov. Midway Campground has 36 sites and is open year-round, Monument Lake Campground sits along US-41 and generally opens from mid-August to mid-April, and Bear Island and Burns Lake offer more remote sites. Hotels and motels are found in Everglades City, Naples and Marco Island to the west and southwest, and in Miami and its western suburbs, each roughly an hour or less from the main visitor areas.
