Torreya State Park

How to Get There

From Tallahassee (50 mi): Take I-10 west to Exit 166, follow FL-12 west toward Bristol, continue north on County Road 271, then follow Torreya Park Road to the entrance of Torreya State Park overlooking the Apalachicola River.

Overview

Torreya State Park is a 13,735-acre Florida state park in the Panhandle, on the east bank of the Apalachicola River, with steep ravines and high bluffs more like southern Appalachia than the Gulf Coastal Plain. It is named for the Florida torreya, a critically endangered conifer found naturally only on these river bluffs. The park was developed by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, which also moved and rebuilt the 1849 Gregory House, a plantation home brought here from the opposite bank of the river and now on the National Register of Historic Places. Visitors come for some of the state's most challenging hiking, including a section of the Florida National Scenic Trail, river views from bluff-top campsites, and fall color.

Access

The park is in Liberty County, about 13 miles north of Bristol, at 2576 NW Torreya Park Road, and is reached from SR-12 near Bristol, following the signs along the county road to the park entrance. It is about 50 miles west of Tallahassee, and most visitors come from I-10. Roads inside the park are paved, but the area is remote, with limited services and cell coverage, so bring food and supplies. The park is best treated as a day trip or overnight stay in the Panhandle rather than a stop on a coastal route.

Seasons

Fall, winter and spring are the best times to visit, with cool, comfortable hiking weather. Fall color along the bluffs is a highlight in late fall, which is unusual for Florida. Spring brings wildflowers and flowing streams, and the Weeping Ridge waterfall is best after rain. Summers are hot and humid, with biting insects, ticks and afternoon thunderstorms, so trails are less pleasant. Hurricane Michael struck the area in 2018, though many protected torreya trees survived, and some trails or facilities may still show storm damage.

Top Trails

Torreya Challenge Loop

Length: About 7 miles loop
Difficulty: Strenuous

Highlights include ravines, river bluffs, and overlooks; one of Florida's toughest hikes.

Gregory House Trail

Length: About 1 mile round trip
Difficulty: Easy

Highlights include historic house and Apalachicola River views.

River Bluff Trails

Length: Varies
Difficulty: Moderate

Highlights include rare plant communities and river overlooks.

Backcountry Trail System

Length: More than 16 miles
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

Highlights include remote terrain for backpackers.

Visitor Centers

Torreya State Park does not have a large visitor center. Rangers at the park entrance provide maps, trail information and details on touring the Gregory House, which is the park's main historic attraction and is open for tours for a small fee. Check with the park for tour days and hours before visiting.

Camping and Lodging

The park offers camping on the bluffs above the river, including a family campground with water and electric hookups, a cabin and a yurt, and primitive backcountry camping along the trail system for backpackers; confirm current availability with Florida State Parks. Motels and hotels are limited nearby. Bristol and Blountstown are the closest towns, and Quincy, Marianna and Tallahassee, within about an hour, offer the widest choice of hotels and restaurants.
