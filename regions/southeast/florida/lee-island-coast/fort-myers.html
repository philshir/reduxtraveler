Overview

Fort Myers is a city on the Caloosahatchee River in southwest Florida and the gateway to the Lee Island Coast, known as the "City of Palms" for the royal palms that line its streets, planted by Thomas Edison. It is the winter home of two of America's greatest inventors, Thomas Edison and Henry Ford, whose adjoining estates are one of the area's major attractions. The city is also close to the beaches of Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel Island, and Captiva Island, and is a popular winter retreat.

Visitors come to Fort Myers for the Edison and Ford Winter Estates, for the downtown River District, and for the beaches of the Gulf. They also come for the J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge on Sanibel Island, for fishing and boating, and for baseball spring training of the Boston Red Sox and the Minnesota Twins.

History

The area was home to the Calusa people, and the U.S. Army built a fort here in 1850 during the Seminole Wars, which was named for Colonel Abraham Myers, who was serving in Florida. The city was incorporated in 1886, and in 1885 Thomas Edison visited and decided to build a winter home and laboratory here, later joined by his friend Henry Ford in 1916. Edison planted the first royal palms along McGregor Boulevard, which became the city's trademark. The city grew with citrus, cattle, and tourism, and Hurricane Ian in 2022 caused major damage to Fort Myers Beach and the surrounding area, from which the region has been recovering.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Edison and Ford Winter Estates include the inventors' homes, Edison's laboratory, a botanical garden, and a museum, and the Caloosahatchee River forms the backdrop. The downtown River District has restaurants, shops, and a riverfront promenade, and McGregor Boulevard is lined with royal palms. Cayo Costa Island and Gasparilla Island are reached by boat, and the Imaginarium Hands-On Museum and the Southwest Florida Museum of History are other attractions.

Culture and Museums

The Edison and Ford Winter Estates have tours and a museum with a collection of Edison's inventions and Ford's early cars, and the Southwest Florida Museum of History covers the region's past. The Sanibel Island Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum has a world-class collection of seashells, and the Barbara B. Mann Performing Arts Hall hosts concerts and shows. The Edison Festival of Light each February celebrates the inventor with a parade.

Parks

The Lee Island Coast has a variety of natural areas. The J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge on Sanibel protects mangroves and wading birds, with a four-mile wildlife drive, and Cayo Costa State Park has undeveloped beaches and camping. Lovers Key State Park and Mound Key Archaeological State Park offer paddling and trails, and Manatee Park has manatees in winter.

Seasons and Weather

Fort Myers has a tropical savanna climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the low 90s°F and a rainy season from June to October, and winters are warm and dry, with highs in the upper 70s°F. The peak season is from December to April, and hurricane season runs from June to November.

Things to Do

  • Edison and Ford Winter Estates: the historic homes, botanical gardens, laboratory, and museum
  • Fort Myers River District: brick streets, galleries, restaurants, theaters, and a lively waterfront downtown
  • Beaches and islands: shelling, swimming, and sunsets at Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel, and Captiva
  • Nature outings: dolphin cruises, manatee encounters, and kayaking through mangroves
  • Other sights: the Imaginarium Science Center, Southwest Florida Museum of History, and Calusa Nature Center and Planetarium
