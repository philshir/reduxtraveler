Overview

Venice is a small city on Florida's Gulf Coast in Sarasota County, known for its Italian Renaissance-style architecture, its quiet beaches, and its title of "Shark Tooth Capital of the World," because of the fossilized shark teeth that wash up on its shores. Its walkable downtown has arcaded buildings and a historic island setting, and its relaxed pace makes it popular with retirees and families alike. Unlike its Italian namesake, it has no canals in the historic center, but it has an Intracoastal Waterway and bays.

Visitors come to Venice for the beaches, particularly Caspersen Beach and Venice Beach, where visitors can hunt for fossilized shark teeth, and for the downtown shops and restaurants on West Venice Avenue. They also come for the Venice Fishing Pier, for the Legacy Trail, and for the nearby Myakka River State Park, and it is about an hour south of Tampa and a short drive from Sarasota.

History

The area was inhabited by the Calusa people, and the first European settlers arrived in the late 1800s, with the town originally named Horse and Chaise. In the 1920s, the Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers purchased the land to create a planned city and hired architects to design it in Northern Italian Renaissance style, with a grid and street names that reflect its Mediterranean theme. After the land boom collapsed, the city was incorporated in 1927. The Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus held winter quarters in Venice from 1960 to 1992, and the city has since become a popular retirement community.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Venice Historic Downtown has Italian-style arcades with shops, galleries, and restaurants, and the Venice Pier at Venice Beach is a favorite for fishing and sunsets. Caspersen Beach is a long, wild beach popular for shark tooth hunting, and the Venice Train Depot is a historic building on the Legacy Trail. The Venice Jetty offers views of the inlet, and the Venice Art Center and the Venice Museum & Archives display local art and history.

Culture and Museums

The Venice Museum & Archives tells the story of the city's history, and the Venice Art Center and Venice Theatre host exhibits and performances. The Venice Symphony and the Venice Little Theatre are among the cultural organizations, and the city hosts the Venice Shark's Tooth Festival in April. The nearby Sarasota area has many more museums and cultural attractions.

Parks

The Legacy Trail is a 10-mile paved trail that runs from Venice to Sarasota, and Venice Beach and Caspersen Beach offer quiet Gulf shores. Oscar Scherer State Park has trails, a freshwater lake, and gopher tortoises, and the Venice Rookery is a nesting area for wading birds. Myakka River State Park and the Venice Area Audubon Rookery are within a short drive.

Seasons and Weather

Venice has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the low 90s°F and daily thunderstorms, and winters are mild and dry, with highs in the 70s°F. The busiest season is from December to April, and hurricane season runs from June to November.

Things to Do

  • Hunt for shark teeth at Venice Beach and Caspersen Beach, and visit during the annual Shark's Tooth Festival
  • Stroll downtown Venice for shops, galleries, and restaurants
  • Walk the Venice Fishing Pier for Gulf views, dolphin sightings, and sunsets
  • Paddle and bike along the waterways and coastal habitats
