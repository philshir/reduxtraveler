How to Get There

From Orlando (49 miles): Get on FL-408 E. Continue on FL-408 E. Take FL-50 E to FL-406 E/Garden St in Titusville. Take exit 220 from I-95 N. Drive to State Hwy 402

Overview

Canaveral National Seashore is a unit of the National Park System on a barrier island between New Smyrna Beach and Titusville, covering about 57,000 acres of beach, dunes, hammock, mangrove and the Mosquito Lagoon. Congress established it on January 3, 1975, and it protects 24 miles of undeveloped Atlantic beach, the longest stretch of undeveloped shoreline on Florida's east coast. The seashore shares its barrier island with NASA's Kennedy Space Center, whose security buffer has helped keep the area wild. Visitors come for quiet beaches, paddling and fishing in the lagoon, the chance to see nesting sea turtles, and views of rocket launches.

Access

The seashore has two main road entrances that are not connected to each other. Apollo Beach, on the north end, is reached via State Road A1A south of New Smyrna Beach, and Playalinda Beach, on the south end, is reached from Titusville via State Road 402, which passes through Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge. Klondike Beach in the middle has no road access and can be reached only on foot. The National Park Service headquarters is at 212 S. Washington Avenue in Titusville. Access, especially to Playalinda, is often restricted around rocket launches at Kennedy Space Center, so check conditions before you go.

Seasons

Summer is nesting season for loggerhead, green and leatherback sea turtles, and it is also hot, humid and buggy, with afternoon thunderstorms and the risk of hurricanes from June through November. Winter and spring bring milder weather, fewer insects and good birding and manatee watching in the lagoon. Fishing for redfish and seatrout is good in the lagoon through much of the year. Launches and NASA operations can close access roads and beaches with little notice in any season.

Things to Do

Beach at Apollo or Playalinda
The seashore's two main access points both offer miles of uncrowded Atlantic coastline and dune scenery for swimming, sunbathing, and beachcombing. Playalinda also happens to be one of the best public spots to watch a rocket launch from nearby Kennedy Space Center when one's scheduled.

Paddle Mosquito Lagoon
This shallow estuary — part of the Indian River Lagoon system — is one of Florida's best paddling and fishing destinations, with redfish, spotted seatrout, manatees, dolphins, and plenty of birdlife in its calm waters.

Watch for Nesting Sea Turtles
Thousands of loggerhead, green, and leatherback sea turtles nest here each summer, making this one of the most important sea turtle nesting beaches in the country.

Top Trails

Eldora Hammock Trail

Length: Approximately 0.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short interpretive trail explores a maritime hammock ecosystem near the historic community of Eldora. Visitors can learn about native plants, local history, and the natural habitats that characterize Florida's barrier islands.

Castle Windy Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located in the Apollo Beach area, this trail leads through coastal vegetation and provides scenic views of Mosquito Lagoon. Interpretive signs highlight the area's ecology, wildlife, and cultural history.

Playalinda Beach Walking Route

Length: Up to 24 miles of shoreline access
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The undeveloped beach offers outstanding opportunities for long walks along the Atlantic Ocean, with pristine coastal scenery, shell collecting, and occasional views of rocket launches from nearby space facilities.

Turtle Mound

Along State Road A1A south of New Smyrna Beach, Turtle Mound is the largest shell midden on the mainland United States — a turtle-shaped heap of oyster shells and refuse built up by the prehistoric Timucuan people, once as tall as 75 feet before 19th- and 20th-century shellrock mining reduced it. Archaeologists believe it may have served as a high-ground hurricane refuge, and for centuries it also served as a landmark for passing mariners. A short boardwalk climbs to the top for a sweeping view over the lagoon.

One more thing worth knowing: Kennedy Space Center sits at the southern end of this same barrier island, so seashore access is sometimes restricted during launches — and Playalinda Beach is one of the best public spots to watch one from Launch Complex 39A/B when timing lines up.

Visitor Centers

The Apollo Beach Visitor Center is the seashore's main visitor facility, and the National Park Service headquarters in Titusville also provides information. The Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge visitor center, nearby on the route to Playalinda, can help with maps and conditions for the south end. Confirm current hours with the National Park Service, as they can change.

Camping and Lodging

There is no developed campground in the seashore, but backcountry camping is available; check with the National Park Service for permits, locations and rules. Lodging and restaurants are in Titusville, about 8 miles from the seashore, and New Smyrna Beach, about 9 miles away, with additional options in Cocoa Beach and Orlando, about an hour away. Tomoka State Park near Ormond Beach has a campground within roughly an hour.
