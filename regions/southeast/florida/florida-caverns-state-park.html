How to Get There

From Marianna (2 mi): Take Caverns Road west from downtown Marianna and continue approximately two miles to the entrance of Florida Caverns State Park.

Overview

Florida Caverns State Park is a Florida state park near Marianna, in the Panhandle, and the only Florida state park that offers guided tours of a dry cave system. The state bought the first land in 1935, and Civilian Conservation Corps crews, who arrived in 1938, developed the tour cave, roads, trails and the limestone visitor center; the caverns opened to the public in 1941 and the park in 1942. Inside the cave, a ranger-led walk passes stalactites, stalagmites, flowstone and columns in lit passageways. Above ground, the park's roughly 1,450 acres of upland forest, sinkholes and Chipola River floodplain offer hiking, biking, horseback riding, paddling, fishing and camping, and the swimming hole at Blue Hole Spring is a summer favorite.

Access

The park is in Jackson County, a few miles north of Marianna on the Chipola River, in the Florida Panhandle, west of Tallahassee. Visitors arrive by car on paved roads from Marianna, off I-10 and US 90. Cave tour tickets are sold at the gift shop on a first-come, first-served basis and can sell out on weekends and holidays, so arrive early. Leashed pets are allowed in outdoor areas but not in the cavern or park buildings.

Seasons

The park is open daily from 8am to sunset year-round, and cave tours run seven days a week, subject to availability and the schedule posted by the park. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for hiking and the cave stays cool all year, which makes a tour a good summer escape. Summer is hot and humid and is the main season for swimming at Blue Hole Spring. The Chipola River floods in wet periods, and high water can close Blue Hole, the River Trail, the boat ramp and the campgrounds, so check the park's alerts before visiting. Some bat caves are closed in winter to protect hibernating bats, and the cave tour is not affected. Hurricane Michael in 2018 destroyed most of the park's trees and closed the caves for nine months, so some areas remain recovering forest.

Things to Do

Tour the Florida Cavern

The ranger-guided walk through the lit cave is the park's signature activity and passes stalactites, stalagmites, flowstone and columns. Tickets are sold at the gift shop and can sell out, so arrive early on weekends and holidays.

Hike and Bike the Woodland Trails

Hiking and multi-use trails wind past sinkholes, rock outcrops and the Chipola River floodplain, and the CCC-built Bluff and Beech Magnolia trails pass rock outcrops and Appalachian plant species.

Paddle, Fish and Swim

A boat ramp near the entrance serves canoes, kayaks and small boats on the Chipola River, and Blue Hole Spring is open for swimming without lifeguards. Both can close when the river floods.

Camp and Ride

The park has a campground and an equestrian camping area, and its multi-use trails are open to horseback riders as well as hikers and cyclists.

Top Trails

Sinkhole Trail

Length: 1.1 miles
Elevation Gain: Not published
Difficulty: Not rated

A multi-use trail open to hikers, cyclists and horseback riders that passes through forest and past sinkholes.

Bumpnose Trail

Length: 6.8 miles
Elevation Gain: Not published
Difficulty: Strenuous

The park's longest trail, open to hikers, cyclists and horseback riders, for visitors who want a full day on the park's upland and floodplain terrain.

Visitor Centers

The limestone visitor center, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps, houses a museum with interpretive exhibits on the cave, geology and park history, and an audiovisual program about touring the cavern. The gift shop next door sells cave tour tickets and handles ticket questions (850-526-2650). The park is open 8am to sunset, with cave tours generally running 9am to 4pm Central Time. Check the Florida State Parks website for current schedules and any flood-related closures.

Camping and Lodging

The park has 38 campsites, 32 in the Blue Hole campground and 6 in an equestrian camping area with a stable; reservations are made through the Florida State Parks system. Lodging is plentiful in Marianna, 2 to 3 miles away, and in nearby Cottondale, with chain hotels along I-10 and US 90. Within about an hour or so, Tallahassee, Dothan (Alabama) and Panama City offer more choices, and Falling Waters State Park near Chipley also has a campground.
