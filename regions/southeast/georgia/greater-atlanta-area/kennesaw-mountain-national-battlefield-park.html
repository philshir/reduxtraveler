How to Get There

From Atlanta, GA (25 mi): Take I-75 N to exit 269 for Barrett Parkway. Turn left onto Barrett Parkway, then right onto Old 41 Highway. Follow signs to the park entrance at Kennesaw Mountain Drive.

Overview

Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park preserves the site of a pivotal June 1864 battle in General William T. Sherman's Atlanta Campaign, in which Confederate forces under General Joseph E. Johnston held off Union assaults. Set aside as a battlefield site in 1917 and redesignated a national battlefield park in 1935, it protects nearly 3,000 acres of well-preserved earthworks, artillery positions and monuments. The mountain itself rises 1,808 feet, and from the summit there are sweeping views of the Atlanta skyline and the terrain that shaped the battle. With more than 18 miles of trails, the park is as popular with local hikers and runners as with history visitors.

Access

The park is in Kennesaw and Marietta, Georgia, in Cobb County about 25 miles northwest of downtown Atlanta. From Atlanta, take I-75 north to Barrett Parkway and follow signs to the main entrance and visitor center on Old Highway 41, with other trailheads at Kolb Farm and Cheatham Hill. Parking at the visitor center fills quickly on pleasant weekends. On weekends and holidays, the summit road is closed to private vehicles, and a shuttle bus carries visitors to the top for a small fee.

Seasons

Spring brings dogwoods and wildflowers along the trails, and the weather is pleasant for hiking, while fall color is also popular. Summers are hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms, so early morning is the best time to climb the mountain. Winters are generally mild, though cold snaps and occasional ice can make steep trails slippery, and bare trees open up longer views. Special ranger programs, living history events and battle anniversary commemorations are held throughout the year, with many in June around the anniversary of the battle.

Things to Do

Hike to the Summit
The most popular activity is the climb to the top of Kennesaw Mountain for panoramic views over the battlefield and Atlanta.

Visit the Visitor Center and Museum
Start with the film and exhibits explaining the Atlanta Campaign and the battle.

See the Earthworks and Battle Positions
Preserved Confederate trenches and artillery positions make the fighting tangible.

Join a Ranger Program
Ranger-led tours and living history events bring the battle to life.

Visitor Centers

The Visitor Center on Old Highway 41 is the main stop, with exhibits, artifacts, maps, a film on the Atlanta Campaign and a bookstore. Rangers can recommend trails and explain the battlefield, and ranger-led tours and programs begin from here. Other sites in the park, such as Kolb Farm and Cheatham Hill, have trailhead signs and interpretive markers.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the park. Hotels and inns are available in Kennesaw, Marietta and Acworth, all within about 15 minutes, with many more options in Cartersville and downtown Atlanta within about 30 to 45 minutes. For camping, Red Top Mountain State Park on Lake Allatoona, roughly 30 minutes north, has a campground, cottages and a lodge, and Stone Mountain Park east of Atlanta also has a campground.
