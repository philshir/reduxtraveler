How to Get There

From Helen, GA (41 mi): Take GA-75 N to US-76 E through Hiawassee and Clayton, then turn left onto Black Rock Mountain Parkway in Mountain City and follow the steep road to the summit entrance.

Overview

Black Rock Mountain State Park is Georgia's highest state park, perched at 3,640 feet near Mountain City in the Blue Ridge Mountains. The park straddles the Eastern Continental Divide, and its overlooks offer vistas of up to 80 miles across Georgia and neighboring states, with temperatures that run noticeably cooler than in the lowlands. It protects about 1,700 acres of mountain forest with four hiking trails and a 17-acre lake. Visitors come for the sweeping views, the blooming rhododendrons in spring, the fall foliage, and the cool summer air. The steep, winding road to the summit is part of the adventure.

Access

The park is in Rabun County in the northeast corner of Georgia, about 3 miles from Clayton and a short drive from Mountain City. Drivers leave US-441/23 and take Black Rock Mountain Parkway, a narrow, winding road that climbs steeply to the summit. The drive is not suited for large trailers or long RVs, so check the park's guidance before towing. The park is roughly two hours from Atlanta, and there is no public transportation. A daily parking pass is required, and the visitor center and overlooks are near the top of the mountain.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, but weather at this elevation can change quickly and is often much colder than in the valleys. Spring is a favorite time, when rhododendron, mountain laurel, and wildflowers bloom. Summers are mild and a welcome break from the heat, though afternoon storms are common. Fall brings brilliant color across the ridges and is the busiest season. In winter, snow, ice, and strong winds can close the park or the mountain road, so check conditions before driving up.

Things to Do

Take In the Mountain Overlooks
Multiple overlooks give sweeping views of the southern Appalachians, with long-range vistas across Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Tennessee.

See the Fall Colors
The hardwood forests turn brilliant red, orange, and yellow in autumn, making this one of Georgia's most popular foliage destinations.

Stay Overnight on the Mountain
Choose a mountaintop cottage, an RV site, or a walk-in or backcountry campsite. The summit visitor center has a gift shop.

Visitor Centers

A visitor center near the summit has exhibits on the area's natural history, a gift shop, and staff who can give directions to overlooks and trails. It is a good first stop for a map and information about conditions on the mountain. Hours are seasonal, so check the Georgia State Parks website, and a few of the best overlooks are right beside the parking areas and need only a short walk.

Camping and Lodging

The park has mountaintop cottages, RV and tent sites, and walk-in and backcountry campsites with bear-proof storage. Reservations are recommended for weekends and in October. In nearby Clayton, about 3 miles away, motels, inns, and cabin rentals are plentiful. Within an hour, Highlands, North Carolina, offers resorts and inns, and the Lake Burton area has cabins and Moccasin Creek State Park, which has a campground. Helen and Dahlonega, roughly an hour or more away, offer additional hotels and bed-and-breakfasts.
