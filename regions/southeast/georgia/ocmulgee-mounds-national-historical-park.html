Ocmulgee Mounds National Historical Park

★5.0
  1. Southeast
  2. Georgia
  3. Ocmulgee Mounds National Historical Park

Thomson20192, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Prehistoric ceremonial site featuring ancient earthen mounds, a museum with Native American artifacts, and trails through historically significant landscapes.

How to Get There

From Macon, GA (2 mi): Take US-80 E/GA-19 N/Emery Highway eastbound for about 2 miles; the park entrance will be on the right just past Maynard Street.

Overview

Ocmulgee Mounds National Historical Park preserves more than 12,000 years of continuous human habitation, centered on massive earthen mounds built by the Mississippian culture around 900 CE, including the Great Temple Mound and the Earth Lodge with its original 1,000-year-old clay floor. The Visitor Center museum displays artifacts spanning multiple cultural periods. The park remains a place of living heritage for the Muscogee (Creek) Nation, whose ancestors built and used these earthworks, and hosts annual Indigenous celebrations and ranger-led programs. It began as a national monument in the 1930s and was later redesignated a national historical park, reflecting its importance among the Southeast's most significant Native American sites. Today it is a popular, free-to-enter stop for history, archaeology and short walks on the edge of Macon.

Access

The park sits just east of downtown Macon, Georgia, about 2 miles from the city center along Emery Highway (US-80 and GA-19), and is easy to reach from I-16 and I-75. Macon is roughly 85 miles southeast of Atlanta, making the park an easy day trip or a stop on the way to the coast. Parking is free at the Visitor Center. A railroad line runs through the park, so trains can be heard and sometimes seen near the trails and mounds, and visitors should use care when crossing at designated points.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and spring and fall offer the most pleasant weather for walking the mounds and trails. Summers in central Georgia are hot and humid, so plan outdoor visits for the morning, and thunderstorms are common in the afternoon. Winters are mild with occasional chilly days, and crowds are light. Special programs and Indigenous celebrations are typically held in the fall, so check the National Park Service website for the current schedule. The park is closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day.

Things to Do

Step Inside the Earth Lodge
Enter the reconstructed council chamber built over the original 1,000-year-old clay floor, complete with its eagle-shaped platform.

Climb the Great Temple Mound
The park's largest mound rewards the climb with sweeping views over the Ocmulgee River valley and ceremonial complex below.

Tour the Visitor Center Museum
See pottery, tools, weapons, and ceremonial objects recovered from the site, with exhibits on the cultures that built the mounds.

Top Trails

Mounds and Earth Lodge Trail
Distance: 1.5 miles round trip
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Connects the Earth Lodge, ceremonial mounds, and interpretive exhibits in one loop.

Great Temple Mound Trail
Distance: 1 mile round trip
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Direct route to the park's largest mound and surrounding ceremonial grounds.

Ocmulgee Heritage Trail Connection
Distance: Variable
Difficulty: Easy
Highlights: Links to the wider Ocmulgee Heritage Trail through forests and wetlands along the river.

Visitor Centers

The Visitor Center and museum near the park entrance is the best place to start. Exhibits trace the area's human history through pottery, tools, weapons and ceremonial objects spanning many cultural periods, and rangers can explain the layout of the mound complex and answer trail questions. The Earth Lodge, a reconstructed council chamber, is open during the same daily hours as the Visitor Center.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging within the park. A full range of chain hotels, restaurants and services is available in nearby Macon, which is only a few minutes away, and in Warner Robins, about 20 miles south. For camping, High Falls State Park near Jackson has a campground within about 30 miles, and there are additional campgrounds around the lakes of central Georgia. Atlanta is about an hour and a half away for those who want a bigger selection of lodging.
