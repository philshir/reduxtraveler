Overview

Atlanta is the capital and largest city of Georgia and the economic and cultural center of the Southeast, a city of skyscrapers set among rolling, tree-covered hills. It is famous as the birthplace of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and a center of the civil rights movement, as the headquarters of Coca-Cola, CNN, and Delta Air Lines, and as the host of the 1996 Summer Olympics. Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport is the busiest airport in the world, making the city a major hub for travelers.

Visitors come to Atlanta for the Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park and the National Center for Civil and Human Rights, and for attractions near Centennial Olympic Park, including the Georgia Aquarium and the World of Coca-Cola. They also come for the High Museum of Art, Piedmont Park, the Atlanta BeltLine, and the city's famous Southern food and music scene.

History

The city began in 1837 as "Terminus," the end of a planned railroad line, and was renamed Atlanta in 1845, growing rapidly as a rail hub. During the Civil War, it was a key supply center for the Confederacy, and General William Tecumseh Sherman captured it in September 1864 and burned much of it before beginning his March to the Sea. The city rose from the ashes, and in 1868 it became the state capital. In the twentieth century, Atlanta became the center of the civil rights movement, with leaders such as Martin Luther King Jr., John Lewis, and Andrew Young, and in the 1960s it called itself "the city too busy to hate." The 1996 Olympics brought new development and international attention.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Centennial Olympic Park in downtown is the legacy of the 1996 Games, with a fountain of the Olympic rings, and it is surrounded by the Georgia Aquarium, one of the largest aquariums in the world with whale sharks and beluga whales, and the World of Coca-Cola. The Georgia State Capitol has a gold-leaf dome, and the Fox Theatre is a landmark 1929 movie palace. Ponce City Market, the BeltLine, and Little Five Points are popular neighborhoods, and the SkyView Atlanta Ferris wheel offers views of the city.

Culture and Museums

Atlanta has a wide range of museums. The Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park includes King's birth home, Ebenezer Baptist Church, and the King Center, where he and Coretta Scott King are entombed. The High Museum of Art, the Atlanta History Center, the Fernbank Museum of Natural History, the Jimmy Carter Presidential Library, the Margaret Mitchell House, and the CDC Museum are among its major institutions, and the College Football Hall of Fame is downtown.

Parks

Piedmont Park, a 200-acre park in Midtown, is the city's central green space, with views of the skyline and hosts festivals, while the Atlanta BeltLine is a 22-mile loop of trails and parks along old railroad corridors. Grant Park is home to Zoo Atlanta, and the Atlanta Botanical Garden has a canopy walk and orchid displays. Stone Mountain Park and the Chattahoochee River National Recreation Area are within a short drive, and Freedom Park and Woodruff Park are other green spaces.

Sports

Atlanta has several major league teams. The Atlanta Falcons of the NFL and Atlanta United of Major League Soccer play at Mercedes-Benz Stadium, and the Atlanta Hawks of the NBA play at State Farm Arena. The Atlanta Braves of Major League Baseball play at Truist Park, just northwest of the city in Cumberland, and won the World Series in 2021, and the Atlanta Dream of the WNBA play nearby in College Park.

Seasons and Weather

Atlanta has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the low 90s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and occasional ice storms. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, with dogwoods and azaleas in bloom in spring and colorful foliage in fall. Thunderstorms are common in summer, and the city's tree canopy gives it a green look through most of the year.
