Overview

The Golden Isles are four barrier islands and the mainland port of Brunswick on Georgia's Atlantic coast, midway between Savannah and Jacksonville. Mild winters, beaches, salt marshes, maritime forests, golf, and history draw about 2.4 million visitors a year. The islands are St. Simons Island (the largest), Jekyll Island, exclusive Sea Island, and undeveloped, boat-only Little St. Simons Island.

The coast was contested by Spain and England, and General James Oglethorpe built Fort Frederica in 1736 to defend the new colony, winning the 1742 Battle of Bloody Marsh. Brunswick, laid out in the "Oglethorpe grid," was named one of the original five U.S. ports of entry in 1789. Jekyll Island later became a winter retreat for millionaires such as J.P. Morgan and William Vanderbilt.

St. Simons Island has beaches, golf, dining, and the St. Simons Lighthouse, plus Fort Frederica and Cannon's Point Preserve. Jekyll Island offers 10 miles of beach, Driftwood Beach, the Jekyll Island Club Historic District, and the Georgia Sea Turtle Center. Sea Island is home to the Cloister resort, and Little St. Simons Island has a small lodge for a wilderness getaway. Also on the coast are Cumberland Island National Seashore and the Dungeness Ruins, Hofwyl-Broadfield Plantation, Crooked River State Park, and Sapelo Island.

Golf is a big draw, with the PGA Tour's RSM Classic played each November on Sea Island.

Cumberland Island National Seashore

Barrier island sanctuary featuring undeveloped beaches, maritime forests, historic ruins, and diverse wildlife including wild horses and sea turtles.

Dungeness Ruins
★ 5.0

Gilded Age mansion remains surrounded by wild horses, maritime forest, and historic outbuildings, offering a glimpse into the island’s layered past.

Judson McCranie, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Fort Frederica National Monument
★ 5.0

Preserves the archaeological remnants of a fort and town built by James Oglethorpe between 1736 and 1748 to protect the southern boundary of the British colony of Georgia from Spanish raids

Judson McCranie, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Hofwyl-Broadfield Plantation
★ 5.0

A preserved coastal rice plantation featuring an antebellum home filled with original family heirlooms, historic rice dike remnants, moss-draped live oaks, and marsh nature trails along a tidal river delta.

Dsdugan, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Jekyll Island
★ 5.0

One of the Sea Islands, one of the Golden Isles of Georgia barrier islands and a popular tourist destination with beaches frequented by vacationers

Thomson20192, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Sapelo Island Wildlife Management Area
★ 5.0

Expansive coastal preserve offering primitive camping, hunting, and wildlife viewing across maritime forests, tidal creeks, and salt marshes.

St. Simons Island
★ 5.0

Both a seaside resort and residential community, it is the largest of Georgia's renowned Golden Isles known for its warm climate, beaches, variety of outdoor activities, shops and restaurants, historical sites, and its natural environment

St. Simons Island Lighthouse Museum
★ 5.0

Historic light station featuring a climbable tower, restored keeper’s quarters, and exhibits on maritime navigation and coastal heritage.
