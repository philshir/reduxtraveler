How to Get There

Overview

Jekyll Island is the southernmost of Georgia's Golden Isles, a state-owned barrier island operated by the Jekyll Island Authority and protected by a unique state law requiring that at least 65 percent of its land remain undeveloped. In 1886 a group of wealthy industrialists, including the Rockefellers, Morgans and Vanderbilts, created the Jekyll Island Club here as an exclusive winter retreat, and a secret 1910 meeting at the club helped shape the Federal Reserve System. The State of Georgia bought the island in 1947 and opened it to the public, and today its Gilded Age Jekyll Island Club National Historic Landmark District is the centerpiece. Visitors come for the historic district, the sculptural trees of Driftwood Beach, the Georgia Sea Turtle Center, quiet beaches and more than 20 miles of paved bike trails, and the island is known for being uncrowded and natural.

Access

Jekyll Island is reached by a causeway from the mainland near Brunswick, about 70 miles south of Savannah and 70 miles north of Jacksonville, Florida, and a short drive from I-95. Most visitors arrive by car, and a parking pass is required for vehicles on the island, available for a day, a week or a year. Once there, the flat terrain and extensive network of paved paths make bicycles a popular way to get around, and rentals are widely available. Public transportation to the island is limited.

Seasons

Jekyll Island has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F and the busiest beach crowds, while winters are mild, with highs in the 60s°F. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons for cycling and beachcombing, and loggerhead sea turtles nest on the beaches from May to August, with hatchlings emerging into early fall. Hurricane season runs from June to November. Driftwood Beach is especially photogenic at sunrise and low tide.

History

The island was home to Native Americans and was named in 1734 by General Oglethorpe for his friend Sir Joseph Jekyll. In 1886, a group of wealthy industrialists purchased it and created the Jekyll Island Club, an exclusive winter retreat for members such as J. P. Morgan, William Rockefeller, and Joseph Pulitzer, who built "cottages" there. In November 1910, a secret meeting at the club by bankers and a senator drew up the plan that became the Federal Reserve System. The club closed in 1942 because of World War II, and the State of Georgia bought the island in 1947 to open it to the public as a state park.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Jekyll Island Club National Historic Landmark District has restored mansions and the grand Jekyll Island Club Resort, with tours that tell the story of the Gilded Age elite. Driftwood Beach, at the northern end, has dramatic bleached trees on the sand and is a favorite of photographers. The Georgia Sea Turtle Center is a hospital and education center for injured sea turtles, and the Horton House ruins, a tabby house from the 1730s, are on the north end. The Faith Chapel, with Tiffany windows, is also notable.

Culture and Museums

The Mosaic Museum tells the story of Jekyll Island's history, including the Gilded Age, and the Georgia Sea Turtle Center has exhibits and a rehabilitation hospital. The island also has the Jekyll Island Arts Association and the Jekyll Island Convention Center. The Wanderer, a slave ship that landed illegally on Jekyll Island in 1858, is an important part of the island's history, and is covered in exhibits.

Parks

The island has 10 miles of beaches, including Driftwood Beach and Great Dunes Park, and the state park has more than 20 miles of bike trails through maritime forest and along the marsh. The Clam Creek Picnic Area and fishing pier, the Summer Waves water park, and the campground are other attractions, and kayaking tours of the salt marsh are popular. The Georgia Sea Turtle Center runs beach walks in summer to watch sea turtles nest.

Things to Do

Tour the Historic District
Tram tours leave several times daily from the Jekyll Island Museum on Stable Road, or explore the cottages, museums, and galleries on a self-guided 1- to 2-mile walk.

Visit Driftwood Beach
Weathered trees and driftwood sculptures line the northern shoreline, making it one of Georgia's most distinctive coastal landscapes.

Relax on the Beach
Eight miles of wide, flat beach are ideal for swimming, beachcombing, and walking or biking on the hard-packed sand.

Visit the Georgia Sea Turtle Center
This rehabilitation, research, and education center lets visitors see recovering sea turtles and learn about coastal conservation.

Bike the Island
More than 20 miles of paved, mostly flat trails link beaches, historic sites, maritime forest, and marsh. Kayaking, paddleboarding, and golf are also popular, and Summer Waves water park is open in season.

Visitor Centers

The Jekyll Island Welcome Center, near the causeway entrance, provides maps, passes and information on events and tours. The Jekyll Island Museum Visitor Center in the Historic District, on Stable Road, is the starting point for tram tours of the Jekyll Island Club landmark district. The Georgia Sea Turtle Center is both a working rehabilitation hospital and a museum, and the Mosaic Museum covers the island's history.

Camping and Lodging

Lodging on the island includes the historic Jekyll Island Club Resort, other hotels and inns, and vacation rentals, and the Jekyll Island Campground offers tent and RV camping. Brunswick and St. Simons Island, within about 20 to 30 minutes, have many hotels, inns and vacation rentals, and Kingsland and St. Marys are within about 45 minutes. Fernandina Beach, Florida, and Jacksonville are about an hour south. Other camping nearby includes Crooked River State Park and Fort Clinch State Park in Florida.
