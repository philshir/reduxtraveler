Overview

South Carolina is a Southeastern state of about 5.4 million people, known for its historic Lowcountry, beaches, and Southern cuisine. Columbia is the capital, Charleston is among the most visited historic cities in the South, and the state takes its name from Carolus, the Latin form of Charles, for King Charles I.

Visitors come for the cobblestone streets and mansions of Charleston, the beaches and golf of Myrtle Beach and Hilton Head Island, and the coastal villages of Beaufort and Murrells Inlet. Others come for the Blue Ridge foothills near Greenville, the waterfalls of the Upstate, and the old-growth forest of Congaree National Park.

Geography

South Carolina has three regions. The Atlantic Coastal Plain, or Lowcountry, has marshes, barrier islands, and sea islands, the Piedmont is rolling red-clay hills, and the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northwest are the state's highest area. Sassafras Mountain, at 3,553 feet (1,083 m), is the highest point. The state's rivers include the Savannah, Santee, and Pee Dee, and the Sea Islands are famous for Gullah culture.

History

Cherokee, Catawba, and other Native peoples lived in the region before English settlers founded Charles Town in 1670. Rice and indigo plantations made the Lowcountry rich through enslaved labor, and South Carolina became the 8th state in 1788. In 1860 it was the first state to secede, and the Civil War began with the attack on Fort Sumter in 1861. The 20th century brought textile mills, military bases, tourism, and the civil rights movement, and Gullah Geechee culture preserves African traditions on the Sea Islands.

Charleston

Charleston is South Carolina's most visited city and one of the best-preserved historic cities in the United States. Founded in 1670 as Charles Town, it is known for its cobblestone streets, pastel houses on Rainbow Row, church steeples, and the waterfront promenade at White Point Garden and The Battery. The Civil War began in Charleston Harbor with the attack on Fort Sumter in April 1861, and visitors can also see Fort Moultrie and the Patriots Point Naval and Maritime Museum. Nearby plantations such as Middleton Place, Magnolia Plantation, and Boone Hall tell the story of the Lowcountry, including the lives of enslaved people, as does the Old Slave Mart Museum. The city is also known for its Lowcountry cooking, including shrimp and grits, and for the beaches of Folly Beach and Sullivan's Island.

Myrtle Beach

Myrtle Beach anchors the Grand Strand, a 60-mile stretch of wide, sandy beaches along the northeast coast of South Carolina, and it draws about 20 million visitors a year. The area is known for the oceanfront Boardwalk and the SkyWheel, family attractions, mini-golf, live shows, seafood buffets, and more than 80 golf courses. Visitors can also walk the live oaks and sculpture gardens of Brookgreen Gardens, a former rice plantation turned botanical garden and wildlife preserve, and the seafood restaurants and the Marshwalk of Murrells Inlet. Huntington Beach State Park and Myrtle Beach State Park offer quieter stretches of shoreline. Summer is the peak season, with warm water and crowds, while spring and fall bring lower prices, and hurricane season runs from June through November.

Greenville

Greenville is the largest city in the Upstate, set against the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains, and it has become one of the South's most admired small cities. Its downtown is centered on Falls Park on the Reedy, where a curved pedestrian bridge overlooks a waterfall in the middle of the city, and on Main Street, which is lined with trees, shops, galleries, and some of the state's best restaurants. The Swamp Rabbit Trail is a popular 20-mile greenway for walking and biking, and the Greenville County Museum of Art and the Upcountry History Museum are cultural highlights. The mountains nearby offer waterfalls and hiking at Table Rock and Caesars Head, and the region is known for peach orchards and fall color.

National Protected Areas

South Carolina's national park sites include Congaree National Park, the largest intact old-growth bottomland hardwood forest in the Southeast, and Fort Sumter and Fort Moultrie National Historical Park in Charleston Harbor. Cowpens National Battlefield preserves a pivotal Revolutionary War victory, and Kings Mountain National Military Park, Ninety Six National Historic Site, Charles Pinckney National Historic Site, and the Reconstruction Era National Historical Park in Beaufort also tell the state's story. The Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor spans the coast.

State Parks and Historic Sites

South Carolina has 47 state parks. Hunting Island has beaches and a lighthouse that can be climbed, Caesars Head has dramatic overlooks in the Blue Ridge, and Table Rock and Jones Gap are popular Upstate hikes. Edisto Beach, Huntington Beach, and Myrtle Beach State Parks protect coastal habitats, and Glendale Shoals has a historic mill ruin and falls. Brookgreen Gardens combines sculpture with a historic rice plantation.

Seasons and When to Visit

Spring and fall are the best seasons, with azaleas and wisteria in the Lowcountry in March and April and mild weather in October. Summer is hot and humid, with the busiest beach season from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Winters are mild on the coast, and the Upstate sees occasional snow. Hurricane season runs June through November, with the highest risk from August to October.
