How to Get There

From Asheville, NC: Take I‑26 East toward Spartanburg, then continue on I‑85 South and exit toward the downtown area.

Overview

Greenville is a city in the Upstate region of South Carolina, at the foot of the Blue Ridge Mountains, and it is known for its award-winning downtown, which is centered on Falls Park on the Reedy and a walkable Main Street lined with trees, shops, and restaurants. Once a textile manufacturing center, Greenville has reinvented itself as a hub for advanced manufacturing, with BMW's largest plant in the world nearby, and as a lively place for food, arts, and outdoor recreation. It has been repeatedly ranked among the best small cities in the country.

Visitors come to Greenville for Falls Park and the Liberty Bridge, for the restaurants and shops of Main Street, and for the Swamp Rabbit Trail. They also come for the Greenville County Museum of Art and its Andrew Wyeth collection, and for nearby mountains and waterfalls such as Caesars Head and Table Rock State Parks.

History

The area was home to the Cherokee, and the land was settled by Europeans in the late 1700s, with Richard Pearis establishing a trading post on the Reedy River Falls. The town of Greenville was incorporated in 1831 and became a resort for lowcountry planters seeking a cooler climate. After the Civil War, the Reedy River powered cotton mills, and by the early twentieth century Greenville was known as the "Textile Capital of the World." When the mills closed in the late twentieth century, the city and community leaders worked to revitalize downtown, removing a highway bridge to reveal the Reedy River Falls and creating Falls Park in 2004. Michelin built its North American headquarters in the area, and BMW opened a plant in nearby Spartanburg in 1994.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Falls Park on the Reedy has a series of waterfalls in the center of downtown, spanned by the Liberty Bridge, a 355-foot curved suspension footbridge. Main Street has more than 100 restaurants and shops, and public art, including the "Mice on Main" sculptures hidden along the street. The Peace Center is a performing arts center on the river, and the Upcountry History Museum tells the story of the region.

Culture and Museums

The Greenville County Museum of Art has a collection of American art, with works by Andrew Wyeth and Jasper Johns, and the Upcountry History Museum has exhibits on the area's textile and Southern history. The Children's Museum of the Upstate and Roper Mountain Science Center are popular with families, and the Peace Center hosts Broadway shows and concerts. The Euphoria food and music festival and the Artisphere arts festival are annual events.

Parks

The Swamp Rabbit Trail is a 22-mile rail trail from downtown Greenville to Travelers Rest, and Falls Park offers gardens and the waterfalls. Paris Mountain State Park has lakes and trails just north of the city, and Caesars Head State Park, Table Rock State Park, and Jones Gap State Park have waterfalls and views in the Blue Ridge. The Reedy River also has paddling routes.

Seasons and Weather

Greenville has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and about 5 inches of snow. Its elevation makes it a bit cooler than the rest of the state, and spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, with colorful foliage in the nearby mountains in October.
