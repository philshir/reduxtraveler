How to Get There

From Charleston (3 mi): Take US-17/Savannah Highway west from downtown Charleston, turn onto Old Towne Road, and follow the signs to Charles Towne Landing State Historic Site at 1500 Old Towne Road.

Overview

Charles Towne Landing State Historic Site in West Ashley preserves the original site of the first permanent English settlement in South Carolina, where colonists landed on the west bank of the Ashley River in 1670. The settlement was later moved across the peninsula to the location of present-day Charleston, leaving this first site as an important archaeological landscape. Opened in 1970 for the state's tricentennial, the 664-acre South Carolina state historic site combines an exhibit hall, a natural habitat zoo, ongoing archaeological digs, and miles of trails. It is popular with history lovers, families and walkers who want a green break from downtown Charleston.

Access

The site is on Old Towne Road in the West Ashley area of Charleston, only about 3 miles from downtown. Most visitors drive: from downtown, take US-17/Savannah Highway west and turn onto Old Towne Road, following signs to the entrance at 1500 Old Towne Road. Parking is available on site, and the grounds are large enough that many visitors walk or bike between areas. Leashed pets are welcome on the grounds and trails, but some indoor exhibits and animal areas restrict them.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, and spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for exploring the gardens, trails and historic areas. Summers in the Lowcountry are hot, humid and buggy, with afternoon thunderstorms, so mornings are the best time to visit. Winters are mild, with fewer visitors and good walking weather. Hurricane season runs from June through November, and storms can cause temporary closures, so check before you go.

What to See

  • Adventure: a replica 17th-century ketch, the type of cargo vessel that sailed between New York, Barbados, and the colonies
  • Fortified area: a reconstructed palisade wall, earthworks, and six fireable replica cannon on the original footprint
  • Animal Forest: a natural habitat zoo of species native to the area in the 1670s, including puma and bison
  • Experimental Crop Garden: crops early colonists tried for food and profit, such as sugarcane and indigo
  • Archaeology: Native American, English, and African artifacts from excavations under way since 1967

Visitor Centers

The main visitor facility is the exhibit hall, where displays, artifacts and interpretive materials tell the story of the 1670 settlement and the early colony. The hall also serves as the starting point for the grounds, with information, maps and restrooms. Hours for the park and the Animal Forest differ slightly, so check the General Information box above or the park's website before you go.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at Charles Towne Landing. Hotels, inns and restaurants of every type are in Charleston, just a few miles away, and in neighboring Mount Pleasant and North Charleston, all within about 30 minutes. Beach rentals and hotels are also found at Folly Beach and the Isle of Palms. For camping, James Island County Park near Charleston has a campground, and Edisto Beach State Park is about an hour south.
