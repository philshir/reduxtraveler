Pawleys Island

How to Get There

From Charleston, SC (73 mi): Take US-17 N for approximately 1.5 hours, then follow signs to Pawleys Island via the South Causeway.

Overview

Pawleys Island is a small barrier island town on South Carolina's Grand Strand, about 25 miles south of Myrtle Beach, and it is known as one of the oldest summer resorts on the East Coast, dating back to the early 1700s. Its slogan is "arrogantly shabby," a description of its weathered, historic beach cottages, which have no neon signs, chain hotels, or high-rises. The island is also the birthplace of the Pawleys Island Hammock, a rope hammock made here since 1889, and its quiet beaches, marsh views, and Southern charm make it a favorite for families.

Visitors come to Pawleys Island for the beach, the historic cottages, and the Hammock Shops Village on the mainland, where hammocks are still made. They also come for nearby Brookgreen Gardens, Hobcaw Barony, and the Pawleys Island Nature Preserve, and for fishing, crabbing, and the local legend of the "Gray Man," a ghost said to warn of hurricanes.

History

The island is named for Percival Pawley, a colonial planter who owned land here in the early 1700s, and in the following decades, wealthy rice planters from the mainland built simple summer cottages on the island to escape the heat and mosquitoes. Pawleys Island became known as a resort for the "Lowcountry aristocracy," and some of the original cottages still stand. In 1889, a local man, Captain Joshua John Ward, is said to have invented the rope hammock here, which was marketed as the Original Pawleys Island Hammock. Hurricane Hugo in 1989 and other storms damaged the island, but its historic character has been preserved, and the town was incorporated in 1985.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The island itself is small, about four miles long, with historic cottages along the beach and the marsh, and the Pawleys Island Chapel and the Pelican Inn are local landmarks. The Hammock Shops Village on the mainland is a collection of shops and restaurants around a hammock factory. Brookgreen Gardens is a short drive north, and Hobcaw Barony, a former rice plantation and the winter home of financier Bernard Baruch, has tours.

Culture and Museums

The Waccamaw Neck's culture is a mix of Lowcountry history and art. Brookgreen Gardens has a large collection of sculpture and a museum, and Hobcaw Barony offers tours of the historic house and the Belle W. Baruch Foundation's research areas. The Pawleys Island Festival of Music & Art is held each year, and the Hammock Shops is a local landmark. The "Gray Man" legend is a favorite among locals and visitors.

Parks

Pawleys Island's beach is open to the public at several access points, and the Pawleys Island Nature Preserve on the marsh side offers trails and birdwatching. Huntington Beach State Park and Brookgreen Gardens are nearby, and the Waccamaw River and the tidal creeks are popular for kayaking, crabbing, and fishing. The Waccamaw National Wildlife Refuge protects marshland and forests along the river.

Seasons and Weather

Pawleys Island has a humid subtropical climate. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 50s°F and almost no snow. The beach season runs from May to September, and hurricane season runs from June to November.

Things to Do

Enjoy the Beach
Uncrowded sandy beaches suit swimming, shell collecting, and long walks.

Shop for a Rope Hammock
Browse local hammock shops, including the Hammock Shops Village on the mainland, and see the historic cottages.

Kayak and Paddleboard
Paddle the salt marshes and tidal creeks, where dolphins and shorebirds are common.

Visit Nearby Attractions
Brookgreen Gardens, Huntington Beach State Park, Atalaya Castle, and the Waccamaw Neck Scenic Byway are close by.
