Lincoln Boyhood National Memorial

★5.0

National memorial preserving the boyhood home of Abraham Lincoln, featuring a visitor center, pioneer cemetery, historical farm, and scenic walking trails.

General Information

Hours:
Park Grounds: Open daily, year-round from daylight to dusk
Visitor Center: Open daily from 9am to 4pm Central Time
Closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year’s Day
Fees:
Admission is free
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed on all trails and at the farm; must be leashed (max 6 feet) and not left unattended
Seasons:
All year; spring and fall offer the most comfortable weather for outdoor exploration
Location:
3027 E South St, Lincoln City, IN 47552
Website:
nps.gov/libo

How to Get There

From Evansville, IN (47 mi): Take I-64 E to IN-162 N via Exit 63 toward Lincoln City; follow signs to the memorial entrance on East South Street.

Overview

The Lincoln Boyhood National Memorial commemorates the formative years of Abraham Lincoln, who lived in southern Indiana from age 7 to 21. This national park site includes the preserved location of the Lincoln family farm, a pioneer cemetery where his mother Nancy Hanks Lincoln is buried, and a living historical farm that recreates 1820s frontier life. The Memorial Visitor Center, completed in 1943, houses two memorial halls, a museum, and an auditorium, offering insight into Lincoln’s early life and the environment that shaped his character and values.

Visitors can explore over two miles of scenic trails that connect the various historic features of the park. The Living Historical Farm is staffed seasonally with interpreters in period clothing who demonstrate traditional farming techniques. The site offers a peaceful and educational experience for families, history enthusiasts, and those seeking to understand the roots of one of America’s most influential presidents. With its blend of natural beauty and historical significance, the Lincoln Boyhood National Memorial is a compelling destination for reflection and discovery.

Things to Do

Explore the Childhood Home of Abraham Lincoln

Lincoln Boyhood National Memorial preserves the farm where Abraham Lincoln lived from 1816 to 1830 during his formative years. Located near Lincoln City, Indiana, the memorial commemorates the experiences and influences that helped shape the character, values, and leadership of the future sixteenth President of the United States.

Visit the Memorial Visitor Center

The visitor center features exhibits, artifacts, interpretive displays, and films that explore Lincoln's childhood, family life, education, and years spent in southern Indiana. Visitors can gain insight into the challenges and opportunities that influenced Lincoln's development before he moved to Illinois.

Walk Through the Living Historical Farm

The park includes a reconstructed pioneer homestead that represents the farm where the Lincoln family lived. Demonstrations and exhibits illustrate frontier life during the early nineteenth century, including farming practices, household activities, and the daily routines of pioneer families.

Visit the Lincoln Boyhood Memorial

The memorial building honors Abraham Lincoln's youth and includes symbolic architectural features and interpretive exhibits. Nearby are the gravesite of Lincoln's mother, Nancy Hanks Lincoln, and the historic Pioneer Cemetery, which provide important connections to the family's history in Indiana.

Experience Nature and Reflection

The memorial's grounds include woodlands, open fields, and walking paths that encourage visitors to reflect on Lincoln's life and the environment in which he grew up. The peaceful landscape offers opportunities for photography, wildlife observation, and learning about the natural setting of early Indiana.

Top Trails

Boyhood Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This interpretive trail connects major features of the memorial, including the visitor center, memorial building, pioneer farm, and historic sites. Educational exhibits help visitors understand Lincoln's life and the significance of the landscape.

Pioneer Cemetery Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short walk leads to the gravesite of Nancy Hanks Lincoln and the historic cemetery. The trail provides historical context and an opportunity for quiet reflection.

Woodland Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

The trail winds through forest habitats that resemble portions of the landscape encountered by the Lincoln family during the early nineteenth century. Visitors can enjoy nature observation and seasonal wildflower displays.

Lincoln State Park Trail Connections

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Adjacent Lincoln State Park offers additional hiking opportunities through forests, rolling hills, and lakeside environments, allowing visitors to extend their outdoor experience in the area where Lincoln spent his youth.
