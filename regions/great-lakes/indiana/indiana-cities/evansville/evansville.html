How to Get There

From Newburgh (8 mi): Follow IN-66 W or Lloyd Expressway west toward downtown, continuing into the city center near the Ohio River and major visitor attractions.

Overview

Evansville is the third-largest city in Indiana, in the southwest corner of the state on a big bend of the Ohio River, across from Kentucky. It is known as the "River City" and is a regional center for healthcare, manufacturing, and shopping for southern Indiana, southeastern Illinois, and western Kentucky. Its best-known attractions are the USS LST-325, a World War II tank landing ship, Bosse Field, one of the oldest ballparks in the country, and the Mesker Park Zoo.

Visitors come to Evansville for its riverfront, its historic homes such as the Reitz Home Museum, its family-friendly attractions, and a riverboat casino. It is also a good value, with a relaxed pace and a good food scene that includes Southern Indiana specialties such as the Hoosier pork tenderloin sandwich, and it is about three hours from Indianapolis, Nashville, and St. Louis.

History

Evansville was founded in 1812 by Hugh McGary Jr. and named for Colonel Robert M. Evans, an early Indiana political leader, and it became a significant river port after the arrival of steamboats. The Wabash and Erie Canal reached the city in 1853, and the railroads and the Civil War helped it grow into a major manufacturing center for furniture, stoves, refrigerators, and automobile parts. In the twentieth century it was home to Servel refrigerators and Whirlpool plants, and its industrial base and river location made it a major hub for the region. The city is also known for its German immigrant heritage and its historic neighborhoods such as Riverside and Haynie's Corner.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The USS LST-325 is the last fully operational LST from World War II and is open for tours at its dock on the riverfront. Bosse Field, which opened in 1915, is the third-oldest ballpark in the United States and was used for the filming of A League of Their Own, and the Reitz Home Museum is a French Second Empire mansion built in 1871 with original furnishings. Other highlights include the Evansville Museum of Arts, History and Science, the Old National Bank Plaza, and Haynie's Corner Arts District with its galleries and restaurants.

Culture and Museums

The Evansville Museum of Arts, History and Science has art galleries, a planetarium, and a replica of an 1850s street, and the Reitz Home Museum and the nearby Reitz Home's neighborhood show the city's Victorian heritage. The Evansville Philharmonic Orchestra, the Victory Theatre, and the Old National Events Plaza provide performances and events, and the Children's Museum of Evansville (CME) is popular with families. The Angel Mounds State Historic Site, east of town, preserves a Native American village and is one of the best-preserved Mississippian sites in the country.

Parks

Evansville has a large park system. Garvin Park is a historic city park with a lake, and the Pigeon Creek Greenway Passage is a trail that runs through the city. Mesker Park Zoo and Botanic Garden is one of the best-known zoos in the state, and Wesselman Woods Nature Preserve is one of the largest tracts of old-growth forest within a city in the United States. Burdette Park has a swimming complex, and the Ohio River Riverfront Walkway is great for walking.

Seasons and Weather

Evansville has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild to cool winters. Summer highs are in the upper 80s°F, winter highs are in the 40s°F, and snowfall averages about 10 inches a year. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, and severe thunderstorms and the occasional tornado are possible in spring.
