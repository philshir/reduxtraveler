How to Get There

From Indianapolis (47 mi): Take I-65 S to IN-46 W in Columbus. Follow IN-46 W through scenic hills to Nashville’s downtown district.

Overview

Nashville, Indiana, is a small town in the wooded hills of Brown County in south-central Indiana, and it is known as an art colony and one of the most popular weekend getaways in the Midwest. Its downtown has rustic shops, galleries, restaurants, and inns, and it is the gateway to Brown County State Park, Indiana's largest state park, which is nicknamed the "Little Smokies" for its rugged hills. The town is not to be confused with Nashville, Tennessee, and it has a very different, quiet charm.

Visitors come to Nashville to browse the more than 200 shops, to see art by local painters, to hike and ride horses in the park, and to see the fall colors, which are among the best in the Midwest. The town also has live music at the Brown County Music Center, local wineries and breweries, and a lively fudge and candy tradition. It is about an hour from Indianapolis and works well as a weekend trip or as part of a drive through southern Indiana.

History

Nashville was platted in 1836 as the seat of the newly created Brown County and named for Nashville, Tennessee, and its early economy relied on farming and logging in the hilly terrain. Beginning in the early twentieth century it became a gathering place for artists, led by painter T. C. Steele of the "Hoosier Group" who built a home and studio nearby in 1907, and by the 1920s it was known as the art colony of the Midwest. Visitors began to arrive in numbers when the automobile and better roads came, and the creation of Brown County State Park in 1929 and later Yellowwood State Forest helped establish the area as a tourist destination. Many of the town's original log and frame buildings have been preserved.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Downtown Nashville's main attractions are Main Street and Van Buren Street, which are lined with galleries, craft stores, candy shops, and restaurants in historic buildings. The Brown County Art Gallery and the Brown County Historical Museum show the work and history of the art colony, and the Brown County Playhouse, run by Indiana University, has been a theater since 1949. The T. C. Steele State Historic Site, a few miles away, preserves the painter's home, studio, and gardens, and the Story Inn, in the nearby village of Story, is a well-known restaurant and inn.

Culture and Museums

The art colony tradition is the heart of Nashville's culture, and the Brown County Art Guild and the Brown County Art Gallery show works by local artists. The T. C. Steele State Historic Site has a collection of his paintings and a historic house, and the Brown County Historical Society operates a museum with a pioneer village. The Brown County Playhouse and the Brown County Music Center offer live performances, and the Bill Monroe Bluegrass Hall of Fame and the Bean Blossom Bluegrass Festival, in the nearby village of Bean Blossom, honor the roots of bluegrass.

Parks

Brown County State Park has about 16,000 acres of rolling hills with hiking trails, mountain biking, horseback riding, camping, a nature center, and scenic overlooks such as Hesitation Point. Yellowwood State Forest and Hoosier National Forest are also nearby and have lakes, trails, and camping, and the Ogle Hollow Nature Preserve has quiet trails near town. Fall color season, usually in mid to late October, is the busiest time of year, so lodging should be booked early.

Seasons and Weather

Nashville has a humid subtropical to humid continental climate, with warm, humid summers and cool winters. Summer highs are in the 80s°F, winter highs are in the 30s and 40s°F, and snowfall averages about 15 inches. Spring brings wildflowers and dogwoods, summer is good for hiking and festivals, and October is the peak season for fall color, when traffic and crowds are heavy on weekends.

Things to Do

Discover the Heart of Brown County

Nashville, Indiana, is a charming small town nestled in the hills of Brown County and is widely known as the "Artist Colony of the Midwest." Surrounded by scenic forests and rolling terrain, Nashville offers visitors a blend of outdoor recreation, artisan shops, galleries, music venues, local restaurants, and historic charm.

Explore Brown County State Park

Just outside town, Brown County State Park is Indiana's largest state park and one of its most popular outdoor destinations. Known as the "Little Smokies" because of its rugged hills and sweeping vistas, the park features scenic overlooks, forests, hiking trails, mountain biking routes, horseback riding trails, and exceptional fall foliage.

Stroll Through Historic Downtown Nashville

Downtown Nashville is filled with locally owned shops, art galleries, craft studios, bookstores, antique stores, and specialty food shops. Visitors can browse handcrafted artwork, pottery, woodwork, jewelry, and other creations made by local artisans.

Experience Live Music and Arts

Nashville has a long tradition as an arts community and offers numerous opportunities to enjoy live music, theater performances, festivals, and gallery exhibitions throughout the year. The town's creative atmosphere attracts artists and visitors from across the Midwest.

Enjoy Scenic Drives and Fall Colors

The rolling hills surrounding Nashville provide some of Indiana's best scenic drives, especially during autumn when the forests display vibrant shades of red, orange, and gold. The area is a premier destination for leaf-peeping, photography, and countryside exploration.

Top Trails

Trail 5 at Brown County State Park

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This popular trail leads to several scenic overlooks and showcases the rugged landscape that makes Brown County State Park famous. Visitors enjoy forest scenery, ridgelines, and excellent photography opportunities.

Trail 2 and Ogle Hollow Nature Preserve

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail passes through hardwood forests, ravines, and natural habitat areas while offering a quieter hiking experience away from some of the park's busier locations.

Hesitation Point Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

One of the area's most famous viewpoints, Hesitation Point provides panoramic views of Brown County's rolling hills and forests. The overlook is particularly spectacular during the fall foliage season.

Yellowwood State Forest Trails

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Located a short drive from Nashville, Yellowwood State Forest offers miles of hiking trails through remote woodlands, around Yellowwood Lake, and across scenic ridges. The area is popular for hiking, birdwatching, camping, and wildlife observation.
