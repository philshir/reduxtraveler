How to Get There

From South Bend, IN (38 mi): Take US-20 E to IN-5 S in LaGrange County, then follow signs to Shipshewana and turn onto S Van Buren St to reach the market entrance.

Overview

Shipshewana, Indiana, is a small town in LaGrange County in the northeastern corner of the state, and it is the heart of Indiana's Amish country. It is best known for the Shipshewana Auction and Flea Market, one of the largest in the Midwest, and for its Amish furniture, quilts, baked goods, and horse-drawn buggies that share the roads with cars. The town is also known for its shops, restaurants serving family-style meals, and a unique cultural experience that attracts visitors from across the region.

Visitors come to Shipshewana to shop at the flea market and in the downtown stores, to eat at Amish restaurants, to learn about Amish and Mennonite history at the Menno-Hof visitor center, and to take horse-drawn buggy rides and backroad tours. The surrounding countryside of farms, covered bridges, and quiet roads is a draw in itself, and the town is about two hours from Chicago and Indianapolis and works well as a weekend trip or as a stop on the way to Michigan.

History

The town was named for a Potawatomi chief, Shipshewana, and was founded in 1889 when the Lake Shore and Michigan Southern Railroad came through the area. Amish settlers, who arrived in the 1840s, built farms in the surrounding prairie, and the community remains one of the largest Amish settlements in the United States. The Shipshewana Auction began in 1922 as a livestock auction and grew into a large flea market that now draws thousands of visitors on summer Tuesdays and Wednesdays. The town today is a blend of tourism and traditional Amish life, and the Menno-Hof visitor center opened in 1988 to explain the history of the Amish and Mennonites.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Shipshewana Auction and Flea Market, with hundreds of vendors, is the biggest attraction, and the Davis Mercantile and the Blue Gate Restaurant and Theater offer shopping, Amish-style meals, and shows. Main Street has quilt shops, bakeries, furniture stores, and a historic water tower, and Menno-Hof tells the story of Amish and Mennonite history through exhibits. Visitors can also take a tour of Amish farms, visit a buggy shop or a cheese factory, and drive the Heritage Trail through LaGrange and Elkhart counties.

Culture and Museums

Menno-Hof is the main cultural attraction, with exhibits on the history of the Anabaptists in Europe and North America. The Blue Gate Theater has live music and plays, and the Shipshewana Quilt Gardens along the Heritage Trail are a summer attraction, with large flower gardens designed as quilt patterns. The Midwest Museum of American Art in Elkhart and the Elkhart County Historical Museum are nearby, and the Northern Indiana Amish culture can also be seen at the LaGrange County Historical Museum.

Parks

The area has a number of small parks and trails. The Pumpkinvine Nature Trail is a paved rail trail that passes through Amish farmland and connects to Goshen, Middlebury, and Shipshewana, and Bonneyville Mill County Park has a restored gristmill from the 1830s. Fidler Pond Park and the Chain O'Lakes State Park to the south are good for fishing and paddling, and the quiet backroads are good for biking, though visitors should be careful of buggies.

Seasons and Weather

Shipshewana has a humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 50 inches of lake-effect snow. The flea market and the Quilt Gardens are best from May through October, and fall color and Christmas events draw crowds as well. Many Amish-owned businesses are closed on Sundays.

Things to Do

Experience the Heart of Northern Indiana Amish Country

Shipshewana is one of the most popular destinations in Indiana Amish Country and is home to one of the largest Amish communities in the United States. Visitors are drawn to its rural charm, Amish culture, handcrafted goods, scenic countryside, and welcoming small-town atmosphere.

Visit the Famous Shipshewana Auction & Flea Market

Shipshewana is best known for the Shipshewana Auction & Flea Market, one of the largest outdoor flea markets in the Midwest. Visitors can browse hundreds of vendors selling antiques, handcrafted products, home décor, clothing, produce, baked goods, and unique specialty items.

Explore Amish Heritage and Culture

The surrounding countryside offers opportunities to learn about Amish traditions, farming practices, craftsmanship, and daily life. Guided tours, heritage centers, and scenic drives provide visitors with insights into a community that continues to maintain many traditional ways of life.

Shop for Handmade Crafts and Furniture

Shipshewana is renowned for locally crafted furniture, quilts, leather goods, baskets, foods, and home décor. Visitors can explore family-owned shops and workshops that showcase the skill and craftsmanship for which Amish artisans are widely known.

Enjoy Entertainment and Local Dining

The Blue Gate Theatre and Event Center hosts concerts, theatrical productions, and special events throughout the year. Visitors can also enjoy traditional Amish-style meals, homemade pies, baked goods, cheeses, and other regional favorites at local restaurants and bakeries.

Top Trails

Pumpkinvine Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 17 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular paved multi-use trail connects Shipshewana with Middlebury and Goshen through beautiful farmland, woodlands, and rural communities. It is ideal for walking, running, cycling, and experiencing the peaceful scenery of Amish Country.

Bonneyville Mill County Park Trails

Length: More than 20 miles of trails
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Located a short drive from Shipshewana, Bonneyville Mill County Park offers hiking trails through forests, along streams, and around one of Indiana's oldest operating grist mills. The park is popular for wildlife viewing and nature photography.

Fidler Pond Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This easy walking trail circles a scenic pond and provides opportunities for birdwatching, photography, and enjoying a peaceful outdoor experience near downtown Shipshewana.

Shipshewana Downtown Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 to 2 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A self-guided walk through downtown allows visitors to explore local shops, markets, restaurants, historic buildings, and cultural attractions while experiencing the charm of one of Indiana's most beloved small towns.
