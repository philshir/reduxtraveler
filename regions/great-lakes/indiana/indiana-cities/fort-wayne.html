How to Get There

From Indianapolis (121 mi): Take I-69 N from Indianapolis and follow it to Exit 302B for Jefferson Blvd toward Downtown. Continue on Jefferson Blvd, then turn right on Harrison Street to reach the Visitors Center.

Overview

Fort Wayne is the second-largest city in Indiana, in the northeastern part of the state at the confluence of the St. Marys, St. Joseph, and Maumee Rivers. It is known for its highly rated Children's Zoo, its historic downtown, and the Allen County Public Library, whose Genealogy Center is among the largest in the country. The city is also named for General "Mad" Anthony Wayne, who built a fort here in 1794, and it has a nickname, the "City of Three Rivers."

Families come to Fort Wayne for the Fort Wayne Children's Zoo, the Foellinger-Freimann Botanical Conservatory, while history lovers visit Historic Fort Wayne and the Fort Wayne Museum of Art. The city has a revitalized riverfront at Promenade Park, a growing food scene downtown, and many events and festivals, such as Johnny Appleseed Festival in September. It is a good value for visitors and is about two hours from Indianapolis, Chicago, and Detroit.

History

The confluence of the three rivers was the site of Kekionga, the principal village of the Miami people, and the portage between the Maumee and Wabash Rivers made it a strategic location. The Americans built Fort Wayne in 1794 after General Anthony Wayne's victory at the Battle of Fallen Timbers, and the city was platted in 1824. The Wabash and Erie Canal arrived in the 1840s, followed by the railroads, and the city became a major manufacturing center, with companies such as Magnavox, International Harvester, and General Electric. Johnny Appleseed (John Chapman) died near Fort Wayne in 1845 and is buried in the city's Johnny Appleseed Park.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Fort Wayne Children's Zoo is regularly ranked among the best zoos in the country, with an African savanna and Indonesian rain forest, and it is located in Franke Park. Historic Fort Wayne is a reconstruction of the 1816 fort with costumed interpreters, and the Embassy Theatre is a restored 1928 movie palace with a Grand Page Organ. Promenade Park on the St. Marys River has a playground, a carousel, and a boardwalk. The Lincoln Tower, the Allen County Courthouse, and the historic Wells Street Corridor are other sights.

Culture and Museums

The Fort Wayne Museum of Art has a collection of American art and hosts traveling exhibits, and the History Center, in the old City Hall, tells the story of the region's past. The Allen County Public Library's Genealogy Center draws family historians from across the country, and the Fort Wayne Philharmonic, the Fort Wayne Ballet, and the Fort Wayne Civic Theatre are part of the performing arts scene. The Embassy Theatre hosts Broadway tours and concerts, and the Foellinger-Freimann Botanical Conservatory has seasonal flower shows and a tropical house.

Parks

Fort Wayne has more than 80 parks, including Franke Park, which is home to the zoo, and Lakeside Park, which has one of the largest rose gardens in the Midwest. The Rivergreenway is a 25-mile trail system that follows the three rivers, and Promenade Park is the newest riverfront park. Eagle Marsh Nature Preserve, a restored wetland, and Fox Island County Park, with dunes and trails, are also popular, and Johnny Appleseed Park has a memorial to the folk hero.

Seasons and Weather

Fort Wayne has a humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and cloudy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 30 inches of snow per year. Spring and fall are mild, and the best time to visit is from late spring through early fall, when the zoo and riverfront are at their best.

Things to Do

Explore Indiana's Second-Largest City

Fort Wayne is a vibrant city in northeastern Indiana known for its rich history, cultural attractions, outdoor recreation, and family-friendly activities. Located at the confluence of the St. Joseph, St. Marys, and Maumee Rivers, the city offers a mix of urban amenities, historic sites, riverfront trails, museums, and botanical gardens.

Visit the Fort Wayne Children's Zoo

Consistently ranked among the top zoos in the United States, the Fort Wayne Children's Zoo features hundreds of animal species and immersive exhibits representing Africa, Asia, Australia, and North America. It is one of the city's most popular attractions for families and wildlife enthusiasts.

Explore Parkview Field and Downtown Fort Wayne

Downtown Fort Wayne offers restaurants, entertainment venues, museums, public art, and seasonal festivals. Parkview Field anchors a revitalized downtown district and hosts events throughout the year.

Discover History at Historic Fort Wayne

Historic Fort Wayne commemorates the military outpost that helped shape the region's early development. Visitors can learn about frontier history, Native American heritage, and military life through exhibits, reenactments, and educational programs.

Enjoy Gardens, Rivers, and Outdoor Recreation

Fort Wayne features numerous parks, riverfront areas, and nature preserves. Popular destinations include the Foellinger-Freimann Botanical Conservatory, Promenade Park, Eagle Marsh Nature Preserve, and the city's extensive trail network, offering opportunities for walking, cycling, paddling, and wildlife viewing.

Top Trails

Rivergreenway Trail

Length: More than 25 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The Fort Wayne Rivergreenway is one of Indiana's premier urban trail systems, following the city's three rivers through parks, neighborhoods, and natural areas. It is popular for walking, running, cycling, and enjoying scenic waterfront views.

Eagle Marsh Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles of trails
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located southwest of downtown, Eagle Marsh Nature Preserve offers trails through wetlands, grasslands, and wildlife habitats. Visitors may observe migratory birds, turtles, deer, and native plant communities.

Fox Island County Park Trails

Length: More than 10 miles of trails
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Fox Island County Park features wooded trails, wetlands, prairies, and lakes. The park provides opportunities for hiking, birdwatching, fishing, and nature photography in a peaceful setting just outside the city.

Promenade Park River Walk

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic riverfront area in downtown Fort Wayne offers walking paths, overlooks, public art, and access to recreational programs along the St. Marys River. It is an excellent location for sightseeing and enjoying the city's revitalized waterfront.
