How to Get There

From Indianapolis (73 mi): Take I-70 E from Indianapolis and follow it to US-40 E/National Rd E in Richmond. Take exit 156A from I-70 E, then continue on US-40 E to the Visitors Center.

Overview

Richmond, Indiana, is a historic city on the Whitewater River in the eastern part of the state, right on the Ohio border. It is known as the birthplace of recorded jazz in the Midwest because of the Starr Piano Company's Gennett Records, which recorded legends such as Louis Armstrong, Hoagy Carmichael, and Jelly Roll Morton here in the 1920s. The city also has a well-preserved historic downtown, a strong Quaker heritage, and a number of fine small museums and gardens.

Visitors come to Richmond for the Wayne County Historical Museum, the Richmond Art Museum, the Depot District, and the Starr-Gennett Walk of Fame, as well as for outdoor activities on the Cardinal Greenway and in Hayes Arboretum and Glen Miller Park. It is a quiet and affordable stop for travelers along Interstate 70 and U.S. 40, the historic National Road, and it is about an hour from Indianapolis, Dayton, and Cincinnati.

History

Quaker settlers from North Carolina arrived in the early 1800s and founded Richmond in 1806, and the town became a center for Quaker education and abolitionist activity, with Earlham College founded in 1847. The National Road and the railroads, including a major junction for the Pennsylvania Railroad, helped it grow into a manufacturing center, known for lawn mowers, pianos, and roses. The Starr Piano Company began to record music under the Gennett label in 1916, and its studios beside the Whitewater Gorge recorded many of the first jazz, blues, and country artists. The city also has an unusual history as the "Rose City" because of the large rose-growing industry that once existed here.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Depot District is a historic railroad district with a restored train station, shops, and restaurants, and the Starr-Gennett Walk of Fame, at the site of the old recording studio, honors the musicians who recorded there. The Wayne County Historical Museum is one of the largest local history museums in Indiana, with a collection that includes an Egyptian mummy, and the Richmond Art Museum has American and European art. Other sights include the Levi Coffin House in Fountain City, a stop on the Underground Railroad, and the Gaar Mansion, the Hicksite Quaker Meeting House, and the Madonna of the Trail statue.

Culture and Museums

The Wayne County Historical Museum has a wide range of exhibits on the history of the county, the Richmond Art Museum shows regional and national work, and the Joseph Moore Museum at Earlham College displays natural history, including a mastodon skeleton. The Richmond Symphony Orchestra, the Civic Hall Performing Arts Center, and the Starr-Gennett Foundation preserve and celebrate the area's music heritage, and the Levi Coffin House tells the story of the Underground Railroad in the region.

Parks

Richmond has several parks and trails. Glen Miller Park is the city's largest park, with a rose garden, a Quaker meeting house, and trails, and the Hayes Arboretum has 355 acres of forests, meadows, and a nature center. The Cardinal Greenway is a 60-mile rail trail that is the longest in the state, and the Whitewater Gorge Trail runs along the Whitewater River through the middle of the city. Whitewater Memorial State Park and Whitewater Gorge Park are also nearby.

Seasons and Weather

Richmond has a humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 20 inches of snow. Spring and fall are mild, and the best time to visit is from late spring through early fall, when the gardens and trails are at their best.

Things to Do

Explore the Historic City of Richmond

Richmond, Indiana, is a vibrant city in eastern Indiana known for its rich history, cultural attractions, outdoor recreation, and significant contributions to American industry and music. Located along the historic National Road near the Ohio border, Richmond offers visitors a unique blend of historic architecture, museums, parks, and scenic trails.

Discover the Wayne County Historical Museum

One of Richmond's premier attractions, the Wayne County Historical Museum features extensive exhibits on local history, transportation, industry, and culture. The museum is home to a remarkable collection of antique automobiles, historical artifacts, and exhibits highlighting Richmond's role in the development of recorded jazz music.

Visit the Richmond Art Museum

The Richmond Art Museum houses one of Indiana's finest regional art collections, including works by American Impressionists and artists associated with the Richmond Group. Visitors can enjoy rotating exhibitions and permanent collections showcasing the area's artistic heritage.

Explore the Historic Depot District

Richmond's Depot District blends historic railroad architecture with modern restaurants, breweries, boutiques, galleries, and entertainment venues. The district is a popular destination for dining, shopping, and experiencing the city's revitalized downtown atmosphere.

Experience Outdoor Recreation and Gardens

Richmond offers numerous parks and green spaces, including the beautiful Glen Miller Park, Hayes Arboretum, and access to the Whitewater Valley. Visitors can enjoy hiking, cycling, birdwatching, picnicking, and nature photography throughout the region.

Top Trails

Cardinal Greenway

Length: More than 62 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Richmond serves as the eastern terminus of the Cardinal Greenway, Indiana's longest rail-trail. The paved pathway is popular for walking, running, cycling, and wildlife viewing while connecting several communities across east-central Indiana.

Hayes Arboretum Trails

Length: More than 7 miles of trails
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Hayes Arboretum offers wooded hiking trails, prairie habitats, wildflower viewing, and scenic overlooks. It is one of the area's best destinations for nature enthusiasts and provides opportunities to observe native wildlife throughout the year.

Whitewater Gorge Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This scenic trail follows portions of the Whitewater River Gorge, offering views of limestone cliffs, river habitats, and Richmond's industrial heritage. The area is popular for photography, birdwatching, and casual hiking.

Glen Miller Park Trails

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The city's largest park features walking trails, lakes, gardens, playgrounds, and recreational facilities. Visitors can enjoy peaceful walks, wildlife observation, and family-friendly outdoor activities in a beautiful urban park setting.
