How to Get There

Take I-75 South from Toledo, exit at US-20 West toward Perrysburg, then turn left onto West River Road and follow signs to the site.

Overview

Fort Meigs Historic Site in Perrysburg is one of the largest reconstructed wooden-walled forts in the United States. The fort was built in 1813 under General William Henry Harrison to defend the Northwest Territory during the War of 1812, and it withstood sieges by British and Native American forces. These victories helped secure American control of the Great Lakes region. Today the site features blockhouses, artillery batteries and traverses, along with a museum of letters, uniforms and weapons. It is managed by the Fort Meigs Association for the Ohio History Connection, and costumed interpreters and reenactments bring the fort to life.

Access

The fort is on the Maumee River in Perrysburg, about 12 miles southwest of downtown Toledo. It is reached from I-75, I-475 and US-20, and parking is free on site. Most visitors drive, and Toledo is a regional hub that is about 1 hour from Detroit and 2 hours from Cleveland. There is no public transportation to the site.

Seasons

The site is open April through October on Wednesday through Sunday, with winter hours from November through March and reduced rates. Summer is the best time to visit, with living history events and reenactments, but it can be hot and humid. Spring and fall are comfortable, and fall color and cooler weather suit walking the fort. Winter is cold and quiet, with the museum open and the outdoor fort less staffed.

Things to Do

Tour the Reconstructed Fort

Blockhouses, batteries and earthworks show the layout of the 1813 fort and how soldiers defended it.

Visit the Museum

Exhibits feature soldiers' letters, uniforms and weapons and describe the War of 1812 in the Great Lakes.

Watch Living History Programs

Costumed interpreters demonstrate drills, cooking and musket firing, with larger reenactments in summer.

Learn About the 1813 Sieges

Interpretive displays explain how the garrison withstood the British and Native American forces.

Explore the Maumee River Area

Side Cut Metropark and the Maumee River offer additional recreation nearby.

Top Trails

Fort Meigs Perimeter Walk

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This walk follows the walls and blockhouses and shows how the fort was laid out. The ground is mostly flat and grassy.

Maumee River Trail at Side Cut Metropark

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A nearby riverside trail offers views of the Maumee and shaded paths, a good stop after visiting the fort.

Visitor Centers

The museum and visitor center are the starting point, with exhibits, a gift shop and staff to explain the history of the fort. Hours are Wednesday through Saturday and Sunday afternoons, with seasonal changes. Admission is charged for adults, seniors and students, with reduced winter rates.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the fort. Hotels, motels and restaurants are in Perrysburg and Toledo, just minutes away, along with chain options near I-75. Camping and lodging are available at Maumee Bay State Park, about 30 minutes away, which has a lodge, cabins and campground. Sandusky and Cedar Point are about an hour east.
