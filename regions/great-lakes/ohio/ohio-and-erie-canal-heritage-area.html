Ohio and Erie Canalway Heritage Area

★5.0
  1. Great Lakes
  2. Ohio
  3. Ohio and Erie Canalway Heritage Area

Woodlot, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

A nationally designated heritage corridor of historic canals, towpaths, river valleys, industrial landmarks, museums, and communities that preserves the transportation network that helped fuel America's westward expansion and industrial growth.

Hours:
The heritage area is open year-round, with outdoor trails and canal sites generally accessible daily

Individual museums, attractions, and partner sites maintain their own operating hours
Fees:

Some museums, railroad excursions, boat rides, and partner attractions may charge separate admission fees
Pet Policy:

Closest cities with hotels:
Akron, Ohio
Seasons:
All year; spring through fall is the most popular period for cycling, hiking, canal activities, and scenic railroad excursions.
Location:
ohioanderiecanalway.com

How to Get There

From Akron: Travel to downtown Akron via I-76, I-77, or OH-8, then follow local streets to the Richard Howe House at 47 W Exchange Street, a convenient gateway for exploring the heritage area's trails, historic sites, and canal communities.

Overview

Ohio and Erie Canalway National Heritage Area preserves the route of the Ohio and Erie Canal, built between 1825 and 1832 to link Lake Erie with the Ohio River. Designated a National Heritage Area by Congress in 1996, the corridor runs through northeast Ohio from Cleveland through Akron and Canton to New Philadelphia, passing locks, aqueducts, canal towns and industrial landmarks. The canal helped turn Ohio from a frontier into a center of commerce and industry. Visitors come to bike or hike the Towpath Trail, ride the Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad, tour historic towns and explore Cuyahoga Valley National Park.

Access

The corridor extends about 110 miles through Cuyahoga, Summit, Stark and Tuscarawas Counties. The Richard Howe House Visitor Center in Akron serves as the main information stop. Most visitors use I-77, I-71 and I-76 and drive from trailhead to trailhead. Cleveland and Akron are the largest gateways, and several towpath trailheads have parking and connections to local trail networks.

Seasons

The corridor is open year-round, and spring through fall is the most popular time for cycling, hiking and canal activities. Summer is warm and busy, and fall color along the towpath is a major draw. Winter is cold and snowy, with some museums and rides closed, but the trail is still used for walking. Check individual sites for hours and seasonal schedules.

Things to Do

Walk or Bike the Towpath Trail

The multi-use trail follows the old canal route through forests, wetlands and towns, and is one of the region's best outings.

Visit Cuyahoga Valley National Park

The national park contains much of the canal route, with locks, historic buildings and the Canal Exploration Center.

Ride the Scenic Railroad

The Cuyahoga Valley Scenic Railroad runs along the canal and river, and can be combined with trail use.

Tour Canal Towns

Towns such as Zoar, Canal Fulton and Massillon offer museums, shops and restaurants tied to the canal.

Learn at Museums and Visitor Centers

Museums and visitor centers describe how the canal was built and how it changed life in Ohio.

Top Trails

Ohio and Erie Canal Towpath Trail

Length: Approximately 100 miles total (use any segment)
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail follows the canal route through Cuyahoga Valley National Park and many communities. It can be hiked or biked in short segments from trailheads.

Cuyahoga Valley Towpath Segment

Length: Approximately 20 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The segment through the national park passes locks, old canal structures and scenic stretches, and can be combined with the scenic railroad.

Visitor Centers

The Richard Howe House Visitor Center in Akron provides orientation, maps and information about the heritage area, open Monday through Friday from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Cuyahoga Valley National Park's Canal Exploration Center and other partner sites also have their own visitor centers and schedules.

Camping and Lodging

There is no lodging within the heritage area itself, but towns along the corridor offer hotels, inns and bed-and-breakfasts, including Akron, Canton, Cleveland, Massillon and Zoar. Camping is available at Portage Lakes State Park and Atwood Lake Park, and Cuyahoga Valley National Park offers lodging at the Inn at Brandywine Falls and the Stanford House. Akron is the most central base.
