How to Get There

From Columbus, OH (45 mi): Take I-70 E toward Zanesville, then exit onto OH-79 N toward Hebron/Newark; follow OH-79 N and turn right onto US-40 E, then left onto Gratiot Rd SE to reach the preserve entrance.

Overview

The Blackhand Gorge State Nature Preserve is a geological and ecological treasure nestled in central Ohio. Spanning over 775 acres, the preserve is named after a Native American petroglyph once etched into the sandstone cliffs, a symbol lost to canal construction in the 1800s. Today, the gorge showcases dramatic rock formations carved by the Licking River, dense woodlands, and a rare paved multi-use trail—the only one of its kind in Ohio’s state nature preserve system. Visitors can explore over 10 miles of trails, including the scenic Blackhand Trail, which winds through the gorge offering views of towering cliffs and remnants of the historic Ohio-Erie Canal.

This preserve is a haven for nature lovers, especially in spring when wildflowers blanket the forest floor. The diverse terrain supports oak-hickory forests, hemlock groves, and wetlands, making it a hotspot for birdwatching and botany. While some trails are currently closed for maintenance, the remaining open paths still offer a rich experience of Ohio’s natural and cultural heritage. Whether you're hiking, biking, or simply soaking in the views, Blackhand Gorge offers a peaceful retreat into the wild heart of the Buckeye State.

Things to Do

Explore Ohio's Most Scenic Sandstone Gorge

Blackhand Gorge State Nature Preserve protects a spectacular 956-acre portion of the Licking River Valley in east-central Ohio. The preserve is named for a large sandstone cliff that once contained Native American petroglyphs known as the "Black Hand." Today, the area is renowned for its dramatic rock formations, wooded hillsides, river scenery, and rich natural and cultural history.

Walk Through a Historic Canal Corridor

The preserve contains remnants of the Ohio and Erie Canal era, including portions of the old canal, locks, and towpath routes. Visitors can explore historical features while learning about the transportation systems that helped shape Ohio's development during the nineteenth century.

Enjoy Hiking, Biking, and Nature Observation

Blackhand Gorge offers miles of scenic multi-use trails suitable for hiking, cycling, running, birdwatching, and photography. Visitors can experience towering sandstone cliffs, river views, wildflowers, and peaceful forest environments throughout the preserve.

Observe Wildlife and Unique Habitats

The preserve supports a diverse range of wildlife, including white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, foxes, beavers, songbirds, hawks, and numerous native plant species. The sandstone cliffs, river corridor, wetlands, and hardwood forests provide habitat for a rich variety of flora and fauna.

Enjoy Kayaking and Fishing

The Licking River offers opportunities for kayaking, canoeing, and fishing. Paddlers can enjoy a peaceful journey through the gorge while experiencing the preserve from a unique perspective and observing wildlife along the riverbanks.

Top Trails

Blackhand Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved multi-use trail follows the former railroad corridor through the heart of the gorge. Visitors enjoy scenic views of sandstone cliffs, the Licking River, and historic canal features while walking or cycling through one of Ohio's most beautiful natural areas.

Chestnut Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The Chestnut Trail climbs into the surrounding forested hills and provides elevated views of the gorge. Hikers experience hardwood forests, seasonal wildflowers, and more rugged terrain than the riverside trail.

Quarry Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail explores an old quarry area and offers interesting geological features, forest scenery, and views overlooking portions of the preserve. It is especially popular among hikers seeking a shorter but rewarding route.

Canal and River Access Walks

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Several short paths connect visitors to historic canal remnants, river access points, and scenic overlooks. These routes provide opportunities for photography, wildlife viewing, and learning about the preserve's cultural and natural history.
