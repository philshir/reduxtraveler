Overview

Dayton, Ohio, is known as the "Birthplace of Aviation," the hometown of Orville and Wilbur Wright, who built and tested the first practical airplane here in the early 1900s. It is also known for being a city of inventors, with the cash register, the automobile self-starter, and many other innovations coming from local firms such as NCR and Delco. Located in the Miami Valley in southwest Ohio, Dayton is a mid-sized city that remains a center for aerospace research because of nearby Wright-Patterson Air Force Base.

Most visitors come to Dayton to see the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force, the largest military aviation museum in the world, and the Wright brothers sites that make up the Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park. Others come for history and art, for basketball with the University of Dayton Flyers, and for the Dayton Air Show. The city offers inexpensive admission, many free museums, and a growing downtown with breweries, restaurants, and a revived Oregon District.

History

Dayton was founded in 1796 and named for Jonathan Dayton, a signer of the U.S. Constitution, at the confluence of the Great Miami, Stillwater, and Mad Rivers. The Miami and Erie Canal and then the railroads turned it into a manufacturing center, and a devastating flood in 1913 led to one of the first major flood-control systems in the country. Wilbur and Orville Wright ran a bicycle shop in the city and made their first powered flights in North Carolina in 1903, but they perfected flight at Huffman Prairie near Dayton in 1904 and 1905. Inventors such as John H. Patterson of NCR, Charles Kettering, and Thomas Midgley contributed to a reputation for innovation, and in 1995 the Dayton Peace Accords ending the war in Bosnia were negotiated at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force has more than 350 aircraft and missiles, including presidential planes and the B-2 bomber, and it is free to enter. The Dayton Aviation Heritage National Historical Park includes the Wright Cycle Company building and the Paul Laurence Dunbar House, and Carillon Historical Park has the 1905 Wright Flyer III, the world's first practical airplane, as well as a historic village. America's Packard Museum is housed in a former Packard dealership, and SunWatch Indian Village is a reconstructed Native American village built around a solar calendar. The Oregon District, a historic neighborhood of Victorian houses, is a popular area for dining and nightlife.

Culture and Museums

The Dayton Art Institute, an Italian Renaissance-style building overlooking downtown, has a collection of more than 25,000 objects, and the Boonshoft Museum of Discovery combines natural history, science, and a small zoo. The Dayton Philharmonic, Dayton Ballet, Dayton Opera, and Victoria Theatre provide performing arts, and the Dayton Metro Library and the Dayton Funk heritage, which produced bands such as the Ohio Players and Zapp, are also part of the city's culture. The history museums connected with the Wright brothers and with local industry make Dayton one of the best places in the country to learn about the history of invention.

Parks

Dayton has an extensive system of parks and trails managed by Five Rivers MetroParks, including RiverScape MetroPark downtown, with its fountain and ice rink, and Hills & Dales MetroPark, with gardens and trails. The Wright-Dunbar area and Huffman Prairie Flying Field are also part of the aviation park, and the Great Miami River Recreation Trail is part of one of the largest paved trail networks in the country. Outdoor enthusiasts can also visit Cox Arboretum MetroPark and nearby John Bryan State Park and Glen Helen Nature Preserve in Yellow Springs.

Seasons and Weather

Dayton has a humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and around 20 inches of snow. Spring and fall are generally mild and pleasant, and the area is occasionally hit by severe thunderstorms and tornadoes in the spring. The best times to visit are late spring and early fall, and the Dayton Air Show in summer is a highlight.
