Detroit Travel Guide

City with a rich history and vibrant culture. Known as the "Motor City," it has been a hub for the automotive industry and has a strong musical heritage, particularly in Motown. Visitors can explore the city's revitalized downtown area, visit world-class museums like the Detroit Institute of Arts, and enjoy a diverse culinary scene.

Overview

Detroit, Michigan, is the largest city in the state and one of the most influential cities in American history, known as the "Motor City" for its role in the automobile industry and as "Motown" for the record label that changed popular music. Situated on the Detroit River across from Windsor, Ontario, it is the only major U.S. city from which you look south to see Canada. Detroit is also known for its architecture, its professional sports teams, and a comeback story that has brought new energy to its downtown and neighborhoods.

Visitors come to Detroit for the Detroit Institute of Arts, the Motown Museum, the Henry Ford Museum and Greenfield Village in nearby Dearborn, and the Ford Rouge Factory Tour. They also come for the Detroit Riverwalk, Eastern Market, the architecture of the Fisher Building and the Guardian Building, and Tigers, Lions, Red Wings, and Pistons games. The city has a thriving food scene with Coney dogs, Detroit-style pizza, and Middle Eastern cuisine.

History

The French explorer Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac founded Fort Pontchartrain du Detroit in 1701, and the city passed to the British in 1760 and to the United States in 1796. A fire destroyed most of the town in 1805, and Judge Augustus Woodward's plan, inspired by Washington, D.C., created the radial street pattern in the center. Detroit was a stop on the Underground Railroad, and in the early twentieth century Henry Ford and the Dodge brothers, along with General Motors and Chrysler, made it the center of the global auto industry, while the Great Migration brought Black workers from the South. Racial tensions, deindustrialization, and the 1967 unrest led to decades of decline and the city's bankruptcy in 2013, but new investment in downtown and Midtown has been bringing it back.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Downtown Detroit has the General Motors Renaissance Center, Campus Martius Park, and the Spirit of Detroit statue, and the Detroit Riverwalk runs for miles along the river. The Fisher Building and the Guardian Building are two of the finest Art Deco buildings in the country, and Michigan Central Station has been restored as an innovation hub. Belle Isle, Eastern Market, the Heidelberg Project, and the Monument to Joe Louis are other sights.

Culture and Museums

The Detroit Institute of Arts has one of the best art collections in the country, including Diego Rivera's Detroit Industry murals. The Motown Museum is in Berry Gordy's original "Hitsville U.S.A." studio, and the Charles H. Wright Museum of African American History is the largest of its kind in the world. Other museums include the Detroit Historical Museum, the Arab American National Museum, the Michigan Science Center, and the Museum of Contemporary Art Detroit, and in Dearborn, the Henry Ford Museum and Greenfield Village are among the best history museums in the nation. The Detroit Symphony Orchestra performs at Orchestra Hall, and the Fox Theatre hosts concerts and shows.

Parks

Belle Isle Park, a 982-acre island in the Detroit River, has a conservatory, an aquarium, a nature center, beaches, and a scenic drive, and the Detroit RiverWalk and Dequindre Cut greenway are popular for walking and biking. Campus Martius Park downtown has a beach in summer and an ice rink in winter, and the Eastern Market has farmers markets on Saturdays. The nearby Lake St. Clair Metropark and Stony Creek Metropark offer beaches and trails, and the Detroit Zoo in Royal Oak is one of the best in the Midwest.

Sports

Detroit is one of the few cities with teams in all four major professional sports leagues, and all four play downtown or close to it. The Detroit Lions of the NFL play at Ford Field, the Detroit Tigers of Major League Baseball play at Comerica Park, and the Detroit Pistons of the NBA and the Detroit Red Wings of the NHL play at Little Caesars Arena. Detroit is also known as "Hockeytown" and has a long history of championships, including the Red Wings' 11 Stanley Cups.

Seasons and Weather

Detroit has a humid continental climate with four distinct seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and cloudy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. Spring and fall are mild but changeable, and summer and early fall are the best times to see the riverfront and parks.
