Holland Travel Guide

City with a rich history and vibrant culture. Known as the "Motor City," it has been a hub for the automotive industry and has a strong musical heritage, particularly in Motown. Visitors can explore the city's revitalized downtown area, visit world-class museums like the Detroit Institute of Arts, and enjoy a diverse culinary scene.

Overview

Holland, Michigan, is a lakeshore city on the Lake Michigan coast of West Michigan that is famous for its Dutch heritage, its tulips, and its beaches. Settled by Dutch immigrants in the 1840s, it is best known for the Tulip Time Festival each May, when millions of tulips bloom and residents in traditional Dutch costume dance in the streets. It is also known for Holland State Park, the "Big Red" lighthouse, and a charming downtown with shops and restaurants.

Visitors come to Holland to see Windmill Island Gardens and its authentic Dutch windmill De Zwaan, the Nelis' Dutch Village, and the Veldheer Tulip Gardens. They also come for the Lake Michigan beaches, boating, and sunsets, and for downtown's events and holiday celebrations, including the Winter Wonderland festival. It is about three hours from Chicago and Detroit and is a popular summer vacation spot.

History

The city was founded in 1847 by Dutch Calvinist separatists led by Reverend Albertus van Raalte, who came to America seeking religious freedom and settled on the shore of Black Lake, near Lake Michigan. They named the settlement Holland after their homeland and built churches, schools, and farms, and Hope College was founded in 1851 as a school for Dutch settlers. A great fire in 1871 destroyed much of the town, and it was rebuilt and became a manufacturing center, with furniture, tanneries, and later Heinz and automotive suppliers. The city began the Tulip Time Festival in 1929 after a local teacher proposed planting tulips to celebrate the area's Dutch heritage.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The De Zwaan windmill, brought from the Netherlands in 1964, is the only authentic working Dutch windmill imported from the Netherlands to the United States and stands in Windmill Island Gardens, which also has a Dutch carousel and a Little Netherlands miniature village. The Nelis' Dutch Village has a Dutch-themed amusement park and wooden-shoe carving, and the Veldheer Tulip Gardens has fields of tulips in spring. Downtown Eighth Street has the Window on the Waterfront, Centennial Park, and the Holland Museum, and Holland State Park's Big Red lighthouse is a top photo spot.

Culture and Museums

The Holland Museum has exhibits on Dutch American culture and local history, including a collection of Dutch decorative arts. Hope College hosts concerts, theater, and art exhibits, and the Holland Area Arts Council and the Park Theatre are part of the cultural scene. Tulip Time in May is the biggest event, with parades, Dutch dancing, and food such as stroopwafels, and the Dutch heritage is also seen in the many bakeries, wooden shoe stores, and Dutch-themed buildings in the city.

Parks

Holland State Park has one of the most popular beaches on Lake Michigan, with a wide sandy shore, a campground, and views of the Big Red lighthouse, and nearby Tunnel Park and Kirk Park have dune walks and beaches. Centennial Park downtown has gardens and a fountain, and Window on the Waterfront has views of Lake Macatawa. The Macatawa Area Express and the Lakeshore trails are good for walking and biking, and nearby Saugatuck and Douglas have more beaches and dunes.

Seasons and Weather

Holland has a humid continental climate that is moderated by Lake Michigan. Summers are warm and pleasant, with highs in the upper 70s and low 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 75 inches of lake-effect snow. The best time to see the tulips is in early to mid May, and summer is the best time for the beaches. Downtown has snowmelt streets heated by waste heat, which keep the sidewalks clear in winter.
