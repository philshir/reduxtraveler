Group of small mountains spanning the northwestern Upper Peninsula of Michigan near the shore of Lake Superior home to the most extensive stand of old growth northern hardwood forest in North America west of the Adirondack Mountains

General Information

Hours:
Open daily year-round from sunrise to sunset
Some park roads close seasonally in winter and reopen in late spring depending on conditions
Fees:
Michigan Residents: $14 annual Recreation Passport
Non-Residents: $11 daily pass
Non-Residents: $42 annual Recreation Passport
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed throughout the park on a leash not exceeding 6 feet. Owners must maintain control of pets and properly dispose of pet waste.
Seasons:
All year (summer for hiking, camping, and waterfalls; fall for peak color viewing; winter for skiing, snowshoeing, and snowmobiling; spring for waterfalls and wildflowers)

Website:

How to Get There

From AmericInn by Wyndham Silver City (approximately 3 mi): Take M-107 east into Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park and follow park signs to the Visitor Center and main recreation areas within the park.

Overview

The Porcupine Mountains, or "the Porkies," are a range of low mountains in Michigan's western Upper Peninsula near Lake Superior, named by the Ojibwe for a silhouette resembling a crouching porcupine. They protect the largest stand of old-growth northern hardwood forest in North America west of the Adirondacks, most of it within Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park.

History and Geology

The mountains were mined for copper in the 19th century, and their long basalt escarpment overlooking Lake of the Clouds, the park's signature view, is part of the same copper-bearing bedrock found on the nearby Keweenaw Peninsula. Summit Peak, on a second inland ridge, is the range's highest point.

Flora and Fauna

The old-growth forest shelters moose, gray wolves, black bears, and other wildlife, along with several rare and endangered plant and animal species.

State Park

Established in 1945 and redesignated a wilderness state park in 1972, the park offers 87 miles of backcountry trails, rustic cabins, campgrounds, and a small ski area in winter.
