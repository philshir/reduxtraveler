How to Get There

From AmericInn by Wyndham Calumet (approximately 41 mi): Take US-41 N from Calumet, continue through Phoenix and along the Keweenaw Peninsula to the northern terminus of US-41, then follow signs into downtown Copper Harbor.

Overview

Copper Harbor is a community located in Keweenaw County, Michigan. The community is located at the northern tip of the Keweenaw Peninsula. Copper Harbor is also the northernmost permanently populated community and northernmost point of mainland in the state of Michigan. Due to its natural environment and surroundings, Copper Harbor is marketed as an all-season tourist destination and also contains Fort Wilkins Historic State Park.

Etymology

The name of the community, Copper Harbor, alludes to the former use of its harbor as a port for shipping copper mined from local deposits during the mid-19th century.

Things to Do

Visit the Northern Tip of Michigan's Keweenaw Peninsula

Copper Harbor is a picturesque harbor village located at the northern end of Michigan's Keweenaw Peninsula on Lake Superior. Surrounded by forests, mountains, rocky shorelines, and stunning Great Lakes scenery, it is one of the premier outdoor recreation destinations in the Midwest. Visitors are drawn by the area's rugged beauty, historic sites, waterfalls, scenic drives, and extensive trail systems.

Drive Brockway Mountain Drive

One of Michigan's most famous scenic roads, Brockway Mountain Drive climbs more than 700 feet above Lake Superior and offers breathtaking panoramic views of Copper Harbor, the Keweenaw Peninsula, and surrounding waters. The route is especially popular during sunrise, sunset, and fall color season.

Explore Fort Wilkins Historic State Park

Fort Wilkins Historic State Park preserves an 1840s military outpost established during the Copper Rush. Visitors can tour restored buildings, view living history demonstrations, explore exhibits, and learn about the early settlement and mining history of Michigan's Copper Country.

Visit Estivant Pines Nature Sanctuary

This beautiful preserve protects one of Michigan's largest remaining old-growth white pine forests. Some trees are more than 300 years old, and visitors can enjoy peaceful walks through towering forests while observing wildlife and native plants.

Experience Mountain Biking and Outdoor Adventure

Copper Harbor has earned national recognition as one of the top mountain biking destinations in the United States. The area's trail systems range from beginner-friendly routes to advanced downhill terrain, attracting riders from across North America. Hiking, kayaking, boating, fishing, birdwatching, and photography are also popular activities.

Top Trails

Brockway Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail explores the Brockway Mountain area and features several overlooks with outstanding views of Lake Superior, Copper Harbor, and the Keweenaw Peninsula. It is especially popular during fall foliage season.

Estivant Pines Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This family-friendly trail winds through a magnificent old-growth forest of towering white pines. Interpretive signs provide information about the area's ecology, conservation efforts, and natural history.

Hunter's Point Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located on a scenic peninsula extending into Lake Superior, this trail offers beautiful shoreline views, unique geological formations, wildflowers, and opportunities for birdwatching and photography.

Copper Harbor Trail System

Length: More than 35 miles of interconnected trails
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Strenuous
Difficulty: Easy to Expert

This nationally recognized trail network provides exceptional opportunities for hiking, trail running, and mountain biking. The routes traverse forests, ridges, rocky outcrops, and scenic overlooks while showcasing the rugged beauty of the Keweenaw Peninsula.

Ferry services

Copper Harbor contains a ferry port that provides transportation to Isle Royale National Park. The Isle Royale Line, Inc. (formerly named Isle Royale Ferry Service) operates the Isle Royale Queen IV out of Copper Harbor during the warmer months from May to September. The ferry takes 3.5 hours to travel 60 miles (97 km) across Lake Superior to Rock Harbor.

The historic Copper Harbor Lighthouse is situated at the opening of the physical harbor itself. The lighthouse was rebuilt in 1866, replacing an original light from 1849, and is only accessible via a short ride in a compact, open vessel from the Copper Harbor Marina. Taken out of service in 1933, the historic navigational aid is one of the oldest lighthouses on Lake Superior. Within the yellow-bricked maritime post, furnishings from the era remain to conceptualise the life of the lighthouse keepers. Exhibits in the lighthouse museum cover both the lighthouse's history and the local shipwreck culture of the area.

Characterized as "the most beautiful road in Michigan," Brockway Mountain Drive is a 8.883 mile route that follows the spine of a 735-foot-high ridge between the communities of Copper Harbor and Eagle Harbor, and is the highest-paved road between the Rocky Mountains to the west and the Allegheny Mountains in the east. The picturesque road was constructed during the 1930s in the midst of the Great Depression by the Works Progress Administration. It offers views of Lake Superior, and the Keweenaw Peninsula, as well the archipelago of Isle Royale on an especially clear, transparent day. There is also a close on the drive called Brockway Mountain Lookout that offers a view of the community of Copper Harbor itself.

In addition to the main 1849 Copper Harbor Lighthouse, the Copper Harbor Front Range and Rear Range Lights, both completed in 1869, still remain and are locally considered to be iconic.
