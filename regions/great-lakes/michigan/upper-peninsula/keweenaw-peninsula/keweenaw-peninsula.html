Overview

The Keweenaw Peninsula is the northernmost tip of Michigan's Upper Peninsula, jutting into Lake Superior. Site of the first copper boom in the United States, it earned the nickname "Copper Country," and today its economy centers on tourism, logging, and Michigan Technological University and Finlandia University.

History and Geology

Ancient lava flows from the Midcontinent Rift, over a billion years old, produced the only deposits on Earth of large-scale, economically recoverable pure native copper. Industrial-scale mining began in the mid-19th century and employed thousands of workers until hard-rock mining ceased in 1967.

Copper Island

The peninsula's northern tip, known as Copper Island, is separated from the mainland by the dredged Keweenaw Waterway between Houghton and Hancock and can be circled by the roughly 125-mile Keweenaw Water Trail. The Keweenaw Fault, running the length of the peninsula, has produced dramatic cliffs followed by roads such as Brockway Mountain Drive.
