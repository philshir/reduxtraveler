How to Get There

From Mackinaw City, MI (8 mi): Take a ferry from Mackinaw City to Mackinac Island, then walk, bike, or take a horse-drawn carriage to Mackinac Island State Park, which covers more than 80% of the island and includes many of its historic and natural attractions.

Overview

Mackinac Island State Park is a state park located on Mackinac Island in Michigan. The island park encompasses 1,800 acres (7.3 km2), which is approximately 80% of the island's total area. . M-185 circles the perimeter of the park as the only motorless highway in the state due to the island's ban of motorized vehicles.

The park was first established as Mackinac National Park in 1875, which was the second national park established in the United States after Yellowstone National Park. In 1895, it was transferred to state control and reorganized as Mackinac Island State Park, which was the first state park in Michigan. The park contains many important historical and geological features, such as Fort Mackinac, Fort Holmes, other historic buildings, historic sites, limestone caves, and other unique rock formations. The park also operates the Mackinac Island Airport. Many of these sites are formally operated by the Mackinac Island State Park Commission, which also operates two other parks around the Straits of Mackinac area: Colonial Michilimackinac and Historic Mill Creek Discovery Park.

Things to Do

Explore the Nation's Largest State Park on an Island

Mackinac Island State Park covers approximately 80 percent of Mackinac Island and preserves a remarkable blend of natural beauty, historic landmarks, scenic shorelines, and cultural heritage. Established in 1895, it is Michigan's first state park and one of the oldest state parks in the United States. Visitors can explore forests, limestone formations, historic forts, and breathtaking views of Lake Huron while enjoying a car-free island environment.

Visit Arch Rock

Arch Rock is the island's most famous natural landmark and one of Michigan's most photographed locations. This massive limestone arch rises nearly 150 feet above the Lake Huron shoreline and offers spectacular views of the surrounding water and coastline from nearby observation areas.

Tour Historic Fort Mackinac

Perched on a bluff overlooking the harbor, Fort Mackinac is a restored military outpost dating to the American Revolutionary War era. Visitors can explore historic buildings, exhibits, cannon demonstrations, and interactive programs while learning about the island's military and cultural history.

Bike, Hike, and Explore the Island

Mackinac Island is famous for its extensive network of trails and roads. With automobiles prohibited, bicycling and walking are among the most popular ways to explore the island. Visitors can enjoy scenic shoreline rides, wooded trails, and picturesque overlooks throughout the state park.

Discover Natural and Historic Landmarks

The park contains numerous attractions, including Sugar Loaf, Skull Cave, British Landing, Fort Holmes, Surrey Hill, and scenic bluff overlooks. These sites showcase the island's geology, Native American heritage, military history, and natural beauty.

Top Trails

Mackinac Island Perimeter Route (M-185)

Length: Approximately 8.2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic paved route circles the entire island along the shoreline and offers spectacular views of Lake Huron, forests, rock formations, and historic landmarks. It is one of the most popular cycling and walking routes in Michigan.

Tranquil Bluff Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This wooded trail leads to overlooks above the island's western shoreline and provides excellent views of Lake Huron, Mackinac Bridge, and surrounding islands.

Arch Rock Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

A popular route connecting the interior of the island to Arch Rock. Visitors enjoy forest scenery, limestone formations, and one of the island's most impressive viewpoints.

Fort Holmes Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs to Fort Holmes, the highest point on Mackinac Island. Visitors are rewarded with panoramic views of the island, Lake Huron, and the Mackinac Bridge while exploring an important historic site.
