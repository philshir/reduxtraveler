Overview

Rockford, Illinois, is the largest city in northern Illinois outside the Chicago area, set on both banks of the Rock River about 90 miles northwest of Chicago. Nicknamed the "Forest City," it is known for its manufacturing heritage, particularly furniture, fasteners, and machine tools, for the Rockford Peaches, the women's professional baseball team made famous by the movie A League of Their Own, and for rock band Cheap Trick, which formed here. It is also known for some of the best cultural attractions in the region, including a nationally recognized Japanese garden.

Visitors come to Rockford for the Anderson Japanese Gardens, the Burpee Museum of Natural History, the Klehm Arboretum & Botanic Garden, and the Tinker Swiss Cottage Museum, as well as for its riverfront parks and its affordable cost compared with Chicago. The city is a convenient stop for travelers heading between Chicago, Madison, and the Mississippi River towns.

History

The city was founded in 1834 by settlers Germanicus Kent and Thatcher Blake on the site of a ford over the Rock River, and the name Rockford came from that crossing. Swedish immigrants arrived in large numbers from the 1850s and shaped the city's culture and its furniture industry, and by the early twentieth century Rockford was a major producer of furniture, tools, and screws. During the Great Depression and after World War II the city's machine tool industry remained strong, and in recent decades it has diversified into aerospace, healthcare, and logistics. The Rockford Peaches played from 1943 to 1954 in the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Anderson Japanese Gardens is a 12-acre garden with a teahouse, waterfalls, and koi ponds that is considered one of the best Japanese gardens in North America, and the Tinker Swiss Cottage is a Victorian-era home built in the style of a Swiss chalet and overlooking Kent Creek. Downtown Rockford has a growing riverfront with the Prairie Street Brewhouse, the Rockford City Market on summer Fridays, and the historic Coronado Theatre, which opened in 1927. The Midway Village Museum, Aldeen Golf Club, and the Discovery Center Museum are among other local attractions, and the Rockford Art Museum has a good collection of works by regional artists.

Culture and Museums

The Burpee Museum of Natural History is known for "Jane," one of the best-preserved juvenile Tyrannosaurus rex skeletons ever found, and it also has exhibits on geology and local history. The Rockford Art Museum, the Discovery Center Museum, and the Midway Village Museum cover art, science, and local history, and the Coronado Performing Arts Center hosts concerts, Broadway tours, and the Rockford Symphony Orchestra. The Tinker Swiss Cottage Museum and Gardens is a historic house museum with period furnishings and gardens.

Parks

Rockford has a large park system, run by the Rockford Park District, that includes more than 150 parks and several unique garden spaces. The Klehm Arboretum & Botanic Garden has more than 150 acres of trees, perennials, and woodland trails, and Anderson Japanese Gardens offers a quieter experience. Other popular spots include Sinnissippi Gardens and Lagoon, the Rock River Recreation Path, Alpine Park, and Rock Cut State Park, which is about 10 miles north of the city and has lakes, camping, and trails.

Seasons and Weather

Rockford has a humid continental climate with four distinct seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and average snowfall of about 35 inches. Spring and fall are mild and relatively short, and the best time to visit is late spring through early fall, when the gardens are in bloom and events such as the City Market take place.

Featured Locations

Anderson Japanese Gardens
★ 5.0

Twelve-acre garden featuring koi-filled ponds, waterfalls, and meticulously landscaped paths designed in traditional Japanese style.

Burpee Museum of Natural History
★ 5.0

Four-story museum featuring dinosaur fossils, ancient artifacts, and hands-on exhibits that explore the natural world through science and discovery.

Klehm Arboretum and Botanic Garden
★ 5.0

Expansive 155-acre living museum featuring themed gardens, rare trees, and miles of paved and wooded trails for year-round exploration.

Tinker Swiss Cottage Museum and Gardens
★ 5.0

Historic 19th-century Swiss-style cottage offering guided tours through original Victorian-era furnishings, gardens, and preserved artifacts.
