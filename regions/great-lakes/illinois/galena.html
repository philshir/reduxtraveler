How to Get There

From Dubuque, IA (16 mi): Take US-20 E across the Mississippi River into Illinois and continue directly into downtown Galena.

Overview

Galena, Illinois, is a small historic city in the hills of the Driftless Area in the northwest corner of the state, and it is one of the best-preserved nineteenth-century towns in the Midwest. It is famous for its brick Main Street, which is lined with more than 100 shops and restaurants, for the home of Civil War general and U.S. president Ulysses S. Grant, and for a historic district that includes nearly the whole town. The town got its name from galena, the lead ore that brought prosperity in the first half of the nineteenth century.

People come to Galena for a weekend of strolling, shopping, dining, and history, as well as for outdoor activities such as hiking, skiing, golf, and scenic drives through the rolling countryside of Jo Daviess County. It is a popular getaway for visitors from Chicago, Madison, and the Quad Cities, with historic bed-and-breakfasts, wineries, and festivals that include a big Fourth of July and a fall color season.

History

Native Americans and later French and American traders mined lead here, and a lead rush in the 1820s brought thousands of settlers to the Fever River area, so that the town was incorporated in 1835. By the 1850s Galena was a booming river port with steamboats carrying lead and goods to the Mississippi, and for a time it was larger than Chicago. The Galena River silted up and the railroads bypassed the city, and its decline preserved its mid-nineteenth-century architecture. Ulysses S. Grant, who was working in his family's leather shop in Galena when the Civil War began, returned to the city to a hero's welcome in 1865, and the town was home to a number of Civil War generals.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Main Street has some of the best-preserved nineteenth-century commercial architecture in the country, and it ends at the old Market House, a National Historic Landmark. The Ulysses S. Grant Home State Historic Site is an Italianate house that the citizens of Galena gave to Grant after the war, and the Belvedere Mansion and the Dowling House, the oldest house in Galena, are also open for tours. The Galena History Museum has exhibits on the lead-mining era, and visitors can also ride a trolley or take a walking tour of the historic district.

Culture and Museums

The Galena History Museum holds an extensive collection of artifacts, photographs, and Civil War items, and the Old Market House State Historic Site is a Greek Revival building that once held a farmers' market. Art galleries, antique stores, and wine tasting rooms line Main Street, and the town hosts festivals such as the Galena Country Fair and the Galena Arts Festival. The Galena Territory and Eagle Ridge Resort offer golf, horseback riding, and spa services.

Parks

Galena is surrounded by hills and valleys that are good for outdoor recreation. The Galena River Trail runs along the river, Casper Bluff Land and Water Reserve has views of the Mississippi, and Horseshoe Mound Preserve has a short hike with views of town. Chestnut Mountain Resort has skiing in winter and views of the Mississippi in summer, and Apple Canyon Lake and the nearby Mississippi Palisades State Park at Savanna are popular with hikers and campers. Fall colors in the hills make October one of the busiest months.

Seasons and Weather

Galena has a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. Summer highs are in the 80s°F, winter highs are in the 20s and 30s°F, and the hills can receive more than 40 inches of snow annually. Spring and fall are the most pleasant seasons, and fall foliage and holiday events in December attract many visitors. Weekends in summer and October are busiest, so lodging should be reserved well in advance.

Things to Do

Discover One of the Midwest's Best-Preserved Historic Towns

Galena is one of Illinois' most charming and historic destinations, located in the rolling hills of the state's northwestern corner near the Mississippi River. Once a booming lead-mining center in the nineteenth century, Galena today is renowned for its beautifully preserved architecture, historic sites, scenic landscapes, boutique shopping, and vibrant dining scene. More than 85 percent of the town is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Stroll Along Historic Main Street

Galena's Main Street features more than a mile of historic storefronts housing unique boutiques, art galleries, specialty shops, restaurants, wineries, and cafés. Visitors can explore one of the most picturesque downtown districts in the Midwest while enjoying the town's rich history and welcoming atmosphere.

Visit the Ulysses S. Grant Home State Historic Site

One of Galena's most famous attractions is the home of General and President Ulysses S. Grant. Visitors can tour the beautifully preserved residence and learn about Grant's life, military career, and connection to the community that gifted him the home after the Civil War.

Explore Galena's Historic Architecture

Galena contains hundreds of nineteenth-century buildings, churches, mansions, and landmarks. Guided trolley tours, walking tours, and museum visits provide insight into the city's role in lead mining, river commerce, and early Illinois history.

Enjoy Outdoor Recreation and Scenic Views

The surrounding Driftless Area offers rolling hills, river valleys, forests, and scenic overlooks. Visitors can enjoy hiking, cycling, golfing, hot air balloon rides, kayaking, wildlife viewing, and beautiful fall foliage throughout the region.

Top Trails

Galena River Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This scenic rail-trail follows the Galena River and passes through woodlands, wetlands, and historic areas. It is ideal for walking, running, cycling, birdwatching, and enjoying the natural beauty surrounding the city.

Horseshoe Mound Preserve Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles of walking paths
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

Horseshoe Mound Preserve features spectacular overlooks of the surrounding Driftless Area. Visitors enjoy panoramic views of rolling hills, farms, and valleys while learning about local conservation efforts and regional geology.

Casper Bluff Land and Water Reserve Trails

Length: Approximately 2 miles of trails
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This scenic preserve contains Native American mound sites, prairie habitats, and Mississippi River Valley viewpoints. The trails provide opportunities for wildlife viewing, photography, and exploration of the area's cultural history.

Chestnut Mountain Trail System

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Located just outside Galena, these trails offer beautiful views of the Mississippi River and surrounding bluffs. The area is popular for hiking, mountain biking, skiing, and outdoor recreation throughout the year.
