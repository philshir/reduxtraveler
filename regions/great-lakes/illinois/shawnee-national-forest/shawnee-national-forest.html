Shawnee National Forest

★5.0

A vast southern Illinois landscape of sandstone bluffs, deep forests, waterfalls, and historic trails stretching between the Ohio and Mississippi rivers.

General Information

Hours:
Open 24 hours daily
Ranger district offices generally open weekdays, 8am–4:30pm
Fees:
Free admission; no general entrance fee
Pet Policy:
Leashed pets are allowed in developed recreation areas and on most trails
Seasons:
Spring and fall are most popular for wildflowers and fall color; summer is lush but hot and humid; winter is quiet with occasional icy conditions
Location:
Southern Illinois, between the Mississippi and Ohio Rivers
Website:
fs.usda.gov

How to Get There

From St. Louis, take I‑64 east toward southern Illinois, then use IL‑145, IL‑34, or IL‑127 to access various recreation areas and trailheads throughout the forest.

Overview

Shawnee National Forest is Illinois's only national forest, covering nearly 290,000 acres of rugged hills, sandstone bluffs, canyons, wetlands and oak-hickory woods in the southern tip of the state between the Ohio and Mississippi Rivers. After years of farming left the land eroded and depleted, conservation work in the 1930s restored it into the diverse forest seen today. The Shawnee is famous for Garden of the Gods, Rim Rock, Bell Smith Springs, the Little Grand Canyon and the 160-mile River to River Trail. Visitors come to hike, camp, climb, ride horses and paddle in the most scenic and wild landscape in Illinois.

Access

The forest stretches across southern Illinois through Jackson, Union, Johnson, Pope, Hardin, Gallatin, Saline and Williamson Counties. It is about 5 hours from Chicago and 2 hours from St. Louis, and the main gateways are Carbondale, Marion, Harrisburg and Vienna. Nearly all visitors drive, because the attractions are spread over many miles of rural roads, some of which are gravel. Ranger offices in Harrisburg, Vienna and Jonesboro can provide maps and road updates.

Seasons

Spring and fall are the most popular seasons, with wildflowers, waterfalls and mild weather in spring and brilliant color in October. Summer is lush but hot and humid, with ticks and mosquitoes, and swimming and paddling are most popular then. Winter is quiet, with occasional ice and snow that can make trails slick and some gravel roads difficult. Heavy rain can raise creeks and close low-water crossings, so check conditions.

Things to Do

Hike the Famous Rock Formations

Garden of the Gods, Rim Rock, Bell Smith Springs and the Little Grand Canyon are among the best hiking destinations in the Midwest, with sandstone bluffs and sweeping views.

Walk the River to River Trail

The 160-mile trail crosses the forest from the Ohio River to the Mississippi, and many people hike shorter segments.

Climb, Paddle and Swim

Jackson Falls and other cliffs draw climbers, and lakes such as Lake Glendale and Pounds Hollow offer swimming, boating and fishing.

Camp and Ride Horses

Campgrounds, backcountry sites and horse trails allow for longer stays and quiet escapes.

Visit Historic Sites

The Illinois Iron Furnace, Cave-In-Rock and Sand Cave add history to the natural attractions.

Top Trails

River to River Trail

Length: Approximately 160 miles end to end (many shorter segments)
Elevation Gain: Varies
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This long-distance trail crosses the Shawnee from east to west and passes through some of its best scenery, including bluffs, creeks and forests. Most visitors hike short segments.

Garden of the Gods Observation Trail

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This short paved and rocky loop passes rock formations and viewpoints across the Shawnee Hills. It is the most popular short walk in the forest.

Visitor Centers

The Shawnee National Forest Supervisor's Office in Harrisburg and ranger district offices in Vienna and Jonesboro serve as the main information stops, with maps, permits and trail updates. Many recreation areas have only signs and kiosks, so it is helpful to stop at an office or check with the Forest Service before heading into the backcountry.

Camping and Lodging

The forest has campgrounds at places such as Pounds Hollow, Lake Glendale and Johnson Creek, along with many primitive sites. State parks nearby, including Giant City State Park, which has a lodge and cabins, and Ferne Clyffe State Park, also have camping. Hotels and motels are in Carbondale, Marion, Harrisburg, Vienna and Elizabethtown, along with bed-and-breakfasts and cabins throughout the Shawnee Hills.
