How to Get There

From Springfield, IL (20 mi): Take IL-97 N toward Petersburg; continue for approximately 20 miles and follow signs to the historic site entrance on History Lane.

Overview

The Lincoln's New Salem State Historic Site is a meticulously reconstructed pioneer village that brings to life the formative years of Abraham Lincoln. Nestled in the rolling hills near Petersburg, Illinois, the site recreates the 1830s frontier town where Lincoln lived as a young adult, working as a store clerk, postmaster, and surveyor. Visitors can explore over 20 log buildings, including homes, shops, and a tavern, all staffed by costumed interpreters who demonstrate period crafts and share stories of daily life in early Illinois.

Managed by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources, New Salem offers a rich blend of education and entertainment through seasonal events like candlelight walks, bluegrass festivals, and living history weekends. The site also features a visitor center with exhibits, a theater, and a gift shop. Whether you're a history enthusiast or a family seeking a hands-on learning experience, Lincoln’s New Salem offers a vivid window into the past and a deeper understanding of the man who would become one of America’s most revered presidents.

Things to Do

Step Back into Abraham Lincoln's Frontier Years

Lincoln's New Salem State Historic Site preserves the reconstructed village where Abraham Lincoln lived from 1831 to 1837 during his early adulthood. Located near Petersburg, Illinois, the site recreates the frontier community where Lincoln worked as a store clerk, postmaster, surveyor, militia volunteer, and aspiring politician. Visitors can explore the village and gain insight into the experiences that helped shape the future sixteenth President of the United States.

Explore the Reconstructed Pioneer Village

The historic site features more than 20 reconstructed log buildings representing homes, shops, mills, and businesses that existed in the original village. Visitors can walk through the settlement and experience what daily life was like in rural Illinois during the 1830s.

Visit Living History Demonstrations

Costumed interpreters often demonstrate frontier-era crafts, cooking, blacksmithing, farming, woodworking, and other traditional skills. These programs help bring New Salem's history to life and provide a deeper understanding of pioneer life on the Illinois frontier.

Learn About Lincoln's Early Career

Many significant events in Lincoln's early life occurred in New Salem. Visitors can learn about his first political campaigns, friendships, self-education, business ventures, and personal development during the years before he became a lawyer and national political figure.

Enjoy the Sangamon River Landscape

The park overlooks the Sangamon River Valley and includes scenic woodlands, prairies, and wildlife habitats. Visitors can enjoy peaceful walks, photography, birdwatching, and beautiful views of the surrounding countryside while exploring the site.

Top Trails

New Salem Village Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This self-guided route explores the reconstructed village and its historic buildings. Interpretive exhibits and markers explain the significance of each location and its connection to Abraham Lincoln's life.

Carden Woods Nature Trail

Length: Approximately 1.3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

This wooded trail winds through forested areas near the village and offers opportunities for wildlife observation, spring wildflower viewing, and exploration of the natural landscape surrounding New Salem.

Sangamon River Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Following portions of the Sangamon River corridor, this trail provides scenic views of the river and illustrates the natural environment that influenced the growth of the original settlement.

Village and Prairie Walking Areas

Length: Variable distances available
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Additional paths around the historic site allow visitors to explore prairie restorations, picnic areas, and scenic overlooks while learning about the ecology and history of central Illinois.
