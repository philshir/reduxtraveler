How to Get There

From Collinsville (3 mi): Take Collinsville Road west and follow signs toward Ramey Street to reach the historic site entrance and parking areas.

Overview

Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site preserves the remains of the largest pre-Columbian city north of Mexico, built by the Mississippian culture across the river from modern St. Louis. At its peak around 1100 CE, the city covered about six square miles and may have had a population rivaling that of London at the time. The 2,200-acre site protects about 80 of the original 120 earthen mounds, including Monks Mound, which rises 100 feet and covers 14 acres. Cahokia is a National Historic Landmark and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visitors come to climb Monks Mound, see the reconstructed Woodhenge solar calendar and learn about a civilization that flourished more than 1,000 years ago.

Access

The site is at 30 Ramey Street in Collinsville, in southwestern Illinois, about 8 miles east of downtown St. Louis. It is easily reached from I-55/70 and I-255, and parking is free. Most visitors arrive by car, and the site is a short drive from the Gateway Arch. Public transportation is limited. The grounds and trails are open daily from dawn to dusk, while the Interpretive Center has been undergoing renovation, so check the website for current status.

Seasons

The grounds are open year-round, and spring through fall is the most comfortable time to walk the mounds and trails. Summer is hot and humid, with little shade, so plan visits for the morning. Fall brings pleasant weather and color, and special events such as the Mississippian-culture programs and equinox sunrise observances at Woodhenge draw crowds. Winter is cold and windy but quiet, and snow or ice can make the mounds slippery.

Things to Do

Climb Monks Mound

The largest prehistoric earthen structure in the Americas north of Mexico, Monks Mound has a staircase to the summit and views of the site and the St. Louis skyline.

See Woodhenge

The reconstructed circle of posts served as a solar calendar to mark the solstices and equinoxes. Seasonal sunrise events are held there.

Walk the Grand Plaza and Mounds

The Grand Plaza was the heart of the ancient city, and trails lead past smaller mounds and the remains of the palisade.

Visit the Interpretive Center

When open, the center has exhibits and a film about Cahokia and the Mississippian people, including a life-size village scene. Check the current status before visiting.

Attend Special Events

Cultural events, lectures and festivals celebrate the history and Native American heritage of the site.

Top Trails

Monks Mound and Grand Plaza Loop

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet (stairs)
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This walk passes the Grand Plaza and climbs the staircase on Monks Mound for views across the site. It is flat except for the climb up the mound.

Woodhenge and Palisade Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This route leads to the reconstructed Woodhenge and along the remains of the stockade, with signs about the city's layout and defenses.

Visitor Centers

The Interpretive Center is the site's visitor hub, with exhibits, a film, a gift shop and staff to answer questions, though it has been closed for renovation, so confirm hours and status before visiting. The grounds and trails remain open from dawn to dusk, and signs describe the key features. The Cahokia Mounds Museum Society also provides information and supports programs.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. Hotels and motels are in Collinsville, about 3 miles away, and in Fairview Heights and St. Louis, which has a full range of options. Camping is available at Horseshoe Lake State Park, about 15 minutes away, and Pere Marquette State Park, about an hour north, which also has a lodge.
