Split Rock Lighthouse State Park

★5.0
  1. Great Lakes

State park best known for the picturesque Split Rock Lighthouse, one of the most photographed lighthouses in the United States.

General Information

Hours:
Park open daily 8 AM–10 PM; Lighthouse/Museum: 10 AM–6 PM (mid-May through mid-October)
Fees:
$7 daily vehicle permit; $35 annual pass; additional fee for lighthouse tour
Pet Policy:
Pets allowed (6 ft max); not permitted in lighthouse or museum buildings
Closest cities with major hotel chains:
Duluth, MN, 47 miles
Seasons:
All year
Location:
3713 Split Rock Lighthouse Road, Two Harbors, MN 55616
Website:
dnr.state.mn.us

How to Get There

From Duluth, MN (47 mi): Take MN-61 E (North Shore Scenic Drive) northeast from Duluth approximately 47 miles, then turn right onto Split Rock Lighthouse Road to the state park and lighthouse entrance.

Overview

Split Rock Lighthouse State Park surrounds one of the most photographed lighthouses in the United States, perched on a 130-foot cliff above Lake Superior on Minnesota's North Shore. The lighthouse was built in 1910 after a powerful storm in 1905 wrecked many ships, and it served as a beacon for iron ore freighters until 1969. The Minnesota Historical Society operates the lighthouse and a history center, and the state park offers trails, a campground and a beach. Visitors come for the lighthouse tour, the shoreline views and hiking.

Access

The park is on MN-61 about 47 miles northeast of Duluth and 20 miles from Two Harbors, near Beaver Bay. Visitors drive the North Shore Scenic Drive for about an hour from Duluth, and the entrance to the lighthouse is clearly signed. Most visitors arrive by car, and the Gitchi-Gami State Trail passes through the park.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with the lighthouse and museum open from mid-May to mid-October and on select days in winter. Summer is busy and pleasant, though Lake Superior is cold and fog is common. Fall brings colors and storm watching. The lighthouse is lit on November 10 each year in memory of the Edmund Fitzgerald. Winter is cold with skiing and snowshoeing.

Things to Do

Tour the Split Rock Lighthouse

Split Rock Lighthouse State Park's lighthouse is open for tours, with the lens room, keeper's house and fog signal building. Costumed interpreters share stories of the keepers.

Visit the History Center

The center has exhibits on the lighthouse, shipwrecks and the North Shore, with a film. It is operated by the Minnesota Historical Society.

Hike the Shoreline Trails

Trails lead along the cliffs and to a pebble beach with views of the lighthouse. They are scenic and varied.

Camp on Lake Superior

The cart-in campground and backcountry sites offer a quiet place beside the lake. Sites are popular and need reservations.

Watch the Beacon Lighting

Every November 10, the lighthouse is lit to honor the Edmund Fitzgerald. It draws crowds.

Top Trails

Lighthouse Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved path leads from the visitor center to the lighthouse, with views. It is accessible and short.

Split Rock River Loop

Length: Approximately 4 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 300 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A trail follows the river through forest to the lake and back. It is popular and shady.

Day Hill Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 300 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs to views of the lake and lighthouse from above. It is rocky in places.

Visitor Centers

The Split Rock Lighthouse History Center, run by the Minnesota Historical Society, is the main visitor facility, with exhibits, a film and a gift shop. It is open daily from mid-May to mid-October, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., and has limited winter days. A lighthouse tour has a separate fee. The state park office provides permits and maps. Check the Minnesota Historical Society and DNR websites for hours.

Camping and Lodging

The park's cart-in campground has sites near the lake, and backcountry and group sites are available, with reservations strongly recommended. Two Harbors, about 25 minutes south, has the Superior Shores Resort and chain motels, and Beaver Bay and Silver Bay, about 10 minutes away, have cabins and lodges. Duluth, about an hour southwest, has many hotels. Check each property's website for current availability.
