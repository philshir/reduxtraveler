Gooseberry Falls State Park

★5.0
  1. Great Lakes
  2. Minnesota
  3. Gooseberry Falls State Park

Yinan Chen, Public Domain, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Park surrounds the mouth of the Gooseberry River and includes Upper, Middle and Lower Gooseberry Falls

General Information

Hours:
Open daily 8 AM–10 PM; Visitor Center: 9 AM–4 PM (seasonal hours may vary)
Fees:
$7 daily vehicle permit; $35 annual pass
Pet Policy:
Pets allowed (6 ft max); not permitted in visitor center or rental facilities
Closest cities with major hotel chains:
Duluth, MN, 40 miles
Seasons:
All year
Location:
3206 Highway 61, Two Harbors, MN 55616
Website:
dnr.state.mn.us

How to Get There

From Duluth, MN (40 mi): Take MN-61 E (North Shore Scenic Drive) northeast from downtown Duluth, following Lake Superior's shoreline for approximately 40 miles to the Gooseberry Falls State Park entrance on the right at 3206 MN-61.

Overview

Gooseberry Falls State Park is one of the most popular state parks in Minnesota, a Lake Superior shoreline park with a series of five waterfalls on the Gooseberry River, including the 30-foot Upper Falls and the Lower Falls near the lake. The park lies along MN-61 on the North Shore, 40 miles from Duluth, and it was developed by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s. Its visitor center, trails and campground make it an ideal first stop on the North Shore. Visitors come for the falls, the Lake Superior shoreline, hiking and camping.

Access

The park is directly on MN-61, the North Shore Scenic Drive, about 40 miles northeast of Duluth and 13 miles from Two Harbors. Most visitors drive from Duluth in about 50 minutes, and the entrance is well signed. The visitor center and falls are easy to reach, and parking lots are close. The Gitchi-Gami State Trail passes through the park for cyclists.

Seasons

The park is open year-round. Spring and early summer have the strongest waterfalls from snowmelt and rain. Summer is busy with camping and hiking, and fall brings brilliant colors, especially in late September. Winter turns the falls to ice, and the park is popular for snowshoeing and skiing. Lake Superior waters are always cold, and fog is common.

Things to Do

See Five Waterfalls

Gooseberry Falls State Park's Upper, Middle and Lower Falls are short walks from the visitor center. The falls are strongest in spring.

Explore the Lake Superior Shoreline

Rocky shores near the river mouth are great for exploring and watching waves. Visitors should be careful on the wet rocks.

Hike the Superior Hiking Trail

The famed trail crosses the park and links to longer routes. Day hikes offer views.

Visit the Visitor Center

The center has exhibits, a gift shop and rangers, and the building is a CCC-era style. It is a good first stop.

Camp Near the Falls

The campground has sites in the forest near the river. Reservations are needed in summer.

Top Trails

Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved and stone path visits the Upper, Middle and Lower Falls, with viewpoints and bridges. It is the most popular walk.

Fifth Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 300 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A longer route continues upstream to more falls and quiet forest. It is a good option to escape crowds.

Gitchi-Gami State Trail Section

Length: Approximately 5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved trail runs along the shore and through the park. It is suitable for cyclists and walkers.

Visitor Centers

The Gooseberry Falls State Park Visitor Center is open daily with seasonal hours, typically 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and has exhibits on the falls and the CCC, a gift shop, restrooms and staff who provide maps and advice. Check the Minnesota DNR website for hours and events. A state park permit is required.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a campground with drive-in and walk-in sites, and a group camp. Two Harbors, about 15 minutes away, has lodging such as the Superior Shores Resort, the Larsmont Cottages and chain hotels, and Silver Bay and Beaver Bay offer cabins and resorts. Duluth, about 50 minutes south, has many hotels. Check each property's website for current availability.
