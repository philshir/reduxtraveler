How to Get There

From Philadelphia (40 mi): Take I-95 N to Exit 51 for Taylorsville Rd, continue north to River Rd, then follow signs to New Hope via PA-32 N.

Overview

New Hope is a picturesque town of about 2,500 people in Bucks County, Pennsylvania, on the Delaware River about 40 miles north of Philadelphia. It is famous as an arts colony and theater town, for its galleries, shops, boutique inns and restaurants, for the Bucks County Playhouse and as one of the East Coast's most welcoming LGBTQ+ destinations. The bridge across the Delaware leads to Lambertville, New Jersey, and the two towns are often visited together.

People come to New Hope to stroll Main Street and Ferry Street, browse independent shops, see a show at the Playhouse, ride the New Hope & Ivyland Railroad, take a mule-drawn canal boat ride, walk the Delaware Canal towpath and eat at riverside restaurants. It is a popular weekend trip from New York and Philadelphia in every season.

History

The town began as a ferry crossing on the Delaware, called Coryell's Ferry, and later as a mill town. After a fire in 1790 destroyed Benjamin Parry's mill, it was rebuilt and the settlement was renamed New Hope Mills, which became New Hope. The Delaware Canal, opened in 1832 to carry coal from Easton to Bristol, brought mule-drawn barges and commerce. In the early 20th century a group of landscape painters called the New Hope School or Pennsylvania Impressionists, led by William Lathrop, settled here, and in the 1930s writers, actors and musicians followed. The Bucks County Playhouse opened in 1939 in an old grist mill and became known for plays trying out before Broadway.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

New Hope's walkable center has historic stone buildings, art galleries, craft shops, bookstores and restaurants along Main Street and Bridge Street. The Parry Mansion, built in 1784, is a museum of the town's founding family. The Delaware Canal towpath offers walking and biking, and mule-drawn canal boat rides leave from the town. The New Hope & Ivyland Railroad runs vintage steam and diesel excursions. The bridge to Lambertville is an easy walk, and Washington Crossing Historic Park, Bowman's Hill Wildflower Preserve and Peddler's Village are nearby.

Culture and Museums

The Bucks County Playhouse presents plays and musicals in a restored grist mill beside the river. The town's many galleries show works by regional artists, and the nearby James A. Michener Art Museum in Doylestown has a large collection of Pennsylvania Impressionist paintings. New Hope has long been known for its LGBTQ+ community, and events include the Arts & Crafts Festival, Pride Week, the Fourth of July parade and a popular Halloween ghost-tour season. Local restaurants serve everything from casual pubs to elegant French and farm-to-table menus.

Parks

The Delaware Canal State Park runs along the river for 59 miles, with a flat towpath for walking, biking and running right through town. Bowman's Hill Wildflower Preserve has native plant trails, and Washington Crossing Historic Park includes the place where Washington's army crossed the Delaware on Christmas night in 1776. The Delaware River itself is popular for tubing, kayaking and fishing.

Seasons and Weather

New Hope has a humid continental to humid subtropical climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s °F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and occasional snow. Spring and fall are the most popular seasons, with blooming gardens and fall foliage along the river. The Delaware River can flood after heavy rain or snowmelt, which has damaged the canal towpath and downtown several times, and summer weekends bring heavy traffic and limited parking.
