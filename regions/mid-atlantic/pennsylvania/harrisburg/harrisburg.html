Overview

Harrisburg is the capital of Pennsylvania, a city of about 50,000 people on the east bank of the Susquehanna River in south-central Pennsylvania, about 100 miles west of Philadelphia. It is famous for the Pennsylvania State Capitol, a Beaux-Arts landmark with a dome inspired by St. Peter's in Rome, for its history as a river crossing and Civil War staging point, for the Pennsylvania Farm Show and for being close to Hershey.

People come to Harrisburg to tour the Capitol, visit its museums, walk along the river and City Island, watch a minor league baseball game, and eat at the Broad Street Market and in downtown restaurants. It is a good base for exploring Hershey, Gettysburg, Lancaster County and the Appalachian Trail, and it is easy to reach by Amtrak and the Pennsylvania Turnpike.

History

John Harris Sr. started a trading post and ferry here around 1719, and his son John Harris Jr. laid out the town in 1785. Harrisburg became the state capital in 1812. In the 19th century it grew as a hub for canals and railroads, including the Pennsylvania Railroad, and during the Civil War it was a major Union training and staging area, with Camp Curtin as one of the largest. The present Capitol was designed by Joseph Huston and dedicated in 1906 by President Theodore Roosevelt. In 1979, the nuclear accident at Three Mile Island, a few miles south of the city, drew national attention. Harrisburg has since worked on riverfront and downtown revitalization after fiscal crises.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The State Capitol, with its green dome, grand staircase and murals, offers free tours. City Island, in the middle of the Susquehanna River, has a baseball stadium, walking paths, a small beach and a railroad, and downtown Harrisburg has restaurants, bars and shops. The Pride of the Susquehanna riverboat cruises the river in summer, and the Broad Street Market, dating from 1860, is one of the oldest continuously operating farmers markets in the country. Saint Patrick Cathedral is a historic downtown church.

Culture and Museums

The State Museum of Pennsylvania features Pennsylvania history, archaeology and art, including a famous painting of the Battle of Gettysburg and a planetarium. The National Civil War Museum in Reservoir Park tells the story of the whole war, not only one side. The Pennsylvania National Fire Museum, the Susquehanna Art Museum and the Midtown Scholar Bookstore, one of the largest used bookstores in the region, add variety. The Pennsylvania Farm Show each January at the Farm Show Complex is one of the largest indoor agricultural events in the country, and Kipona, a Labor Day riverfront festival, is a local tradition.

Parks

The Susquehanna riverfront is Harrisburg's most important green space, with Riverfront Park's long promenade and cherry trees along Front Street, and City Island. Reservoir Park has views and gardens, Italian Lake is a small park with a lake in uptown, and the 20-mile Capital Area Greenbelt loops around the city for walking and biking. Wildwood Park and its lake, north of the city, has trails and a nature center, and the Kittatinny Ridge and the Appalachian Trail are a short drive away.

Sports

The Harrisburg Senators, the Double-A affiliate of the Washington Nationals in the Eastern League, play at FNB Field on City Island. Baseball has been played on the island for more than a century, and a game on a summer evening with the river and the skyline in the background is a local favorite. Fans also follow Penn State and the major league teams in Philadelphia, Pittsburgh and Baltimore.

Seasons and Weather

Harrisburg has a humid subtropical to humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s °F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and moderate snowfall. Spring brings cherry blossoms along the river, and fall is colorful and comfortable. The Susquehanna River can flood after heavy rain or snowmelt, as it did in 1972 during Tropical Storm Agnes, and winter storms can close roads and state offices.
