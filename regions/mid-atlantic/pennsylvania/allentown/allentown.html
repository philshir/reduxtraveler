Overview

Allentown is the third-largest city in Pennsylvania, with about 125,000 residents, and the largest city of the Lehigh Valley in the eastern part of the state, about 60 miles north of Philadelphia and 90 miles west of New York City. It is famous as the hiding place of the Liberty Bell during the Revolutionary War, for the Billy Joel song "Allentown," for the Allentown Fair and for being the home of Mack Trucks. It is a former manufacturing center with a revitalized downtown of museums, entertainment venues and public art.

People come to Allentown to visit its art and science museums, ride roller coasters at nearby Dorney Park & Wildwater Kingdom, see minor league baseball and hockey, and explore the Lehigh Valley's wineries, parks and trails. It is also the gateway to neighboring Bethlehem, with its SteelStacks campus, Moravian history and Christmas traditions, and the Lehigh Valley has become a popular weekend destination for the New York and Philadelphia areas.

History

William Allen, a wealthy Philadelphia merchant and chief justice of Pennsylvania, founded the town in 1762 and named it Northamptontown. During the Revolutionary War, the Liberty Bell and other bells from Philadelphia were hidden in the Zion Reformed Church in 1777 to keep them from the British army, and the bell remained there until it was returned in 1778. The town was renamed Allentown in 1838. The Lehigh Canal and later the railroads made it an industrial center for iron, silk, cement and later trucks, and Mack Trucks moved its headquarters here in the early 1900s. Manufacturing declined in the late 20th century, and the city has focused on downtown revitalization and healthcare and warehouse jobs.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Allentown's downtown includes the PPL Center arena, the Allentown Art Museum and a growing number of restaurants and shops. The Liberty Bell Shrine at Zion Reformed Church tells the story of the bell's hiding place. SteelStacks, in nearby Bethlehem, preserves the blast furnaces of the old Bethlehem Steel plant as an arts and music venue, and Historic Bethlehem shows the 18th-century Moravian settlement. Dorney Park, which opened in 1884, is one of the oldest amusement parks in the country, and the Allentown Fair, held each September since 1852, is a big annual event.

Culture and Museums

The Allentown Art Museum has European and American art and a room designed by Frank Lloyd Wright. The Da Vinci Science Center is a hands-on science museum for families, and the America On Wheels Museum displays classic cars and trucks. The Lehigh Valley Heritage Museum and the Haines Mill Museum explain local history, and the National Museum of Industrial History in Bethlehem is part of the SteelStacks complex. The Allentown Symphony and the Miller Symphony Hall present concerts, and Musikfest in Bethlehem each August is one of the largest free music festivals in the country.

Parks

Lehigh Parkway is a large city park along the Little Lehigh Creek with trails and picnic areas. Cedar Beach Park and Trexler Nature Preserve in Schnecksville, which has bison and elk, offer more open space, and the Delaware and Lehigh Trail, part of the Delaware and Lehigh National Heritage Corridor, runs along the old canal and rail routes. The Lehigh Valley Zoo in Schnecksville and the nearby Blue Mountain ridge also give visitors places to hike and explore.

Sports

Allentown has two professional teams. The Lehigh Valley IronPigs, the Triple-A affiliate of the Philadelphia Phillies, play at Coca-Cola Park, and the Lehigh Valley Phantoms, the American Hockey League affiliate of the Philadelphia Flyers, play at the PPL Center downtown. Lehigh University and Lafayette College, rivals in the nearby area, play college football in the most-played rivalry in the sport. Philadelphia's major league teams are about an hour away.

Seasons and Weather

Allentown has a humid continental climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s °F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s °F and snowfall of about 30 inches a year. Spring and fall are pleasant, with fall foliage in the Lehigh Valley, and summer is the best time for Dorney Park, Musikfest and outdoor events. Nor'easters and winter storms can bring heavy snow, and summer thunderstorms are common.
