Overview

Scranton is a city of about 76,000 people in the Lackawanna Valley of northeastern Pennsylvania, known as the "Electric City" for its early electric streetcar system and as the center of the anthracite coal industry. It is famous as the setting of the TV show The Office and as the birthplace of President Joe Biden. The city has a historic downtown, railroad and mining museums and surrounding mountains.

People come to Scranton to visit Steamtown National Historic Site, go underground at the Lackawanna Coal Mine, see The Office sites such as the "Welcome to Scranton" sign, enjoy Nay Aug Park and eat Pennsylvania Dutch and Northeastern Pennsylvania favorites. The Poconos and the Wyoming Valley are close by, and the city is about two hours from both New York and Philadelphia.

History

The Lackawanna Valley was home to the Lenape before European settlement. In the 1840s the Scranton brothers, George and Selden, founded an iron works that grew into the Lackawanna Iron and Coal Company, and the town that grew up around it was named Scranton and incorporated as a city in 1866. Anthracite coal mining and railroads, including the Delaware, Lackawanna and Western, fueled the economy, and thousands of immigrants from Wales, Ireland, Italy, Poland and elsewhere came to work in the mines. In 1886 Scranton opened one of the first successful electric streetcar systems in the United States, earning the "Electric City" nickname. The coal and railroad industries declined after the 1930s, and the city has since turned to health care, education and heritage tourism.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Steamtown National Historic Site, in the old Lackawanna railroad yard, preserves steam locomotives and offers excursions and a museum. The Lackawanna Coal Mine Tour in McDade Park takes visitors 300 feet underground by mine car. The Scranton Iron Furnaces preserve the stone blast furnaces that started the city. The Lackawanna Station, a 1908 railroad station that is now a hotel, and the Scranton Cultural Center in a 1930 Masonic Temple are downtown landmarks. The Office fans come for the "Welcome to Scranton: The Electric City" sign and the Steamtown Mall, which appeared in the show's opening.

Culture and Museums

The Anthracite Heritage Museum tells the story of the coal miners and immigrants, and the Electric City Trolley Museum preserves the city's trolley history and offers rides. The Everhart Museum, in Nay Aug Park, has natural history, art and science exhibits. The Houdini Museum, the Scranton Cultural Center and the Scranton Fringe Festival add to the arts scene, and the city is known for its Irish and Italian heritage festivals, including a large St. Patrick's Day parade.

Parks

Nay Aug Park, a large city park with a gorge, waterfalls and trails, is one of the best parks in the region. McDade Park, where the Lackawanna Coal Mine and the Anthracite Heritage Museum are located, has trails and picnic areas. The Lackawanna River Heritage Trail follows the river for walking and biking, and Montage Mountain in nearby Moosic offers skiing in winter and a water park in summer. The Poconos and Lackawanna State Park provide more outdoor recreation.

Sports

The Scranton/Wilkes-Barre RailRiders, the Triple-A affiliate of the New York Yankees, play at PNC Field in nearby Moosic. The Wilkes-Barre/Scranton Penguins, the AHL affiliate of the Pittsburgh Penguins, play in nearby Wilkes-Barre. Local fans also follow the major league teams in New York, Philadelphia and Pittsburgh.

Seasons and Weather

Scranton has a humid continental climate with four distinct seasons. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s °F and about 45 inches of snow a year. Spring and fall are pleasant, with fall foliage in the surrounding mountains in October. Winter storms can close roads and attractions, and some seasonal activities, like the trolley rides and steam excursions at Steamtown, run on limited schedules outside summer and fall.
