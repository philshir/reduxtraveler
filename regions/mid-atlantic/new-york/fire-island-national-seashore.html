Fire Island National Seashore

★5.0

National seashore that protects a 26-mile section of Fire Island, an approximately 30-mile long barrier island separated from Long Island by the Great South Bay

General Information

Hours:
Open year-round, 24 hours/day
Visitor centers open Memorial Day through Labor Day (hours vary by location)
Watch Hill and Sailors Haven visitor centers: 9am–5pm (seasonal)
Fees:
Free to access the national seashore
Ferry fares: approximately $10–$15 per person round trip from mainland
Robert Moses State Park parking: varies seasonally
Pet Policy:
Leashed pets permitted in most areas; prohibited on some beaches April–August during piping plover nesting season; not allowed inside visitor centers
Closest cities with hotels:
Bay Shore, NY; Patchogue, NY
Seasons:
Year-round; summer for beaches and swimming; spring and fall for birding and hiking; Otis Pike Wilderness accessible all year
Location:
Fire Island, Suffolk County, NY (accessible by ferry from Bay Shore, Sayville, or Patchogue; or by car via Robert Moses Causeway)
Website:
nps.gov/fiis

How to Get There

From Bay Shore, NY (ferry — approximately 30 min to Ocean Beach): Take the Long Island Rail Road to Bay Shore station, then walk to the Fire Island Ferry terminal on Maple Ave. By car, take the Southern State Parkway to Exit 40 (Robert Moses Causeway / NY-908F) south to reach Robert Moses State Park at the western end of the seashore.

Overview

Fire Island National Seashore protects 26 miles of a roughly 30-mile barrier island separated from Long Island by the Great South Bay, established as a National Park Service unit in 1964. Seventeen private communities, including Saltaire, Fire Island Pines and Ocean Beach, lie within it. There are no public roads within the seashore. The Sunken Forest, the Otis Pike Fire Island High Dune Wilderness, the Fire Island Lighthouse and the William Floyd Estate on Long Island are among its highlights.

Access

Visitors reach the seashore by the Robert Moses Causeway to the west end, or William Floyd Parkway to the east end, or by ferry from Bay Shore, Sayville and Patchogue. A parking fee is charged at Robert Moses State Park. There is no road across the island, so planning is needed. Long Island Rail Road trains connect to the ferry terminals.

Seasons

The seashore is open all year, but ferries run mostly from May through October. Summer brings swimming, crowds and heat. Spring and fall are quiet and good for birds. Winter is cold, windy and lonely. Hurricane season runs from June through November.

Things to Do

Walk the Sunken Forest

A boardwalk passes a rare maritime forest of holly and sassafras.

Climb the Lighthouse

The Fire Island Lighthouse has views of the ocean and the bay.

Swim and Surf

Ocean beaches have lifeguards in summer.

Hike the Wilderness

The Otis Pike High Dune Wilderness is the only federally designated wilderness in New York.

Tour the William Floyd Estate

A Long Island unit preserves the home of a signer of the Declaration of Independence.

Top Trails

Sunken Forest Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A boardwalk through the maritime forest at Sailors Haven.

Lighthouse to Robert Moses Walk

Length: Approximately 2 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A boardwalk and beach route from the lighthouse.

Wilderness Trail

Length: Approximately 7 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Moderate

A walk along the beach in the High Dune Wilderness, with soft sand.

Visitor Centers

The Sailors Haven Visitor Center, the Fire Island Lighthouse Visitor Center, the Watch Hill Visitor Center and the William Floyd Estate offer exhibits, bookstores and ranger programs. Rangers lead walks in season.

Camping and Lodging

Camping is available at Watch Hill, with a campground and a marina, and wilderness camping at Smith Point. Hotels and inns are in the communities of Ocean Beach, Fair Harbor and Cherry Grove, and on the mainland in Bay Shore, Sayville and Patchogue.
