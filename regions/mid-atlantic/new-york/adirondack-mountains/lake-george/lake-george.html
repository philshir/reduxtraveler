How to Get There

From Glens Falls (11 mi): Take US-9 North or Interstate 87 North to the Lake George exits and follow signs directly into the village waterfront district.

Overview

Lake George is a village in Warren County, New York, at the southern end of the 32-mile-long lake of the same name in the southeastern Adirondacks, about 55 miles north of Albany. The lake is called the "Queen of American Lakes" for its clear water, mountain views and 170 islands, and the village is its tourism hub, with a lakefront strip of restaurants, shops, miniature golf, beaches and boat cruises.

People come to Lake George to swim, boat and cruise on the lake, hike the nearby mountains, visit Revolutionary War and French and Indian War sites, and spend a classic family summer vacation. The nearby Great Escape amusement park and the Gilded Age mansions on the lake add variety, and in fall and winter the lake is a base for foliage trips and winter sports.

History

The Mohawk and other Native Americans used the lake long before Europeans. Father Isaac Jogues, a French Jesuit, reached it in 1646 and named it Lac du Saint-Sacrement, and in 1755 Sir William Johnson renamed it Lake George for King George II. The lake lay on the route between Albany and Canada and was the scene of major fighting in the French and Indian War, including the Battle of Lake George in 1755, the building of Fort William Henry and the siege and surrender of the fort in 1757, later made famous by James Fenimore Cooper's The Last of the Mohicans. In the late 19th century the railroad and steamboats made Lake George one of the country's earliest vacation destinations, with grand hotels and estates, and the Lake George Association was formed in 1885 to protect the water quality.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Fort William Henry is a reconstructed 1755 fort and museum with cannon firing demonstrations and tours. The Lake George Steamboat Company's cruises, including the paddlewheeler Minne-Ha-Ha and the Lac du Saint Sacrement, are a village tradition. Prospect Mountain has a scenic highway to a summit with views of the lake, Million Dollar Beach is the village's main swimming area, and Shepard Park hosts concerts and summer fireworks. Fort Ticonderoga, at the northern end of the lake, is about an hour's drive away.

Culture and Museums

Fort William Henry Museum tells the story of the French and Indian War, and the Lake George Historical Association museum covers local history. The Lake George Arts Project and summer concerts at Shepard Park add culture, and the Lake George Winter Carnival in February features ice sculptures, snow events and outdoor games. Local traditions include fireworks, classic wooden boat shows, and the Gilded Age mansions of the "Millionaires' Row" along the shore.

Parks

Shepard Park and Million Dollar Beach are the main village parks with swimming and lakefront. Lake George Battlefield Park preserves the site of the 1755 battle and the 1757 fort camp. The Warren County Bikeway offers a long paved path for walking and biking, and the Lake George Wild Forest has hiking trails, including Shelving Rock Falls and Sleeping Beauty Mountain. The lake's islands include state-run camping areas for boaters and paddlers.

Seasons and Weather

Lake George has a humid continental climate with warm summers, cold snowy winters and colorful autumns. Summer highs are in the 70s and 80s °F, with the lake warming enough for swimming in July and August, and the village is busiest from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Fall foliage usually peaks in late September and early October. Winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s °F, and the lake often freezes, which makes ice fishing and skating popular. Many seasonal shops, attractions and boat cruises close from October to May.

Featured Locations

Fort William Henry
★ 5.0

Restored military fort and living history museum featuring period exhibits, battlefield stories, guided tours, and immersive demonstrations of colonial frontier life.

Million Dollar Beach
★ 5.0

Popular public beach offering swimming, picnic areas, lake access, family recreation, and expansive views of the surrounding Adirondack Mountains.

Prospect Mountain
★ 5.0

Mountain summit destination featuring a scenic highway, hiking trails, panoramic Adirondack views, and one of the most celebrated overlooks in the Northeast.

Shepard Park
★ 5.0

Lakefront public park featuring a sandy beach, swimming area, playground, fishing access, community events, and panoramic views of the surrounding mountains.
