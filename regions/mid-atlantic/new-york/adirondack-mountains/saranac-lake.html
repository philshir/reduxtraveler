How to Get There

From Lake Placid (10 mi): Take NY-86 West through the Adirondack Mountains and continue directly into downtown Saranac Lake.

Overview

Saranac Lake is a lively village of about 5,000 people in the heart of the Adirondack Mountains of northern New York, surrounded by more than a hundred lakes and ponds and by the Adirondack Park, the largest protected area in the contiguous United States. It is famous for its history as a center for tuberculosis treatment, whose "cure cottages" still line the streets, for the Saranac Lake Winter Carnival with its ice palace, and as a gateway for paddling, hiking and skiing.

People come to Saranac Lake to paddle the Saranac chain of lakes, hike the nearby peaks, ski and snowshoe in winter, shop and eat in the walkable downtown on Lake Flower, and see the Winter Carnival in February. It is only about 10 miles from Lake Placid, and many travelers combine the two villages for an Adirondack vacation.

History

In the 19th century, the Adirondacks drew hunters, anglers and artists, and in 1876 Dr. Edward Livingston Trudeau, himself a tuberculosis patient, came to Saranac Lake and found that the cold, clear mountain air helped his recovery. He opened the Adirondack Cottage Sanitarium in 1884, and Saranac Lake became a nationally known tuberculosis treatment center, with patients resting on "cure porches" attached to cottages. Robert Louis Stevenson spent the winter of 1887-88 in a cottage here, and today a museum preserves it. After antibiotics ended the TB era in the 1950s, the village shifted to tourism and recreation. The Winter Carnival began in 1897, and the ice palace tradition has continued ever since.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Downtown Saranac Lake has restored buildings, shops, galleries, restaurants and breweries on Main Street and along Lake Flower. The Robert Louis Stevenson Cottage, the Historic Saranac Lake museum and walking tours of the cure cottages tell the sanitarium story. The Adirondack Carousel in William Morris Park has hand-carved animals of local wildlife, and Mount Pisgah has a small ski hill and hiking trail with views of the village. Nearby attractions include the Wild Center natural history museum in Tupper Lake, Whiteface Mountain, and the High Peaks, including Mount Marcy, New York's tallest mountain.

Culture and Museums

The Historic Saranac Lake museum and the Robert Louis Stevenson Memorial Cottage explain the village's TB history and its famous visitors. The Pendragon Theatre presents plays, and the Adirondack Artists Guild, local galleries and concerts keep the arts scene lively. The Saranac Lake Winter Carnival, one of the oldest winter festivals in the United States, takes place in late January and February with an ice palace, parades, fireworks and ice sculptures. The Adirondack Carousel and the Wild Center are good family stops.

Parks

The Saranac Lakes and Lake Flower are the main outdoor attractions, with paddling, fishing and boating. The St. Regis Canoe Area, northwest of the village, is a wilderness of ponds and portages for canoeing, and the Saranac Lakes Wild Forest has trails for hiking and mountain biking. The Saranac Lake 6er challenge encourages hikers to climb six nearby peaks, and in winter, trails host cross-country skiing, snowshoeing and snowmobiling.

Seasons and Weather

Saranac Lake has a cold humid continental climate. Winters are long and snowy, with highs in the 20s °F, nights well below zero and heavy snowfall, which is why the village is known for winter sports. Summers are mild and pleasant, with highs in the 70s °F, ideal for paddling and hiking, though black flies are bothersome in May and June. Fall foliage peaks in late September and early October, and spring is "mud season," when trails can be wet and some lakes remain icy into April.
