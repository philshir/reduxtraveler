Overview

Niagara Falls is three waterfalls at the southern end of the Niagara Gorge on the U.S.-Canada border: Horseshoe Falls (Canadian Falls), the largest and most powerful in North America by flow, and the smaller American and Bridal Veil falls, entirely in the U.S. Goat and Luna islands separate them. The combined falls have the highest flow rate of any North American waterfall with a vertical drop of more than 160 feet, with more than six million cubic feet of water a minute going over the crest at peak hours, and they also generate hydroelectric power. Summer is peak season, and floodlights light the falls at night.

On the U.S. Side

  • Maid of the Mist boat rides since 1846
  • Niagara Falls State Park, American Falls, Prospect Point Observation Tower, and Goat Island with the Tesla statue and the Cave of the Winds
  • Niagara Gorge, Whirlpool and Devil's Hole state parks
  • The Niagara Scenic Trolley, helicopter tours, and the Niagara Gorge Discovery Center

On the Canadian Side

Queen Victoria Park has manicured gardens and views of all three falls, the Journey Behind the Falls tunnels, the Skylon Tower observation deck, and the Whirlpool Aero Car (1916). The Rainbow Bridge links the two countries; bring a passport.
