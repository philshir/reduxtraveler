General Information

Hours:
City open year-round; Niagara Falls State Park open daily
Peak tourist season: June through August; falls illuminated nightly year-round
Fees:
City and outdoor areas free to visit; parking $5–$15 at state park; individual attractions charge separately
Pet Policy:
Leashed pets welcome in most outdoor public areas; restrictions at individual attractions and state park buildings
Closest cities with hotels:
Hotels throughout Niagara Falls, NY; Buffalo, NY (approximately 20 miles south) for additional options; Niagara Falls, Ontario (across Rainbow Bridge) for Canadian-side hotels with falls views
Seasons:
Year-round; summer (June–August) peak season; winter for dramatic ice formations and far fewer crowds; fall for foliage and comfortable weather; spring for ice breakup views
Location:
Niagara Falls, NY 14301 (western New York on the US–Canada border)
Website:
niagarafallsusa.com

How to Get There

From Buffalo, NY (approximately 20 miles): Take I-190 N to Exit 22 (Grand Island/Niagara Falls), then follow Robert Moses Pkwy N into downtown Niagara Falls and the state park. From Toronto, Ontario (approximately 90 miles): Take Queen Elizabeth Way (QEW) west to the Rainbow Bridge or Lewiston-Queenston Bridge into the US. From New York City (approximately 380 miles): Take I-90 W (NYS Thruway) to I-190 N.

Overview

Niagara Falls, New York is a city of about 48,000 people in Niagara County on the U.S. side of the Niagara River, directly across from Niagara Falls, Ontario, and about 20 miles north of Buffalo. It is famous as the gateway to Niagara Falls, one of the world's most powerful waterfalls, and as home to Niagara Falls State Park, the oldest state park in the United States.

People come to the city to see the American Falls, Bridal Veil Falls and the view of Horseshoe Falls, ride the Maid of the Mist boat into the mist, walk the pathways of Goat Island, get soaked at the Cave of the Winds, and cross the Rainbow Bridge to Canada. The falls are lit up at night, and fireworks and winter lights add to the experience.

History

Native peoples, including the Neutral and later the Seneca, lived in this area long before French explorers arrived. Louis Hennepin, a Franciscan priest, was among the first Europeans to describe the falls in 1678. In the 19th century, the falls became a famous honeymoon and tourist destination, and the city was also a crossing point for fugitives on the Underground Railroad. The "Free Niagara" movement led to the creation of Niagara Falls State Park in 1885, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux. In 1895 the Adams power plant began generating alternating current from the falls using the designs of Nikola Tesla and George Westinghouse, and in 1896 it sent power to Buffalo; cheap electricity made the city an industrial center. Environmental problems such as the Love Canal disaster in the 1970s marked the later chapters of its history.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Niagara Falls State Park offers the best views of American Falls, Bridal Veil Falls and Horseshoe Falls, with the Prospect Point Observation Tower and Goat Island with its Cave of the Winds and Tesla monument. The Maid of the Mist boat tour, offered since 1846, takes visitors to the base of the falls. The Niagara Gorge, Whirlpool State Park and Devil's Hole State Park offer trails along the cliffs and rapids. The Rainbow Bridge leads to Canada, so bring a passport.

Culture and Museums

The Niagara Falls Underground Railroad Heritage Center tells the story of freedom seekers who crossed here. The Aquarium of Niagara has sea lions, penguins and sharks, and the Niagara Gorge Discovery Center explains the geology of the gorge. The Niagara Power Vista at the nearby power project in Lewiston describes the region's hydroelectric history, and the Seneca Niagara Resort & Casino offers entertainment. Old Fort Niagara in nearby Youngstown, built by the French in 1726, is one of the oldest military sites in North America, and the Winter Festival of Lights brings holiday displays to the city and park from November through January.

Parks

Niagara Falls State Park is the main park, with Goat Island, Luna Island, walkways and the Niagara Scenic Trolley. Whirlpool State Park, Devil's Hole State Park and the Niagara Gorge have hiking trails down to the river. De Veaux Woods State Park, Artpark in Lewiston and Fort Niagara State Park in Youngstown offer more trails, concerts and views of Lake Ontario.

Seasons and Weather

Niagara Falls has a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. Summer highs are in the 70s and low 80s °F, and the mist from the falls is refreshing, so July and August are the busiest months. Fall brings fewer crowds and colorful foliage in the gorge. Winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s °F and lake-effect snow, and the falls partially freeze, making ice formations along the rocks. Some attractions, including the Maid of the Mist and the Cave of the Winds, operate seasonally.
