Overview

The Hamptons are the villages and hamlets of the South Fork of Long Island’s East End, in the towns of Southampton and East Hampton. It is a seaside resort and one of the historic summer colonies of the northeastern U.S., known for beaches, estates, farm stands, and celebrity-filled summers.

The Hamptons stretch from Westhampton Beach, Quogue, and Hampton Bays through Southampton and East Hampton to Montauk. Visitors come for wide Atlantic beaches, shopping and dining, art galleries, and surfing and sailing.

Geography

The South Fork is a narrow peninsula between the Atlantic Ocean on the south and Peconic and Gardiners bays on the north. Long white-sand beaches line the ocean side, while the bay side has calm harbors, creeks, and wetlands. The land is mostly flat farmland and pine and oak woodland, with hills near Montauk and a famed golf-course landscape at Shinnecock Hills. The island is subject to coastal storms and erosion.

History

The Shinnecock and Montaukett peoples lived on the South Fork before English settlers founded Southampton in 1640 and East Hampton in 1648. Sag Harbor was a major whaling port in the early 1800s. The arrival of the Long Island Rail Road in the 1870s brought summer visitors, and artists, including William Merritt Chase and later Jackson Pollock and Lee Krasner, made the area an art colony. Wealthy New Yorkers built grand estates in the Gilded Age, and the Hamptons became a fashionable getaway in the 20th century.

Cities and Towns

Southampton is a historic village known for its beaches and mansions, and East Hampton is known for its village green, windmills, and Main Beach. Sag Harbor is a former whaling port with a charming waterfront, and Amagansett is known for its beaches and farm stands. Montauk sits at the end of the island and is known for surfing, fishing, and the Montauk Point Lighthouse.

National Protected Areas

There are no national park units in the Hamptons themselves, but the Amagansett National Wildlife Refuge and the Elizabeth A. Morton National Wildlife Refuge near Sag Harbor protect beach, wetlands, and bird habitat. Fire Island National Seashore lies to the west and can be reached from the western Hamptons.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Montauk Point State Park is home to the historic Montauk Lighthouse, and Hither Hills State Park has a beach, dunes, and a campground. Camp Hero State Park has hiking trails and a former Cold War radar base, and Shadmoor State Park offers bluff trails and ocean views. Historic sites include the Pollock-Krasner House and Study Center in Springs, the Sag Harbor Whaling and Historical Museum, and Mulford Farm in East Hampton.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

Summer is the high season, from Memorial Day to Labor Day, with crowded beaches, heavy traffic on weekends, and the Hampton Classic Horse Show in late August. September is often the best month, with warm water and fewer visitors. Fall offers pumpkin patches, vineyards, and surf fishing, and winter is quiet, with many businesses closed, though Montauk is popular for seal watching and storm watching. Spring brings blooming gardens and the start of the sailing season.
