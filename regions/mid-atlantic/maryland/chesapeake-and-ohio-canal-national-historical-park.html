How to Get There

From Frederick, MD (25 mi to Point of Rocks): Take US-15 S to MD-28 W, then follow signs to the C&O Canal access point at Point of Rocks or other nearby trailheads.

Overview

Chesapeake and Ohio Canal National Historical Park preserves the 184.5-mile canal and towpath from Georgetown in Washington, D.C., to Cumberland, Maryland, built from 1828 to 1850 to move coal, lumber and farm goods along the Potomac. The towpath is now a nearly level multi-use trail for hiking, cycling and paddling, past historic locks, lockhouses, aqueducts and culverts. Highlights include the Great Falls of the Potomac, the Monocacy Aqueduct, the 3,118-foot Paw Paw Tunnel and Harpers Ferry.

Access

The canal can be reached from dozens of access points between Georgetown and Cumberland. Major visitor areas include Great Falls Tavern at 11710 MacArthur Boulevard in Potomac, Williamsport, Hancock, Paw Paw and Cumberland. Visitors drive on I-495, I-270, I-70 and I-68. Parking is free at most places, and entrance fees apply at Great Falls. The Great Allegheny Passage meets the canal at Cumberland.

Seasons

The towpath is open all year, from dawn to dusk. Spring and fall bring mild temperatures, wildflowers and color. Summer is hot and humid, with mosquitoes, and flooding can close sections after heavy rain. Winter is cold, and ice and snow can affect the towpath. Great Falls is busiest on weekends.

Things to Do

Walk or Bike the Towpath

A level surface follows the Potomac for 184.5 miles, ideal for day rides or multi-day bike tours.

See Great Falls

The Potomac drops through Mather Gorge, with overlooks.

Walk Through Paw Paw Tunnel

A 3,118-foot tunnel is one of the canal's engineering marvels.

Take a Mule-Drawn Boat Ride

Seasonal canal boat rides at Great Falls and Georgetown show how the canal worked.

Paddle and Fish the River

The river and watered sections of the canal are good for boating and fishing.

Top Trails

C&O Canal Towpath

Length: 184.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

The whole canal for day rides or multi-day bike tours, with camping at hiker-biker sites.

Great Falls Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Overlooks of the falls and Mather Gorge, starting at Great Falls Tavern.

Paw Paw Tunnel Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A walk through the historic tunnel and over the Tunnel Hill Trail.

Billy Goat Trail (Section A)

Length: Approximately 4.7 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

Rock scrambling and river views near Great Falls.

Visitor Centers

The Great Falls Tavern Visitor Center has exhibits, a bookstore, canal boat tickets and maps. Other centers include the Williamsport Visitor Center, the Hancock Visitor Center and the Cumberland Visitor Center, each with exhibits and information on the canal. Rangers lead walks and boat rides, and a few centers are open seasonally.

Camping and Lodging

The park has free hiker-biker campsites roughly every 5 miles along the towpath, plus drive-in campgrounds at Marsden Tract, Antietam Creek and Fifteen Mile Creek, and group campsites. Hotels and inns are in Georgetown, Potomac, Frederick, Hagerstown, Hancock and Cumberland. Harpers Ferry and Shepherdstown are also on the route.
