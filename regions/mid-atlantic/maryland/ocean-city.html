Ocean City

★5.0

Major Atlantic beach resort area along the East Coast of the US featuring the Ocean City Boardwalk, a historical landmark consistently rated as one of the "best boardwalks for food"

General Information

Hours:
Ocean City beaches are generally open daily from 5:00am to midnight.
The iconic Boardwalk is open year-round, with businesses and attractions operating on seasonal schedules.
Fees:
Ocean City's beaches are free to access.
No beach tags or admission fees are required.
Pet Policy:
Pets are permitted on the beach and boardwalk only from October 1 through April 30 and must remain leashed. Pets are not allowed on the beach or boardwalk from May 1 through September 30.
Seasons:
All year (summer for beaches, boardwalk attractions, water sports, and festivals; fall for quieter beaches and fishing; winter for holiday events; spring for outdoor recreation and sightseeing).
Location:
Ocean City, MD 21842
Website:
oceancitymd.gov

How to Get There

From Salisbury Regional Airport (approximately 30 mi): Take US-50 East directly into Ocean City. The route ends near the downtown area, Boardwalk, beaches, and inlet district.

Overview

Ocean City is Maryland's only Atlantic Ocean resort, a long, narrow barrier-island city in Worcester County with about 7,000 year-round residents and more than 300,000 people on a busy summer weekend. It is famous for its 2.5-mile wooden Boardwalk, its wide beach, its amusement rides and its fishing, especially the White Marlin Open, one of the biggest billfish tournaments in the world. It is the closest big ocean beach for millions of people in Baltimore, Washington, D.C., Pennsylvania and Virginia.

People come to Ocean City to swim and sunbathe, walk the Boardwalk, eat Thrasher's french fries and Fisher's popcorn, ride the amusement rides, fish and go out to the bars and nightclubs. Families, college graduates and fishing enthusiasts all find something to do, and the town offers miniature golf, water parks, charter fishing and a long list of restaurants. Assateague Island, with its wild ponies, is a short drive south.

History

Native peoples fished and hunted on the barrier island long before the first English settlers. The resort began in 1869 when Isaac Coffin opened a boarding house for visitors, the Atlantic Hotel followed in 1875, and a railroad arrived later in the 1870s. Ocean City was incorporated in 1880, and its first boardwalk was built in 1902. A hurricane on August 23, 1933 cut a channel through the island, creating the Ocean City Inlet, which became the harbor for fishing boats. The Chesapeake Bay Bridge opened in 1952, bringing a boom in visitors from the Washington and Baltimore areas, and the Ash Wednesday storm in 1962 destroyed much of the Boardwalk, which was rebuilt. In later decades high-rise hotels and condominiums transformed the skyline.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Boardwalk runs from the Ocean City Inlet to 27th Street, with food stands, shops, arcades and the Ocean City Pier. Trimper's Rides, a family-run amusement park with an antique carousel, and the Jolly Roger park at the Pier have rides and a water park. At the south end, the inlet area has fishing boats, the Ocean City Life-Saving Station Museum and the harbor. Midtown has the bay side's restaurants and the famous Seacrets bar, and to the north, the quieter beachfront condominiums lead to the Delaware line.

Culture and Museums

The Ocean City Life-Saving Station Museum, located in a restored 1891 building at the inlet, tells the story of the beach, the lifesavers and the shipwrecks along the coast with maritime exhibits. The Ocean City Center for the Arts hosts exhibits and classes, and summer events include fireworks, concerts on the beach, the Ocean City Air Show in June, SunFest in September and the Winterfest of Lights, a holiday light display at Northside Park, in November through January. Seafood is a big part of the local culture, with steamed crabs, crab cakes and fresh oysters at waterfront restaurants.

Parks

The beach itself is the main park, with lifeguards from late spring to early fall. Northside Park at 125th Street has playing fields, a pond, a playground and a walking path, and Sunset Park on the bay is a quiet spot for watching sunsets. Assateague Island National Seashore and Assateague State Park, about 10 miles south, have wild horses, uncrowded beaches, camping and kayaking. Bay-side boating, kayaking and fishing are popular, and Ocean City has many charter boats for offshore fishing.

Seasons and Weather

Ocean City has a humid subtropical climate moderated by the ocean. Summers are warm to hot, with highs in the 80s °F and ocean temperatures in the 70s °F in July and August, and the high season runs from Memorial Day to Labor Day. September is warm and less crowded, and many visitors consider it one of the best months. Spring and fall are mild, and winters are cold and quiet, with highs in the 40s °F, and many restaurants and hotels are closed or have limited hours. Hurricanes and nor'easters can cause flooding and beach erosion.
