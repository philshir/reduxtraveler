Overview

Cape May is a Victorian seaside resort at the southern tip of New Jersey, where Delaware Bay meets the Atlantic Ocean. It is one of the oldest vacation destinations in the United States, and the whole city is a National Historic Landmark district, famous for more than 600 colorful gingerbread-style Victorian houses, inns and B&Bs, the second-largest concentration in the country after San Francisco. It is also known for its beaches, its lighthouse and some of the best bird watching in North America.

People come to Cape May to stay in historic inns, stroll the Washington Street Mall, tour Victorian houses, relax on the beach, hunt for "Cape May diamonds" (quartz pebbles), watch migrating birds and hawks, and ride the ferry to Delaware. Its mild position, cool breezes and walkable streets make it a favorite romantic weekend trip from Philadelphia, New York and Washington.

History

Dutch explorer Cornelius Jacobsen Mey sailed here in 1620, and the cape is named for him. English whalers settled in the 1630s, and by the 1760s Cape May was known as a seaside resort, with visitors arriving by boat from Philadelphia. In the 19th century Presidents including Franklin Pierce and James Buchanan stayed here, and President Benjamin Harrison used Congress Hall as a summer White House in 1890. The Great Fire of November 9, 1878 destroyed much of the downtown, and the city was rebuilt quickly in the Victorian style that gives it its look today. Cape May became a National Historic Landmark district in 1976, and the Coast Guard's only enlisted training center is located here.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Cape May Lighthouse, built in 1859 and 157 feet tall, can be climbed for views of the cape, the bay and the ocean, and it stands in Cape May Point State Park. The pedestrian Washington Street Mall is the main shopping and dining street, and the Emlen Physick Estate, an 1879 mansion, is a showpiece of Victorian architecture and the base for the Mid-Atlantic Center for the Arts. Congress Hall, a grand hotel on a site that has had a hotel since 1816, and the Chalfonte Hotel are historic landmarks, and the Cape May–Lewes Ferry connects to Lewes, Delaware. At Sunset Beach, visitors watch the nightly flag-lowering ceremony and see the remains of the SS Atlantus, a concrete ship that ran aground in 1926.

Culture and Museums

The Mid-Atlantic Center for the Arts & Humanities runs trolley tours, house tours, ghost walks and a Victorian Weekend each October from the Emlen Physick Estate. The Cape May Lighthouse and the Cape May Bird Observatory educate visitors about local history and migrating birds. Cape May has a long tradition of live music, theater and festivals, including the Cape May Music Festival, the Cape May Film Festival and the Christmas candlelight house tours. Seafood restaurants serve local scallops, clams and fish.

Parks

Cape May Point State Park has a beach, a lighthouse, a bird observation platform and walking trails through dunes and freshwater ponds. Higbee Beach Wildlife Management Area on Delaware Bay is a premier spot for migrating birds, and the Cape May National Wildlife Refuge and the Nature Conservancy's Cape May Migratory Bird Refuge protect marshes and forests along the coast. The city's own beaches, including the beach along Beach Avenue, have lifeguards in summer, and the wide beach at Sunset Beach is known for sunsets and "Cape May diamonds."

Seasons and Weather

Cape May has a humid subtropical climate moderated by the ocean and bay. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s °F and cooling breezes, and the beaches and promenade are busiest from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Fall is the best season for bird and hawk migration from September to November and for Victorian Weekend, while spring has mild weather and blooming gardens. Winters are quiet, with highs in the 40s °F, and some businesses close for the season. Nor'easters can cause beach erosion and flooding.
