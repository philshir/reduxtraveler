Overview

New Jersey is a Mid-Atlantic state of about 9.3 million people and the most densely populated in the country, with a surprising variety of beaches, farmland, forests, and history. Trenton is the capital, Newark is the largest city, and the "Garden State" is nicknamed for its farms and gardens. It is named for the Channel Island of Jersey.

Visitors come for the Jersey Shore, with the boardwalks of Atlantic City and Seaside Heights and the Victorian charm of Cape May. Others come for the skyline views and parks along the Hudson River waterfront, Princeton University, the Delaware Water Gap, the riverfront town of Lambertville, and the Pine Barrens.

Geography

New Jersey is a peninsula between the Hudson River, Delaware River, and Atlantic Ocean. The northwest has the Appalachian Ridge-and-Valley and the Highlands, with High Point, at 1,803 feet (550 m), the state's highest point. The central Piedmont is rolling and heavily populated, and the Coastal Plain covers the south, including the Pine Barrens, a vast forest over a huge aquifer. The state has 130 miles of Atlantic coast with barrier islands and beaches.

History

The Lenape lived in the region when Henry Hudson explored it in 1609, and the Dutch and Swedes established early settlements before the English took control in 1664. New Jersey became the third state in 1787, the first to ratify the Bill of Rights, and about a quarter of Revolutionary War battles were fought here, including Trenton, Princeton, and Monmouth. Thomas Edison built his laboratory in Menlo Park and West Orange, and the state became an industrial and transportation hub with major ports and railroads.

National Protected Areas

New Jersey's national park sites include the Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area, with waterfalls and river recreation, and Morristown National Historical Park, where Washington's army wintered. Thomas Edison National Historical Park preserves the inventor's laboratory, and Paterson Great Falls National Historical Park protects an early industrial city. Gateway National Recreation Area at Sandy Hook, Pinelands National Reserve, and the Appalachian Trail also include New Jersey sites.

State Parks and Historic Sites

New Jersey has more than 40 state parks and forests. Liberty State Park has views of the Statue of Liberty and ferry access to Ellis Island, and High Point has a monument at the top of the state. Princeton Battlefield marks a key 1777 victory, Millbrook Village is a recreated 19th-century town, and Island Beach State Park protects a barrier beach. Other highlights are the Weehawken Dueling Grounds and the Frelinghuysen Arboretum.

Seasons and When to Visit

Summer is the peak season for the Jersey Shore, from Memorial Day to Labor Day, with warm water, boardwalks, and festivals. Fall is pleasant and quieter, with fall foliage in the northwest and farm stands across the state. Spring has blooming gardens and shad runs on the Delaware, and winter is cold, though beach towns and the Pine Barrens are peaceful. Hurricanes and nor'easters can affect the coast.
