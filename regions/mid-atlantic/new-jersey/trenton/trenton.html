Overview

Trenton is the capital of New Jersey, a city of about 90,000 people on the Delaware River between New York City and Philadelphia. It is famous for the Battle of Trenton on December 26, 1776, when George Washington crossed the Delaware River on Christmas night and surprised the Hessian garrison, a turning point in the Revolutionary War. It is also known for its industrial past, symbolized by the "Trenton Makes, The World Takes" bridge sign, and for the New Jersey State House.

People come to Trenton to see its Revolutionary War sites, tour the State House, visit the state museum and nearby sculpture garden, and walk the Delaware River's historic crossing points. The city is on Amtrak and NJ Transit lines, which makes it an easy day trip from either New York or Philadelphia.

History

Trenton was settled by Quakers in the 1670s and was named after William Trent, a Philadelphia merchant who bought land here in 1714 and built a house still standing today. The Battle of Trenton in 1776, followed by the Second Battle of Trenton on January 2, 1777, and the Battle of Princeton, helped restore American morale. The Continental Congress met in Trenton for a few weeks in 1784 and 1785, and Trenton became New Jersey's permanent capital in 1790. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Trenton was an industrial powerhouse known for pottery, steel wire rope (the Roebling company made the cables for the Brooklyn Bridge) and rubber. Manufacturing declined after World War II, and the city has worked to revitalize its downtown and riverfront.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The New Jersey State House, built in 1792 and later expanded, is one of the oldest state capitols in continuous use, with a gold-leaf dome. The Trenton Battle Monument, a 148-foot column from 1893 topped by a statue of Washington, marks where his artillery opened fire on the Hessians. The Old Barracks Museum, built in 1758, is the only surviving barracks from the French and Indian War and housed the Hessians in 1776. The William Trent House, from 1719, is one of the oldest buildings in the city. The Washington Crossing Historic Park in Pennsylvania and Washington Crossing State Park in New Jersey are upriver, and the Lower Trenton Bridge carries the famous "Trenton Makes" sign.

Culture and Museums

The New Jersey State Museum has exhibits on archaeology, cultural history, fine art and natural science, along with a planetarium. Grounds For Sculpture, in nearby Hamilton, is a 42-acre sculpture park with hundreds of contemporary works. The Trenton Punk Rock Flea Market is a popular music and vintage market, and the Trenton War Memorial and Patriots Theater hosts concerts and shows. Trenton is also known for local favorites such as tomato pie, a thin pizza with the sauce on top.

Parks

Cadwalader Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, is the city's main park, with walking paths and the Trenton City Museum in Ellarslie Mansion. Mill Hill Park downtown is a small park near the Assunpink Creek, and the Delaware and Raritan Canal towpath and the Delaware River waterfront offer paths for walking and biking. Washington Crossing State Park on the New Jersey side has trails, a nature center and the spot where Washington's army landed.

Seasons and Weather

Trenton has a humid subtropical climate with four seasons. Summers are hot and humid, with highs in the upper 80s °F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and occasional snow or ice. Spring and fall are the best seasons for walking historic sites and parks. Each Christmas Day, reenactors recreate Washington's crossing of the Delaware at Washington Crossing Historic Park in Pennsylvania, and the river can flood after heavy rain or snowmelt.
