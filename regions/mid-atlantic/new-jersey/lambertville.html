How to Get There

From Trenton, NJ (14 mi): Take NJ-29 North along the Delaware River for a scenic 25-minute drive directly into Lambertville’s historic downtown.

Overview

Lambertville is a small historic city on the Delaware River in Hunterdon County, New Jersey, directly across the river from New Hope, Pennsylvania. It is known as the "Antiques Capital of New Jersey" and is famous for its Victorian and Federal houses, cobblestone streets, antique shops, art galleries and a lively dining scene. A bridge with pedestrian walkways connects it to New Hope, and the two towns are often visited together.

People come to Lambertville to browse the antique stores and the Golden Nugget Antique Flea Market, walk or bike the towpath of the Delaware and Raritan Canal, eat at riverfront restaurants, attend the Shad Festival in April and enjoy a small-town atmosphere an hour or less from Philadelphia and New York.

History

The area was originally a Lenape homeland and then a ferry crossing known as Coryell's Ferry, which was used by George Washington's army during the Revolutionary War, and Washington crossed the Delaware just a few miles downstream in December 1776. The town was renamed Lambertville in 1814 for John Lambert, a local landowner and later a U.S. Senator who arranged for a post office. In the 19th century the Delaware and Raritan Canal and the railroad brought industry, including rubber, paper and iron, and the shad fishery on the river was important. The mills declined in the 20th century, and from the 1970s artists and antique dealers revived the old buildings.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The main sights are Lambertville's historic streets, lined with brick townhouses, antique shops and galleries, particularly along Bridge and Union streets. The Delaware and Raritan Canal towpath runs along the river for biking and walking, and the bridge to New Hope is only a short stroll. The Golden Nugget Antique Flea Market, held on Wednesdays, Saturdays and Sundays, is a favorite of antique hunters, and Lambertville Station, a restaurant in a restored railroad station, is a local landmark. Across the river, Washington Crossing Historic Park and Bowman's Hill Wildflower Preserve are a short drive south.

Culture and Museums

The Marshall House, home of the Lambertville Historical Society, is a restored 1816 house that shows the life of the town's early families. Lambertville's galleries and studios, and the many small theaters and music venues across the river in New Hope, make the area an art destination. The Shad Festival in April celebrates the return of shad fish to the river with food, crafts and music, and the town also has farmers markets and holiday events. Dining ranges from casual cafes to fine restaurants serving local ingredients.

Parks

The Delaware and Raritan Canal State Park follows the old canal through town, with a flat towpath for walking and biking and connections to the Delaware River. Bull's Island Recreation Area, a few miles north, has camping, fishing, canoeing and a pedestrian bridge to Pennsylvania. On the Pennsylvania side, the Delaware Canal State Park offers a parallel towpath, and the Delaware River is popular for tubing, kayaking and fishing.

Seasons and Weather

Lambertville has a humid continental to humid subtropical climate with four seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the 80s °F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s and 40s °F and occasional snow. Fall is a popular season for foliage and the flea market, and spring brings the Shad Festival. The river can flood after heavy rain, and low-lying streets and the towpath may close.
