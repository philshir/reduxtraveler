Overview

Wilmington is the largest city in Delaware, with about 70,000 residents, on the Christina and Delaware rivers about 30 miles southwest of Philadelphia. It is famous for the du Pont family, whose gunpowder mills along the Brandywine River grew into one of the world's great chemical companies, and for being the corporate capital of the United States, since more than half of all Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in Delaware.

Visitors come to Wilmington for the Brandywine Valley, an area of du Pont estates, gardens and art museums that is one of the East Coast's best day trips. The revitalized riverfront has walkways, theaters, breweries and restaurants, and the historic downtown and the Delaware Art Museum are popular rainy-day stops. Because of its location on the Northeast Corridor, with Amtrak and I-95, Wilmington is also an easy stop between Philadelphia, Baltimore and Washington.

History

Swedish settlers led by Peter Minuit landed at The Rocks on the Christina River in 1638 and built Fort Christina, the first permanent European settlement in the Delaware Valley and the beginning of the colony of New Sweden. The Dutch took over in 1655 and the English in 1664, and the town laid out in the 1730s was named Wilmington in 1739 after the Earl of Wilmington. In 1802 a French immigrant, Eleuthère Irénée du Pont, built a gunpowder mill on the Brandywine, and the du Pont company supplied American wars and later developed nylon, Teflon and many other products. Wilmington grew as a center for shipbuilding, railroad car building and leather, and in the late 20th century it became a banking center after Delaware passed laws favorable to credit card banks and corporations.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Wilmington's best-known sights are the du Pont estates just outside downtown. Hagley Museum stands on the site of the original powder mills along the Brandywine and includes restored mills, workers' houses and the first du Pont family home, Eleutherian Mills. Nemours Estate, built by Alfred I. du Pont in 1910, is a 77-room mansion in the style of Louis XVI with one of the largest formal French-style gardens in North America, and Winterthur nearby is a former du Pont country estate with a huge collection of American decorative arts and a naturalistic garden on a 1,000-acre estate. Downtown, the Wilmington Riverwalk follows the Christina River, and Fort Christina Park marks the 1638 landing site and is the home port of the Kalmar Nyckel, a replica of the original Swedish ship. Old Swedes Church, built in 1699, is one of the oldest churches in the United States still in use.

Culture and Museums

The Delaware Art Museum has one of the largest collections of Pre-Raphaelite art outside the United Kingdom and a large collection of work by illustrator Howard Pyle and his students, including N.C. Wyeth. The Delaware Museum of Nature and Science is a family-friendly museum with dinosaurs and a large seashell collection. The Grand Opera House, a restored 1871 theater, hosts concerts and plays, and the DuPont Theatre and the Playhouse on Rodney Square stage touring Broadway shows. The Brandywine Valley is also the home of the Brandywine River Museum of Art, just across the state line in Pennsylvania, which displays three generations of Wyeth family painters.

Parks

Brandywine Park, along the Brandywine Creek, is Wilmington's grandest city park, with tree-lined paths, stone bridges, cherry blossoms in spring, and the Brandywine Zoo. Rockwood Park and Museum, a Gothic Revival estate built in 1851, has English-style gardens and trails. The riverfront and Riverwalk have bike and walking paths, and Fort Christina Park is a quiet green spot on the Christina River. Within easy driving distance, Longwood Gardens in Pennsylvania and Delaware's state parks such as Bellevue State Park and Alapocas Run State Park give more room to hike and picnic.

Sports

Wilmington's professional team is the Wilmington Blue Rocks, a High-A minor league baseball team affiliated with the Washington Nationals, who play at Frawley Stadium on the Riverfront. College sports are centered nearby at the University of Delaware in Newark, home of the Fightin' Blue Hens, and Wilmington sits only about 30 miles from Philadelphia, so many residents follow the Phillies, Eagles, 76ers and Flyers.

Seasons and Weather

Wilmington has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons. Summers are warm and humid, with highs in the upper 80s °F, and winters are cool to cold, with highs in the 40s °F and occasional snow. Spring and fall are the best times to visit, with blooming gardens at Winterthur and Nemours in April and May and colorful foliage in the Brandywine Valley in October. Late summer and fall may bring tropical storms, and nor'easters can bring heavy rain or snow in winter.
