First State National Historical Park

★5.0

Park covering the early colonial history of Delaware and the role Delaware played in the establishment of the nation, leading up to it being the first state to ratify the U.S. Constitution.

How to Get There

From Wilmington, DE (in city): The main Wilmington unit (Old Swedes Church) is at 606 Church St in downtown Wilmington; take I-95 to Exit 6 (Route 141/Newport) and follow signs into the city.

Overview

First State National Historical Park is a National Park Service unit that lies mostly in Delaware but extends into Chadds Ford, Pennsylvania. It was created as First State National Monument in 2013 and redesignated a national historical park by Congress. The park tells the story of the Dutch, Swedes, Finns and English who settled the Delaware Valley and their relationship with Native Americans, and it follows Delaware's path to becoming the first state to ratify the U.S. Constitution. Sites include the New Castle Court House, the Green, the Sheriff's House, Fort Christina, Old Swedes Church, the Ryves Holt House, Dover Green, Woodlawn and the Brandywine River Valley.

Access

The park is made up of several sites. The visitor center is at 211 Delaware Avenue in Wilmington, and the other sites are in New Castle, Dover, Lewes and the Brandywine Valley. Visitors drive on I-95, US-13 and DE-1, and parking is free at most sites. Amtrak and SEPTA trains serve Wilmington, and DART buses connect the downtowns. Admission is free.

Seasons

The park is open all year, but hours vary by site and most are open Thursday through Saturday or by tour. Spring and fall are the best seasons for walking the greens and grounds. Summers are hot and humid. Winters are cold with occasional snow. Special events, including Return Day and Dover Days, add to the calendar.

Things to Do

Tour the New Castle Court House

One of the oldest courthouses in the country is the center of the 12-mile circle that forms the Delaware boundary.

Walk the New Castle Green

The historic green is surrounded by colonial buildings, including the Sheriff's House.

Visit Old Swedes Church and Fort Christina

Wilmington's Swedish colonial heritage is preserved at the 1699 church and the site of New Sweden.

See Dover Green and the Ryves Holt House

Dover and Lewes sites tell about the early state government and colonial life.

Explore the Brandywine Valley

Woodlawn, a preserved landscape, protects open space and views in Pennsylvania and Delaware.

Top Trails

Woodlawn Trails

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 150 feet
Difficulty: Easy

Loops through fields and woods in the Brandywine Valley with views of the river and historic farms.

New Castle Historic Walk

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk through the cobblestone streets and historic squares of New Castle with signs about each stop.

Visitor Centers

The park's visitor center in Wilmington has exhibits, a film and staff who give out maps and tour information. Smaller contact stations at the New Castle Court House and on Dover Green share information about each site. Rangers lead tours at set times, and the staff can plan an itinerary across the sites.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Camping is available at Brandywine Creek State Park campgrounds nearby (cabins only) and at Delaware Seashore State Park to the south. Hotels, inns and bed-and-breakfasts are in Wilmington, New Castle, Dover and Lewes, and Philadelphia is about 30 minutes north of Wilmington.
