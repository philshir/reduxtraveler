How to Get There

From Wrangell Airport (3 mi): Take Airport Rd into town or use local shuttle/taxi services to reach the downtown harbor area.

Overview

Wrangell is a small, remote town on Wrangell Island in Southeast Alaska, at the mouth of the Stikine River, and it is the only city in Alaska to have been governed under four nations: the Tlingit, Russia, Britain, and the United States. The town has a quiet, authentic character, with a fishing and timber economy, few cruise ships, and a rich Native heritage that includes ancient petroglyphs. It is the gateway to the Stikine River, the fastest free-flowing navigable river in North America, and to the Anan bear observatory.

Visitors come to Wrangell for Petroglyph Beach State Historic Park, for the Chief Shakes Tribal House, and for jet boat tours up the Stikine River. They also come for the LeConte Glacier, for bear viewing at Anan Creek, and for fishing and hiking.

History

The Tlingit Stikine people lived at the mouth of the river for thousands of years, and Chief Shakes was a powerful leader of the Stikine. In 1834, the Russian-American Company built Redoubt St. Dionysius on the site, and in 1840 the British Hudson's Bay Company leased it, renaming it Fort Stikine, and the United States took over in 1867 after the Alaska Purchase. Wrangell was a base for gold rushes up the Stikine River in the 1860s, 1870s, and 1890s, a rowdy supply town. It later became a center for fishing and timber, and today is known for its small-town atmosphere.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Petroglyph Beach has more than 40 ancient rock carvings of spirals, faces, and animals, which visitors can see on boulders at low tide, and the Chief Shakes Tribal House on a small island in the harbor is a replica of the clan house of the Stikine leader, with totem poles. The Wrangell Museum has exhibits on the town's history, and Fort Wrangell, the site of the Russian redoubt, is now a memorial. The Stikine River delta, with eagles and seals, is a short boat ride from town.

Culture and Museums

The Wrangell Museum has Native artifacts, gold rush items, and a collection about the Stikine River, and the Tlingit culture is celebrated at the Chief Shakes Tribal House and the Kiksadi Totem Park. The town hosts the Garnet Festival and the Fourth of July celebration with a logging show, and the Stikine River Birding Festival in the spring. Garnet Ledge, east of town, is one of only two places in the world where garnets are mined for the public.

Parks

The Stikine River, which flows from British Columbia, offers jet boat tours into the Stikine-LeConte Wilderness, with hot springs and views of glaciers. LeConte Glacier, the southernmost tidewater glacier in the Northern Hemisphere, can be reached by boat, and Anan Wildlife Observatory, a 30-minute boat ride, is one of the best places to watch black and brown bears catching salmon in July and August. Rainbow Falls trail is a short hike, and Mount Dewey has a trail with views of the town.

Seasons and Weather

Wrangell has a temperate rainforest climate. Summers are mild, with highs in the 60s°F, and winters are cool, with highs in the 30s and 40s°F and about 60 inches of snow, and rain falls about 80 inches a year. The best time to visit is from May to September, and bear viewing is best in July and August.
