Skagway Historic District and White Pass and Yukon Route

★5.0

Preserved collection of wooden buildings and historic streets paired with a scenic railway route climbing through rugged mountains, showcasing the dramatic journey of gold rush travelers.

How to Get There

From Juneau (90 mi): Take a ferry or short flight to Skagway, then walk from the harbor into downtown along Broadway Street where the historic district and railway depot are located.

Overview

Skagway Historic District and White Pass preserve the gold rush town of Skagway and the route that took thousands of prospectors toward the Klondike in 1898. The National Historic Landmark district has a 23-block downtown of wooden boardwalks and false-front buildings, and it is part of Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park. The narrow-gauge White Pass and Yukon Route Railroad, built in 1898 to 1900, climbs to White Pass at 2,864 feet and gives sweeping mountain views. People come to walk the boardwalks, ride the train, join ranger walks and hike toward the Chilkoot Trail.

Access

Skagway is at the head of the Lynn Canal in Southeast Alaska, reached by cruise ship, Alaska Marine Highway ferry and small plane. It is also connected by the Klondike Highway to Whitehorse in the Yukon, about 110 miles away, which is one of the few roads out of Southeast Alaska. The downtown historic district is compact and easy to walk, and the White Pass Railroad depot is near the cruise ship docks.

Seasons

The best season is May through September, when the railroad runs and the National Park Service visitor center operates. Cruise ship days are crowded in summer, so early morning or evening strolls are quieter. Spring and fall bring fewer visitors and many shops close, and winter is cold and quiet, with fewer services. Weather at White Pass can be cold and rainy, so bring layers.

Things to Do

Walk Broadway and the Historic Buildings

Wooden boardwalks pass the Arctic Brotherhood Hall with its driftwood front, saloons and shops from the 1898 gold rush.

Ride the White Pass and Yukon Route Railroad

The narrow-gauge train climbs to White Pass on a 3-hour round trip with spectacular scenery.

Visit the Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park

The park visitor center offers exhibits, a film and ranger-led walks about the gold rush.

Hike the Chilkoot or Dewey Lakes Trails

Trails around Skagway lead to lakes, waterfalls and the start of the Chilkoot Trail in nearby Dyea.

Drive the Klondike Highway

The road to Yukon passes alpine scenery and the White Pass summit.

Top Trails

Skagway Historic Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk along Broadway and through the historic district passes the main gold rush buildings, with ranger-led tours in summer.

Lower Dewey Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 700 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This forest trail climbs to a mountain lake above town with views of Skagway and the Lynn Canal.

Gold Rush Cemetery and Lower Reid Falls

Length: Approximately 1.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk leads to a historic cemetery and a waterfall in the forest.

Visitor Centers

The Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park Visitor Center in the old White Pass and Yukon Route depot has exhibits, a film and a bookstore, with rangers offering walks and information. The Skagway Visitor Center on Broadway provides maps and tourist information. Both are open mainly from May to September.

Camping and Lodging

Skagway has hotels, inns and bed-and-breakfasts, such as the Historic Skagway Inn and other downtown properties, but they fill quickly in summer. The National Park Service's Dyea Campground is near the Chilkoot Trail, and private RV parks and campgrounds are in town. Larger lodging options are in Haines, reached by a short ferry, and Juneau and Whitehorse, Yukon.
