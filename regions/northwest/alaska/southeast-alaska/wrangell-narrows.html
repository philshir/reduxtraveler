How to Get There

From Petersburg (13 mi): Travel south by boat through the marked Inside Passage route, following navigational aids and buoys into the winding channel that forms the narrows.

Overview

The Wrangell Narrows is a striking marine passage carved between islands in Southeast Alaska, stretching roughly 22 miles as it winds its way through sheltered coastal waters. Known for its tight turns and ever-shifting tidal conditions, the channel is carefully marked by dozens of navigational aids that guide vessels along its intricate path. Surrounded by dense forests, rocky shores, and quiet inlets, it provides a scenic yet technical route for ferries, fishing boats, and smaller vessels navigating the Inside Passage, while larger ships typically avoid its narrow depths.

Beyond its navigational significance, the area offers a glimpse into a landscape shaped by glacial forces and maritime tradition. The channel connects key waterways and communities, supporting regional transportation and commerce while showcasing dramatic natural beauty. Wildlife is commonly spotted along its edges, from seabirds to marine mammals, making the journey as visually rewarding as it is challenging. For travelers and mariners alike, this channel represents both a practical corridor and a unique experience woven into the broader coastal ecosystem.

Things to Do

Navigate One of Alaska's Most Scenic Waterways

Wrangell Narrows is a narrow, winding channel that separates Mitkof Island from Kupreanof Island in Southeast Alaska. Stretching between Petersburg and Frederick Sound, the waterway is often referred to as "Christmas Tree Lane" because of its numerous navigational markers. The Narrows serves as an important marine route while offering visitors spectacular scenery, abundant wildlife, and excellent recreational opportunities.

Take a Scenic Boat Cruise

Boat tours and private vessels provide the best way to experience Wrangell Narrows. The channel winds through forested shorelines, tidal flats, small islands, and mountain-backed landscapes, creating constantly changing views throughout the journey. Visitors can observe commercial fishing boats, pleasure craft, and marine wildlife while traveling through one of Southeast Alaska's most picturesque waterways.

Enjoy Exceptional Wildlife Viewing

The waters and shorelines of Wrangell Narrows support a rich variety of wildlife. Visitors commonly see bald eagles, harbor seals, sea otters, seabirds, and migrating marine mammals. The surrounding region is also known for opportunities to observe bears, moose, and other wildlife in nearby wilderness areas.

Go Sea Kayaking

Kayaking is one of the most popular ways to explore Wrangell Narrows. The relatively protected waters allow paddlers to travel among coves, shorelines, and islands while enjoying close-up views of wildlife and the surrounding Tongass National Forest landscape. Sea kayakers often encounter seals, sea otters, and bald eagles during their excursions.

Watch for Migratory Birds

The surrounding coastal habitats attract numerous bird species throughout the year. Bald eagles are commonly seen, while waterfowl, shorebirds, and seabirds utilize the tidal flats and shoreline environments that characterize the region.

Explore Nearby Petersburg and Kupreanof Island

Wrangell Narrows serves as the gateway to many nearby attractions, including Petersburg's waterfront, LeConte Glacier excursions, Tongass National Forest recreation areas, and the more remote landscapes of Kupreanof Island.

Top Trails

Raven's Roost Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet
Difficulty: Easy

Located near Petersburg, this short trail climbs to a scenic overlook providing excellent views of Wrangell Narrows, Mitkof Island, the surrounding waterways, and nearby mountain ranges.

Papke's Landing Boardwalk Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This accessible boardwalk route offers visitors a chance to experience Southeast Alaska's temperate rainforest ecosystem while observing native vegetation, birdlife, and wetland habitats near Wrangell Narrows.

Kupreanof Island Shore Trails

Length: Various route options
Elevation Gain: Varies
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Trails on nearby Kupreanof Island provide access to secluded beaches, rainforest environments, wildlife viewing areas, and scenic overlooks of Wrangell Narrows and surrounding waters.
