Misty Fjords National Monument

★5.0

Remote wilderness of towering granite cliffs, deep glacial fjords, cascading waterfalls, and dense coastal rainforest teeming with wildlife and accessible primarily by boat or floatplane.

How to Get There

From Ketchikan (40 mi): Travel by boat cruise or floatplane from the harbor or seaplane base, following coastal waterways east into the fjords where access is limited to water or air routes.

Overview

Misty Fjords National Monument near Ketchikan is a wilderness of steep fjords with granite cliffs up to 3,000 feet high, waterfalls, and rain forest, in the Tongass National Forest. John Muir compared it to Yosemite. Because of its remoteness, most visitors arrive by boat or floatplane from Ketchikan or Juneau, on day tours, or on overnight kayak and charter trips, and it is usually misty.

Things to Do

Explore One of Alaska's Most Spectacular Wilderness Areas

Misty Fjords National Monument encompasses more than two million acres within the Tongass National Forest in Southeast Alaska. Located near Ketchikan, the monument is renowned for its deep fjords, soaring granite cliffs, waterfalls, old-growth rainforest, and remote wilderness character. Carved by massive glaciers thousands of years ago, Misty Fjords is often considered one of the most breathtaking landscapes in the state.

Experience the Fjords by Floatplane

One of the most popular ways to explore Misty Fjords is by floatplane. Scenic flightseeing tours departing from Ketchikan provide aerial views of dramatic cliffs, glacier-carved valleys, alpine lakes, waterfalls, and dense rainforest. Many tours include a lake landing that allows visitors to experience the monument from both the air and ground.

Take a Boat Cruise Through the Fjords

Cruises through Behm Canal and the monument's waterways offer close-up views of Misty Fjords' towering granite walls, waterfalls, and secluded coves. Boat tours provide excellent opportunities to photograph the landscape while exploring areas such as Rudyerd Bay, Walker Cove, and Punchbowl Cove.

Go Sea Kayaking

Kayaking allows visitors to experience Misty Fjords at a slower pace while exploring protected coves, narrow inlets, and remote shorelines. Guided day trips and multi-day expeditions provide opportunities to paddle beneath towering cliffs, observe wildlife, and camp within the wilderness.

Observe Incredible Wildlife

Wildlife viewing is a major attraction throughout the monument. Visitors may encounter black bears, brown bears, mountain goats, wolves, river otters, Sitka black-tailed deer, harbor seals, sea lions, porpoises, and orcas. Bald eagles and numerous seabird species are commonly seen throughout the area.

Stay in Remote Forest Service Cabins

Several public-use cabins are scattered throughout the monument and can be accessed by boat, floatplane, or hiking. These cabins provide unique overnight accommodations for visitors seeking extended wilderness experiences while exploring remote sections of the monument.

Explore Alaska's Largest Rainforest

Misty Fjords lies within the Tongass National Forest, the largest temperate rainforest in North America. Visitors can experience lush stands of Sitka spruce, western hemlock, and cedar while exploring one of the continent's most extensive and intact forest ecosystems.

Camp in Remote Wilderness Settings

Backcountry camping opportunities exist throughout the monument for experienced wilderness travelers. Remote beaches, forested coves, and alpine environments provide unforgettable settings for multi-day adventures surrounded by pristine natural landscapes.

Top Trails

Lunch Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

One of the more accessible trails within the monument, this short hike leads visitors through rainforest scenery to a beautiful waterfall. The route provides an opportunity to experience Misty Fjords' lush environment without requiring a strenuous hike.

Punchbowl Cove Area Trails

Length: Various short routes
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Visitors arriving by boat or kayak can explore shoreline routes around Punchbowl Cove, one of the monument's most scenic locations. The area features dramatic cliffs, waterfalls, rainforest vegetation, and excellent wildlife-viewing opportunities.

Walker Cove Recreation Trails

Length: Variable
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The Walker Cove area offers opportunities for hiking, wildlife viewing, photography, and exploration of Misty Fjords' spectacular coastal rainforest. Visitors can enjoy outstanding scenery while experiencing the monument's remote wilderness character.
