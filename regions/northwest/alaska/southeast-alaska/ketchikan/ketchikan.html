How to Get There

From Ketchikan International Airport (5 mi): Take the airport ferry across Tongass Narrows to downtown, then follow Tongass Ave south into the central waterfront area.

Overview

Ketchikan is the southernmost major city in Alaska, on Revillagigedo Island in the Inside Passage, and it is known as the "Salmon Capital of the World" and "First City" because it is the first stop for northbound ships. It is also known for having the most totem poles in the world, for the colorful boardwalks of Creek Street, and for its rainfall, about 150 inches a year. The city of about 8,000 is built on steep hillsides and over the water and is a major cruise port.

Visitors come to Ketchikan for Creek Street, for totem poles at Totem Bight State Historical Park and the Totem Heritage Center, and for salmon fishing. They also come for flightseeing to Misty Fjords National Monument, for the Southeast Alaska Discovery Center, and for lumberjack shows and bear viewing.

History

The Tlingit people fished at the mouth of Ketchikan Creek, whose name means "thundering wings of an eagle," and a salmon cannery and a sawmill, built in 1885 by Mike Martin, founded the modern town. Gold and copper prospecting in the 1890s brought a boom, and Ketchikan was incorporated in 1900. In the early twentieth century, the town's Creek Street was a notorious red-light district, whose most famous madam was Dolly Arthur. The city became the center of the salmon canning industry, and later timber and pulp, and today it is dominated by tourism and fishing.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Creek Street, a boardwalk of colorful buildings on pilings over Ketchikan Creek, has shops, galleries, and Dolly's House Museum, and in summer salmon crowd the creek below. Totem Bight, a few miles north, has restored totem poles and a clan house, and the Totem Heritage Center has original poles from the 1800s. The Misty Fjords National Monument, reached by floatplane or boat, has granite cliffs and waterfalls, and the Tongass Historical Museum and the Saxman Native Village are other attractions.

Culture and Museums

The Totem Heritage Center has one of the largest collections of unrestored nineteenth-century totem poles, and the Southeast Alaska Discovery Center has exhibits on the Tongass National Forest. The Tongass Historical Museum covers the town's past, and the Totem Heritage Center and Saxman Native Village offer demonstrations of Native carving. The Great Alaskan Lumberjack Show is a popular summer attraction.

Parks

Ketchikan is surrounded by the Tongass National Forest, and trails such as Deer Mountain and Rainbird give views of the city and the channel. Misty Fjords National Monument has more than two million acres of fjords and forests, and Settlers Cove State Recreation Site has a beach and a waterfall. Fishing for salmon and halibut is a major activity, and the area has black and brown bears that can be viewed at Neets Bay and Traitors Cove.

Seasons and Weather

Ketchikan has a temperate rainforest climate and is one of the rainiest cities in the United States. Summers are mild, with highs in the upper 50s and low 60s°F, and winters are mild, with highs in the 40s°F, and snow in the town is light. Rain falls on about 230 days a year, and visitors should always bring waterproof clothing.
