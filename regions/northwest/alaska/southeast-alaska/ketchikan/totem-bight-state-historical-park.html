Totem Bight State Historical Park

★5.0
  1. Northwest
  2. Alaska
  3. Southeast Alaska

Scenic coastal park featuring a collection of hand-carved totem poles and a reconstructed Native clan house set within a dense rainforest environment.

General Information

Hours:
Open daily year-round; typical hours approximately 7am to 6pm, with visitor center open in summer only
Fees:
Approx. $5 per person during peak season (May–September)
Free or reduced access in off-season
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed in outdoor areas on leash
Closest cities with hotels:
Ketchikan
Seasons:
Best visited May through September for full access and optimal weather; open year-round
Location:
9883 N Tongass Hwy, Ketchikan, AK 99901
Website:
dnr.alaska.gov

How to Get There

From Ketchikan (10 mi): Head north on Tongass Ave continuing onto N Tongass Hwy for about 10 miles until you reach the park entrance along the shoreline.

Overview

Totem Bight State Historical Park is a 33-acre park north of Ketchikan that preserves a collection of 14 totem poles and a colorful clan house in a rain forest setting on the water. The site is on the location of a traditional Native campground known as Mud Bight Village. In the 1930s, the Forest Service used Civilian Conservation Corps funds to hire skilled Native carvers to repair or copy old poles, and the 8.5-acre historic site was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1970. Visitors come to see the poles up close, learn about Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian art and walk a short forest trail beside Tongass Narrows.

Access

The park is at 9883 N Tongass Highway, about 10 miles north of downtown Ketchikan. Visitors reach it by car, taxi, tour bus or shuttle from the cruise ship docks, and the drive takes about 20 minutes. Ketchikan is reached by Alaska Airlines jet or by Alaska Marine Highway ferry and cruise ship, and the town sits on Revillagigedo Island. A short paved trail leads from the parking lot to the poles.

Seasons

The park is open daily year-round, but the visitor center and fee collection operate mainly in summer, from May to September. This is the busiest time, when cruise ships bring crowds. Spring and fall are quieter and often rainy, which is normal in the temperate rain forest. Winter is gray and wet with short days, though the park is open and free or reduced fee. Bring rain gear in any season.

Things to Do

See the Totem Poles

Fourteen carved poles stand along the path, each representing clan stories and family crests.

Visit the Clan House

A reconstructed clan house shows how Native families lived and held ceremonies.

Learn About Northwest Coast Art

Signs and exhibits explain the symbols, carvers and the CCC restoration project.

Walk the Rain Forest Trail

A short trail through towering spruce and hemlock leads from the parking area to the water.

Visit Nearby Ketchikan Sites

Totem Heritage Center, Saxman Totem Park and Creek Street are all close.

Top Trails

Totem Bight Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved forest trail leads past the totem poles to the clan house on the water. It is flat and suitable for most visitors.

Settler's Cove Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

At nearby Settler's Cove State Recreation Site, a trail through rain forest leads to a rocky beach and waterfall.

Visitor Centers

The park has a small visitor center at the entrance, staffed in summer, with exhibits, a gift shop and information about the poles and the CCC project. A small fee is charged in peak season. Staff can answer questions about Ketchikan's totem collections and nearby sites.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Settler's Cove State Recreation Site, about 8 miles farther north, has campsites, and Ward Lake in the Tongass National Forest has campgrounds. Hotels, lodges and bed-and-breakfasts are in Ketchikan, about 10 miles south, including downtown waterfront hotels near Creek Street. Larger towns include Wrangell and Prince Rupert, British Columbia, reached by ferry.
