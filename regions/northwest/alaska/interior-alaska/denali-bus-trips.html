Denali Bus Trips

★5.0
  1. Northwest
  2. Alaska
  3. Interior Alaska
  4. Denali National Park and Preserve
  5. Denali Bus Trips

Photo (c)2006 Derek Ramsey (Ram-Man), GFDL 1.2, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Park buses are the main way to see Denali's wilderness interior, with narrated tours, hop-on hop-off transit buses, and free shuttles around the entrance area.

General Information
Hours:
Bus season mid-May through mid-September; departure times vary by bus type (free shuttles run approximately 5:00am to 10:10pm in peak summer)
Fees:
Natural History Tour: $116.75 adults (16+), $51.00 children (15 and under)
Tundra Wilderness Tour: $144.75 adults, $65.00 children
Transit and camper buses: $33.50 adults, free for children (15 and under; reserved ticket still required)
Savage River, Riley Creek Loop, and Sled Dog Demonstration shuttles: Free
Park entrance fee: $15 per person (7 days), included in tour bus tickets but not transit bus tickets; free for ages 15 and under
Reservations:
Tour, transit, and camper buses: reservedenali.com or 1-800-622-7275; reservations open December 1 of the prior year. Free shuttles require no tickets.
Pet Policy:
Pets are not permitted on park buses, except service animals
Closest cities with hotels:
Denali Park (1 mi), Healy (12 mi)
Seasons:
Summer; tour buses run approximately May 13 to September 17, and free shuttles run May 15 to September 20 (2026 dates)
Location:
Denali Bus Depot, Denali Park Road, Denali Park, AK 99755
Website:
nps.gov

How to Get There

From Denali Park (1 mi): Take the George Parks Highway to the park entrance at milepost 237 and turn west onto Denali Park Road. Tour, transit, and camper buses depart from the Denali Bus Depot near the park entrance, while the free shuttles also stop at the Denali Visitor Center.

Overview

Only the first 15 miles of the Denali Park Road, to the Savage River area, are open to private vehicles. Beyond that point, the gravel road is open mainly to park buses, which makes a bus trip the most popular way to see the wildlife and scenery of Denali National Park and Preserve. Buses are run by the park concessioner, Doyon/Aramark Joint Venture, and fall into three types: narrated tour buses, non-narrated transit buses, and free shuttles in the entrance area. Since the 2021 Pretty Rocks landslide near mile 45, buses have traveled no farther than East Fork at mile 43. A bridge over the slide was completed in September 2026, and the park plans to restore bus service to the end of the road at Kantishna in phases starting in 2027.

Narrated tour buses (tan) are led by a driver-naturalist who explains the park's natural history, wildlife, and culture along the way. The Natural History Tour travels to Primrose Ridge at mile 17, takes 4.5 to 5 hours round trip, and costs $116.75 for adults and $51.00 for children 15 and under. The Tundra Wilderness Tour goes farther, to the Murie Cabin near the East Fork River at mile 43, passing the Teklanika River and Sable Pass, where bears, caribou, moose, and Dall sheep are often spotted. It takes 5 to 5.5 hours and costs $144.75 for adults and $65.00 for children. The $15 park entrance fee is included in tour tickets, and annual or lifetime pass holders can get it refunded at the Denali Bus Depot or Riley Creek Mercantile. Tour buses do not pick up at campgrounds, and riders are not meant to get off and re-board. Departure times vary with demand, and wheelchair-accessible buses are available for the Tundra Wilderness Tour.

Non-narrated transit buses (green) are the less expensive, more flexible option. They travel to East Fork at mile 43, take about 4.5 hours round trip, and cost $33.50 for adults, plus the separate $15 entrance fee. Children 15 and under ride free but still need a reserved ticket. Drivers share basic park and safety information and stop for wildlife, but there are no formal talks. The big advantage is that riders can get off anywhere to hike or picnic and then flag down any green bus with open seats heading in their direction, although waits can range from 5 minutes to more than an hour. Restroom stops come about every 90 minutes, and no food is sold beyond the bus depot, so riders should pack lunch and carry out their trash. Camper buses are a type of transit bus for visitors with a reservation at an eligible campground along the road or a backcountry permit. They cost the same $33.50 and have rear seats removed for gear and room for up to two bikes.

Three free buses serve the park entrance area and require no tickets or reservations. The Savage River Shuttle leaves the Denali Visitor Center and the Denali Bus Depot every 30 minutes for the Mountain Vista and Savage River trailheads, where parking is extremely limited, and takes about 2 hours round trip. The Riley Creek Loop Shuttle runs every 30 minutes between the Riley Creek Mercantile, Riley Creek Campground, the bus depot, the Horseshoe Lake Trailhead, and the visitor center. The Sled Dog Demonstration Shuttle leaves the visitor center 40 minutes before each demonstration at the Denali Sled Dog Kennels, which have no visitor parking. Visitors on a budget can pair a transit bus ticket with the free shuttles, while first-time visitors who want to learn as much as possible may prefer a narrated tour. Reservations for all ticketed buses open on December 1 of the year before the trip, and summer seats fill quickly.
