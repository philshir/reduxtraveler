How to Get There

From Denali Park (90 mi): Enter the park and travel west along the Denali Park Road by park bus or authorized vehicle for approximately 90 miles to reach the remote area.

Overview

Kantishna is a small community inside Denali National Park at the end of the 92.5-mile Denali Park Road, founded in 1905 as a gold mining camp. It has several remote wilderness lodges: Denali Backcountry Lodge, Kantishna Roadhouse, and Camp Denali/North Face Lodge. Guests travel by park bus (a long day trip from the entrance) or by air taxi to Kantishna Airport, and it has views of Denali up close.

Things to Do

Experience the Remote Heart of Denali National Park

Kantishna is a historic wilderness settlement located deep within Denali National Park & Preserve near the end of the park's 92-mile road. Surrounded by mountains, tundra, forests, rivers, and abundant wildlife, Kantishna offers one of Alaska's most remote and immersive backcountry experiences. Visitors come for spectacular scenery, wildlife viewing, hiking, history, and solitude far from the crowds.

Visit Wonder Lake

Wonder Lake is one of the most famous destinations in Denali National Park and one of the best locations for viewing Denali. On clear days, the mountain's reflection can appear on the lake's calm waters, creating one of Alaska's most iconic photographic scenes.

Explore Gold Rush History

Kantishna was founded during the 1905 gold rush and remains rich in mining history. Visitors can learn about early prospectors, visit historic sites, view mining artifacts, and discover the fascinating stories of those who settled in this remote corner of Alaska.

Pan for Gold

Many visitors enjoy trying their hand at gold panning, following in the footsteps of the prospectors who traveled to Kantishna more than a century ago. Several lodges and historical programs offer opportunities to learn about mining techniques and local history.

Enjoy Exceptional Wildlife Viewing

The Kantishna area provides excellent opportunities to observe wildlife in Denali National Park. Visitors may encounter grizzly bears, black bears, moose, caribou, Dall sheep, wolves, foxes, and numerous bird species throughout the surrounding wilderness.

Experience Guided Wilderness Adventures

Local lodges and guides offer hiking excursions, photography tours, biking, fishing, naturalist programs, cultural experiences, and flightseeing opportunities. The remote setting makes Kantishna one of the most rewarding locations for exploring Denali's backcountry.

Relax in the Alaskan Wilderness

Many visitors travel to Kantishna simply to enjoy the quiet and solitude of the Alaska wilderness. Comfortable wilderness lodges provide opportunities to relax, enjoy mountain views, watch wildlife, and experience the unique atmosphere of the park's remote interior.

Top Trails

Wonder Lake Area Trails

Length: Various routes available
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

The Wonder Lake area offers several hiking opportunities through tundra and wetlands while providing some of the best views of Denali and surrounding wilderness landscapes in the park.

McKinley Bar Trail

Length: Approximately 4 to 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the area's classic hikes, this route travels through tundra landscapes and leads toward outstanding viewpoints of Denali, river valleys, and remote wilderness scenery.

Eagle Point Area Trails

Length: Varies
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

These routes provide opportunities to explore Denali's trail-less tundra environment while viewing wildlife, wildflowers, and expansive mountain landscapes.

Cloudberry Trail Area

Length: Varies
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Popular among visitors staying in Kantishna, these hiking routes travel through tundra and forest environments offering chances to observe wildlife and experience the quiet beauty of Denali's backcountry.
