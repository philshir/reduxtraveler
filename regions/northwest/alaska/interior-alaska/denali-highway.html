How to Get There

From Cantwell (0 mi): Access the route directly from the Parks Hwy (AK-3) at Cantwell and follow Denali Hwy east through the scenic backcountry toward Paxson.

Overview

The Denali Highway (Alaska Route 8) is a 135-mile, mostly gravel road from Paxson on the Richardson Highway to Cantwell on the Parks Highway, across the foothills of the central Alaska Range, with wide-open tundra, glaciers, and mountains. It is lightly traveled and poorly maintained, closed October to mid-May, and only the east and west ends are paved. Maclaren Summit (4,086 feet) is the second-highest road in Alaska.

Highlights

  • Camping: developed campgrounds at Tangle Lakes (MP 22) and Brushkana Creek (MP 104), plus dozens of pullouts
  • Lodging: Denali Highway Cabins & Tours, Maclaren River Lodge, Alpine Creek Lodge, and Backwoods Lodge
  • Tangle Lakes: the start of the Delta River Canoe Trail
  • Wildlife: caribou, moose, and grizzlies, and alpine birds

Things to Do

Drive One of Alaska's Most Remote and Scenic Roads

The Denali Highway is a 134-mile route connecting Paxson and Cantwell across interior Alaska. Originally built as the first road access to Denali National Park, the highway now offers travelers a spectacular wilderness experience featuring tundra landscapes, alpine lakes, mountain passes, glaciers, and panoramic views of the Alaska Range. Most of the road is gravel, adding to its remote and adventurous character.

Enjoy Spectacular Mountain Scenery

The Denali Highway is renowned for its sweeping views of the Alaska Range, Wrangell Mountains, glaciers, tundra valleys, and distant peaks. On clear days, travelers can enjoy some of the most expansive mountain scenery accessible by road anywhere in Alaska.

Visit Maclaren Summit

At approximately 4,086 feet, Maclaren Summit is the highest point along the highway and one of its signature attractions. Easily accessible pullouts provide dramatic views of the Maclaren River Valley, Maclaren Glacier, Mount Hayes, and surrounding alpine tundra landscapes.

Explore Tangle Lakes

The Tangle Lakes area features interconnected lakes, wetlands, hiking opportunities, canoeing routes, and important archaeological sites. The region serves as the headwaters of the Delta Wild and Scenic River and is a popular destination for paddling, fishing, wildlife viewing, and photography.

Look for Wildlife

Wildlife viewing is one of the highlights of the Denali Highway. Travelers may encounter caribou, moose, foxes, pikas, ground squirrels, ptarmigan, raptors, and numerous migratory bird species throughout the route's tundra and wetland ecosystems.

Experience a True Wilderness Adventure

Unlike many of Alaska's more developed roadways, the Denali Highway travels through a largely undeveloped landscape with limited services. Visitors come for backcountry hiking, camping, fishing, hunting, photography, canoeing, off-road exploration, and the opportunity to experience remote Alaska wilderness.

Top Trails

Maclaren Summit Trail

Length: Up to 9.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Beginning near Maclaren Summit, this trail traverses open tundra and provides outstanding views of the Maclaren River Valley, Alaska Range, and surrounding alpine landscapes. The route is popular for hiking, trail running, and photography.

Landmark Gap Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail passes through the Tangle Lakes Archaeological District and leads to scenic views surrounding Landmark Gap Lake. The route travels through sensitive cultural and natural landscapes.

Glacier Gap Lake to Sevenmile Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 8.5 miles
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This trail traverses rugged terrain and provides expansive views of lakes, valleys, wildlife habitat, and mountain scenery in the heart of the Denali Highway corridor.

Rusty Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 0.75 miles
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A shorter hiking option in the Tangle Lakes area, this trail provides access to scenic lake views and opportunities to experience Alaska's tundra environment with a relatively modest hike.
