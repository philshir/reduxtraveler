Overview

Interior Alaska is the huge central region between the Alaska Range and the Brooks Range, largely wilderness, with the Yukon and Tanana rivers and Fairbanks as the largest city. Visitors come for Denali National Park, the northern lights, the midnight sun, and wildlife such as moose, caribou, wolves, and grizzly bears.

The region offers a classic Alaska experience, with scenic drives on the Parks, Denali, Steese, and Elliott highways, river trips, hot springs, dog mushing, and historic gold-rush sites. The town of Talkeetna is a favorite base for flightseeing and climbing Denali.

Geography

The Interior is a vast basin of boreal forest, wetlands, and river valleys bounded by the Alaska Range on the south and the Brooks Range on the north. Denali rises to 20,310 feet and is the tallest peak in North America. The Yukon River, one of the longest rivers in North America, and its tributary the Tanana drain the region. The climate is extreme, with winter lows of −40°F or colder and summer highs in the 70s and 80s, with nearly 24 hours of daylight around the summer solstice.

History

Athabascan peoples have lived in the Interior for thousands of years. Russian and, later, American traders came along the Yukon in the 1800s, and the discovery of gold near Fairbanks in 1902 brought a rush of prospectors. The Alaska Railroad, completed in 1923, and the Alaska Highway, built in 1942, linked the region to the rest of the world, and military bases brought growth in World War II. The Trans-Alaska Pipeline, built in the 1970s, runs through the Interior, and Denali was established as Mount McKinley National Park in 1917.

Cities and Towns

Fairbanks is the hub, with the University of Alaska Fairbanks, the Museum of the North, Pioneer Park, and some of the best aurora viewing in the world. Talkeetna is a funky town on the edge of the Alaska Range, and smaller communities along the Parks Highway, including Healy near Denali and Nenana, serve visitors. Delta Junction marks the end of the Alaska Highway.

National Protected Areas

Denali National Park and Preserve covers six million acres, with a 92-mile park road, wildlife viewing, and the tallest mountain in North America. Yukon-Charley Rivers National Preserve protects a wild stretch of the Yukon River and gold-rush history, and Gates of the Arctic National Park lies north of the region. The Yukon Flats and Tetlin national wildlife refuges protect wetlands and waterfowl habitat, and the Bureau of Land Management’s White Mountains National Recreation Area and Steese National Conservation Area have trails and cabins north of Fairbanks.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Denali State Park has campgrounds and views of Denali along the Parks Highway, and Chena River State Recreation Area east of Fairbanks offers hiking, fishing, and paddling. Big Delta State Historical Park preserves Rika’s Roadhouse, a historic stop on the Valdez-to-Fairbanks trail, and Harding Lake State Recreation Area is a popular summer lake. Pioneer Park in Fairbanks, a city park, has gold-rush-era buildings and the historic sternwheeler Nenana.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

Summer, from late May through mid-September, is the main season, when the Denali park bus operates, days are long, and temperatures are warm. Fall brings colorful tundra in late August and early September, and it is the start of aurora season, which lasts into April. Winter is cold and dark, but it is popular for northern lights viewing, dog mushing, skiing, and hot springs such as Chena Hot Springs. Spring features the sled-dog races and the thaw in April and May.
