How to Get There

From Anchorage, AK (~1,800 mi): Fly Alaska Airlines to Adak Airport (ADK) ~3 hours, then arrange a multi-day boat passage ~300 miles west to uninhabited Attu Island, the westernmost point of the United States.

Overview

Attu is an island in the Near Islands (part of the Aleutian Islands chain). It is the westernmost point of Alaska. The island became uninhabited in 2010, making it the largest uninhabited island in the United States.

The Battle of Attu was the site of the only World War II land battle fought in the United States. The battlefield area is a U.S. National Historic Landmark.

Birding

Attu was an important location in the world of competitive birding, whose goal is to see or hear the largest possible number of bird species within a specific geographic area during a specific time period. Because it is so physically remote from other parts of North America, there are a number of bird species likely to be found on Attu that are not seen anywhere else on the continent. John Fitchen called the island "the Holy Grail of North American birding".

Birding tours can still reach Attu but only by boat, following a multi-day trip from Adak Island.

Things to Do

Visit One of the Most Remote Places in the United States

Attu Island sits at the western end of Alaska's Aleutian Islands and is among the most isolated locations in North America. The island is uninhabited and forms part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge. Its rugged mountains, windswept tundra, rocky coastlines, and dramatic weather create an unforgettable wilderness landscape at the edge of the North Pacific Ocean.

Explore a Historic World War II Battlefield

Attu Island is best known as the site of the Battle of Attu, the only World War II land battle fought on territory that is now part of a U.S. state. Visitors who reach the island can find remnants of military installations, defensive positions, abandoned structures, and memorials that commemorate one of the Pacific Theater's most significant and challenging campaigns.

Learn About Unangax̂ (Aleut) Heritage

For thousands of years, Attu was home to the Unangax̂ people, whose culture was deeply connected to the rich marine environment of the Aleutian Islands. The wartime occupation of Attu led to the displacement of its residents, making the island an important site for understanding both Indigenous history and the lasting impacts of World War II on local communities.

Observe Marine Wildlife

The waters surrounding Attu support a wide variety of marine life. Visitors may observe sea otters, harbor seals, whales, seabirds, and other species that thrive in the productive ecosystems of the North Pacific and Bering Sea.

Visit the Peace Memorial

A Peace Memorial constructed through cooperation between the United States and Japan stands on the island in remembrance of those who lost their lives during the Battle of Attu. The memorial serves as a symbol of reconciliation and reflection amid one of the most remote battlefields of World War II.

Explore Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge Lands

As part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge, Attu protects important nesting habitat for seabirds and other wildlife. The island remains an important conservation area and a living laboratory for wildlife and ecological research in the Aleutian Islands.

Top Trails

Engineer Hill Route

Length: Approximately 1 to 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This route leads to the area of Engineer Hill, one of the most significant locations from the Battle of Attu. Visitors can view remnants of military positions and the Peace Memorial while enjoying expansive views of the surrounding landscape.

Attu Village Exploration Route

Length: Variable
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors can explore the area surrounding the former village of Attu, reflecting on the island's long Unangax̂ history and wartime events. The route provides opportunities for historical interpretation, wildlife observation, and photography.

Holtz Bay Battlefield Area

Length: Variable backcountry route
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

This remote area contains important sites associated with the Battle of Attu and offers dramatic views of the island's mountainous terrain. Travel requires careful planning due to weather, terrain, and the island's extreme remoteness.
