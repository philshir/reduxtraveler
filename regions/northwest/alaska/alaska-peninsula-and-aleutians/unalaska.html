How to Get There

From Anchorage, AK (~800 mi): Fly Alaska Airlines to Unalaska/Dutch Harbor Airport (DUT) ~2.5 hours, then take a taxi or rental car to reach Unalaska's historic sites, restaurants, and waterfront.

Overview

Unalaska is a remote city on Unalaska Island in the Aleutian Islands, 800 miles southwest of Anchorage, and it is the home of Dutch Harbor, the busiest fishing port in the United States by volume of catch. The Bering Sea crab fishery that appears on the television show Deadliest Catch is based here. The windswept, treeless landscape of green volcanic mountains and rocky coast is covered by fog much of the year, and the town has a mix of Unangan, Russian, and Asian communities.

Visitors come to Unalaska to see the Church of the Holy Ascension, a National Historic Landmark, and the World War II sites of the Aleutian campaign. They also come for bird watching, for hiking, and for the fishing fleet, and the Museum of the Aleutians tells the story of the islands and their Unangan people.

History

The Unangan (Aleut) people have lived on the island for at least 9,000 years, and in the 1760s Russian fur traders began to arrive and enslaved many Unangan to hunt sea otters. A Russian Orthodox mission was established in 1825, and the church was built in 1896. After the Alaska Purchase, Americans came to trade, and in 1942, during World War II, the Japanese bombed Dutch Harbor, the only enemy attack on American soil in the Aleutian Campaign, and the Unangan people of the islands were evacuated to camps in Southeast Alaska under harsh conditions. After the war, the town developed as a fishing port, and it has grown into one of the top fishing ports in the world.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Church of the Holy Ascension, built in 1896 and its green-domed spire, is the oldest Russian Orthodox cathedral in North America still in use, and houses an important collection of icons. The Museum of the Aleutians has exhibits on Unangan culture and the war, and Fort Mears and the Aleutian World War II National Historic Area preserve military sites, such as bunkers and gun emplacements. Dutch Harbor's docks, with crab boats and processing plants, are open for viewing.

Culture and Museums

The Museum of the Aleutians has one of the best collections of Unangan baskets, masks, and tools, and exhibits about the Russian period, World War II, and the fishing industry. The Church of the Holy Ascension has a collection of Russian icons and religious objects, and the Unalaska community hosts events such as the Aleutian Islands Fishermen's Memorial. The Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge manages the islands.

Parks

Unalaska Island is a mountainous, treeless island with volcanoes, including Makushin Volcano at 5,691 feet, and trails with views of the sea. Summer Bay, with a beach and a lake, and Ugadaga Bay offer hiking and picnicking, and the island's tundra is full of wildflowers in summer. Bird watchers come for puffins, bald eagles, and rare Asian species, and the Aleutian Islands are part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge.

Seasons and Weather

Unalaska has a maritime subarctic climate with frequent fog, wind, and rain. Summers are cool, with highs in the 50s°F, and winters are mild for Alaska, with highs in the 30s°F and about 80 inches of snow. The weather can change quickly, and flights can be delayed by fog, so visitors should have flexible schedules.

Things to Do

Explore the Heart of the Aleutian Islands

Unalaska, home to Dutch Harbor, is the largest community in the Aleutian Islands and one of Alaska's most unique destinations. Surrounded by volcanic peaks, rugged coastlines, and rich marine ecosystems, the city combines Native Unangax̂ heritage, Russian history, World War II sites, commercial fishing culture, and outstanding outdoor recreation opportunities.

Visit the Museum of the Aleutians

The Museum of the Aleutians preserves the cultural and historical heritage of the Aleutian Islands. Exhibits explore thousands of years of Unangax̂ history, Russian settlement, World War II events, archaeology, and the modern development of Unalaska. The museum is one of the community's most important cultural attractions.

Explore World War II History

Unalaska played a major role during World War II and was the site of Japanese air attacks in 1942. Visitors can tour the Aleutian World War II National Historic Area Visitor Center, explore former military installations, and view bunkers, gun emplacements, and defensive structures that remain throughout the area.

Visit Holy Ascension Russian Orthodox Cathedral

This striking church is a National Historic Landmark and one of the oldest Russian Orthodox cathedrals in North America. Its distinctive onion domes and historic religious artifacts reflect the lasting influence of Russian culture on the Aleutian Islands.

Experience Outstanding Wildlife Viewing

The waters surrounding Unalaska support abundant marine life, including sea otters, harbor seals, sea lions, whales, and countless seabirds. Bald eagles are commonly seen throughout the area, while nearby waters attract birdwatchers seeking puffins, auklets, and other marine bird species.

Enjoy Whale Watching and Marine Tours

Boat tours and local charter operators offer opportunities to view humpback whales, orcas, sea lions, sea otters, and seabird colonies. The dramatic coastline and productive marine environment make Unalaska one of Alaska's premier wildlife-viewing destinations.

Explore Dutch Harbor

One of the busiest commercial fishing ports in the United States, Dutch Harbor offers visitors a fascinating glimpse into Alaska's fishing industry. Walking the harbor provides views of fishing vessels, seafood processing facilities, and the working waterfront that drives the local economy.

Go Fishing in Aleutian Waters

Fishing enthusiasts can pursue halibut, salmon, cod, rockfish, and other species through local charter operators. The rich waters surrounding Unalaska have long supported both commercial and recreational fishing activities.

Drive or Hike Through Volcanic Landscapes

The surrounding terrain includes mountains, tundra, lakes, beaches, and volcanic features that offer excellent opportunities for hiking, photography, and wildlife observation. Scenic viewpoints throughout the island provide sweeping panoramas of bays, harbors, and neighboring islands.

Top Trails

Mount Ballyhoo Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

One of Unalaska's most popular hikes, Mount Ballyhoo offers spectacular views of Dutch Harbor, the Bering Sea, Unalaska Bay, and surrounding islands. The route also passes historic World War II military sites and provides opportunities to observe bald eagles and marine wildlife.

Bunker Hill Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic hike leads to a former coastal defense position and features panoramic views of Captains Bay, Amaknak Island, Iliuliuk Harbor, and nearby mountains. Historical military remnants and abundant birdlife make the trail a favorite among visitors.

Overland Pass Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This route traverses scenic tundra and hillside landscapes while providing expansive views of Summer Bay, Beaver Inlet, and the surrounding Aleutian terrain. The trail is popular for photography, wildlife viewing, and experiencing the region's remote beauty.
