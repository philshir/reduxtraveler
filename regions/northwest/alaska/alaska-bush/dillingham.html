How to Get There

From Anchorage, AK (~330 mi by air): No road connection exists; fly Alaska Airlines (seasonal) or PenAir year-round from Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport to Dillingham Airport (DLG) in approximately 1.5 hours.

Overview

Dillingham is a small city at the northern end of Bristol Bay in southwestern Alaska, at the mouth of the Nushagak River, and it is the hub of the world's largest wild sockeye salmon fishery. Every summer, tens of millions of sockeye return to Bristol Bay, and Dillingham's fishermen, canneries, and sport fishing guides are at the center of it. The town of about 2,200 has a majority Yup'ik population and is the gateway to Wood-Tikchik State Park and Togiak National Wildlife Refuge.

Visitors come to Dillingham for the salmon runs in late June and July, for sport fishing for king and silver salmon, and for the Samuel K. Fox Museum. They also come for flights to Wood-Tikchik State Park, the largest state park in the United States, for the Walrus Islands State Game Sanctuary, and for Yup'ik culture.

History

The Yup'ik and Dena'ina peoples have fished the Nushagak River for thousands of years, and Russian traders built a trading post at Fort Alexandrovski in the 1800s. The first salmon cannery in Bristol Bay opened in 1884, and the town of Dillingham, named for Senator William Dillingham of Vermont, was established as a cannery site. The Spanish flu of 1918 devastated the region's Native people, and canneries drew workers from across the Pacific. After World War II, fishermen began owning their own boats, and Bristol Bay's salmon runs have remained among the world's largest.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Dillingham waterfront has fishing boats, a small boat harbor, and cannery buildings, and the Samuel K. Fox Museum in the Dillingham Public Library has Yup'ik artifacts and a collection of historic photographs. The Nushagak River and Kanakanak Beach, a few miles away, are scenic, and the Bristol Bay Native Corporation offers cultural programs. The Moravian and Russian Orthodox churches in the region are historic.

Culture and Museums

The Samuel K. Fox Museum shows the history of the Nushagak River region, and the Bristol Bay Native Association preserves Yup'ik culture. Fishing season brings a festival atmosphere, with the Bristol Bay Fish Expo and the Beaver Round-Up, a winter carnival that celebrates trapping in March. The Yup'ik language and dancing are an important part of the community.

Parks

Wood-Tikchik State Park, 1.6 million acres of lakes, rivers, and mountains, is the largest state park in the United States and is reached by floatplane, offering fishing, kayaking, and wildlife viewing. The Togiak National Wildlife Refuge and the Walrus Islands State Game Sanctuary, where thousands of walruses haul out in summer, can also be visited from Dillingham. The Nushagak River is a major fishing river for king salmon and has a number of lodges.

Seasons and Weather

Dillingham has a subarctic maritime climate. Summers are cool, with highs in the 60s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the teens and 20s°F and about 60 inches of snow. The salmon runs make late June and July the busiest time, and the bay freezes in winter.

Things to Do

Discover the Gateway to Bristol Bay

Dillingham is the largest community in the Bristol Bay region of Southwest Alaska and serves as the area's economic and transportation hub. Situated at the head of Nushagak Bay near the Wood and Nushagak Rivers, the community provides access to some of Alaska's finest sportfishing, wildlife viewing, and wilderness adventures.

Fish World-Famous Bristol Bay Waters

Dillingham is best known as a destination for exceptional fishing. Bristol Bay supports one of the world's largest salmon fisheries, and visitors can pursue all five species of Pacific salmon along with rainbow trout, Arctic char, and Dolly Varden in nearby rivers and lakes.

Explore Wood-Tikchik State Park

Just west of Dillingham lies Wood-Tikchik State Park, the largest state park in the United States. Covering approximately 1.6 million acres, the park features interconnected lakes, rivers, mountains, and pristine wilderness. Visitors enjoy kayaking, canoeing, rafting, fishing, wildlife viewing, and backcountry camping throughout the park.

Visit Lake Aleknagik

Located about 25 miles north of Dillingham, Lake Aleknagik serves as one of the primary gateways to Wood-Tikchik State Park. The area provides opportunities for boating, fishing, scenic photography, camping, and wildlife observation.

Experience Outstanding Wildlife Viewing

The wilderness surrounding Dillingham supports abundant wildlife populations. Visitors frequently encounter brown bears, moose, bald eagles, foxes, beavers, otters, and numerous bird species while exploring the region's rivers, lakes, and wetlands.

Visit the Walrus Islands State Game Sanctuary

Located in northern Bristol Bay southwest of Dillingham, the Walrus Islands State Game Sanctuary protects a group of remote islands and important marine habitat. Round Island, one of the sanctuary's highlights, supports one of Alaska's largest walrus haul-outs and is a premier destination for wildlife viewing and photography.

Learn About Yup'ik Culture

Dillingham lies within a region rich in Yup'ik heritage. Visitors can learn about Indigenous traditions, subsistence lifestyles, local history, and cultural practices through community events, cultural programs, and regional educational resources.

Take a Flightseeing Adventure

Many visitors explore the surrounding wilderness by bush plane. Flightseeing excursions provide spectacular aerial views of Wood-Tikchik State Park, Bristol Bay, remote lakes, mountain ranges, salmon rivers, and vast stretches of undeveloped wilderness.

Experience the Midnight Sun

During summer, Dillingham enjoys extremely long daylight hours that allow visitors to maximize opportunities for fishing, sightseeing, photography, and outdoor recreation well into the evening.

Top Trails

Snake Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

One of Dillingham's most accessible hiking routes, the trail travels through forested terrain and wetlands near Snake Lake. It provides opportunities for birdwatching, photography, and enjoying quiet natural surroundings close to town.

Gateway Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This local trail offers scenic views of the surrounding landscape and serves as a popular recreational route for hiking, walking, and exploring the natural environment near Dillingham.

Snake Lake Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs to elevated viewpoints overlooking Dillingham, Snake Lake, and portions of the Bristol Bay region. It is one of the area's best options for panoramic photography and wildlife observation.

Wood-Tikchik Backcountry Routes

Length: Varies by itinerary
Elevation Gain: Varies
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

Because much of Wood-Tikchik State Park remains undeveloped, visitors often create their own routes while exploring river corridors, lakeshores, tundra, and mountain terrain. These adventures provide a true Alaska wilderness experience.
