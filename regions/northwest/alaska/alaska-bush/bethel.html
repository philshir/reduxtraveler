How to Get There

From Anchorage, AK (~400 mi by air): No road connection exists; fly Alaska Airlines or Ravn Alaska from Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport to Bethel Airport (BET) in approximately 1 hour.

Overview

Bethel is the largest community in the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta of southwestern Alaska, on the Kuskokwim River, about 400 miles west of Anchorage, and it is the regional hub for about 50 Yup'ik villages. It is not connected to the road system and is reached by air and, in summer, by river, and its roads lead only to the surrounding tundra. Bethel is a center of Yup'ik culture, with a majority Yup'ik population, subsistence fishing, and a vibrant tradition of dance, art, and language.

Visitors come to Bethel to experience Yup'ik culture, to visit the Yupiit Piciryarait Cultural Center, and to join in events such as the Kuskokwim 300 sled dog race and the Camai Dance Festival. They also come for bird watching in the Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge, for fishing, and for a look at life in the Alaska Bush.

History

The Yup'ik people lived along the Kuskokwim River for thousands of years, in a village called Mamterilleq. In 1884, Moravian missionaries established a mission and named the settlement Bethel, after Bethel in Pennsylvania. The community grew as a trading center, with the arrival of the Alaska Commercial Company and the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act in 1971, which gave rise to Native corporations, such as the Calista Corporation, based in the region. Bethel remains a regional center for government, health care, and education, with the Yukon-Kuskokwim Health Corporation hospital and the University of Alaska Fairbanks' Kuskokwim campus.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Yupiit Piciryarait Cultural Center has exhibits on Yup'ik art, including masks, baskets, and kayaks, and the Kuskokwim River waterfront has fish camps and a boardwalk. The Moravian Church and the Bethel Kuskokwim Museum are historic, and the Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge Visitor Center has displays about the birds of the delta. Visitors can see Yup'ik dancing, ivory carving, and grass baskets, and the river is a highway in summer and a road in winter.

Culture and Museums

The Yupiit Piciryarait Cultural Center, the Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge visitor center, and the Bethel Native Corporation preserve the culture and history of the Yup'ik people. The Camai Dance Festival in March draws dancers from across the delta, and the Kuskokwim 300 is a 300-mile sled dog race in January. The Bethel Winter Carnival and the Cama-i Dance Festival celebrate Yup'ik traditions, with dancing, food, and crafts.

Parks

The Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge, the second-largest in the United States, with 19 million acres of tundra, lakes, and wetlands, protects one of the largest nesting areas for waterfowl and shorebirds in the world, and the Kuskokwim River is a major salmon river. The Kisaralik and Kwethluk Rivers, in the Kilbuck Mountains, are popular for rafting, and the tundra is covered with berries in late summer.

Seasons and Weather

Bethel has a subarctic climate. Summers are cool, with highs in the 60s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the teens and 20s°F and about 50 inches of snow. The Kuskokwim River is ice-covered from October to May and used as a winter road, and summer is the best time to visit.

Things to Do

Experience the Cultural Heart of Western Alaska

Bethel is the largest community in the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta and serves as the cultural and transportation hub of southwestern Alaska. Accessible primarily by air and river, the city offers visitors a unique opportunity to experience Yup'ik culture, Arctic landscapes, river adventures, and remote Alaskan wilderness.

Visit the Yupiit Piciryarait Cultural Center

One of Bethel's premier attractions, the Yupiit Piciryarait Cultural Center highlights the traditions, history, and culture of the Yup'ik people. Exhibits include traditional clothing, hunting tools, artwork, household items, and cultural displays that tell the story of life in the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta.

Explore the Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge

The Yukon Delta National Wildlife Refuge is one of the largest wildlife refuges in the United States, encompassing nearly 20 million acres of tundra, wetlands, rivers, and coastline. The refuge supports immense populations of migratory birds, moose, musk oxen, bears, and countless other wildlife species.

Fish the Kuskokwim River

The Kuskokwim River is central to life in Bethel and provides exceptional opportunities for fishing. Anglers can target several salmon species, northern pike, Dolly Varden, Arctic grayling, and rainbow trout while experiencing one of Alaska's largest river systems.

Take a Scenic Flight Over the Delta

Scenic flights provide spectacular aerial views of the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta, winding rivers, wetlands, tundra, and remote villages. Because much of the region lacks roads, flightseeing offers a unique perspective on one of North America's most expansive wilderness landscapes.

Attend the Cama-i Dance Festival

Held each spring, the Cama-i Dance Festival celebrates Indigenous cultures from Alaska and beyond. Traditional dance groups perform colorful dances accompanied by drumming, storytelling, and cultural demonstrations that showcase the region's rich heritage.

Experience the Kuskokwim 300 Sled Dog Race

One of Alaska's most prestigious sled dog races, the Kuskokwim 300 begins and ends in Bethel each winter. Visitors can watch teams of mushers and dogs compete across hundreds of miles of challenging wilderness terrain.

Explore by Kayak or Canoe

The waterways surrounding Bethel provide excellent opportunities for kayaking and canoeing. Paddlers can explore sloughs, lakes, and portions of the Kuskokwim River while observing wildlife and experiencing the unique Delta environment.

Search for the Northern Lights

From late fall through early spring, Bethel offers excellent opportunities to view the aurora borealis. The region's dark skies and limited light pollution create favorable conditions for observing and photographing colorful auroral displays.

Top Trails and Outdoor Routes

Kuskokwim River Front Walk

Length: Approximately 1 to 3 miles depending on route
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This informal walking route follows portions of the Kuskokwim River near Bethel and provides opportunities to observe river traffic, local fishing activities, wildlife, and scenic views of the surrounding Delta landscape.

Brown's Slough Water Trail

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: None
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Popular with kayakers and canoeists, Brown's Slough offers an accessible paddling experience through wetlands and waterways near Bethel. Visitors often encounter waterfowl and other wildlife while exploring the area.

Yukon Delta Wildlife Viewing Routes

Length: Varies by excursion
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Guided wildlife excursions into the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta provide access to birdwatching areas, tundra habitats, and remote wetland environments that support some of Alaska's most impressive concentrations of migratory birds.

Kuskokwim River Float Excursions

Length: Varies from several hours to multi-day trips
Elevation Gain: None
Difficulty: Moderate

River excursions allow visitors to experience the vast Kuskokwim watershed, observe traditional subsistence activities, and explore remote areas of western Alaska that are inaccessible by road.
