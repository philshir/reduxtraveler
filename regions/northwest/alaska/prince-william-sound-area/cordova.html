Cordova

★5.0

A remote coastal community featuring an active fishing harbor, expansive wetlands, and access to rich marine ecosystems and glacier-filled landscapes.

How to Get There

From Anchorage (~150 mi by air/ferry): Travel via commercial flight or ferry service across Prince William Sound directly to town, as there is no road connection to the highway system.

Overview

Cordova is a small fishing town on Orca Inlet in Prince William Sound, and it is one of the few communities in Alaska that can be reached only by air or sea, with no road to the outside world. It is famous for the Copper River red salmon, which are among the most prized in the world, for its raw wilderness, and for the huge Copper River Delta, one of the largest wetlands on the Pacific coast. The town has about 2,500 residents and a strong commercial fishing culture.

Visitors come to Cordova for sport fishing and the Copper River Wild Salmon Festival, for bird watching during the spring shorebird migration, and for the Childs Glacier and Million Dollar Bridge on the Copper River Highway. They also come for the Ski Hill, the Cordova Museum, and the Iceworm Festival in February.

History

The Eyak, Chugach, and Ahtna peoples lived in the region, and the town was founded in 1906 as the terminus of the Copper River and Northwestern Railway, which carried copper ore from the Kennecott mines to the port. The railroad ran from 1911 to 1938, and when the mines closed, the town turned to fishing and canneries. The 1964 earthquake raised the land around Cordova by six feet, and the 1989 Exxon Valdez spill damaged the area's fisheries. The Cordova District Fisheries Union and local fishermen played a leading role in the effort to recover.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The harbor and downtown have fishing boats and historic buildings, and the Cordova Historical Museum has exhibits on the railroad, fishing, and Native culture. The Copper River Highway runs 48 miles into the delta, ending at the Million Dollar Bridge and views of the Childs Glacier, a glacier that calves into the Copper River. Mount Eyak has a ski area and trails with views of the sound and the delta, and the Orca Inlet is a popular place for kayaking and wildlife viewing.

Culture and Museums

The Cordova Historical Museum shows the town's history, and the Prince William Sound Science Center has exhibits on the marine ecosystem. The Copper River Wild Salmon Festival in July, the Cordova Iceworm Festival in February, and the Copper River Delta Shorebird Festival in May celebrate local traditions. The Cordova Center, a new cultural building, has a library, a museum, and a theater.

Parks

The Copper River Delta is a 700,000-acre wetland of the Chugach National Forest where millions of shorebirds, including western sandpipers, stop to feed in spring, and the Childs Glacier Recreation Area has a viewing platform and camping. The Chugach National Forest and the Prince William Sound have kayaking, fishing, and hiking, and the Heney Ridge and Crater Lake trails are popular. Wrangell-St. Elias National Park lies to the east.

Seasons and Weather

Cordova has a rainy maritime climate. Summers are cool, with highs in the 60s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 200 inches of snow, and rain is frequent, more than 150 inches a year. Summer is the main season, and the Copper River salmon season starts in mid-May.

Things to Do

Visit One of Alaska's Most Remote Coastal Communities

Cordova is a small fishing town located on the eastern edge of Prince William Sound and near the Copper River Delta. Accessible only by air or ferry, the community is surrounded by glaciers, mountains, wetlands, forests, and rich wildlife habitat, making it one of Alaska's premier destinations for outdoor recreation and nature enthusiasts.

Drive the Copper River Highway

The Copper River Highway is the primary scenic route near Cordova and provides access to wildlife viewing areas, hiking trails, campgrounds, wetlands, and glacier viewpoints. The road travels through portions of the Copper River Delta and offers opportunities to experience some of Alaska's most remarkable landscapes.

Visit Sheridan Glacier

One of the area's most popular attractions, Sheridan Glacier can be reached via a relatively short hike from the Copper River Highway. Visitors can enjoy views of the glacier, icebergs floating in Sheridan Lake, and surrounding mountain scenery.

Explore the Copper River Delta

The Copper River Delta is one of North America's most important wetland ecosystems and an internationally recognized wildlife habitat. Millions of migratory shorebirds pass through the delta annually, while visitors can also spot moose, bears, trumpeter swans, bald eagles, and numerous other species.

Experience World-Class Wildlife Viewing

Cordova is known for exceptional wildlife viewing opportunities. The surrounding wetlands, coastal waters, and forests provide habitat for sea otters, brown bears, black bears, moose, salmon, shorebirds, and marine mammals found throughout Prince William Sound.

Learn About Local History and Culture

The area's museums and cultural centers highlight the heritage of the Eyak people, the history of the Copper River and Northwestern Railway, commercial fishing traditions, and the development of Cordova as one of Alaska's important maritime communities.

Top Trails

Sheridan Glacier Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This popular trail follows relatively flat terrain to Sheridan Lake, where hikers are rewarded with views of floating icebergs, glacier-fed waters, and Sheridan Glacier in the distance.

Heney Ridge Trail

Length: Approximately 6.1 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

Heney Ridge Trail climbs through forest and muskeg meadows before reaching elevated viewpoints overlooking Hartney Bay, Prince William Sound, Cordova, and surrounding mountains. It is considered one of the area's premier scenic hikes.

Pipeline Lakes and McKinley Lake Loop

Length: Approximately 4 to 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This family-friendly route travels through forests, meadows, and lake country while providing opportunities for wildlife viewing, trout fishing, and views of the surrounding mountain landscape.

Alaganik Slough Trail

Length: Short boardwalk trail
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Located along the Copper River Highway, this accessible boardwalk provides outstanding opportunities for birdwatching and wildlife viewing within the wetlands of the Copper River Delta.
