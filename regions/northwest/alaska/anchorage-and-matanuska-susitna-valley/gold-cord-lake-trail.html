Short alpine hiking trail leading to a striking mountain lake surrounded by steep rocky slopes, historic mining remnants, and expansive high-country scenery.

How to Get There

From Palmer (24 mi): Take AK-3 north to Fishhook-Willow Road, continue into Hatcher Pass, follow signs to Independence Mine State Historical Park, and access the trail from the Gold Cord Mine Road area near the parking lot.

Overview

Gold Cord Lake Trail is one of the most rewarding short hikes in the Hatcher Pass region, leading visitors to a secluded alpine lake perched high above the historic Independence Mine valley. The route begins near the remnants of Alaska's gold-mining era and passes old cabins, pipes, and other artifacts that hint at the area's industrial past. As hikers gradually climb the mountainside, increasingly impressive views open across Fishhook Valley, revealing rugged peaks, glacially carved terrain, and the historic structures of the mining district below. The trail ultimately reaches a natural shelf where the lake remains hidden until the final approach, creating a memorable reveal for first-time visitors.

The lake itself is renowned for its crystal-clear water and dramatic mountain setting. Surrounded by steep rocky slopes and lingering snowfields well into summer, it provides an excellent destination for families, photographers, and casual hikers seeking alpine scenery without a lengthy trek. Wildflowers bloom along the route during the warmer months, while the surrounding ridgelines offer opportunities for additional exploration and panoramic views of Hatcher Pass. Combining natural beauty, accessible hiking, and a fascinating connection to Alaska's mining heritage, Gold Cord Lake Trail remains one of the most popular outdoor destinations in the Mat-Su region.
