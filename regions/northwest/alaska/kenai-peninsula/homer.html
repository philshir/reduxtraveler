How to Get There

From Anchorage (220 mi): Take Seward Hwy (AK-1 S) to Sterling Hwy (AK-1 W/AK-9 S) and follow it southwest for about 220 miles to reach the coastal town.

Overview

Homer is a coastal town at the end of the road on the southwestern Kenai Peninsula, on Kachemak Bay, and it is known as the "Halibut Fishing Capital of the World" and the "Cosmic Hamlet by the Sea." Its famous Homer Spit, a 4.5-mile gravel bar extending into the bay, has a busy small boat harbor, fishing charters, shops, and restaurants, and the views across the bay of glaciers and volcanoes are extraordinary. The town has a strong artistic community and a relaxed, bohemian character.

Visitors come to Homer for halibut and salmon fishing, for bear viewing flights to Katmai National Park, and for the Homer Spit. They also come for the Kachemak Bay boat trips to Seldovia and Halibut Cove, for the Pratt Museum, and for the galleries and eateries of the town.

History

The Dena'ina and Sugpiaq peoples fished and hunted on Kachemak Bay, and coal was mined in the area in the late 1800s. The town is named for Homer Pennock, a gold prospector who arrived in 1896 and established a camp at the base of the spit. A coal mine and a railroad to the spit operated in the early twentieth century, and the community grew with homesteaders. The 1964 earthquake dropped the spit by several feet, and the town rebuilt, while the opening of the Sterling Highway in the 1950s connected it to Anchorage by road.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Homer Spit has the Fishing Hole, a stocked lagoon for kids, the Salty Dawg Saloon in a historic cabin with a lighthouse tower, and the Seafarers' Memorial. Homer's Pratt Museum shows the natural and cultural history of Kachemak Bay and has a botanical garden, and the Wynn Nature Center has trails and a bay view. The Bishop's Beach and the Land's End at the end of the spit offer views of the glaciers across the bay, and the Alaska Islands and Ocean Visitor Center has exhibits on marine life.

Culture and Museums

Homer has a large community of artists, and galleries in town and along the spit show paintings, pottery, and crafts, with the Homer Council on the Arts and the Bunnell Street Arts Center leading the scene. The Pratt Museum has exhibits on the area's natural history, and the Kenai Peninsula's Russian Orthodox churches show the area's colonial past. Events include the Kachemak Bay Shorebird Festival in May and the Winter Carnival in February.

Parks

Kachemak Bay State Park, across the bay, is accessible by water taxi and has trails, glaciers, and tide pools, and Kachemak Bay State Wilderness Park is the first and only wilderness park in Alaska's state park system. The town is also a base for bear viewing flights to Katmai and Lake Clark National Parks, and for tours to Halibut Cove, an artists' village. Fishing for halibut, salmon, and razor clams is a favorite activity.

Seasons and Weather

Homer has a cool maritime climate, milder and drier than much of Alaska. Summers are cool, with highs in the 60s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 50 inches of snow. Summer is the main season for fishing and tourism, and the bay is often foggy in the mornings.

Things to Do

Walk the Famous Homer Spit

The Homer Spit is a narrow 4.5-mile-long gravel bar extending into Kachemak Bay and is the city's most recognizable landmark. Visitors can enjoy waterfront restaurants, shops, fishing charters, harbors, beaches, wildlife viewing, and panoramic views of the surrounding mountains and bay.

Explore Kachemak Bay

Kachemak Bay offers outstanding opportunities for wildlife viewing, kayaking, fishing, boating, photography, and water taxi excursions. Across the bay, visitors can access Kachemak Bay State Park, Alaska's first state park, which features glaciers, alpine terrain, forests, and extensive hiking opportunities.

Enjoy Outstanding Wildlife Viewing

Homer is well known for birdwatching and marine wildlife viewing. Bald eagles, seabirds, sea otters, seals, whales, and other wildlife are commonly seen throughout the area, especially along the Homer Spit, Beluga Slough, and Kachemak Bay.

Experience Homer's Arts and Cultural Scene

The community is known for its galleries, local artists, museums, murals, and cultural events. Downtown Homer features a variety of art installations and creative spaces that reflect the area's unique character and connection to the surrounding landscape.

Visit Beluga Slough

This protected wetland area near downtown provides easy access to nature and is a popular location for birdwatching, photography, and walking. Seasonal wildflowers and migratory birds make it one of Homer's most accessible natural attractions.

Top Trails

Homer Spit Trail

Length: Approximately 4.3 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved trail follows the length of the Homer Spit and offers outstanding views of Kachemak Bay, harbors, mountains, wildlife, shops, and restaurants. It is one of the most popular walking and biking routes in the area.

Beluga Slough Trail

Length: Approximately 1.2-mile loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This accessible wetland trail provides excellent opportunities for birdwatching, wildlife viewing, and enjoying seasonal wildflowers. The trail is suitable for visitors of all ages and abilities.

Diamond Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 4.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate descent and climb
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail descends through forests and meadows to a beach along Cook Inlet. The hike offers opportunities to see marine wildlife, coastal scenery, and tidal features. Visitors should pay attention to tide conditions before hiking.

Grace Ridge Trail

Length: Approximately 8.2 to 9.5 miles
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Strenuous

Located in Kachemak Bay State Park and generally reached by water taxi, Grace Ridge is one of the most celebrated hikes in the Homer area. The trail climbs through forests and alpine terrain to spectacular views of Kachemak Bay, Cook Inlet, and surrounding mountains.
