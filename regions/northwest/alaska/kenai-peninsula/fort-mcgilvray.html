Fort McGilvray

Historic World War II coastal fort featuring concrete bunkers, tunnels, and gun emplacements perched on a high cliff with sweeping ocean and mountain views.

General Information

Hours:
Accessible year-round with no set hours; safest access is during daylight and favorable weather conditions
Fees:
No fee to explore the site itself
Parking at nearby trailheads and water taxi transportation may require fees
Pet Policy:
Dogs are allowed on trails but should be kept under control due to wildlife and steep terrain
Closest cities with hotels:
Seward (7 mi)
Seasons:
Best visited May through September when trails are accessible and tides allow safe coastal hiking
Location:
Fort McGilvray, Seward, AK
Website:
dnr.alaska.gov/parks/aspunits/kenai/cainesheadsra.htm

How to Get There

From Seward (7 mi): Drive south to Lowell Point, then hike along the coastal trail timed with low tide or take a water taxi across the bay and follow the trail from North Beach up to the historic fort.

Overview

Fort McGilvray is a World War II coastal fortification on Caines Head, a 650-foot cliff above Resurrection Bay south of Seward. The fort was built to protect the bay and the Seward railhead, and its concrete bunkers, gun emplacements and tunnels remain in Caines Head State Recreation Area. The fort is reached by a coastal trail that must be hiked at low tide, with a public-use cabin and campsites along the way. People come for the history, the dark tunnels, the views of the bay and mountains and a rewarding wilderness hike close to Seward.

Access

The fort is in Caines Head State Recreation Area, south of Seward on the Kenai Peninsula. From Seward, visitors drive about 2 miles to Lowell Point and the trailhead, then hike about 4.5 miles to Coal Cove and 2 more to the fort. Parts of the trail along the beach are impassable at high tide, so it is essential to follow the tide table. Water taxis from Seward can drop hikers at Derby Cove or North Beach. Seward is about 125 miles from Anchorage by road.

Seasons

The trail is best from May through September, when the weather is relatively dry and the hike is usually snow-free. Summer days are long, giving time for the full hike, but tides control access. Fall brings color but shorter days and more rain, and winter conditions are hazardous, with ice and snow. Weather over Resurrection Bay can change quickly, so bring rain gear and a headlamp for the tunnels.

Things to Do

Explore the Fort Bunkers and Tunnels

Concrete bunkers, tunnels and gun emplacements remain on the cliff, and flashlights are needed inside.

Enjoy Views Over Resurrection Bay

From the cliff, hikers look out over the bay, mountains and sometimes whales and sea otters.

Hike the Coastal Trail

The trail follows the shore and climbs through forest, with beaches and tide pools.

Stay in the Public-Use Cabins or Campsites

Cabins and campsites let hikers spend the night and avoid hurrying to beat the tide.

Take a Water Taxi

Boats from Seward carry hikers to the beach for shorter outings.

Top Trails

Caines Head Trail to Coal Cove

Length: Approximately 9 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 300 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

The beach portion must be hiked at low tide, and the route passes Tonsina Point and Coal Cove.

Coal Cove to Fort McGilvray

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate to Strenuous

The trail climbs steeply from Coal Cove to the bunkers and the overlook at Caines Head.

Visitor Centers

There is no staffed visitor center at the fort, but the state park provides signs, and the Alaska State Parks office and Seward Visitor Center in town provide tide tables, maps and cabin reservations. The Kenai Fjords National Park Visitor Center in Seward is a good place to learn more.

Camping and Lodging

The Caines Head area has primitive campsites, and public-use cabins, such as North Beach, can be reserved. Camping is also available at Lowell Point State Recreation Site. Hotels, inns and bed-and-breakfasts are in Seward, about 7 miles from the fort, and Girdwood and Anchorage offer more choices about 2 to 2.5 hours away.
