Getting Around Alaska During the Winter

Winter travel in Alaska is very different from winter travel in most of the United States. The state is enormous, many communities are not connected by roads, daylight can be limited, and weather conditions can change quickly. The best transportation method depends on where you plan to travel.

Air Travel: The Backbone of Alaska Transportation

For much of Alaska, flying is the primary mode of transportation. Many communities are accessible only by air or water, making commercial flights and regional air carriers essential for travel throughout the state.

Flying is often the fastest and most practical way to travel between major destinations, including:

  • Anchorage
  • Fairbanks
  • Juneau
  • Ketchikan
  • Sitka
  • Nome
  • Kotzebue
  • Bethel
  • Utqiaġvik (Barrow)
  • Kodiak

Visitors hoping to explore multiple regions during winter often find flying more efficient than driving long distances.

Driving on Alaska's Road System

The state's road network primarily serves Southcentral Alaska, Interior Alaska, and parts of the Kenai Peninsula.

Popular winter driving routes include:

  • Anchorage to Fairbanks
  • Anchorage to Seward
  • Anchorage to Homer
  • Anchorage to Valdez
  • Anchorage to Palmer and Wasilla
  • Fairbanks to Delta Junction
  • Fairbanks to Chena Hot Springs

Advantages of driving:

  • Maximum flexibility
  • Easy access to scenic overlooks and attractions
  • Convenient northern lights viewing opportunities

Winter driving challenges:

  • Snow-packed roads
  • Black ice
  • Blowing snow
  • Wildlife crossings
  • Limited daylight hours

Essential winter driving items:

  • Extra winter clothing and boots
  • Food and water
  • Phone charger or power bank
  • Flashlight
  • Blanket or sleeping bag
  • Ice scraper and snow brush
  • Emergency roadside kit

Travelers should keep their fuel tanks at least half full and monitor weather forecasts before departing.

Alaska Railroad

The Alaska Railroad operates year-round and provides a comfortable alternative to winter driving. Rail travel is especially popular between communities along the Railbelt corridor.

Major winter rail destinations include:

  • Anchorage
  • Wasilla
  • Talkeetna
  • Fairbanks

Benefits of train travel:

  • No concerns about road conditions
  • Scenic winter landscapes
  • Comfortable seating and onboard services
  • Convenient access to major communities

Bus and Shuttle Services

Several bus lines and private shuttle operators provide service between major destinations connected by the road system.

Common routes include:

  • Anchorage to Fairbanks
  • Anchorage to Talkeetna
  • Anchorage to Seward
  • Anchorage to Homer
  • Anchorage to Palmer and Wasilla

Winter schedules are typically more limited than summer operations.

Rideshare and Taxi Services

Rideshare services and taxis are generally available in larger communities such as:

  • Anchorage
  • Fairbanks
  • Juneau

These services are most useful for local transportation, airport transfers, and short-distance travel.

Winter Ferry Travel

The Alaska Marine Highway System continues operating during winter, although schedules may be reduced and weather-dependent.

Important ferry-served destinations include:

  • Juneau
  • Sitka
  • Ketchikan
  • Haines
  • Skagway
  • Petersburg
  • Wrangell
  • Kodiak

Ferries are particularly important in Southeast Alaska, where many communities are not connected by roads.

Snowmachines (Snowmobiles)

In many rural regions, snowmachines are an important part of daily life during winter.

They are commonly used for:

  • Transportation between local destinations
  • Accessing cabins and remote properties
  • Ice fishing trips
  • Guided winter recreation tours

Dog Sledding

Dog sledding remains one of Alaska's most iconic winter traditions. While primarily a recreational and sporting activity for visitors, it continues to hold cultural significance throughout the state.

Popular dog sledding areas include:

  • Fairbanks
  • Denali region
  • Willow
  • Talkeetna

Fat Biking and Winter Trails

Many Alaskans use fat-tire bicycles on groomed winter trail systems.

Popular winter biking locations include:

  • Anchorage Trail System
  • Kincaid Park
  • Fairbanks Trail Network
  • Government Peak Recreation Area

Northern Lights Travel Tips

Visitors seeking the Aurora Borealis should consider Interior Alaska, where dark winter skies provide excellent viewing conditions.

Recommended northern lights destinations:

  • Fairbanks
  • Chena Hot Springs area
  • Murphy Dome
  • Hatcher Pass region
  • Glenn Highway corridor
  • Interior Alaska

Common travel options for aurora viewing:

  1. Fly to Fairbanks.
  2. Rent a vehicle or join a guided aurora tour.
  3. Travel away from city lights for better viewing conditions.

Regional Transportation Overview

Southcentral Alaska

  • Anchorage
  • Mat-Su Valley
  • Girdwood
  • Kenai Peninsula
  • Valdez

Best transportation options:

  • Rental car
  • Railroad
  • Shuttle services

Interior Alaska

  • Fairbanks
  • Delta Junction
  • North Pole

Best transportation options:

  • Rental car
  • Flights
  • Railroad

Southeast Alaska

  • Juneau
  • Sitka
  • Ketchikan
  • Skagway

Best transportation options:

  • Flights
  • Alaska Marine Highway ferries

Most Southeast Alaska communities are not connected by roads.

Western Alaska

  • Nome
  • Bethel
  • Kotzebue

Best transportation options:

  • Commercial airlines
  • Regional air taxis

Arctic Alaska

  • Utqiaġvik (Barrow)

Best transportation option:

  • Commercial flights

There are no roads connecting Utqiaġvik to the rest of Alaska's road system.

Recommended Winter Travel Strategies

1-Week Itinerary

  1. Fly into Anchorage.
  2. Explore Southcentral Alaska by rental car.
  3. Fly to Fairbanks.
  4. Depart from Fairbanks or return to Anchorage.

2-Week Itinerary

  1. Explore Anchorage and the Kenai Peninsula.
  2. Visit Talkeetna and the Mat-Su Valley.
  3. Spend several days in Fairbanks for winter activities and aurora viewing.
  4. Add an optional side trip to Juneau or Sitka.

3+ Week Itinerary

  1. Explore Southcentral Alaska.
  2. Visit Interior Alaska.
  3. Travel through Southeast Alaska.
  4. Add optional visits to Western or Arctic Alaska using regional flights.

Bottom Line

Traveling through Alaska during winter often requires a combination of transportation methods. Use rental cars within Southcentral and Interior Alaska, take the Alaska Railroad for comfortable travel along major corridors, rely on commercial flights for travel between regions, and use ferries when exploring Southeast Alaska. Flexibility is important, as weather conditions can affect every mode of transportation during the winter season.
