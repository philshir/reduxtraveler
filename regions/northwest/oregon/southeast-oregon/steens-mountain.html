Steens Mountain

How to Get There

From Burns (60 mi): Drive south on OR-205 toward Frenchglen, then follow signs for Steens Mountain Loop Road, which climbs into the mountain’s upper elevations and provides access to viewpoints and trailheads.

Overview

Steens Mountain is a massive fault-block mountain rising to 9,738 feet in southeastern Oregon, protected as the Steens Mountain Cooperative Management and Protection Area, managed by the Bureau of Land Management. The mountain has a gentle western slope and a steep eastern face that drops nearly a mile to the Alvord Desert. Glaciers carved huge gorges, including Kiger Gorge and Big Indian Gorge, and the high country holds alpine meadows, aspen, wildflowers and wild horses. The Steens Mountain Loop Road, a gravel route, climbs to overlooks and trailheads, and visitors come for the views, wildflowers and wide-open solitude.

Access

The mountain is about 60 miles south of Burns and reached by driving OR-205 to Frenchglen and then the Steens Mountain Loop Road. The loop is a 52-mile gravel road that climbs from Frenchglen, and it is typically open from late June through October depending on snow. High-clearance vehicles are helpful, though many passenger cars manage in good weather. There are almost no services on the mountain, so bring fuel, food and water.

Seasons

The high country is accessible mainly from July to October. Early summer brings snow and wildflowers, July and August are warm days and cool nights, and fall offers golden aspen. The loop road closes with snow, usually by late October. Weather is unpredictable at high elevation, with thunderstorms and cold winds. Lower areas are open longer, and winter access is limited.

Things to Do

Drive the Steens Mountain Loop Road

The 52-mile loop climbs to the high rim, passing glacial gorges and wide-open views. It is the best way to experience the mountain.

See Kiger Gorge

Kiger Gorge is a huge U-shaped glacial valley seen from a viewpoint on the loop road. The aspen and meadows below are stunning, especially in fall.

Look Down on the Alvord Desert

From the East Rim Viewpoint, visitors can see the dry lake bed of the Alvord Desert far below. The contrast is dramatic.

Hike to Wildhorse Lake

A trail leads to a small alpine lake beneath the summit. It is a rewarding hike on a clear day.

Camp and Fish in the High Country

Campgrounds at Fish Lake and Jackman Park provide a base. Trout fishing is popular in the creeks and lakes.

Top Trails

Steens Summit Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 300 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A short trail leads from the upper road to the summit area with sweeping views. The air is thin, so go slowly.

Wildhorse Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 600 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail descends to Wildhorse Lake in a cirque below the summit. The climb back is steep.

Kiger Gorge Overlook Path

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path leads to a viewpoint of the gorge. It is a favorite for photographers.

Visitor Centers

The Steens Mountain area has no visitor center, but the BLM Burns District Office and the Frenchglen Hotel area provide maps and information. Signs and kiosks on the loop road explain the geology and rules. The BLM website lists current road conditions and closures. Because there are few services, prepare before entering.

Camping and Lodging

BLM campgrounds include Page Springs, Fish Lake, Jackman Park, Mann Lake and Lily Lake, with primitive facilities and a short season in the high country. Frenchglen has the historic Frenchglen Hotel and a small store, and Diamond has the Hotel Diamond. Burns, about 60 miles away, has chain motels, and Fields Station has a small motel and cafe on the south side. Reserve ahead, and check each property's website for current availability.
