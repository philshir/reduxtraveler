Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area

★5.0
  1. Northwest
  2. Oregon
  3. Oregon Coast
  4. Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area

Themom51, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Vast coastal landscape featuring North America's largest expanse of coastal sand dunes, with opportunities for hiking, wildlife viewing, camping, beach access, and off-highway vehicle recreation.

General Information

Hours:
Open year-round; Ranger District Office hours are Monday-Friday, 8:00am to 4:00pm (closed federal holidays)
Fees:
Some recreation sites, campgrounds, and OHV areas may require day-use or permit fees
Pet Policy:
Dogs are allowed no longer than 6 feet; some beach areas have seasonal restrictions to protect nesting Western Snowy Plovers
Closest cities with hotels:
Reedsport, OR; Florence, OR; North Bend, OR
Seasons:
All year; May through September typically offers the best weather for recreation
Location:
855 Highway 101, Reedsport, OR 97467
Website:
fs.usda.gov/recarea/siuslaw/recreation/recarea/?recid=42465

How to Get There

From Reedsport: Travel along US Highway 101 to one of several access points within the Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area. The main ranger office and visitor information are located at 855 Highway 101 in Reedsport.

Overview

Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area protects about 31,500 acres of the largest expanse of coastal sand dunes in North America, stretching roughly 40 miles along the Oregon coast between Florence and Coos Bay. Wind-sculpted dunes rise as high as 500 feet and are mixed with forest islands, freshwater lakes, wetlands and wide beaches. Managed by the Siuslaw National Forest and created in 1972, the area offers dune hiking, off-highway vehicle riding, camping, fishing and beach walking. People come for the otherworldly landscape, outdoor recreation and wildlife such as ospreys, elk and snowy plovers.

Access

The recreation area runs along US-101 and is entered from many access points between Florence and Coos Bay. Reedsport, in the middle, is the main hub, with the Oregon Dunes Visitor Center on Highway 101. Most visitors drive, and the highway is paved and easy to follow. The drive from Eugene to Florence takes about an hour and a half, and from Portland about 3.5 hours. Many trailheads are short walks from the road, while others require longer hikes across loose sand.

Seasons

The area is open year-round. Summer is the best time for dune riding, hiking and camping, though coastal fog is common. Spring and fall bring fewer crowds and good light for photography, and winter is rainy, windy and dramatic. Some beaches and dune areas close from spring to late summer to protect nesting western snowy plovers. Riders must use designated areas and follow flag and noise rules.

Things to Do

Hike Through North America's Largest Coastal Dunes

Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area has trails that cross rolling dunes to the beach. Walking in the soft sand is a workout, and the views are rewarding.

Ride the Dunes by ATV

Open riding areas such as Spinreel, Riley Ranch and Sand Lake give off-highway vehicle riders miles of sand. Rentals are available in Florence and Winchester Bay.

Camp Beside the Dunes

Campgrounds along the route offer lakeside and forest sites. Many are close to the dunes and beaches.

Fish and Paddle the Lakes

Freshwater lakes such as Tahkenitch, Cleawox and Siltcoos provide fishing, kayaking and swimming. They are quiet alternatives to the ocean.

Watch Wildlife and Birds

Snowy plovers, ospreys, herons and deer live here. Elk are sometimes seen in the nearby forest and meadows.

Top Trails

John Dellenback Dunes Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail crosses dunes and forest islands toward the ocean, with soft sand and shifting views. Follow markers carefully, since the dunes look alike.

Oregon Dunes Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short, accessible trail leads to a viewing platform over the dunes and ocean. It is a good first stop.

Tahkenitch Dunes Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The trail travels through coastal forest and wetlands before climbing onto the dunes. It rewards hikers with sand and beach solitude.

Visitor Centers

The Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area Visitor Center, at 855 Highway 101 in Reedsport, is the main stop for maps, exhibits, passes and ranger advice. It is typically open on weekdays during standard hours and more days in summer. The Umpqua Discovery Center in Reedsport provides additional information on dunes and wildlife. Check the Forest Service website for current hours and OHV rules.

Camping and Lodging

Campgrounds include Honeyman State Park, Tahkenitch, Carter Lake, Waxmyrtle and Spinreel, with hundreds of sites, along with yurts and cabins at some state parks. Florence, at the north end, has hotels and resorts such as Driftwood Shores and Old Town inns, and Reedsport, Winchester Bay and North Bend provide additional motels and chain hotels. Coos Bay offers the Mill Casino Hotel and other options. Book early for summer weekends, and check each property's website for current availability.
