How to Get There

Overview

Newberry National Volcanic Monument protects about 54,000 acres of volcanic landscapes in the Deschutes National Forest south of Bend, centered on Newberry Volcano, one of the largest volcanoes in the Cascade Range. The monument includes a caldera with two lakes, Paulina and East, along with lava flows, cinder cones, lava tubes and the glassy Big Obsidian Flow. Established in 1990, it shows a wide range of volcanic features within a short drive of Bend. Visitors come to explore lava caves, climb Lava Butte, hike beside waterfalls and lakes and camp in the caldera.

Access

The monument lies about 13 miles south of Bend, with the Lava Lands area right off US-97 and the caldera reached via Forest Road 21 about 25 miles farther. Most visitors begin at the Lava Lands Visitor Center, then drive to Lava Butte, Lava River Cave and the Lava Cast Forest. The road to Paulina and East lakes is paved but climbs steeply, and it closes in winter with snow. Bend is about 3 hours from Portland and the roads are well maintained.

Seasons

The caldera is typically open from late spring to fall, depending on snow, and the Lava Lands area has limited winter hours. Summer is the busiest season, with warm days and cool nights, and it is the best time for camping and boating on the lakes. Fall offers quiet and golden colors, and winter turns the caldera into a snowy playground for snowmobiling and skiing. Lava River Cave and the Lava Butte shuttle operate seasonally and may require timed reservations.

Things to Do

Explore a Giant Caldera

Newberry National Volcanic Monument's caldera holds Paulina Lake and East Lake, hot springs, a waterfall and a spectacular obsidian flow. It is a rare place to see so many volcanic features in one place.

Walk Through Lava River Cave

This mile-long lava tube is one of Oregon's most popular attractions. Visitors need warm clothing and lights, and timed entry reservations are commonly required in summer.

Climb or Ride to the Top of Lava Butte

A cinder cone just off US-97 rises about 500 feet above the lava fields. A shuttle or a trail leads to the summit, with sweeping views of the Cascades.

Hike the Big Obsidian Flow

This glassy lava flow is the youngest in Oregon, about 1,300 years old. A short trail climbs through shining black obsidian.

Fish, Paddle and Camp at Paulina and East Lakes

The lakes offer fishing, kayaking and camping. Resorts and campgrounds are open in the warm season.

Top Trails

Big Obsidian Flow Trail

Length: Approximately 0.7 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy

This loop climbs onto the flow, crossing shiny black obsidian. Stay on the path, since the glass is sharp.

Lava Butte Rim Trail

Length: Approximately 0.25 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A path circles the crater's rim with views of the lava fields and the Cascade volcanoes. The trail starts at the summit, reached by shuttle or road.

Paulina Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path leads to a viewpoint of Paulina Falls, a double waterfall, and the base. It is beautiful in spring and early summer.

Lava River Cave

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Visitors walk through a lava tube with a cool, steady temperature and dark ceilings. Bring a lantern or headlamp, warm layers and sturdy shoes.

Visitor Centers

The Lava Lands Visitor Center, at the base of Lava Butte, is the main stop and has exhibits on the volcanic story, a theater, a bookstore and rangers who provide maps and advice. It is open seasonally, typically from late spring through early fall, with limited winter hours. Another visitor facility operates in the caldera in summer. Check the Deschutes National Forest website for current hours and reservation requirements.

Camping and Lodging

The caldera has campgrounds at Paulina Lake, East Lake, Little Crater and Cinder Hill, and rustic cabins and lodges at Paulina Lake Lodge and East Lake Resort operate in the summer. Bend, 20 to 40 minutes away, has a large range of lodging, from boutique hotels such as the Oxford Hotel and Tetherow to chain hotels. Sunriver, about 20 minutes south, has the Sunriver Resort and vacation rentals, and La Pine has simple motels. Check each property's website for current availability.
