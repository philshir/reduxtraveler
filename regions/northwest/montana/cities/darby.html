How to Get There

From Missoula (85 mi): Take US-93 S through Hamilton and continue south to reach Darby in the Bitterroot Valley.

Overview

Darby is a small town in the southern Bitterroot Valley of western Montana, between the Bitterroot Mountains and the Sapphire Mountains, and it is known for its log buildings, its fly fishing, and its quiet, small-town character. With a population of about 800, it is a gateway to the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness and the Bitterroot National Forest, and a base for outdoor adventures. The valley's sunny climate and open meadows give it the nickname "Banana Belt" of Montana.

Visitors come to Darby for fly fishing on the Bitterroot River and its tributaries, for the Darby Pioneer Memorial Museum, and for hiking in the nearby mountains. They also come for the historic Alta Ranger Station, the Painted Rocks Lake, and a quiet stop on U.S. Highway 93, the route Lewis and Clark followed in 1805.

History

The Salish people lived in the Bitterroot Valley, and in 1805 Lewis and Clark's expedition passed through the southern end of it, at Ross's Hole, near Sula, where they met the Salish. White settlement in the valley began in the 1850s with St. Mary's Mission, and Darby was settled in the 1880s as a timber and ranching community. The Darby Ranger Station, built in 1911, was one of the first ranger stations in the country, and the town became a center for the U.S. Forest Service in the region. Today the town's economy depends on logging, ranching, and tourism.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Darby Pioneer Memorial Museum, in a log building, has exhibits on the valley's pioneer past, and the Darby Ranger Station, a log building from 1911 and now a historic district, shows early Forest Service history. The Alta Ranger Station Historic District, in the mountains, has the oldest ranger station buildings, and the town's Main Street has a log-built library and old-fashioned stores. Lost Horse Canyon, a short drive, has a waterfall and trails.

Culture and Museums

The Darby Pioneer Memorial Museum is the main cultural site, and the Darby Community Library and the Darby Historic Ranger Station have local history. The Ravalli County Museum, in nearby Hamilton, has more exhibits, and the Daly Mansion is a short drive. Darby Logger Days is a local event.

Parks

The Bitterroot National Forest surrounds Darby, with more than 1.6 million acres of forest, trails, and campgrounds, and the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness is one of the largest wilderness areas in the lower 48 states. The Bitterroot River is a famous trout stream, and Painted Rocks Lake, Lake Como, and Lost Horse Canyon are nearby. Skalkaho Falls and Trapper Peak, the highest peak in the Bitterroots, offer hikes.

Seasons and Weather

Darby has a semi-arid continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. The valley is generally milder and drier than the rest of western Montana, and summer wildfire smoke can occur.

Things to Do

Experience the Gateway to Montana's Bitterroot Valley

Darby is a charming mountain town in southwestern Montana, nestled in the scenic Bitterroot Valley and surrounded by the Bitterroot Mountains and Bitterroot National Forest. Known for its outdoor recreation, historic charm, and access to remote wilderness areas, Darby serves as an excellent base for exploring some of Montana's most spectacular landscapes.

Explore Bitterroot National Forest

The Bitterroot National Forest surrounds Darby and provides access to extensive hiking, camping, fishing, wildlife viewing, horseback riding, and backpacking opportunities. Visitors can enjoy pristine mountain scenery, rivers, alpine lakes, and vast wilderness areas throughout the region.

Visit Lake Como

Lake Como is one of the area's most popular outdoor destinations. Visitors can enjoy hiking, swimming, kayaking, paddleboarding, boating, fishing, picnicking, and relaxing along the shoreline while taking in beautiful views of the surrounding mountains.

Drive to Painted Rocks State Park

Located along the scenic West Fork of the Bitterroot River, Painted Rocks State Park features a beautiful reservoir surrounded by mountains. The area is popular for boating, fishing, camping, photography, and wildlife viewing.

Explore Historic Downtown Darby

Downtown Darby offers a glimpse into Montana's western heritage with locally owned shops, restaurants, historic buildings, and a welcoming small-town atmosphere. Visitors can learn about the area's past while enjoying the community's western character.

Visit the Darby Pioneer Memorial Museum

The Darby Pioneer Memorial Museum showcases the history of the Bitterroot Valley through exhibits, artifacts, photographs, and educational displays that highlight local culture, pioneer life, and regional development.

Enjoy Winter Recreation

During winter months, visitors can access nearby Lost Trail Powder Mountain for skiing and snowboarding. Snowshoeing, cross-country skiing, snowmobiling, and winter wildlife viewing are also popular throughout the area.

Top Trails

Lake Como Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This scenic trail circles Lake Como and provides excellent views of the surrounding Bitterroot Mountains. The route is popular for hiking, wildlife viewing, photography, and enjoying lakeside scenery.

Trapper Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 8 to 10 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

Trapper Peak is the highest summit in the Bitterroot Range. Hikers are rewarded with sweeping views of the Bitterroot Valley, surrounding wilderness areas, alpine lakes, and rugged mountain landscapes.

Little Rock Creek Trail

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Significant
Difficulty: Moderate to Challenging

This scenic backcountry trail follows Little Rock Creek through forests and mountain valleys. The route is known for wildflowers, solitude, and access to remote wilderness scenery.

Baker Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 4 to 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

The trail leads hikers to a scenic alpine lake surrounded by mountain peaks. Baker Lake is a popular destination for fishing, photography, and enjoying the beauty of the Bitterroot Mountains.

Blodgett Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 4 to 12 miles round trip depending on destination
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the Bitterroot Valley's most famous hikes, Blodgett Canyon offers dramatic granite cliffs, waterfalls, forest scenery, and excellent opportunities for wildlife viewing and photography.
