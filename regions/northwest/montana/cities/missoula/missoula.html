Overview

Missoula is a city in western Montana, in a valley where five mountain ranges and three rivers meet, and it is the home of the University of Montana and the center of the region's outdoor culture. It is known for its smokejumpers, who parachute into wildfires, for its vibrant downtown with independent shops and breweries, and for being the setting of Norman Maclean's A River Runs Through It. The Clark Fork River flows through the middle of the city, and mountains rise directly above it.

Visitors come to Missoula for the Smokejumper Visitor Center, the National Museum of Forest Service History, and the Historical Museum at Fort Missoula. They also come for Caras Park and its Carousel, for the "M" Trail, and for float trips, fishing, and skiing.

History

The Salish, Kootenai, and Pend d'Oreille peoples lived in the area, which they called "place of frozen water," and Lewis and Clark passed through in 1805 and 1806. A trading post was set up in 1860 in Hellgate Canyon, and the town of Missoula was established in 1864, and it grew with the Northern Pacific Railroad's arrival in 1883, and as a timber center. Fort Missoula was established in 1877, and during World War II it was used as an internment camp for Italian and Japanese Americans. The University of Montana was founded in 1893, and in 1939 the first smokejumpers parachuted into a forest fire from Missoula.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Downtown has historic brick buildings with shops, restaurants, and breweries, and the Hip Strip is a funky street of cafes and stores. Caras Park on the riverfront has the Carousel for Missoula, a hand-carved carousel, and the Clark Fork River's Brennan's Wave is a surf wave in the river. The Missoula County Courthouse, with a mural by Edgar Paxson, and the "M" on Mount Sentinel are landmarks.

Culture and Museums

The Historical Museum at Fort Missoula has exhibits on the fort, the Buffalo Soldiers, and the WWII internment, and the Missoula Art Museum shows contemporary art. The Montana Museum of Art & Culture at the university has a collection of Montana art, and the Boone and Crockett Club has a collection of trophy animals. The Smokejumper Visitor Center has tours of the loft where jumpers sew their gear.

Parks

The "M" Trail zigzags up Mount Sentinel to a big white M on the hillside, and Mount Jumbo has views and wildlife viewing in spring. The Rattlesnake National Recreation Area and Wilderness, just north of town, has trails and a creek, and the Clark Fork and Blackfoot Rivers are used for tubing, fishing, and rafting, with the setting of A River Runs Through It. Lolo Pass and Snowbowl ski area are nearby, and Glacier National Park is about three hours north.

Seasons and Weather

Missoula has a semi-arid continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and cloudy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. In summer, wildfire smoke can affect the air.
