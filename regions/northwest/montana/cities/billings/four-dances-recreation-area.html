Four Dances Recreation Area

★5.0

Natural plateau preserve featuring hiking trails, river overlooks, and native grassland ecosystems with cultural and ecological significance.

General Information

Hours:
Daily: 6am to 9pm
Fees:
Free
Pet Policy:
Pets must be leashed; off-leash not permitted
Seasons:
March through September
Location:
1920 Coburn Rd, Billings, MT 59101blm.gov/visit/four-dances-srma

How to Get There

From Billings, take I-90 E to Lockwood, then south on Coburn Road for 2 miles to the trailhead parking area.

Overview

Four Dances Natural Area is a 765-acre Bureau of Land Management area on a sandstone bluff above the Yellowstone River, a few miles south of Billings. The area is named for a Crow story about a battle, and a prominent hill was a place of ceremony. Ponderosa pines, juniper and prairie cover the bluff, and trails lead to viewpoints over the river and the Billings Rims. The area is popular for hiking, birdwatching and picnics. Visitors come for easy access to nature, with views of the Beartooth and Pryor mountains on clear days.

Access

Four Dances is about 5 miles southeast of Billings, off Coburn Road in Lockwood, with a trailhead parking lot near the entrance. Visitors take I-90 to the Lockwood exit and follow Coburn Road for about 2 miles. The road is paved, and the drive takes about 15 minutes from downtown. Most visitors arrive by car.

Seasons

The area is open daily from spring through fall, typically with hours from morning to evening, and is closed in the winter months. Summer is warm and dry, and best explored in the morning. Spring brings wildflowers and green grass, and fall offers cool weather. Snow and ice can close the area in winter. Rattlesnakes are present in warm months, so watch your step.

Things to Do

Hike Above the Yellowstone River

Four Dances Natural Area's trails climb a bluff with views of the river, the city and the mountains. The area is a short drive from Billings.

Birdwatch in the Pines

The ponderosa pines attract raptors and songbirds. Spring and fall are good times.

Enjoy a Picnic With a View

Picnic tables near the trailhead give a spot to relax. The views are open and bright.

Learn About the Crow Story

Signs describe the Battle of Four Dances and the area's cultural history. They add depth to the hike.

Combine With Pictograph Cave

The Pictograph Cave State Park is nearby. Together they make a half-day outing.

Top Trails

Four Dances Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This loop climbs the bluff and circles the plateau, with views of the river. It is exposed, so bring water.

Four Dances Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 200 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A shorter route leads to an overlook with views of the Yellowstone Valley. It is a good option for a quick visit.

Visitor Centers

Four Dances Natural Area does not have a visitor center. A kiosk at the trailhead provides maps, rules and safety information, and the Bureau of Land Management website lists seasonal closures. The Billings visitor center and Pictograph Cave State Park offer additional information.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping at the natural area. Billings, about 20 minutes away, has a wide range of hotels, including the Northern Hotel, the Crowne Plaza and many chain properties, and campgrounds such as Billings KOA. Lockwood has a few simple stays. Check each property's website for current availability.
