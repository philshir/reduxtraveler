How to Get There

From Bozeman, MT (52 mi): Take US-84 W through Norris, then continue south on US-287 to Ennis.

Overview

Ennis is a small town in the Madison Valley of southwestern Montana, on the banks of the Madison River, and it is known as one of the best fly fishing destinations in the world. The Madison, with its abundant trout and mountain scenery, draws anglers from around the globe. The town of about 900 people has a Western main street with shops, galleries, and restaurants, and it is a gateway to Virginia City, Yellowstone National Park, and the Madison Range.

Visitors come to Ennis for fly fishing and float trips on the Madison River, for the town's Fourth of July rodeo and the Ennis Fly Fishing Festival, and for the nearby ghost towns of Virginia City and Nevada City. They also come for Quake Lake, the Madison Valley's scenery, and hunting and skiing in the area.

History

The Shoshone and Bannock peoples hunted in the Madison Valley, and the Lewis and Clark Expedition passed near the headwaters of the Missouri nearby in 1805. After gold was found at Alder Gulch in 1863, ranchers settled the Madison Valley to supply the mining camps, and the town of Ennis was established in the 1880s, named for William Ennis, who owned the land. The valley became a cattle ranching area, and in the twentieth century anglers discovered the Madison. In 1959, a magnitude 7.3 earthquake triggered a landslide that dammed the Madison River, creating Quake Lake and killing 28 people.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Main Street has false-front buildings with Western shops, galleries, and the Ennis Historic Main Street, and the Madison River runs through the valley, a short drive away. Quake Lake and the Earthquake Lake Visitor Center, 45 miles south, show the results of the 1959 quake, and Virginia City and Nevada City, 14 miles north, are well-preserved gold rush towns. The Madison Valley History Museum has local exhibits.

Culture and Museums

The Madison Valley History Association Museum shows the valley's pioneer and ranching history, and the Ennis Fourth of July Rodeo is one of the biggest events in the area. The Madison Valley Fly Fishing Festival and the Ennis Arts Festival also draw crowds, and the nearby Virginia City has a historic opera house, with summer theater. The town's galleries show Western art.

Parks

The Madison River is a world-class trout stream, and the Lee Metcalf Wilderness, Madison Range, and Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest offer hiking, camping, and hunting. Hebgen Lake and Quake Lake, to the south, have boating and fishing, and Yellowstone National Park's west entrance is about 70 miles away. The Gravelly Range and the Tobacco Root Mountains are scenic backdrops.

Seasons and Weather

Ennis has a semi-arid continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F and cool nights, and winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. The best fishing is from spring to fall, and the salmon fly hatch in June is famous.

Things to Do

Experience Montana's Scenic Madison Valley

Ennis is a historic western town located in the heart of Montana's Madison Valley. Surrounded by the Madison Range, Tobacco Root Mountains, and Gravelly Range, the area is known for its spectacular mountain scenery, outdoor recreation, fly fishing, and authentic small-town charm.

Fish the Legendary Madison River

Ennis is often referred to as the "Fly Fishing Capital of the World" due to its proximity to the renowned Madison River. Anglers travel from around the globe to fish for brown and rainbow trout in one of Montana's most famous blue-ribbon fisheries.

Explore Historic Downtown Ennis

The town's historic Main Street features western-style architecture, local restaurants, galleries, shops, and museums. Visitors can enjoy the welcoming atmosphere while learning about the area's ranching, mining, and frontier heritage.

Visit the Ennis Fish Hatchery

The Ennis National Fish Hatchery offers visitors an opportunity to learn about trout conservation and fishery management in Montana. Educational displays and outdoor viewing areas provide insight into the hatchery's role in supporting regional fisheries.

Explore Nearby Public Lands

Ennis provides convenient access to Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest, the Lee Metcalf Wilderness, and numerous mountain recreation areas. Visitors can enjoy hiking, horseback riding, backpacking, camping, and mountain biking in some of Montana's most scenic landscapes.

Visit Nearby Yellowstone Country

The town is located within driving distance of Yellowstone National Park and serves as a popular stop for travelers exploring southwest Montana's natural attractions, scenic byways, and outdoor adventures.

Top Trails

Bear Trap Canyon Trail

Length: Approximately 9 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Following the Madison River through Bear Trap Canyon, this scenic trail offers impressive canyon views, fishing access, wildlife viewing opportunities, and beautiful rock formations. It is one of the most popular hikes near Ennis.

Hollow Top Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 12.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate to Significant
Difficulty: Moderate

This alpine hike travels through forests, meadows, and mountain terrain before reaching picturesque Hollow Top Lake in the Tobacco Root Mountains. Visitors are rewarded with stunning mountain views and excellent opportunities for wildlife viewing.

Hollowtop Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 7 to 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

Hikers can reach one of the highest peaks in the Tobacco Root Mountains while enjoying panoramic views of the Madison Valley, surrounding mountain ranges, and alpine terrain.

Pioneer Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 6 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail leads to a beautiful waterfall and passes through forests and mountain landscapes. The route is especially popular during summer and early fall.

Bear Basin Trail

Length: Approximately 15 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

A backcountry adventure that offers access to remote wilderness scenery, alpine lakes, mountain meadows, and spectacular views throughout the Tobacco Root Mountains.
