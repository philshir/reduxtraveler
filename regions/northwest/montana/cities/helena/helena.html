How to Get There

From Bozeman (97 mi): Take I-90 west to US-287 north, connect with I-15 north, and continue into Helena in the heart of western Montana.

Overview

Helena is the capital of Montana, in the Prickly Pear Valley of west-central Montana, at the foot of the Rocky Mountains, and it was founded in 1864 after the discovery of gold at Last Chance Gulch. It is known for its Gothic cathedral, its Victorian mansions built on gold money, and the Montana State Capitol, which has paintings by Charles M. Russell. The city is also near the Missouri River's scenic Gates of the Mountains and has access to hiking, fishing, and skiing.

Visitors come to Helena for the State Capitol, the Cathedral of Saint Helena, and the Montana Heritage Center. They also come for Last Chance Gulch, with its downtown pedestrian mall, for Reeder's Alley, and for boat tours of the Gates of the Mountains.

History

On July 14, 1864, four prospectors known as the "Four Georgians" struck gold in a gulch they called Last Chance, and a boomtown of miners sprang up. The camp was named Helena, probably for Helena, Minnesota, and became the territorial capital in 1875 and the state capital in 1894. Placer mining produced about $19 million in gold in the first years, and in the 1880s, Helena was said to have more millionaires per capita than any other city in the world. A series of fires and an earthquake in 1935 damaged the downtown, but many Victorian buildings survived.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Montana State Capitol, completed in 1902, has a copper dome and a collection of western art, including Russell's huge painting Lewis and Clark Meeting the Flathead Indians at Ross' Hole. The Cathedral of Saint Helena, a Gothic Revival church modeled after the Votivkirche in Vienna, has twin spires and stained glass, and Last Chance Gulch is a downtown pedestrian mall following the original gold-bearing creek. Reeder's Alley, built in 1875 for miners, is a historic row of stone buildings, and Acropolis Crest is a hillside viewpoint.

Culture and Museums

The Montana Historical Society's Heritage Center has a large collection of Western art, including Russell's work, and exhibits on Montana's history. The Original Governor's Mansion, a Queen Anne house from 1888, the Holter Museum of Art, and the Archie Bray Foundation for the Ceramic Arts are other attractions. The Helena Symphony and the Myrna Loy Center host performances, and the Last Chance Tour Train takes visitors around the historic districts.

Parks

Mount Helena City Park has trails to a summit with views, and the Gates of the Mountains Wilderness, on the Missouri River, can be seen on boat tours that follow the route Lewis and Clark took. Spring Meadow Lake State Park, Hauser Lake, and Canyon Ferry Lake offer boating and fishing, and the Continental Divide Trail passes close to town. Great Northern Town Center and the Great Northern Carousel are in the city.

Seasons and Weather

Helena has a semi-arid continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 45 inches of snow, with chinook winds common. Spring and fall are pleasant but short.
