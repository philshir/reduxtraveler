How to Get There

From Bozeman, MT (85 mi): Take US-84 W to US-287 S through Ennis, then continue west to Nevada City.

Overview

Nevada City is a historic gold rush town in southwestern Montana, a mile and a half from Virginia City, and it is preserved as an outdoor museum of nineteenth-century Montana buildings. Once the center of the Alder Gulch gold fields, which produced about $30 million in gold from 1863 on, it has more than 100 historic structures, many moved from other places in Montana, and a collection of antique music machines. Together with Virginia City, it is one of the best-preserved gold rush sites in the American West.

Visitors come to Nevada City to walk the dusty main street of the Nevada City Open Air Museum, to ride the Alder Gulch Short Line, a steam train to Virginia City, and to see the Nevada City Music Hall. They also come for gold panning and stagecoach rides, and for the history of the vigilantes and the gold rush.

History

In May 1863, a party of prospectors discovered gold at Alder Gulch, and thousands of miners came, founding towns along the gulch, including Nevada City and Virginia City. In the winter of 1863 to 1864, lawlessness grew, and the vigilantes hanged at least 21 suspected road agents, who were allied with Sheriff Henry Plummer. The gold deposits were worked by dredges until the 1950s, and the town declined to a ghost town. In the 1940s, Charles and Sue Bovey began buying and restoring the historic buildings, and the state of Montana acquired the site in 1997.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Nevada City Open Air Museum has more than 100 buildings, including the Nevada City Hotel, saloons, a blacksmith shop, and a schoolhouse, which line the town's main street, and some are open for visits. The Nevada City Music Hall has a collection of automated musical instruments, such as player pianos and orchestrions, and the Alder Gulch Short Line Railroad carries visitors between Nevada City and Virginia City in vintage cars. The Alder Gulch is also home to the Boot Hill cemetery of Virginia City.

Culture and Museums

The Montana Heritage Commission operates Nevada City and Virginia City, with living history programs, with costumed interpreters, who demonstrate gold panning, blacksmithing, and period crafts. The Nevada City Music Hall has one of the largest collections of coin-operated musical machines in the country, and the Virginia City Players put on summer plays at the nearby Opera House. The area hosts Civil War reenactments and Christmas events.

Parks

Alder Gulch is surrounded by the Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest, with trails in the Tobacco Root Mountains and the Gravelly Range. The Madison River, Ennis Lake, and Quake Lake are within an hour's drive, and Yellowstone National Park is about 70 miles away. Visitors can pan for gold at the museum and in nearby creeks.

Seasons and Weather

Nevada City has a high-elevation continental climate, at about 5,800 feet. Summers are warm, with highs in the 70s and 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. The museum is open from May to September, and its buildings are closed in winter.

Things to Do

Step Back into Montana's Gold Rush Era

Nevada City is a preserved historic mining town located in southwest Montana near Virginia City. Originally established during the Alder Gulch Gold Rush of 1863, the town now operates as a living history museum where visitors can experience Montana's frontier heritage through historic buildings, exhibits, and interactive demonstrations.

Explore the Nevada City Living History Museum

One of Nevada City's main attractions is its collection of restored and relocated historic buildings from across Montana. Visitors can walk through authentic homes, businesses, schools, churches, and government buildings that illustrate life during the late 1800s and early 1900s.

Visit the Music Hall Collection

Nevada City is home to one of the largest public collections of antique music machines, player pianos, and mechanical musical instruments in the United States. Demonstrations allow visitors to experience the sounds and entertainment of the Old West.

Ride the Alder Gulch Shortline Railroad

Visitors can enjoy a scenic train ride between Nevada City and Virginia City aboard the Alder Gulch Shortline Railroad. The route travels through the historic mining district while providing insight into the area's rich gold rush history.

Experience Living History Demonstrations

During the operating season, costumed interpreters recreate daily life in a frontier mining town. Demonstrations may include blacksmithing, cooking, school lessons, laundry, and other activities that bring Montana's history to life.

Explore Nearby Virginia City

Located just over a mile away, Virginia City complements a visit to Nevada City with historic streets, museums, shops, restaurants, theaters, and additional gold rush attractions. Together, the two towns offer one of the best preserved Old West experiences in the United States.

Discover Alder Gulch History

Nevada City and the surrounding Alder Gulch area were the center of one of the richest placer gold strikes in Rocky Mountain history. Interpretive exhibits and historic sites throughout the region tell the story of miners, settlers, merchants, and vigilantes who helped shape Montana Territory.

Top Trails

Virginia City to Nevada City Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This pleasant walking route connects Nevada City and Virginia City while offering views of historic Alder Gulch. The trail provides an excellent opportunity to experience both towns while enjoying the surrounding scenery.

Alder Gulch Trail

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy

Following portions of historic Alder Gulch, this trail allows visitors to explore the landscape where the area's famous gold discoveries occurred. Interpretive opportunities help bring Montana's mining history to life.

Historic Nevada City Walking Tour

Length: Approximately 1 mile or less
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A self-guided walking experience through Nevada City's living history museum featuring dozens of historic structures, exhibits, and interpretive displays highlighting life during Montana's frontier era.

Granite Mountain Trail

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

Located in the surrounding mountains, this trail offers panoramic views of the Ruby Valley, Alder Gulch, and southwest Montana's rugged landscapes. It is popular among experienced hikers seeking a more strenuous adventure.

Baldy Mountain Trail

Length: Varies by route
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

This mountain trail rewards hikers with expansive views of the Madison Valley, Ruby Valley, and surrounding mountain ranges. The route provides excellent opportunities for photography and wildlife viewing.
