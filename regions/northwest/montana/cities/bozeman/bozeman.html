Overview

Bozeman is a college town in southwestern Montana, in the Gallatin Valley, surrounded by mountain ranges, and it is the home of Montana State University and one of the best dinosaur museums in the country, the Museum of the Rockies. It is a gateway to Yellowstone National Park, about 90 miles south, and a center for skiing, fly fishing, and outdoor recreation. The city's historic Main Street, with brick buildings, breweries, and independent shops, has made it one of the fastest-growing communities in the Mountain West.

Visitors come to Bozeman for the Museum of the Rockies, for skiing at Bridger Bowl and Big Sky, and for fly fishing on the Gallatin, Madison, and Yellowstone Rivers. They also come for Bozeman Hot Springs, for hiking on the College "M" Trail and Drinking Horse Mountain, and for the downtown restaurants.

History

The Crow, Shoshone, and Blackfeet peoples hunted in the Gallatin Valley, and Lewis and Clark passed through in 1806. Bozeman was founded in 1864 by John Bozeman, who opened the Bozeman Trail to the Montana gold fields, and it grew as a supply center for settlers and farmers, with the first Montana State Agricultural College established in 1893. The arrival of the Northern Pacific Railway in 1883 helped it grow, and in the late twentieth century it developed into a center for tourism, technology, and recreation. Montana State University, founded in 1893, has about 17,000 students.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Main Street has historic buildings with restaurants, galleries, and shops, and the Gallatin History Museum is in the old county jail. The Museum of the Rockies has one of the largest collections of dinosaur fossils in the world, including a Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton, and a planetarium. The American Computer and Robotics Museum shows the history of computing, and the nearby Madison Buffalo Jump State Park is a Native American site.

Culture and Museums

The Museum of the Rockies, run by Montana State University, was the museum of paleontologist Jack Horner, and has a Smithsonian affiliation, with exhibits on Yellowstone, Native American cultures, and the Gallatin Valley. The American Computer and Robotics Museum has historical computers, and the Emerson Center for the Arts and Culture hosts exhibits and concerts. Events include the Sweet Pea Festival in August and the Big Sky Documentary Film Festival.

Parks

Bozeman is surrounded by the Gallatin National Forest, the Bridger Range, the Gallatin Range, and the Spanish Peaks, with trails such as the "M" Trail, which climbs to a big white M on a hillside, and Drinking Horse Mountain. Hyalite Canyon, a short drive south, has waterfalls, ice climbing, and a reservoir, and Bridger Bowl is a ski area 16 miles north. Yellowstone National Park's north entrance is at Gardiner, about 90 miles away.

Seasons and Weather

Bozeman has a continental climate at an elevation of 4,800 feet. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F and cool nights, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 80 inches of snow. Ski season runs from late November to April, and summer is popular for hiking and fishing.
