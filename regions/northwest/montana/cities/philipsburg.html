How to Get There

From Butte, MT (60 mi): Take I-90 W to Drummond, then follow MT-1 S (Pintler Scenic Route) for 25 miles to reach downtown Philipsburg.

Overview

Philipsburg is a small, well-preserved Victorian mining town in the Flint Creek Valley of western Montana, about 50 miles from Anaconda and Missoula. Founded in 1867 after the discovery of silver, it has a historic main street of brick buildings, a famous old-fashioned candy store, and nearby sapphire fields where visitors can dig for gems. With about 800 residents, it has a quiet, nostalgic charm and is a gateway to the Pintler Scenic Route.

Visitors come to Philipsburg for the Sweet Palace candy store, for sapphire panning at Gem Mountain, and for the nearby Granite ghost town. They also come for the Philipsburg Brewing Company, for the Discovery Basin ski area, and for the town's historic Main Street, the Granite County Museum, and the Opera House.

History

Prospectors found silver in the Flint Creek Valley in 1864, and the town of Philipsburg was platted in 1867 and named for Philip Deidesheimer, a German-born mining engineer. The nearby Granite Mountain mine, discovered in 1872, became one of the richest silver mines in the world, producing $40 million until the Panic of 1893, when the price of silver crashed, and the town of Granite, nearby, was abandoned. Philipsburg survived by mining manganese, and today the town has a number of historic buildings from the 1880s. Sapphires, found in the area's gravel, have been mined since the 1890s.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Broadway Street has a row of brick buildings, such as the Sweet Palace, a Victorian-era candy store with more than 1,000 varieties of candy, a saltwater taffy machine, and a soda fountain, and the Opera House and the Granite County Museum, which have exhibits on the area's history. The Philipsburg Mercantile and the Hotel Broadway are historic buildings, and the Philipsburg Brewing Company makes handcrafted beer. Granite Ghost Town State Park, an abandoned silver mining town, is a short drive.

Culture and Museums

The Granite County Museum and Cultural Center has exhibits on mining, ranching, and the town's Victorian past, and the Philipsburg Opera House hosts local theater productions. The Sapphire Gallery and Gem Mountain offer sapphire mining and sales, with a chance to find a gem in a bucket of gravel. The Pintler Scenic Route leads to Georgetown Lake and Anaconda.

Parks

Philipsburg is surrounded by the Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest, and Discovery Basin Ski Area, near Georgetown Lake, offers skiing in winter. Georgetown Lake is popular for fishing and boating, and the Pintler Wilderness has hiking trails. Granite Ghost Town State Park has the ruins of an old mining town, and Fairmont Hot Springs is a short drive.

Seasons and Weather

Philipsburg has a cool continental climate at an elevation of 5,200 feet. Summers are warm, with highs in the 70s and 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 60 inches of snow. Summer is the busiest season, with a number of festivals and events.

Things to Do

Discover a Historic Montana Mining Town

Philipsburg is a charming mountain town in western Montana known for its rich mining heritage, beautifully preserved historic downtown, outdoor recreation, and scenic setting between the Pintler Mountains and Flint Creek Valley. Originally established during Montana's silver mining boom, the town today offers a blend of history, culture, and adventure.

Explore Historic Downtown Philipsburg

Downtown Philipsburg features restored nineteenth-century buildings, locally owned shops, restaurants, galleries, and historic landmarks. Visitors can stroll along Broadway Street and experience the atmosphere of one of Montana's best-preserved mining communities.

Visit Granite Ghost Town State Park

Located high in the mountains above Philipsburg, Granite Ghost Town preserves remnants of one of Montana's most productive silver mining camps. Visitors can explore historic structures, mining ruins, and interpretive exhibits while enjoying panoramic mountain views.

Try Sapphire Mining

Philipsburg is famous for sapphire mining opportunities. Several local businesses allow visitors to sift through sapphire-rich gravel and search for gemstones that can be cut and polished into jewelry or keepsakes.

Relax at Georgetown Lake

A short drive from town, Georgetown Lake offers year-round recreation including boating, fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, swimming, camping, and wildlife viewing. The lake is surrounded by mountain scenery and is one of the region's most popular outdoor destinations.

Explore the Pintler Mountains

The nearby Anaconda-Pintler Wilderness provides access to alpine lakes, mountain peaks, forests, and wilderness landscapes. Hikers, backpackers, and photographers enjoy the area's rugged scenery and extensive trail network.

Visit Local Museums and Historic Attractions

Philipsburg's museums and interpretive attractions tell the story of Montana's mining era, railroad development, and frontier heritage. Visitors can learn about the people and industries that helped shape the region.

Top Trails

Boulder Lakes Trail

Length: Approximately 6 to 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the area's most popular hikes, Boulder Lakes Trail leads visitors through forests and mountain terrain to a series of beautiful alpine lakes nestled beneath the Pintler Mountains.

Echo Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This family-friendly hike leads to a scenic mountain lake surrounded by forested hills and wilderness scenery. It is a popular option for visitors seeking a shorter outdoor adventure.

West Pintler Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 10 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

Hikers climbing West Pintler Peak are rewarded with sweeping views of the Anaconda-Pintler Wilderness, Georgetown Lake, and surrounding mountain ranges. The route is best suited for experienced hikers.

Warren Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

This scenic mountain hike travels through alpine terrain and offers spectacular views of the surrounding wilderness, lakes, and forested valleys.

Storm Lake and Storm Lake Pass Trail

Length: Approximately 10 to 11 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Challenging

This wilderness route leads to beautiful alpine lakes and mountain passes while offering access to some of the most dramatic scenery in the Philipsburg area.
