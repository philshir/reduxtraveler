Beaverhead Rock State Park

★5.0
  1. Northwest
  2. Montana
  3. Beaverhead Rock State Park

PD-USGov-Interior-FWS, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Prominent limestone landmark celebrated for its connection to the Lewis and Clark Expedition, featuring scenic views and interpretive displays highlighting exploration of the American West.

fwp.mt.gov

How to Get There

From Dillon (15 mi): Take I-15 south from Dillon, follow signs toward Beaverhead Rock State Park near Twin Bridges, and continue to the park entrance along the Beaverhead River corridor.

Overview

Beaverhead Rock State Park protects a prominent landmark in the Beaverhead River valley of southwestern Montana, a large rock outcrop that resembles a swimming beaver's head. Sacajawea, the Shoshone guide of the Lewis and Clark expedition, recognized the rock in 1805 and told the explorers they were nearing her people's homeland, a key moment in the journey. The rock was also a guidepost for Native peoples and later for fur trappers, miners and settlers. The small, undeveloped state park offers a place to stop, read about the expedition and enjoy the open valley.

Access

The park is along MT-41 between Twin Bridges and Dillon, about 14 miles south of Twin Bridges and 15 miles north of Dillon, in Madison and Beaverhead counties. Visitors reach it by car along the highway, and the rock is visible from the road. Parking and interpretive signs are close to the rock. Bozeman is about 1.75 hours away, and Butte about 1.25 hours.

Seasons

The park is open year-round during daylight hours. Summer is the most pleasant, with warm days and long light. Fall offers cool, clear weather and golden cottonwoods along the river. Winters are cold and snowy, and the valley can be icy and windy. Spring runoff makes the Beaverhead River fast. The site has no facilities, so bring water.

Things to Do

See the Rock Sacajawea Recognized

Beaverhead Rock State Park's landmark is a prominent rock that can be seen for miles. It marks the spot where the expedition knew they were near the Shoshone.

Read the Interpretive Signs

Signs describe the rock's role in the Lewis and Clark journey and in the lives of Native peoples. They add context to the roadside stop.

Photograph the Valley

The open valley, the Ruby Mountains and the rock make a classic Montana scene. Early morning and evening light are best.

Fish the Beaverhead River

The river nearby is known for trout fishing. Access points are available along the highway.

Visit Dillon and Twin Bridges

Dillon has the Beaverhead County Museum, and Twin Bridges has fishing shops and a small-town feel. They are good additions to a day trip.

Top Trails

Beaverhead Rock Viewing Path

Length: Less than 0.25 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path leads from the parking area to a good vantage point of the rock. It is level and accessible.

Beaverhead River Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

Informal paths along the river nearby offer birdwatching and fishing access. Be respectful of private land.

Visitor Centers

Beaverhead Rock State Park has no visitor center or staff. Interpretive panels at the site describe the history of the rock and the expedition. For more information, check the Montana State Parks website, and the visitor centers in Dillon and Twin Bridges provide maps and regional advice.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the park. Dillon, about 20 minutes south, has chain hotels such as the Best Western Paradise Inn and local motels, and Twin Bridges has a few small inns and fishing lodges. Campers can use Clark Canyon Reservoir campgrounds south of Dillon and national forest sites in the Ruby and Tendoy ranges. Butte, about 1.25 hours north, has more lodging. Check each property's website for current availability.
