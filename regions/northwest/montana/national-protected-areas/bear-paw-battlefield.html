Bear Paw Battlefield

★5.0

A preserved battlefield, interpretive trail, and memorial landscape commemorating the final battle of the Nez Perce War of 1877 and the resilience of the Nez Perce people.

How to Get There

From Chinook (16 mi): Travel south from Chinook on Cleveland Road/Route 240 for approximately 16 miles across the northern Montana plains to reach the battlefield entrance and interpretive trailhead.

Overview

Bear Paw Battlefield is a quiet site on the northern Montana plains that preserves the place where the Nez Perce flight of 1877 came to an end. After a 1,170-mile journey across four states, Chief Joseph and about 700 Nez Perce men, women and children were stopped here, just 40 miles from the Canadian border, by U.S. Army troops in a five-day battle and siege from September 30 to October 5. Chief Joseph's surrender speech, which includes the line "I will fight no more forever," was given here. The site is a unit of Nez Perce National Historical Park, and visitors come to walk the battlefield, read the story and honor the people who lived and died here.

Access

The battlefield is in the Bear Paw Mountains foothills of Blaine County, about 16 miles south of Chinook on Route 240, in a remote part of north-central Montana. Most visitors arrive by car from Chinook or Havre, about 30 miles to the west, and the route is paved to the site with gravel on the last part. The drive from Great Falls takes around 3 hours. There are no services at the site, so bring water and supplies.

Seasons

The battlefield is open year-round during daylight hours, but the climate is demanding. Summer is the best time to visit, with warm days, long light and seasonal ranger programs, although the plains are hot and windy. Fall brings cool, clear weather and is close to the anniversary of the battle, when commemorations are sometimes held. Winters are bitterly cold and snowy, and gravel roads can be difficult, so the site is best seen in the warm months.

Things to Do

Walk the Site of the Nez Perce Surrender

Bear Paw Battlefield's interpretive trail follows the ground where the Nez Perce camp and the army lines stood. Signs describe each stage of the five-day fight.

Hear Chief Joseph's Story

Interpretive signs include Chief Joseph's surrender speech and accounts from Nez Perce and army participants. The story of the Nez Perce flight is one of the most moving in American history.

Join a Ranger-Guided Tour

Seasonal tours, offered by reservation, lead visitors through the battlefield and explain the events. They add detail and tribal perspective.

Visit the Blaine County Museum

In Chinook, the museum displays local history and has information about the battle. It is a good stop before or after the visit.

Enjoy the Plains Landscape

The wide-open plains and the Bear Paw Mountains create a peaceful setting. Birds and pronghorn are often seen.

Top Trails

Bear Paw Battlefield Interpretive Trail

Length: Approximately 1.2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail circles the main battlefield area, with signs at key locations such as the Nez Perce camp and the army positions. The terrain is open and exposed, so bring water and sun protection.

Visitor Centers

Bear Paw Battlefield does not have a staffed visitor center. A parking area and trailhead have interpretive panels, restrooms and a brochure box, and rangers from Nez Perce National Historical Park lead seasonal programs. Additional information is available from the National Park Service website, and the Blaine County Museum in Chinook provides more context.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the battlefield. Chinook, about 20 minutes north, has small motels, restaurants and the Blaine County Museum. Havre, about 45 minutes west, has chain hotels and more services, and campers can use Beaver Creek Park, south of Havre, which has campgrounds in the Bear Paw Mountains. Harlem and Fort Belknap offer simple lodging. Because lodging is limited in this remote region, book ahead, and check each property's website for current availability.
