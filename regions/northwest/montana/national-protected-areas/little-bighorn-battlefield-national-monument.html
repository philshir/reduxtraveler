How to Get There

From Billings (61 miles): Get on I-90 E in Lockwood from 1st Ave N and I-90BL E. Follow I-90 E to US-212 E in Crow Agency. Take exit 510 from I-90 E. Continue on US-212 E to your destination in Garryowen.

Overview

Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument preserves the site of the June 25 to 26, 1876 battle in which Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors defeated the 7th Cavalry led by Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer. The fight, known as Custer's Last Stand, left Custer and more than 260 soldiers dead and was the most significant Native American victory in the Plains Indian Wars. The monument, on the Crow Indian Reservation, includes Last Stand Hill, the Reno-Benteen Defense Site, a national cemetery and the Indian Memorial, which honors the warriors. Visitors come to learn about the battle from multiple perspectives and to reflect on the history.

Access

The monument is near Crow Agency in southeastern Montana, about 60 miles southeast of Billings along I-90, and is reached by exit 510 and US-212. The entrance is clearly signed, and a 4.5-mile tour road leads past key sites. Most visitors arrive by car, and the drive from Billings takes about an hour. The visitor center is at the start of the tour road.

Seasons

The monument is open year-round, closing only on Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. Summer is the busiest and hottest, with long hours and ranger talks, and the plains can reach the 90s and 100s. Spring and fall are milder, and the anniversary events in late June draw crowds. Winters are cold and windy, with reduced hours and sometimes snow on the tour road.

Things to Do

Visit Last Stand Hill

Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument's most famous spot has markers for the soldiers and a monument. It offers wide views of the valley.

See the Indian Memorial

The memorial, dedicated in 2003, honors the Native American warriors and uses the symbol of a spirit gate. Its message of respect adds balance to the site.

Drive the Battlefield Tour Road

A 4.5-mile road links Last Stand Hill with the Reno-Benteen Defense Site, with pullouts, markers and interpretive signs. The drive helps visitors understand the sweep of the battle.

Tour the Visitor Center and Museum

The center has exhibits, a film and artifacts, along with a bookstore. Rangers and Native interpreters share their knowledge.

Honor the Fallen at the National Cemetery

The cemetery nearby holds soldiers from many wars. It is a quiet and moving place.

Top Trails

Last Stand Hill Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A paved path leads to Last Stand Hill, the monument and the Indian Memorial, with signs describing the battle. It is accessible and most visitors' first stop.

Deep Ravine Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail descends to the ravine where soldiers' bodies were found. It offers a close look at the terrain.

Reno-Benteen Entrenchment Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A trail around the defensive position shows where the surviving soldiers held out. Signs explain the siege.

Visitor Centers

The Little Bighorn Battlefield Visitor Center and museum, near the entrance, is the main stop, with exhibits, a bookstore, a short film and rangers who lead talks and tours. Hours vary by season, generally 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. in summer and shorter in winter, so check the National Park Service website for current times. The monument is on the Crow Reservation, and tours led by Crow guides are offered locally.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the monument. Hardin, about 15 minutes north, has chain motels and restaurants, and the Little Bighorn Casino in Crow Agency offers a place to eat. Billings, about an hour northwest, has a full range of hotels, including the historic Northern Hotel. Campgrounds are available in Hardin and at Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area. Check each property's website for current availability.
