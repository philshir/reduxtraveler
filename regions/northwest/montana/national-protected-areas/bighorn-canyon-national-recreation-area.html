How to Get There

From Billings (114 miles): Get on I-90 W from S 27th St. Take US-310 E to WY-37 N in Big Horn County. Turn left onto WY-37 N.

Overview

Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area straddles the Montana-Wyoming border and protects the 71-mile Bighorn Lake and the deep canyon of the Bighorn River, with limestone cliffs rising up to 1,000 feet above the water. The lake was created by Yellowtail Dam in 1966, and the surrounding area holds the Pryor Mountain Wild Horse Range, historic ranches, desert, forests and ancient Native American sites. Established in 1966, the recreation area is known for boating, fishing, scenic overlooks and wild horses. People come for the dramatic canyon views, water recreation and a sense of remote country.

Access

The recreation area has two districts: the Fort Smith District in Montana, reached through Hardin and Fort Smith at the north end, and the Lovell District in Wyoming, reached from the town of Lovell at the south end. There is no road through the canyon, and travelers must drive around the Pryor and Bighorn mountains to reach the other side. From Billings, it is about 2 hours to either end. Most visitors arrive by car and use boat launches for lake access.

Seasons

The recreation area is open year-round, but access varies. Summer is the main season, when the Yellowtail Dam Visitor Center is open and the lake is busy with boaters and anglers. Spring and fall are quieter, with pleasant weather and good wildlife watching. Winter brings snow and ice, and some roads and facilities close. Summers can be very hot in the canyon, so hike early and carry water.

Things to Do

See the Canyon From the Overlooks

Bighorn Canyon National Recreation Area has scenic overlooks, such as Devil Canyon Overlook, with views thousands of feet above the lake. The vistas are among the best in the Rocky Mountain region.

Boat, Fish and Camp on Bighorn Lake

The lake offers boating, fishing for walleye, trout and catfish, and camping from marinas and boat ramps. Boat-in campsites provide a quiet escape.

Look for Wild Horses

The adjacent Pryor Mountain Wild Horse Range is home to wild mustangs, which are often seen along the road. Visitors should stay at a safe distance.

Tour Historic Ranches

Ranches such as the Ewing-Snell and Mason-Lovell show how homesteaders lived in the canyon. Interpretive signs and tours are available in season.

Visit the Yellowtail Dam

The dam is the highest in the nation on the Bighorn River, and a visitor center explains its history. Tours are sometimes available.

Top Trails

Devil Canyon Overlook Trail

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short path leads to an overlook with sweeping views of the canyon walls. It is accessible and good for photography.

Bad Pass Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip (portions)
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This route follows an ancient trail through the Pryor Mountains and along the canyon rim, passing historic sites. It is exposed and best in cool weather.

Ewing-Snell Ranch Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk around the historic ranch buildings shows the lives of early settlers. Interpretive signs describe the site.

Visitor Centers

The recreation area has two visitor centers. The Cal S. Taggart Bighorn Canyon Visitor Center in Lovell, Wyoming, is open daily, and the Yellowtail Dam Visitor Center in Fort Smith, Montana, is open from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Both have exhibits, a film, a bookstore, restrooms and rangers who provide maps and advice on boating and wildlife. Check the National Park Service website for hours and closures.

Camping and Lodging

Campgrounds include Horseshoe Bend, Afterbay and Barry's Landing, with tent and RV sites near the lake, and boat-in sites on the water. Lovell, Wyoming, has motels and inns, and Hardin, Montana, has chain hotels. Cody, Wyoming, about 1 hour from Lovell, has many options, including the Irma Hotel, and Billings, about 2 hours away, has a full range of lodging. Check each property's website for current availability.
