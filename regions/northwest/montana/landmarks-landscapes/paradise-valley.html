How to Get There

From Bozeman, MT (53 mi): Take I-90 E to Livingston, then head south on US-89 through the valley toward Gardiner.

Overview

The Paradise Valley is a breathtaking expanse of Montana wilderness nestled between the Absaroka and Gallatin mountain ranges. Carved by the Yellowstone River, the valley is a haven for outdoor enthusiasts, offering fly fishing, hiking, hot springs, and scenic drives. Visitors can soak in historic geothermal pools, explore rustic ranches, or simply enjoy the sweeping views of snow-capped peaks and golden meadows. Wildlife sightings are common, with elk, deer, and eagles often visible from the roadside.

This region also serves as a gateway to Yellowstone National Park, just 15 miles south. The valley’s rich history includes Native American heritage and early pioneer routes, and today it blends rustic charm with modern comforts. Whether you're glamping under the stars or enjoying a gourmet meal at a riverside lodge, Paradise Valley offers a quintessential Montana experience.

Things to Do

Experience One of Montana's Most Scenic Valleys

Paradise Valley stretches between Livingston and Gardiner along the Yellowstone River in south-central Montana. Surrounded by the Absaroka Mountains to the east and the Gallatin Range to the west, the valley is renowned for its breathtaking scenery, abundant wildlife, outdoor recreation, and close proximity to Yellowstone National Park.

Fish the Legendary Yellowstone River

The Yellowstone River flows through the heart of Paradise Valley and is considered one of the premier trout fisheries in the United States. Anglers come from around the world to pursue rainbow, brown, and cutthroat trout while enjoying spectacular mountain scenery.

Relax at Chico Hot Springs

One of the valley's most famous attractions, Chico Hot Springs offers natural mineral pools surrounded by mountain views. Visitors can enjoy soaking, dining, spa services, and a relaxing Montana atmosphere throughout the year.

Explore the Custer Gallatin National Forest

Paradise Valley provides access to extensive public lands within the Custer Gallatin National Forest. Visitors can enjoy hiking, camping, horseback riding, wildlife viewing, photography, and backpacking adventures throughout the surrounding mountains.

Enjoy Wildlife Viewing

The valley is home to elk, deer, bighorn sheep, moose, bald eagles, osprey, and many other species. Wildlife viewing opportunities can be found throughout the area, particularly along the Yellowstone River and nearby mountain foothills.

Take a Scenic Drive Through Paradise Valley

The valley's scenic roads offer panoramic views of ranchlands, mountains, rivers, and wildlife. Many visitors travel between Livingston and Yellowstone National Park through Paradise Valley specifically to enjoy the spectacular landscapes.

Visit Yellowstone National Park

The southern end of Paradise Valley lies near the North Entrance of Yellowstone National Park in Gardiner. The valley serves as an excellent base for exploring Yellowstone's geysers, wildlife, waterfalls, and geothermal features.

Top Trails

Pine Creek Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the area's most popular hikes, this trail follows Pine Creek through a scenic canyon to a beautiful waterfall. Hikers can continue beyond the falls toward Pine Creek Lake for a more challenging adventure.

Pine Creek Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 11 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 3,000 feet
Difficulty: Challenging

This strenuous hike climbs into the Absaroka Mountains and rewards visitors with spectacular alpine scenery, mountain lakes, and dramatic views of the surrounding wilderness.

Passage Creek Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal to Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A family-friendly route leading to a picturesque waterfall. The hike offers beautiful views and relatively gentle terrain, making it a popular choice for visitors seeking a rewarding day hike.

Big Creek Trail

Length: Up to 14 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Gradual
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

Following Big Creek through the mountains, this trail provides opportunities for wildlife viewing, fishing access, backpacking, and peaceful exploration of Montana wilderness landscapes.

Emigrant Peak Trail

Length: Approximately 10 to 12 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Significant
Difficulty: Very Challenging

Emigrant Peak towers above Paradise Valley and offers one of the area's most demanding hikes. Experienced hikers are rewarded with expansive views of the Yellowstone River, Paradise Valley, and surrounding mountain ranges.
