Historic coastal park featuring dramatic ocean cliffs, scenic beaches, lighthouses, military history sites, and spectacular views where a major river meets the Pacific Ocean.

General Information
Hours:
Park grounds: Daily, dawn to dusk
Visitor center and lighthouse hours vary seasonally
Fees:
Washington Discover Pass required for vehicle access
Annual Discover Pass: $30
One-day Discover Pass: $10
Pet Policy:
Leashed pets are allowed on most trails, beaches, and outdoor areas but are prohibited inside park buildings and designated restricted areas
Closest cities with hotels:
Long Beach, Washington (about 5 mi)
Seasons:
All year; summer offers the driest weather, while fall and winter provide dramatic storm watching and whale migration opportunities
Location:
244 Robert Gray Drive, Ilwaco, WA 98624
Website:
parks.wa.gov

How to Get There

From Long Beach (5 mi): Take WA-100 south toward Ilwaco, continue onto Robert Gray Drive, and follow signs directly into the park and lighthouse areas.

Overview

Cape Disappointment State Park occupies a rugged headland at the mouth of the Columbia River in southwestern Washington, where the river meets the Pacific. The park is known for its two historic lighthouses, the Cape Disappointment Lighthouse, built in 1856, and the North Head Lighthouse, built in 1898, as well as the Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center and Civil War-era Fort Canby. The Columbia Bar here is called the Graveyard of the Pacific because of its many shipwrecks. People come for storm watching, hiking, beaches, history and camping on one of the most dramatic stretches of the Pacific Northwest coast.

Access

The park is on the Long Beach Peninsula, about 3 miles southwest of Ilwaco and about 165 miles from Seattle, reached by WA-100 and US-101. Visitors cross the Astoria-Megler Bridge from Oregon or drive from Olympia and Aberdeen. The park entrance is well signed, with roads to the lighthouse, the interpretive center and the campgrounds. A Discover Pass is required for day-use parking.

Seasons

The park is open year-round and each season has its draw. Summer is the driest and busiest, with the lighthouse and interpretive center in full operation, although fog is common. Fall and winter bring storm watching, with huge waves and wind, and gray whales pass in winter and spring. Spring is mild, with wildflowers and fewer crowds. The weather is wet much of the year, so rain gear is essential, and trails can be muddy.

Things to Do

Visit Two Historic Lighthouses

Cape Disappointment State Park has the Cape Disappointment Lighthouse and the North Head Lighthouse, both reached by trails. North Head offers seasonal tours.

Explore the Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center

This hilltop center has exhibits on the Corps of Discovery's arrival at the Pacific. Windows look over the river mouth and ocean.

Watch Storms and Waves

The park is one of the best places in Washington for storm watching, with massive waves breaking at the river bar. Stay on safe viewpoints.

Walk the Beaches

Long, wide beaches such as Benson Beach and Waikiki Beach are good for beachcombing and sunsets. Driftwood and tidepools add interest.

Camp or Stay in a Yurt

The park has one of the largest campgrounds on the coast, plus cabins and yurts. Reservations are essential.

Top Trails

Cape Disappointment Lighthouse Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 250 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail leads from the parking area through forest to the lighthouse on the cliff, with views of the river mouth. It can be muddy.

North Head Lighthouse Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 150 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A trail through coastal forest leads to the lighthouse and viewpoints. The area is exposed and windy.

Discovery Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This long trail links Cape Disappointment with Long Beach along the dunes. Hikers can walk part of it for ocean views.

Visitor Centers

The Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center, in the park, serves as the main visitor center, with exhibits about the expedition and the area, a gift shop and views of the river mouth. Park staff at the entrance and the campground provide maps and advice. Hours vary by season, so check the Washington State Parks website, and note that the lighthouse tours are limited.

Camping and Lodging

The park has a large campground with tent and RV sites, cabins, yurts and vacation houses, and reservations are strongly advised. Ilwaco, 5 minutes away, offers inns and motels, and Long Beach and Seaview on the peninsula have hotels, including the Adrift Hotel and historic Shelburne Inn. Astoria, Oregon, about 30 minutes south across the Astoria-Megler Bridge, has inns such as the Cannery Pier Hotel. Check each property's website for current availability.
