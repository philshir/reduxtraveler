Beacon Rock State Park

★5.0
  1. Northwest
  2. Oregon

Geologic preserve and public recreation area in the Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area and includes Beacon Rock, an 848-foot (258 m) basalt volcanic plug on the north shore of the Columbia River

Location:

Website:
parks.wa.gov

How to Get There

From Vancouver (35 mi): Take WA-14 east along the Columbia River Gorge and follow signs for the park entrance near Beacon Rock and the hiking trail parking areas.

Overview

Beacon Rock State Park protects a 848-foot basalt monolith on the Washington bank of the Columbia River, the core of an ancient volcano that remained after the surrounding rock eroded. Lewis and Clark noted the rock in 1805 and the explorers named it, and a trail built in the early 1900s now zigzags up the face with handrails and bridges. The park also includes Hamilton Mountain, waterfalls, forests and miles of trails, as well as river views toward Oregon. Visitors come for the climb, the views, rock climbing, camping and hiking in the Columbia River Gorge.

Access

The park is on WA-14 about 35 miles east of Vancouver and about 7 miles west of Stevenson, in Skamania County. Visitors drive WA-14 along the Columbia River, with the monolith visible from the road. The Beacon Rock trailhead and main parking area are just off the highway, and the Hamilton Mountain trailhead is a short drive east. Portland is about an hour away.

Seasons

The park is open year-round. Spring brings wildflowers and waterfalls on Hamilton Mountain, summer is busy and warm, and fall offers color. Winter can bring ice and snow, making the Beacon Rock trail slick, and strong east winds. The Gorge is often windy. Rock climbing on Beacon Rock is limited by season for raptor nesting, and a Discover Pass is required.

Things to Do

Climb Beacon Rock

Beacon Rock State Park's famous trail climbs 848 feet by a series of switchbacks and metal bridges. The summit offers sweeping views of the Columbia River Gorge.

Hike Hamilton Mountain

This longer hike climbs through forest past waterfalls and viewpoints to a summit with views of the Gorge. It is among the best day hikes in the area.

Rock Climb on Beacon Rock

The monolith offers a range of climbing routes. Climbers should check seasonal closures and register.

Camp Beside the River

The park has campgrounds along the river and in the forest, with tent and RV sites. It is a popular base for exploring the Gorge.

Boat or Fish on the Columbia

A boat launch gives access to the river, and visitors can fish, paddle or watch barges. The river views are excellent.

Top Trails

Beacon Rock Trail

Length: Approximately 1.8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 650 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail climbs the rock's face with handrails and switchbacks to the summit. Views extend across the Columbia and the Gorge, and the trail is not recommended for those afraid of heights.

Hamilton Mountain Loop

Length: Approximately 7.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

A longer loop climbs past Hardy and Rodney Falls to the summit of Hamilton Mountain. Bring water and allow most of a day.

Rodney Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This portion of the Hamilton Mountain route leads to Hardy and Rodney Falls and the Pool of the Winds. It is shorter and rewarding.

Visitor Centers

Beacon Rock State Park does not have a large visitor center. A ranger station and information kiosks near the main trailheads provide maps, rules and updates on trail conditions. The Columbia River Gorge Interpretive Center in Stevenson and the Washington State Parks website offer additional information.

Camping and Lodging

The park has campgrounds with tent and RV sites, including a campground near the river and a group camp, and reservations are advised in summer. Lodging nearby includes Stevenson, about 10 minutes east, with the Skamania Lodge, a large Gorge resort, and the Bonneville Hot Springs area. Cascade Locks and Hood River across the river, about 20 to 40 minutes away, have inns and hotels, and Vancouver and Portland, about an hour west, offer many more options. Check each property's website for current availability.
