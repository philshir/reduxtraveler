Overview

Boise is the capital and largest city of Idaho, in the Treasure Valley of southwestern Idaho, where the Boise River flows from the foothills of the Rocky Mountains into the high desert. It is known for its outdoor recreation, its tree-lined greenbelt along the river, its blue football field at Boise State University, and its vibrant Basque community, one of the largest in the United States. Boise is one of the fastest-growing cities in the country, with a strong technology sector and a friendly, laid-back downtown.

Visitors come to Boise for the Boise River Greenbelt, for the Basque Block and the Basque Museum, and for the Idaho State Capitol. They also come for the Old Idaho Penitentiary, for the Idaho Botanical Garden, and for hiking, rafting, and skiing within minutes of downtown.

History

The Shoshone and Bannock peoples lived in the valley, and French-Canadian trappers called the area "la rivière boisée," the wooded river, in the early 1800s. The U.S. Army built Fort Boise in 1863 after the discovery of gold in the Boise Basin, and the town grew as a supply center for the mining districts. Boise became the territorial capital in 1864 and the state capital when Idaho became a state in 1890. In the twentieth century, irrigation turned the valley into farmland, and Basque sheepherders arrived in large numbers. The city has grown rapidly since the 1990s, as high-tech companies such as Micron Technology have expanded.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Idaho State Capitol, built of sandstone quarried at nearby Table Rock and completed in 1920, has a copper-covered dome and tours of the interior. The Old Idaho Penitentiary, a territorial prison that operated from 1872 to 1973, is open as a museum, and the Basque Block downtown has restaurants, shops, and cultural sites. Freak Alley Gallery is a mural-covered alley, and Table Rock, a mesa above the city, has a hike with a view and a cross.

Culture and Museums

The Idaho State Museum has exhibits on the state's history, the Boise Art Museum shows contemporary art, and the Basque Museum and Cultural Center tells the story of Basque immigration. The Anne Frank Human Rights Memorial is a quiet park, and the Idaho Military History Museum and the Idaho Museum of Mining and Geology add to the museum scene. The Idaho Shakespeare Festival and the Treefort Music Fest are popular events.

Parks

The Boise River Greenbelt is a 25-mile path along the river, with parks such as Julia Davis Park, which has the Idaho State Museum and Zoo Boise, and Kathryn Albertson Park, a bird sanctuary. The Boise Rose Garden and the Idaho Botanical Garden are next to the Old Penitentiary, and the Foothills have more than 190 miles of trails, including Camel's Back Reserve and Hillside to Hollow. Bogus Basin ski area is 16 miles away, and the Boise River is popular for floating in summer.

Seasons and Weather

Boise has a semi-arid climate with four seasons. Summers are hot and dry, with highs in the 90s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 18 inches of snow. Spring and fall are pleasant, and the city gets about 12 inches of precipitation a year.
