Bruneau Dunes State Park

★5.0
  1. Northwest

Desert park featuring North America's tallest single-structured sand dune, stargazing observatory, sandboarding, and spring-fed lakes for fishing and kayaking.

How to Get There

From Boise (60 mi): Take I-84 east to Exit 95 for ID-78 toward Hammett/Bruneau, then follow signs to Bruneau Dunes State Park.

Overview

Bruneau Dunes State Park in southwestern Idaho is home to the tallest single-structured sand dune in North America, rising about 470 feet above the desert plain. The 4,800-acre park protects a field of dunes formed by wind-blown sand from the Snake River Plain, along with two small lakes, grasslands and sagebrush. It also has the Bruneau Dunes Observatory, one of the few public observatories in Idaho. Visitors come to hike or slide down the dunes, to camp, to fish in the lakes and to stargaze in one of the state's darkest skies.

Access

The park is about 60 miles southeast of Boise and 20 miles south of Mountain Home, reached by taking I-84 to exit 95 and following Idaho 78 south for about 18 miles. The road is paved and well signed, and the drive from Boise takes about an hour and a quarter. Most visitors arrive by car, and a day-use fee applies. The park has a visitor center near the entrance.

Seasons

The park is open year-round, with the best weather in spring and fall. Summer is hot, with sand surface temperatures soaring, so visit early or late. Winter is cold and can be snowy, but the dunes are quiet and clear. The observatory hosts public star parties on weekends from spring through fall. Spring wildflowers appear in good years.

Things to Do

Climb North America's Tallest Single-Structured Dune

Bruneau Dunes State Park's big dune is a workout, with views over the desert from the top. Hikers should bring water and avoid midday heat.

Slide or Sandboard Down

Sandboards can be rented at the visitor center. The slope is great for adventurous visitors.

Stargaze at the Observatory

The Bruneau Dunes Observatory has telescopes for public viewing on scheduled nights. The dark skies reveal stars, planets and nebulae.

Fish and Paddle in the Lakes

Two small lakes at the base of the dunes offer fishing for bass and bluegill, plus paddling. Boating is limited to non-motorized craft.

Camp Beside the Sand

The campground has tent and RV sites and cabins near the dunes. It is a good base for a night under the stars.

Top Trails

Big Sand Dune Trail

Length: Approximately 2.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 470 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

This route climbs the big dune's ridge, with steep, soft sand and wide views. Allow extra time for the descent.

Dunes Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 100 feet
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

A loop explores the base of the dunes and the lakeshore, with views and wildlife. It is good for families.

Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short trail leads to the lakes and fishing spots. It is a calm walk with birds.

Visitor Centers

The Bruneau Dunes visitor center, near the entrance, has exhibits on the dunes, geology and wildlife, a gift shop, restrooms and rangers who answer questions and rent sandboards. Hours vary seasonally, and the observatory holds public events on set evenings. Check the Idaho Parks and Recreation website for schedules and fees.

Camping and Lodging

The park's campground has basic and electric sites, group camps and cabins, and reservations are recommended in busy seasons. Nearby, Mountain Home, about 25 minutes north, has chain hotels such as the Hampton Inn and the Best Western, along with restaurants. Boise, about an hour and a quarter away, has a full range of lodging, including the Grove Hotel and Hotel 43. Bruneau has a few small stays. Check each property's website for current availability.
