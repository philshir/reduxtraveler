How to Get There

From Twin Falls, ID (85 mi): Take US-93 North to Shoshone, then continue on ID-75 North through the Wood River Valley to reach downtown Ketchum.

Overview

Ketchum is a small mountain town in Idaho's Wood River Valley, next to the Sun Valley resort and at the base of Bald Mountain, and it is known for its world-class skiing, its mining history, and as the final home of author Ernest Hemingway. The town, at an elevation of 5,800 feet, has a charming main street with shops, galleries, and restaurants, and a laid-back atmosphere that has attracted celebrities and outdoor enthusiasts. It is surrounded by the Sawtooth National Forest and has access to hiking, fishing, and biking in summer.

Visitors come to Ketchum for skiing on Bald Mountain, for the Hemingway Memorial and his grave in the Ketchum Cemetery, and for the town's galleries and restaurants. They also come for the Sun Valley Center for the Arts, for the Sawtooth National Recreation Area just north, and for hiking, biking, and fly fishing.

History

The Shoshone and Bannock peoples hunted and fished in the valley, and the town was founded in 1880 after the discovery of lead and silver, named for David Ketchum, a trapper and early settler. It was a mining and smelting center until the silver crash of 1893, then a sheep-shipping center, and the arrival of the railroad made it a livestock hub. In 1936, the Union Pacific Railroad's chairman, Averell Harriman, developed the Sun Valley resort nearby, the first destination ski resort in America, and Ketchum became the resort's working town. Ernest Hemingway lived in Ketchum from 1959 and died there in 1961.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Main Street has historic buildings with shops and restaurants, and the Ernest Hemingway Memorial, a bust on a stone pedestal beside Trail Creek, honors the author. Hemingway's grave is in the Ketchum Cemetery, and the Community Library has a Hemingway collection. Bald Mountain, with a vertical drop of 3,400 feet, is accessed by gondola, and Galena Summit has a viewpoint over the Sawtooth Valley. Trail Creek Cabin and the Sun Valley Lodge are in neighboring Sun Valley.

Culture and Museums

The Sun Valley Center for the Arts has exhibits, performances, and classes, and the Ketchum-Sun Valley Heritage and Ski Museum shows the history of the area and its skiing. The Sun Valley Summer Symphony and the Sun Valley Writers' Conference attract visitors, and the Wagon Days celebration each Labor Day weekend has a parade of historic ore wagons. Ketchum's restaurants and music venues reflect the town's lively social life.

Parks

Ketchum is surrounded by the Sawtooth National Forest and the Sawtooth National Recreation Area, with hundreds of miles of trails, alpine lakes such as Redfish Lake and Stanley Lake, and the Sawtooth Mountains. The Wood River Trail is a paved path that runs the length of the valley, and the Big Wood River offers fishing for trout. Craters of the Moon National Monument is about 60 miles southeast.

Seasons and Weather

Ketchum has a high-elevation continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 70s and 80s°F and cool nights, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 80 inches of snow. Ski season runs from late November to April.

Things to Do

Discover Idaho's Premier Mountain Town

Ketchum is a vibrant mountain community in central Idaho that serves as the gateway to Sun Valley Resort and the Sawtooth Mountains. Known for its world-class skiing, outdoor recreation, arts scene, and rich history, Ketchum offers visitors year-round opportunities to explore some of the most beautiful landscapes in the American West.

Explore Historic Downtown Ketchum

Downtown Ketchum features locally owned shops, art galleries, restaurants, breweries, and cultural attractions. The walkable downtown area blends western heritage with a modern mountain-town atmosphere and serves as the center of community life throughout the year.

Experience Sun Valley Resort

Located adjacent to Ketchum, Sun Valley Resort is recognized as America's first destination ski resort and offers skiing, snowboarding, scenic gondola rides, golf, tennis, horseback riding, ice skating, and numerous summer recreation opportunities.

Visit the Ernest Hemingway Memorial and Legacy Sites

Ketchum is closely associated with Ernest Hemingway, who spent time living and writing in the area. Visitors can explore locations connected to his life, including memorials and historical sites that celebrate his lasting connection to the Wood River Valley.

Enjoy Outdoor Adventures in the Sawtooth Region

The mountains surrounding Ketchum provide exceptional opportunities for hiking, mountain biking, fly fishing, rafting, camping, climbing, backpacking, and wildlife viewing. The region is known for its extensive trail network and access to multiple mountain ranges.

Top Trails

Adams Gulch Trail

Length: Approximately 5 to 10 miles depending on route
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the most popular trail systems near Ketchum, Adams Gulch offers scenic mountain views, forested terrain, wildflowers, and excellent opportunities for hiking, trail running, and mountain biking.

Bald Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 3,000 feet
Difficulty: Strenuous

This challenging route climbs Sun Valley's iconic Bald Mountain and rewards hikers with panoramic views of the Wood River Valley, surrounding mountain ranges, and the Sawtooth region.

Proctor Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,500 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A favorite local hike, Proctor Mountain Trail offers scenic overlooks above Ketchum and provides excellent views of Bald Mountain and downtown Ketchum below.

Wood River Trail

Length: Approximately 20+ miles one way
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved multi-use pathway connects Ketchum with nearby communities throughout the Wood River Valley. The route is ideal for walking, running, cycling, wildlife viewing, and enjoying mountain scenery.
