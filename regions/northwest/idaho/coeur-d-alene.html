How to Get There

From Spokane, Washington (34 mi): Take Interstate 90 East across the Washington-Idaho state line and continue directly into downtown Coeur d'Alene.

Overview

Coeur d'Alene is a resort city in northern Idaho, on the shore of Lake Coeur d'Alene, and it is known for its deep blue lake, its mountain scenery, its golf, and its relaxed summer lifestyle. The lake, about 25 miles long, is surrounded by forested hills, with a boardwalk, a public beach, and a resort with a famous floating golf green. The city has about 55,000 residents and has long been a vacation spot for visitors from Spokane, 30 miles west, and from across the Northwest.

Visitors come to Coeur d'Alene for boating, fishing, and swimming on the lake, for the Tubbs Hill hiking trail, and for the Coeur d'Alene Resort and its boardwalk. They also come for the Trail of the Coeur d'Alenes, a paved rail trail, for skiing at Silver Mountain and Schweitzer, and for Winter and Fourth of July events.

History

The Coeur d'Alene people, whose name was given by French-Canadian trappers and means "heart of an awl," have lived in the area for thousands of years, and their reservation is south of the city. The U.S. Army established Fort Sherman here in 1878, and the city was founded in 1887 and grew as a timber and mining center, supplying the silver mines of the nearby Silver Valley. Steamboats carried passengers and ore on the lake, and the Spokane and Inland Empire Railroad brought tourists. Over the twentieth century, as logging declined, the city developed into a vacation destination, with the building of the Coeur d'Alene Resort in 1986.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Coeur d'Alene Resort has the world's only floating golf green, reached by boat, and a 3,300-foot boardwalk, the longest floating boardwalk in the world. Downtown has shops and restaurants along Sherman Avenue, and the McEuen Park and City Beach are popular for swimming and festivals. Tubbs Hill is a 120-acre park on the lakeshore with a hiking trail and views, and the Museum of North Idaho has exhibits on the area's history. Lake cruises on the Coeur d'Alene Lake and nearby Hayden Lake are popular.

Culture and Museums

The Museum of North Idaho shows the logging, mining, and steamboat history of the region, and the Fort Sherman Museum has exhibits on the army post. The Coeur d'Alene Casino and the Coeur d'Alene Tribe's Heyburn State Park and Heritage Park are nearby cultural sites. The Fourth of July fireworks over the lake, the Ironman Coeur d'Alene triathlon, and the holiday lights are popular events.

Parks

The Trail of the Coeur d'Alenes is a 73-mile paved rail trail through the lake and river valley, and Heyburn State Park, the oldest state park in the Northwest, has camping and hiking on the lake's southern end. Tubbs Hill and Canfield Mountain have trails on the city's edge, and the Coeur d'Alene River and the St. Joe River are popular for fishing and floating. Silver Mountain and Schweitzer Mountain offer skiing and mountain biking.

Seasons and Weather

Coeur d'Alene has a warm-summer continental climate. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 60 inches of snow. Summer is the busiest season, and fall color is spectacular around the lake.

Things to Do

Experience Lake Coeur d'Alene

Lake Coeur d'Alene is the city's signature attraction and one of the most scenic lakes in the Pacific Northwest. The clear waters and surrounding mountain views make it a popular destination for boating, swimming, paddleboarding, kayaking, fishing, and sightseeing. Visitors can enjoy lake cruises, waterfront dining, or simply relax along the shoreline.

Hike Tubbs Hill

Located within walking distance of downtown, Tubbs Hill offers one of the area's most popular outdoor experiences. The network of trails winds through forests, rocky outcroppings, and scenic overlooks with spectacular views of Lake Coeur d'Alene. Visitors can choose from short walks or longer loop hikes while enjoying a natural setting in the heart of the city.

Explore Downtown Coeur d'Alene

Downtown Coeur d'Alene features locally owned shops, restaurants, coffee houses, art galleries, and boutiques. Sherman Avenue serves as the city's main street and is known for its walkable atmosphere, charming storefronts, and convenient access to the waterfront.

Walk the Coeur d'Alene Boardwalk

Adjacent to the Coeur d'Alene Resort, the boardwalk provides beautiful waterfront views and opportunities for sightseeing. Visitors can enjoy views of the marina, the lake, and surrounding mountains while strolling along one of the city's most iconic landmarks.

Visit McEuen Park

McEuen Park is a popular gathering place featuring open green spaces, walking paths, playgrounds, picnic areas, and scenic lake views. Its central location near downtown and Tubbs Hill makes it an excellent place to relax or begin a day of exploring.

Bike the North Idaho Centennial Trail

The North Idaho Centennial Trail is a paved multi-use pathway that follows scenic sections of the Spokane River and Lake Coeur d'Alene. The trail is popular with walkers, runners, and cyclists and provides beautiful river, lake, and forest views.

Take a Scenic Lake Cruise

Boat cruises offer a relaxing way to experience Lake Coeur d'Alene and learn about the area's history, wildlife, and natural beauty. Sightseeing cruises, sunset cruises, and seasonal excursions are available throughout much of the year.

Go Golfing

The Coeur d'Alene area is known for outstanding golf courses, including the famous Coeur d'Alene Resort Golf Course. The region's courses offer mountain, forest, and lake views that attract golfers from around the world.

Visit Local Art Galleries and Markets

The city has a vibrant arts community with galleries showcasing local and regional artists. Seasonal farmers markets, festivals, and cultural events provide additional opportunities to experience the character of North Idaho.

Enjoy Outdoor Adventures

Beyond the city, visitors can enjoy ziplining, ATV tours, mountain biking, wildlife viewing, rafting, and hiking. The forests, rivers, and mountains surrounding Coeur d'Alene make it an excellent base for outdoor recreation.

Top Trails

Tubbs Hill Loop Trail

Length: Approximately 2 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This popular trail circles Tubbs Hill and features forested paths, rocky viewpoints, and spectacular views of Lake Coeur d'Alene. The route provides one of the best combinations of scenery and accessibility in the city.

North Idaho Centennial Trail

Length: Over 20 miles within the region
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This paved recreational trail connects parks, waterfront areas, and scenic viewpoints throughout the Coeur d'Alene region. It is ideal for walking, running, cycling, and sightseeing.

Mineral Ridge National Recreation Trail

Length: Approximately 3.3 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

Located a short drive east of Coeur d'Alene, this trail climbs to scenic overlooks above Wolf Lodge Bay and Lake Coeur d'Alene. The route is especially popular during eagle-watching season and provides excellent panoramic views.
