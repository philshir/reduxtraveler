How to Get There

From Salt Lake City (294 miles): Get on I-15 N. Follow I-15 N and I-84 W to US-93 N in Jerome County. Take exit 173 from I-84 W. Follow US-93 N and State Hwy 75 N to Inn Entry Ln in Sun Valley.

Overview

Sun Valley is a resort town in the Wood River Valley of central Idaho, next to Ketchum, and it is known as America's first destination ski resort, created in 1936 by Union Pacific Railroad chairman Averell Harriman. The resort at the base of Bald Mountain, with its Swiss-style lodge, was where the first chairlifts in the world were invented, and it became a retreat for Hollywood stars and writers, including Ernest Hemingway. Sun Valley is known for its skiing, its sunny days, and a rich calendar of cultural events, including the Sun Valley Summer Symphony.

Visitors come to Sun Valley for skiing and snowboarding on Bald Mountain and Dollar Mountain, for ice skating, and for the historic Sun Valley Lodge. In summer, they come for hiking, mountain biking, golf, fly fishing, and music festivals, and for the nearby Sawtooth National Recreation Area.

History

In 1935, Averell Harriman, seeking to increase passenger traffic on the Union Pacific, hired Count Felix Schaffgotsch, an Austrian, to find the best place for a ski resort in the American West, and he chose a sheep-grazing valley near Ketchum. Sun Valley Lodge opened in December 1936 with the world's first chairlifts, invented by Union Pacific engineers, and with celebrities such as Gary Cooper, Clark Gable, and Ernest Hemingway as guests. During World War II, the resort was used as a Navy hospital, and the movie Sun Valley Serenade was made in 1941. In the decades since, Sun Valley has remained a top ski resort, and the Wood River Valley has become a home for the wealthy and the famous.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Sun Valley Lodge and the Sun Valley Inn are historic buildings at the center of the resort, with an ice rink, a shopping village, and a pool, and the Sun Valley Opera House hosts concerts and films. Bald Mountain, with a 3,400-foot vertical drop, is considered one of the best ski mountains in the country, and Dollar Mountain is a gentler area for beginners. The Trail Creek Cabin is a rustic restaurant, and the Sun Valley Nordic Center has groomed trails.

Culture and Museums

The Sun Valley Center for the Arts and the Sun Valley Summer Symphony are major cultural institutions, and the Sun Valley Writers' Conference brings writers and readers together each summer. The Sun Valley Film Festival and the Sun Valley Jazz Jamboree are other annual events, and the Hemingway Memorial and the Ketchum-Sun Valley Heritage and Ski Museum are nearby. The Sun Valley Resort offers ice shows on the outdoor rink in summer.

Parks

Sun Valley is surrounded by the Sawtooth National Forest, and the Wood River Trail is a paved path through the valley. The Sawtooth National Recreation Area, 30 miles north, has jagged mountains, lakes, and hot springs, and Craters of the Moon National Monument, a lava field, is about an hour south. Fly fishing on Silver Creek Preserve and the Big Wood River is a major summer activity.

Seasons and Weather

Sun Valley has a continental mountain climate and claims more than 300 days of sunshine a year. Summers are warm and dry, with highs in the 70s and 80s°F and cool nights, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 150 inches of snow on the mountain. Ski season runs from late November to April, and summer is the season for hiking and festivals.

Areas

  • Sun Valley village: centered on Sun Valley Lodge and Inn, with shops, condos, and the original golf course winding up Trail Creek
  • Elkhorn: a separate, drier area near Dollar Mountain, developed in 1972, with its own golf course
  • Ketchum: the older town center, a mile down Trail Creek

Things to Do

Experience Idaho's Original Mountain Resort Destination

Sun Valley is one of America's most famous mountain destinations, known for its world-class skiing, scenic landscapes, outdoor recreation, and vibrant arts and culture scene. Surrounded by multiple mountain ranges, the area offers year-round opportunities for adventure and relaxation.

Explore Miles of Hiking Trails

The Sun Valley area features an extensive network of hiking trails ranging from easy valley walks to challenging mountain summits. Visitors can explore trails throughout the Wood River Valley, Bald Mountain, the Sawtooth Mountains, Pioneer Mountains, Boulder Mountains, and surrounding wilderness areas.

Visit Bald Mountain

Bald Mountain is the iconic centerpiece of Sun Valley and offers spectacular views of Ketchum, Sun Valley, and the surrounding mountains. During the summer season, visitors can hike mountain trails and enjoy panoramic alpine scenery.

Enjoy Outdoor Recreation in Every Season

The region offers hiking, mountain biking, fishing, horseback riding, wildlife viewing, golf, skiing, snowboarding, and scenic drives. Its combination of mountain terrain and accessible trails makes it a popular destination for outdoor enthusiasts throughout the year.

Explore the Sawtooth National Recreation Area

Located a short drive from Sun Valley, the Sawtooth National Recreation Area features dramatic peaks, alpine lakes, rivers, and extensive hiking opportunities. It is one of Idaho's premier destinations for backpacking, camping, photography, and wilderness exploration.

Discover Ketchum and Sun Valley Village

Visitors can enjoy shopping, dining, galleries, festivals, and cultural attractions throughout the Sun Valley and Ketchum area. The communities blend outdoor adventure with resort amenities and historic mountain-town charm.

Top Trails

Pioneer Cabin Trail

Length: Approximately 8 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 2,600 feet
Difficulty: Challenging

One of the most iconic hikes near Sun Valley, Pioneer Cabin rewards hikers with spectacular alpine scenery and panoramic views of the surrounding mountain ranges. The famous cabin is known for its photo-worthy setting and unforgettable vistas.

Proctor Mountain Trail

Length: Approximately 3.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 1,000 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A popular local hike near Sun Valley, Proctor Mountain offers excellent views of Bald Mountain and the surrounding valley. The trail passes through forests and mountain terrain while providing a rewarding workout close to town.

Fox Creek Trail

Length: Approximately 5 to 7 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 800 to 1,200 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This scenic trail follows portions of the Big Wood River and passes through forests, meadows, and mountain landscapes. Fox Creek is popular with both hikers and mountain bikers and offers beautiful views throughout the route.

Adam's Gulch Trail

Length: Approximately 6.1 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Approximately 780 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

One of the area's most popular trails, Adam's Gulch features mountain scenery, streams, wildflowers, and diverse terrain. The trail network allows visitors to customize their route based on desired distance and difficulty.

Baker Lake Trail

Length: Approximately 3 to 4 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 870 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

This family-friendly hike leads to a beautiful alpine lake surrounded by mountain scenery. Baker Lake is a favorite destination for hiking, fishing, photography, and relaxing beside the water.
