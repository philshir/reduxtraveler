Minidoka National Historic Site

★5.0

Preserved World War II-era incarceration site featuring historic buildings, interpretive trails, and exhibits honoring Japanese American resilience.

How to Get There

From Twin Falls (20 mi): Take US-93 north to Eden, turn right onto 1400 E, then left onto Hunt Road and follow signs to the site entrance.

Overview

Minidoka National Historic Site preserves the remains of the Minidoka War Relocation Center, one of ten camps where the U.S. government incarcerated Japanese Americans during World War II. From 1942 to 1945, more than 13,000 people, mostly from Washington, Oregon and Alaska, were held in the camp in the Idaho desert. The site, established as a national monument in 2001 and a national historic site in 2008, includes a restored barrack, a guard station, a waiting room and a trail with interpretive signs. Visitors come to learn this chapter of American history and to honor those incarcerated.

Access

The site is about 17 miles northeast of Twin Falls near Jerome, reached by driving US-93 north to Eden and then east on Hunt Road. The route is mostly paved, and the final miles are on county roads. The drive from Twin Falls takes about 30 minutes, and from Boise about 2.5 hours. Most visitors arrive by car, and the site has a parking area at the trailhead.

Seasons

The grounds are open year-round from sunrise to sunset, and the visitor center is open on weekends in summer. Summers are hot and dry, with little shade, and the Minidoka winters were bitterly cold for those held here. Spring and fall are the most comfortable for visiting. The site is windy and exposed in all seasons. Special events and pilgrimages take place at times.

Things to Do

Walk the Site of the Camp

Minidoka National Historic Site's trail leads past foundations, a restored barrack and the original entrance. Signs share stories of the people held here.

See the Restored Barrack and Mess Hall

A restored barracks and a reconstructed building show what living conditions were like. Interpretive exhibits explain daily life.

Visit the Honor Roll Site

A memorial honors the Japanese Americans from the camp who served in the military. It is a moving place.

Learn From Rangers and Survivors' Stories

Exhibits and talks include accounts from people who lived at Minidoka. Their voices add to the story.

Reflect on Civil Liberties

The site invites consideration of rights during wartime and the importance of protecting them.

Top Trails

Minidoka Historic Trail

Length: Approximately 1.6 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

This trail circles the camp site, past foundations, the waiting room and the guard house. It is flat and open, so bring water and sun protection.

Entrance Area Walk

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk around the entrance features provides a quick overview. It is good for visitors with limited time.

Visitor Centers

The Minidoka visitor center and contact station, at the former waiting room and a nearby building, offers exhibits, a bookstore and rangers or volunteers who answer questions. It is generally open on Fridays through Sundays from Memorial Day to Labor Day, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Restrooms are available only when it is open. Check the National Park Service website for current hours.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. Twin Falls, about 30 minutes away, has many hotels, including the Hampton Inn, the Red Lion and other chain properties, and campgrounds such as Dierkes Lake. Jerome, about 15 minutes away, has simple motels. Boise, about 2.5 hours west, offers a larger selection. Check each property's website for current availability.
