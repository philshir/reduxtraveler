Overview

Grand Teton National Park in northwestern Wyoming covers about 310,000 acres, including the main peaks of the 40-mile-long Teton Range and most of the northern part of the valley of Jackson Hole. The Tetons rise abruptly nearly 7,000 feet above the valley floor with no foothills, and the Grand Teton is 13,775 feet. The mountains are reflected in lakes along their base, such as Jenny Lake and Jackson Lake, and the Snake River winds through sagebrush flats where moose, elk, and bison graze. The park lies just south of Yellowstone, linked by the John D. Rockefeller Jr. Memorial Parkway.

People have lived in the area for at least 11,000 years. The park was created in 1929 and expanded in 1950 with land that John D. Rockefeller Jr. bought and donated, over local opposition. It is popular because the scenery is visible from the road, with the jagged peaks rising above the valley, and because of the range of activities, from climbing to floating the Snake River to viewing wildlife. Many visitors combine it with Yellowstone and the town of Jackson.

Park Sections

The park is organized along the base of the Tetons. In the south, Moose is the headquarters, with the Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center, the Moose-Wilson Road to the Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve, and the Snake River landing. The Teton Park Road runs north to Jenny Lake, a hub for hikes into Cascade Canyon, with String Lake and Leigh Lake nearby. In the north, Jackson Lake has Colter Bay, Jackson Lake Lodge, and Signal Mountain. On the east side, Mormon Row and Antelope Flats are off US-26/89/191.

Sightseeing and Exploring the Park

The best scenic drive is the 20-mile Teton Park Road from Moose to Jackson Lake Junction, with viewpoints including the Snake River Overlook, Teton Point Turnout, and Jenny Lake Overlook. The Signal Mountain Summit Road climbs 800 feet to a view over the valley. Classic photography stops include Oxbow Bend, Schwabacher Landing, and Mormon Row, with its historic barns against the mountains, best at sunrise. The Chapel of the Transfiguration and the Cunningham Cabin offer a look at the area's history.

Hiking

Grand Teton has more than 200 miles of trails. Easy hikes include the String Lake loop (3.3 miles) and the walk to Taggart Lake (about 3 miles round trip). A boat shuttle across Jenny Lake shortens the hike to Hidden Falls and Inspiration Point. The trail into Cascade Canyon is longer, and Lake Solitude is a strenuous day hike of about 18 miles round trip. Amphitheater Lake (10 miles round trip) and Death Canyon are other demanding hikes. Bear spray is recommended, and snow can linger on high trails into July.

Other Activities

The park is known for mountaineering, and the Grand Teton is a major climb, with guides available from Exum Mountain Guides and Jackson Hole Mountain Guides. Floating the Snake River with a commercial outfitter is a popular way to see wildlife, and boating, kayaking, and fishing are available on Jackson Lake and Jenny Lake. Other activities include horseback riding, bicycling on the multi-use pathway, camping, ranger programs, and winter skiing and snowshoeing on the closed Teton Park Road. There are no cave tours.

Flora and Wildlife

Wildlife is a main attraction. Moose are often seen in the Willow Flats and along the Moose-Wilson Road, and elk, bison, and pronghorn are found on the flats. Black bears and grizzly bears live in the park, as do wolves, coyotes, bald eagles, ospreys, trumpeter swans, and pikas in the high rocks. Plants include sagebrush flats, lodgepole pine, subalpine fir, and aspens that turn gold in fall, with wildflowers in the mountains in July. Keep a safe distance from wildlife. Viewing is on your own, and Snake River float trips are among the best ways to see animals.

Access

The park is in Jackson Hole, with the town of Jackson about 5 miles south of the Moose entrance. Jackson Hole Airport, the only commercial airport inside a national park, has flights from many cities. Idaho Falls is about 1.75 hours (90 miles) west, Salt Lake City about 5 hours (280 miles) south, and Yellowstone's south entrance about an hour north. Most visitors drive, and START Bus public transit connects Jackson with Teton Village. Summer traffic and parking at popular spots like Jenny Lake can be difficult, so arrive early.

Seasons

Summer, from June to September, is the main season, with all roads and facilities open and wildflowers in July. Fall is a favorite for golden aspens and cottonwoods, elk rutting, and fewer crowds. Winter is long and snowy: the Teton Park Road closes to vehicles from about November to May and is groomed for skiing, the Moose-Wilson Road closes, and most lodges close, though the town of Jackson and its ski resorts remain busy. Spring is a muddy season of snowmelt, with bears emerging and baby animals appearing, and high trails remain snowy into June or July.

Camping and Lodging

The park has six campgrounds: Colter Bay, Jenny Lake, Signal Mountain, Lizard Creek, Gros Ventre, and Colter Bay RV Park, and reservations are recommended. Lodging in the park includes Jackson Lake Lodge, Jenny Lake Lodge, Colter Bay Village, Signal Mountain Lodge, and Dornan's Spur Ranch Cabins in Moose. Outside the park, the town of Jackson, 15 minutes south, has many hotels, including the Wort Hotel and Snow King Resort, and Teton Village has resort lodging near the ski area. Headwaters Lodge at Flagg Ranch is on the north boundary near Yellowstone.

Visitor Centers

The Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center at Moose is the main visitor center, with exhibits, a film, a bookstore, and rangers. The Colter Bay Visitor Center in the north has exhibits, including the Indian Arts Museum, and the Jenny Lake Visitor Center is open in summer. The Laurance S. Rockefeller Preserve Center near Phelps Lake is open seasonally and focuses on conservation and nature. Hours vary by season, so check the National Park Service website.

Top Sights

  • Lakes: Jenny, Jackson, Leigh, String, and Taggart
  • Canyons and falls: Cascade Canyon, Hidden Falls, and Death Canyon
  • Views: Signal Mountain, Mormon Row, and Schwabacher Landing
  • Rivers: the Snake River, with float trips
  • History: Cunningham Cabin and the Rockefeller Preserve

Good to Know

  • Recreation: more than 200 miles of trails and over 1,000 drive-in campsites, plus climbing, floating, and backcountry camping
  • Base town: Jackson, about five miles south
  • Wildlife: follow rules on keeping your distance from bears, moose, and bison
