Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming
Erik Whalen, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Dramatic canyon carved by the Yellowstone River featuring colorful volcanic rock walls and two major waterfalls that rank among the park’s most iconic natural landmarks.

General Information

Hours:
Open 24 hours daily year-round; access to overlooks and roads varies seasonally based on weather and park conditions.
Fees:
Included with Yellowstone National Park admission:
Private Vehicle: $35 (7-day pass)
Motorcycle: $30 (7-day pass)
Individual (walk-in, bicycle): $20 (7-day pass)
Pet Policy:
Pets are allowed only in developed areas and must remain within 100 feet of roads, parking areas, and campgrounds on a leash no longer than 6 feet; pets are not permitted on trails, boardwalks, or in thermal areas.
Closest cities with hotels:
West Yellowstone, Montana (approximately 40 mi)
Seasons:
Late spring through fall offers the best access to overlooks and trails; summer provides the most services, while winter access is limited.

How to Get There

From West Yellowstone (40 mi): Enter Yellowstone National Park through the West Entrance on US-20 E, continue to Madison Junction, follow the Grand Loop Road through Norris to Canyon Village, then follow signs to the canyon overlooks and waterfall viewing areas.

Overview

The Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone is a 24-mile-long, 800- to 1,200-foot-deep canyon on the Yellowstone River, colored yellow, orange, and red by thermally altered volcanic rock. It starts below Yellowstone Falls.

Highlights

  • Yellowstone Falls: the river plunges over the Upper Falls (109 feet) and then the Lower Falls (308 feet), among the largest waterfalls in the Rockies
  • North Rim Drive: viewpoints including Lookout Point, Red Rock Point, and Inspiration Point
  • South Rim Drive: Artist Point, the classic view of the Lower Falls, and Uncle Tom's Trail
