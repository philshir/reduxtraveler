How to Get There

From Pinedale, WY (15 mi): Take Skyline Drive northeast from town to Elkhart Park, the primary trailhead for hikes into the eastern Wind River Range.

Overview

The Wind River Range is a 100-mile mountain range of the Rockies in western Wyoming, with the Continental Divide along its crest. It is one of the most remote and rugged mountain ranges in the Lower 48, and visitors come for backpacking, mountaineering, fishing, and horseback trips.

The range has more than 1,300 lakes and the largest glaciers in the American Rockies. Gannett Peak, at 13,804 feet, is the highest point in Wyoming, and the granite spires of the Cirque of the Towers are a world-famous climbing destination.

Geography

The range runs northwest to southeast for about 100 miles and is up to 30 miles wide. Its crest holds more than 40 peaks above 13,000 feet, glacial cirques, and the Continental Divide, with rivers flowing west to the Green River and east to the Wind River. The west side drops steeply to the Green River Valley, while the east side is gentler and lies partly on the Wind River Indian Reservation. Much of the range is wilderness reached by long drives, partly on gravel roads.

History

The Eastern Shoshone and Northern Arapaho, along with earlier peoples, have long used the mountains, and today the Wind River Indian Reservation lies on the east side. Fur trappers held the Green River Rendezvous near Pinedale in the 1830s, and the Oregon, Mormon, and California trails crossed South Pass at the range’s southern end. John C. Frémont’s 1842 expedition climbed what is now called Fremont Peak, and mountaineers began exploring the range’s high peaks in the early 20th century.

Cities and Towns

Pinedale on the west side is the main base for backpackers and has the Museum of the Mountain Man. Dubois serves the north, and Lander, Fort Washakie, and Riverton serve the east and south. Services are limited, so most visitors stock up in these towns before heading to the trailheads.

National Protected Areas

There is no national park in the range, but the Bridger, Fitzpatrick, and Popo Agie wilderness areas protect most of the high country; they lie within national forests, which are not covered here. Grand Teton National Park and Yellowstone National Park are within a half-day’s drive, and the Oregon, California, Mormon Pioneer, and Pony Express national historic trails cross South Pass to the south.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Sinks Canyon State Park near Lander is known for the Popo Agie River, which disappears into a cave and reappears in a nearby pool. Fremont Lake and Boulder Lake offer lakeside camping and fishing. The South Pass City State Historic Site preserves a gold-rush town from the 1860s and 1870s, and the Green River Rendezvous is commemorated near Pinedale.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

The main hiking and backpacking season is July through mid-September, since snow can linger on high passes into July and mosquitoes are worst in early summer. August and early September typically have the most stable weather, though afternoon thunderstorms are common. Fall brings cold nights, golden aspen, and hunting season. Winter is long and snowy, with snowshoeing, skiing, and snowmobiling in the foothills, and the high country is largely inaccessible.
