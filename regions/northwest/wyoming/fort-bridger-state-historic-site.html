Fort Bridger State Historic Site

How to Get There

From Evanston (24 mi): Take I-80 E to Exit 34 at Fort Bridger, follow the business loop into town, and continue to 37000 I-80 Blvd at Fort Bridger State Historic Site.

Overview

Fort Bridger State Historic Site in southwestern Wyoming preserves a fort established in 1843 by mountain man Jim Bridger as a trading post for travelers on the Oregon, California and Mormon trails. The fort later served as a Pony Express station, an Army post and a stop on the Overland Stage, and it was bought by Mormon settlers in 1855. The site has restored military buildings, a museum and reconstructed trading post, set among cottonwoods. Visitors come for the history, the annual Labor Day rendezvous and a look at life on the westward trails.

Access

The fort is in the town of Fort Bridger, about 3 miles south of I-80 exit 34 and about 24 miles east of Evanston. Visitors take I-80 and follow signs to the site, which is on the Fort Bridger business loop. The drive from Salt Lake City takes about 2 hours. Most visitors arrive by car, and the site is an easy stop on the interstate.

Seasons

The grounds are open year-round from sunrise to sunset, while the museum and historic buildings are open daily from May through September, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. October through April, the museum is closed except by special arrangement. Summer is the busiest, with living history events. The Labor Day weekend Rendezvous brings crowds. Winter is cold and windy, and the grounds are quiet.

Things to Do

Walk Through an Overland Trails Fort

Fort Bridger State Historic Site has historic buildings from the Army era, including barracks and officers' quarters. A reconstructed trading post shows the original fort.

Tour the Museum

Exhibits cover the fort's history, the Pony Express, the Mormon Battalion and Native American peoples. The museum is in a historic building.

Attend the Labor Day Rendezvous

Every Labor Day weekend, the fort hosts the Mountain Man Rendezvous with demonstrations, trade booths and costumes. It is a popular event.

Learn About Jim Bridger

Exhibits describe the life of the famous trapper and guide. His stories and the trails he explored are highlights.

Visit Other Historic Sites

Evanston, Church Butte and Fort Bridger are linked to the emigrant trails. Interpretive stops are along I-80.

Top Trails

Fort Grounds Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk visits the historic buildings and trading post with interpretive signs. The route is flat and accessible.

Pony Express Station Path

Length: Approximately 0.25 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A path leads to a reconstructed station that tells of the Pony Express. It is easy and quick.

Visitor Centers

The Fort Bridger museum and visitor center, in the historic commissary building, is the main stop, with exhibits, a gift shop, restrooms and staff who give tours and information. It is open daily from May through September. Admission is charged to out-of-state visitors. Check the Wyoming State Parks website for current hours and events.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping at the fort, but nearby Fort Bridger has a small campground, and Evanston has the Bear River State Park, which offers camping. Evanston, about 25 minutes west, has chain hotels such as the Best Western Dunmar Inn and the Holiday Inn Express. Lyman has small motels, and Green River and Rock Springs, about an hour east, offer more options. Check each property's website for current availability.
