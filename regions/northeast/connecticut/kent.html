How to Get There

From New Milford (15 mi): Follow US Route 7 north through the Housatonic River Valley and continue directly into Kent's historic village center along Main Street.

Overview

Kent is a small, scenic town in the Litchfield Hills of northwestern Connecticut, on the Housatonic River near the New York border, and it is known for its waterfalls, its art galleries, and the Appalachian Trail, which passes through the town. With a population of about 3,000, it is a quiet village with a historic Main Street, a boarding school, and a creative community of artists and craftspeople. Kent Falls State Park, with its 250-foot cascade, is one of the best-known natural attractions in the state.

Visitors come to Kent for Kent Falls State Park, for hiking on the Appalachian Trail, and for the galleries, antique shops, and cafes on North Main Street. They also come for the Housatonic River, which is popular for canoeing and fly fishing, for Macedonia Brook State Park, and for the nearby covered bridge at Bull's Bridge.

History

Kent was settled in the 1730s by colonists from Windsor, who bought the land from the Schaghticoke people, and it was incorporated in 1739. In the nineteenth century, the town developed iron mining and smelting, using the area's iron ore and waterpower, and its furnace was an important source of iron for the Revolution and the Civil War. The Kent Furnace operated until the 1890s, and Kent School, a private boarding school, was founded in 1906. In the twentieth century, the town became a quiet resort for artists and weekenders from New York, and the Schaghticoke Tribal Nation maintains a reservation in the town.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Kent Falls State Park, a short drive north of the village, has a series of cascades dropping about 250 feet over a quarter mile, with a trail alongside and a picnic area at the bottom. The Bull's Bridge, a historic covered bridge over the Housatonic River, is a few miles south, and the Sloane-Stanley Museum has a collection of early American tools. North Main Street has galleries, antique shops, and restaurants, and the Kent Land Trust protects many of the town's open spaces.

Culture and Museums

The Sloane-Stanley Museum houses the tool collection of artist Eric Sloane, with a reconstructed Kent Iron Furnace site next door, and the Connecticut Antique Machinery Association has a museum of vintage machinery. The town has a number of art galleries, and the Kent Memorial Library and the Kent Art Association host exhibits and events.

Parks

The Appalachian Trail follows the Housatonic River through Kent, and St. John's Ledges is a popular climbing and hiking area. Macedonia Brook State Park has trails and camping, and Kent Falls State Park is the main attraction. The Housatonic is a favorite for canoeing, kayaking, and trout fishing, and nearby Lake Waramaug State Park has a lake for swimming and boating.

Seasons and Weather

Kent has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 50 inches of snow. Fall color in the Litchfield Hills is outstanding in October, and spring brings high water to the waterfalls.
