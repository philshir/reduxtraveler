Wadsworth Falls State Park

Rugged woodland park featuring twin waterfalls along a scenic river, with miles of hiking trails, stone bridges, and designated areas for swimming, fishing, and picnicking.

How to Get There

From Middletown (3 mi): Take Route 66 east, turn left onto Route 157 toward Middlefield, and follow signs to Wadsworth Street where the park entrance and parking areas are located.

Overview

Wadsworth Falls State Park is a 285-acre Connecticut state park in Middletown and Middlefield along the Coginchaug River, known for two waterfalls, Big Falls and Little Falls, each reached by a short walk. Big Falls drops about 30 feet over a shelf of basalt, and Little Falls descends about 40 feet over sandstone, rock that dates back some 200 million years. The park is named for Colonel Clarence S. Wadsworth, who developed the land as part of his Long Hill estate in the early 1900s; after his death a family corporation gave most of the land to the state, and it was established as a state park in 1942. About 130 acres are included in the Wadsworth Estate Historic District, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1996. Visitors come to see the falls, swim at the designated beach in summer, fish the river, and walk and bike the park trails.

Access

The park is at 721 Wadsworth Street on the Middletown-Middlefield town line, about 3 miles from downtown Middletown in central Connecticut. Nearly all visitors arrive by car, following Route 66 and Route 157 to Wadsworth Street, where the park entrance and parking areas are located. There is no public transportation to the park. The Big Falls viewing area is a short visit, and swimming and picnicking are not allowed there; picnic areas and the swimming area are elsewhere in the park. Buses seating 30 or more passengers need a permit, and all-terrain wheelchairs can be reserved in advance.

Seasons

The park is open daily from 8:00 a.m. to sunset year-round. Spring brings the strongest water flow over the falls, and the falls can thin out in dry summer weather. Summer is the busiest season, when the designated swimming area is open, with water quality reports updated weekly from Memorial Day through Labor Day. Fall foliage brings hikers to the wooded trails, and winter is a quiet time for walking with fewer visitors, though trails can be icy near the falls.

Things to Do

See Big Falls and Little Falls

The two waterfalls are the park's highlights. Big Falls is a short walk from the parking area, and Little Falls can be reached by trail through woods that were once part of the Wadsworth family's estate.

Swim and Picnic

The park has a designated swimming area and picnic areas with tables. Dogs are not permitted on the beach.

Hike and Bike

About 4.5 miles of trails are open for hiking and biking, and a trail connects the park to the adjacent Wadsworth Mansion at Long Hill. Dogs are allowed on trails if leashed.

Fish the Coginchaug River

Anglers can fish the cold waters of the river. Check river conditions and Connecticut fishing regulations before going.

Visitor Centers

Wadsworth Falls has no visitor center or museum. A trail map can be downloaded from the Connecticut State Parks website, and the park can be reached at (860) 345-8521. The adjacent Wadsworth Mansion at Long Hill, about half a mile away, is a separate historic property.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging in the park. Hotels, restaurants and services are available in Middletown, about 3 miles away, and in nearby Cromwell, Meriden and Chester. Hartford and New Haven are within about 30 to 45 minutes and offer a wider range of hotels. Campers can look to Hammonasset Beach State Park on Long Island Sound, which has a large campground and is roughly 30 to 40 minutes away.
