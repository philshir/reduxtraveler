Devil's Hopyard State Park

★5.0

Wooded natural area featuring a dramatic multi-tiered waterfall, scenic river gorge, and hiking trails shaped by unique geological formations and local folklore.

How to Get There

From Old Saybrook (15 mi): Take Route 9 North to Exit 7, follow Route 82 East, then turn right onto Hopyard Road and continue to the park entrance.

Overview

Devil's Hopyard State Park is a Connecticut state park of about 1,000 acres in East Haddam, centered on 60-foot Chapman Falls, which cascades in steps over a schist ledge on the Eightmile River. The state acquired the land in 1919 to stop logging, and the falls once powered Beebe's Mills, a mill attacked by the Sons of Liberty in 1775. The name's origin is disputed, with explanations ranging from a landowner named Dibble to legends about the round potholes worn into the rock near the falls. Visitors come to see the waterfall and potholes, hike to the Vista Point cliff 150 to 175 feet above the river, fish the Eightmile River, picnic and camp in the woods.

Access

The park is in the town of East Haddam in southeastern Connecticut, about 15 miles from Old Saybrook on the coast. Visitors arrive by car from Route 9 and Route 82 and then Hopyard Road, which leads to the park entrance and parking area near Chapman Falls. There is no public transportation to the park, and buses seating 30 or more passengers are not allowed. Parking is limited, so arrive early on summer weekends and fall foliage days. Admission is free.

Seasons

The park is open daily from 8:00 a.m. to sunset year-round. Spring is the best time to see Chapman Falls at full volume, when snowmelt and rain fill the Eightmile River, and the falls shrink in dry summer weather. Summer is shaded and cooler near the river, which makes it popular for picnics, fishing and camping. Fall foliage draws many visitors to the vista and river trails, and winter is quiet, with icy falls in cold snaps and trails that can be slick. The campground is open seasonally, from mid-April to mid-October, and the picnic shelter is closed from mid-November through March.

Things to Do

See Chapman Falls and the Potholes

The main attraction is Chapman Falls, which drops more than 60 feet in a series of steps over schist rock. A short walk from the parking area leads to viewing spots at the falls, where round potholes worn into the rock are also a draw.

Hike the Park's Trails

The park's trail network runs along the Eightmile River and up to ridge-top views, with blazed trails for short walks and longer hikes. Pets are allowed on leash on trails and in picnic areas, but not in the campground.

Fish the Eightmile River

The Eightmile River, a designated National Wild and Scenic River, is known for fishing, and the park has a fishing platform. Check current Connecticut fishing regulations and license requirements before you go.

Picnic and Camp

A large open-air picnic shelter and picnic areas sit near the river, and the wooded campground has 21 sites. Birding is also a highlight of the park.

Top Trails

Chapman Falls Viewing Path

Length: Approximately 0.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk from the parking area leads to overlooks of Chapman Falls and the potholes beside the river. It is the easiest way to see the falls and works well for families.

Orange Trail to Vista Point

Length: Approximately 1.5 miles loop
Elevation Gain: Moderate
Difficulty: Moderate

This trail starts near the covered bridge downstream of the falls and climbs through forest to the Vista Point cliff, 150 to 175 feet above the Eightmile River, before returning along the river. Watch for a fork early in the trail that is easy to miss, and expect rocky, sometimes wet footing.

Visitor Centers

Devil's Hopyard has no visitor center or museum. Seasonal park staff are based at the park office, which can be reached at (860) 526-2336, and trail maps are available on the Connecticut State Parks website at ctparks.com. Chapman Falls, the picnic areas and the trailheads have limited facilities, so plan to bring your own drinking water.

Camping and Lodging

The park campground has 21 wooded sites with portable toilets but no running water, and it is open from mid-April to mid-October; reservations are made through the state's reservation system. Pets are not allowed in the campground. Two private campgrounds, Salem Farms Campground and Witch Meadow Lake Campground, are a few miles away. Hotels and inns are found in Old Saybrook, about 15 miles to the south, and in the Connecticut River valley towns of Essex and Middletown within about 30 to 45 minutes. Norwich and New London also offer a wider range of hotels within about an hour.
