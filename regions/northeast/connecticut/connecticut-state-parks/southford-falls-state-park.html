How to Get There

From Southbury (3 mi): Take Route 67 East to Route 188, turn onto Quaker Farms Road, and follow signs to the park entrance and parking area.

Overview

Southford Falls State Park is a compact Connecticut state park in Oxford and Southbury, known for a tiered waterfall and a covered bridge over Eight Mile Brook. The brook drops steeply toward the Housatonic River and once powered an industrial complex of gristmills, sawmills, ironworks and button makers; around 1900 the Diamond Match Company built a matchbox factory on the site, and after a second fire destroyed it the state acquired the land in 1927. It became a state park in 1932. Today the park is a designated Trout Park, stocked from state hatcheries, and visitors come to see the falls and the old mill setting, fish, picnic and walk a short trail network.

Access

The park is at 175 Quaker Farms Road in Southbury, in the hills of western New Haven County, about 3 miles from the center of Southbury. Visitors reach it by car, following Route 67 and Route 188 to Quaker Farms Road, and there is no public transportation. A parking area is at the park entrance, and the falls and covered bridge are a short walk from it. Admission is free, and pets are allowed on leash in picnic areas and on trails.

Seasons

The park is open daily from 8:00 a.m. to sunset year-round. Water flow over the falls is strongest in spring and after heavy rain, and it can dwindle in dry summers. Summer is popular for picnics and trout fishing, and fall foliage frames the pond and covered bridge. Winter brings cross-country skiing, ice skating and ice fishing when conditions allow. The picnic shelter is closed from early November through March and reservable from late May to early September.

Things to Do

See the Falls and Covered Bridge

The tiered waterfall on Eight Mile Brook and the covered bridge are the park's signature sights, and both are an easy walk from the parking area.

Fish for Trout

Paper Mill Pond is a regularly stocked, designated trout park. Anglers should check current water conditions and Connecticut fishing license requirements before visiting.

Hike and Picnic

A two-mile network of trails winds through the woods of this former mill site. An octagonal picnic shelter holds up to 50 people and can be reserved in season.

Winter Recreation

When conditions allow, the park is used for cross-country skiing, ice skating and ice fishing.

Visitor Centers

Southford Falls has no visitor center or museum. The park has a restroom near the picnic shelter, and a trail map can be downloaded from the Connecticut State Parks website. For questions, call the park at (203) 264-5169.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the park. Hotels, motels and restaurants are found in Southbury and in Waterbury, Naugatuck and Seymour, all within about 20 to 30 minutes. Danbury and Shelton offer additional hotels within about 30 to 45 minutes, and New Haven is roughly an hour away. Campers can find campgrounds at other Connecticut state parks, such as Kettletown State Park in Southbury, which is a short drive away.
