How to Get There

From Providence, RI (45 mi): Take I‑95 South into Connecticut, continue to Exit 90 in Stonington/Groton, follow signs toward downtown, and proceed along local roads into the village center near the riverfront.

Overview

Mystic is a historic seaport village on the Mystic River in southeastern Connecticut, about 10 miles from the Rhode Island border, and it is best known for Mystic Seaport Museum, the largest maritime museum in the United States. The village was a major shipbuilding center in the nineteenth century, producing clipper ships and whalers, and its bascule drawbridge over the river is one of its best-known sights. Mystic also gained fame as the setting of the 1988 film Mystic Pizza.

Visitors come to Mystic for Mystic Seaport, with its recreated nineteenth-century village and tall ships, for Mystic Aquarium, and for the Mystic River Bascule Bridge. They also come for the shops and restaurants of downtown, for boat cruises on the Mystic River and Fishers Island Sound, for nearby Stonington and Noank, and for the Mashantucket Pequot Museum and Foxwoods Resort Casino, about 20 minutes away.

History

The area was home to the Pequot people, and English settlers arrived in the 1650s, with the 1637 Mystic Massacre, in which colonists destroyed a Pequot fort, shaping the region's history. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, Mystic became a major shipbuilding and whaling port, and its shipyards built hundreds of vessels, including famous clipper ships such as the Charles Mallory and the Andrew Jackson. The decline of shipbuilding in the late nineteenth century was followed by the creation of Mystic Seaport in 1929 to preserve maritime heritage. Mystic's draw as a tourist destination grew with the 1988 movie.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Mystic Seaport Museum is a 19-acre living history museum with more than 60 historic buildings and four ships, including the Charles W. Morgan, the last wooden whaling ship in the world. The Mystic River Bascule Bridge, built in 1922, opens about 30 times a day to let boats pass, and the village's shops and restaurants are on both sides. Mystic Aquarium has beluga whales and sea lions, and Olde Mistick Village is a shopping center built to look like a colonial village.

Culture and Museums

Mystic Seaport has exhibits on shipbuilding, whaling, and maritime crafts, with demonstrations by shipwrights and sailmakers, and the Mystic Arts Center and the Mystic Art Association show works by local artists. The Mashantucket Pequot Museum and Research Center is one of the largest Native American museums in the world, with life-size dioramas of Pequot life. The Denison Pequotsepos Nature Center and Denison Homestead cover local history and nature.

Parks

Mystic has a number of parks and nature areas. Bluff Point Coastal Reserve in nearby Groton has trails along the shore, and Harkness Memorial State Park in Waterford has a mansion with Italian gardens and a beach. Ocean Beach Park in New London and Rocky Neck State Park are popular with families, and Denison Pequotsepos Nature Center has trails through woodland. Kayaking and sailing on the Mystic River are popular in summer.

Seasons and Weather

Mystic has a humid continental climate moderated by Long Island Sound. Summers are warm, with highs in the 80s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 30 inches of snow. Summer is the busiest season, and spring and fall are quieter and good for walking and sightseeing. Coastal storms and nor'easters can occur in fall and winter.
