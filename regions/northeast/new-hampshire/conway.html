How to Get There

From Portland, Maine (65 mi): Take US-302 West through Fryeburg and continue into the Mount Washington Valley, following the highway directly into Conway and its historic village center.

Overview

Conway is a town in the Mount Washington Valley of northern New Hampshire, and it includes the village of North Conway, the center of the valley's tourism and a gateway to the White Mountains. It is known for its scenic setting at the foot of Mount Cranmore, its outlet shopping, the Conway Scenic Railroad, and its easy access to hiking, skiing, and rock climbing in the White Mountain National Forest. The valley has been a popular retreat since the 1800s, when artists of the White Mountain School painted its views.

Visitors come to Conway for the Conway Scenic Railroad, for the shops and restaurants of North Conway, and for the Kancamagus Highway, a scenic road through the White Mountains. They also come for Cathedral Ledge and Whitehorse Ledge, for swimming holes such as Diana's Baths and tubing on the Saco River, and for skiing at Cranmore and Attitash.

History

The area was home to the Abenaki, whose village of Pequawket was in the Saco River valley, and the town was settled in the 1760s and named for Henry Seymour Conway, a British general who opposed taxing the colonies. The arrival of the railroad in the 1870s brought tourists to the grand hotels of the valley, and painters of the White Mountain School, such as Benjamin Champney, made North Conway an artists' colony. In 1938, the first American ski school was established here, and Cranmore Mountain helped make the valley a skiing destination. In the 1970s and 1980s, New Hampshire's lack of a sales tax attracted outlet stores, which became a draw for shoppers.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Conway Scenic Railroad, with its restored 1874 North Conway Depot, runs vintage steam and diesel trains through the valley, and Cathedral Ledge, a 700-foot cliff, has a drive-up summit with views of the valley. Diana's Baths is a series of cascades and pools on Lucy Brook, and Echo Lake State Park has a beach and a short walk to the White Horse Ledge viewpoint. Mount Washington, the highest peak in the Northeast, is a short drive north, and the Kancamagus Highway starts in Conway.

Culture and Museums

The Conway Scenic Railroad's depot is a historic landmark, and the Mount Washington Observatory's Weather Discovery Center in North Conway has exhibits on the world's "worst weather." The Eastern Slope Playhouse hosts performances, and the Mount Washington Valley Arts Association shows local art. The town's villages, such as Conway Village, Center Conway, and Intervale, have historic buildings and small shops.

Parks

The White Mountain National Forest surrounds Conway, with hundreds of miles of trails, and Echo Lake State Park has a lake for swimming and a trail to a view of the cliffs. The Saco River is popular for canoeing, kayaking, and tubing, and the Kancamagus Highway has scenic overlooks and trailheads. Cathedral Ledge State Park and Whitehorse Ledge are popular for rock climbing, and Mount Willard has an easy hike to a view of Crawford Notch.

Seasons and Weather

Conway has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 90 inches of snow. Fall foliage in late September and October is a major draw, and winter is popular for skiing and snowshoeing. Some businesses close in April and November.
