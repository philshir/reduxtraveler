How to Get There

From North Conway (12 mi): Follow US-302 west into the White Mountains, climbing through forested valleys and mountain scenery until reaching Crawford Notch State Park in the heart of the mountain pass.

Overview

Crawford Notch State Park preserves one of New Hampshire's most spectacular mountain landscapes, a dramatic pass cut between the peaks of the White Mountains. The notch has served as a travel corridor for centuries, first for Indigenous peoples and later for settlers, stagecoaches, railroads and modern travelers. Steep cliffs, cascading waterfalls and dense forests surround the road. A network of trails leads to waterfalls, summits and overlooks, and winter brings cross-country skiing and snowshoeing.

Access

The park is on US-302 in Hart's Location, about 20 miles from North Conway and 2.5 hours north of Boston. Visitors take US-302 or NH-16 and follow signs. Roads are paved, and parking is free at the Willey House and Dry River. A day-use fee is charged at some trailheads. There is no public transportation.

Seasons

The park is open all year, and the Dry River Campground is open from spring to fall. Summer is warm and busy. Fall is the most popular season for the foliage. Winters are cold and snowy, and the notch road can close in storms. Spring brings strong waterfalls.

Things to Do

Hike to the Waterfalls

Silver Cascade, Flume Cascade and Arethusa Falls are close to the road.

Drive the Notch

US-302 passes through the pass with views of the cliffs.

Ride the Conway Scenic Railroad

The Notch Train runs through the pass in season.

Climb the Summits

Trails lead to Mount Willard and Mount Willey.

Camp in the Notch

The Dry River Campground is a short walk from the river.

Top Trails

Arethusa Falls Trail

Length: Approximately 3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 900 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A forest path to one of the tallest waterfalls in New Hampshire at 160 feet.

Mount Willard Trail

Length: Approximately 3.2 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Approximately 900 feet
Difficulty: Moderate

A steady climb to a ledge with a view down the notch.

Silver and Flume Cascade Walk

Length: Approximately 0.5 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk from the highway to waterfalls.

Visitor Centers

The Crawford Depot Information Center and the Willey House Visitor Center have maps, exhibits and staff who give trail information. The White Mountain National Forest visitor information is nearby. Hours vary by season.

Camping and Lodging

The park has the Dry River Campground with tent and RV sites, reserved through New Hampshire State Parks. The AMC Highland Center offers lodging and meals. Hotels and inns are in Bartlett, North Conway and Bretton Woods, and the Omni Mount Washington Resort is about 20 minutes away.
