Overview

Portsmouth is a historic seaport on the Piscataqua River in southeastern New Hampshire, near the Maine border, and it is one of the oldest cities in the United States, with a well-preserved colonial and Federal-era downtown. It is known for its brick-lined streets, its restaurants and breweries, its harbor with tugboats and ships, and its living history museum, Strawbery Banke. The city is the center of New Hampshire's short 18-mile coastline, and a popular destination for food, history, and the arts.

Visitors come to Portsmouth for Strawbery Banke Museum, for the shops and cafes of Market Square and Congress Street, and for the waterfront at Prescott Park. They also come for the Portsmouth Harbor Lighthouse, for the historic houses such as the Jackson House, and for nearby beaches and the Isles of Shoals.

History

English settlers arrived at the mouth of the Piscataqua River in 1623, and the settlement, called Strawbery Banke for the wild strawberries growing on its banks, was renamed Portsmouth in 1653. It was the capital of the royal colony of New Hampshire, and a major center for shipbuilding and trade, and in December 1774 locals raided Fort William and Mary in New Castle in one of the first acts of armed resistance before Lexington and Concord. The Portsmouth Naval Shipyard, across the river in Kittery, Maine, built ships for the U.S. Navy, including the first American submarines, and in 1905, the Treaty of Portsmouth ended the Russo-Japanese War. In the late twentieth century, Portsmouth revitalized its downtown and became a vibrant arts and food city.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Market Square, with its North Church, is the heart of downtown, and the streets around it are lined with brick buildings that house shops, restaurants, and pubs. Strawbery Banke Museum is a ten-acre outdoor museum with more than 30 historic buildings spanning the 1690s to the 1950s, and the Jackson House, built in about 1664, is the oldest surviving wooden house in New Hampshire. The John Paul Jones House, the Moffatt-Ladd House, and the Wentworth-Gardner House are other historic houses, and the Portsmouth Harbor Light stands at the harbor entrance in New Castle.

Culture and Museums

Strawbery Banke's museum houses tell stories of residents from different eras, from colonial merchants to immigrant families, and the Portsmouth Athenaeum, a private library founded in 1817, preserves historic collections. The Music Hall, an 1878 theater, hosts concerts, films, and shows, and the Seacoast Repertory Theatre is a local theater company. Prescott Park has an annual Arts Festival with music and theater in summer, and the Portsmouth Black Heritage Trail tells the stories of African Americans in the city.

Parks

Prescott Park is a waterfront park with formal gardens, a pier, and views of the harbor and the tugboats, and Pierce Island and Peirce Island have trails and a swimming pool. Odiorne Point State Park, in nearby Rye, has trails along the coast and a history of coastal defense, and the beaches of Rye, Hampton, and Wallis Sands are a short drive away. Boat tours go to the Isles of Shoals, a group of rocky islands off the coast.

Seasons and Weather

Portsmouth has a humid continental climate moderated by the ocean. Summers are comfortable, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 30s°F and about 55 inches of snow. Summer and fall are the busiest seasons, and nor'easters can bring heavy snow in winter.
