How to Get There

From Burlington (38 mi): Take I-89 S toward Montpelier, use Exit 8, and follow US-2 and local signs into downtown Montpelier, Vermont's capital city.

Overview

Montpelier is the capital of Vermont and the smallest state capital in the United States, with a population of about 8,000, and it is nestled in a valley along the Winooski River. It is known for its gold-domed State House, its walkable downtown of independent shops, restaurants, and bookstores, and the fact that it is the only state capital without a McDonald's. The city has a quiet, small-town atmosphere with a strong sense of community, and it is surrounded by the hills and forests of the central Green Mountains.

Visitors come to Montpelier for the Vermont State House, for the Vermont Historical Society's museum, and for the shops and cafes of Main Street. They also come for Hubbard Park and its stone tower, for the Morse Farm Maple Sugarworks, and for the nearby Rock of Ages granite quarry in Barre and the Cabot Creamery, and the city is a good base for exploring central Vermont.

History

Montpelier was chartered in 1781 and settled in the 1780s, and named after the French city of Montpellier by Colonel Jacob Davis, who admired the French for their help in the Revolution. In 1805, it was chosen as the state capital because of its central location, and the first State House was built in 1808. The arrival of the railroad in the 1840s brought growth, and the nearby granite industry in Barre brought immigrants from Scotland and Italy. In the twentieth century, Montpelier became the home of the insurance company National Life, and it has remained a small government and cultural center.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Vermont State House, completed in 1859, is a Greek Revival building with a gold-leaf dome topped by a statue of Ceres, the goddess of agriculture, and it offers free tours of its legislative chambers. The Vermont Historical Society's museum, in the Pavilion Building next door, has exhibits on the state's history, and Main Street and State Street have historic brick buildings. The Hubbard Park stone tower has views of the town and the mountains, and the Morse Farm Maple Sugarworks is a family-owned maple farm.

Culture and Museums

The Vermont Historical Society Museum covers the state's past, and the T.W. Wood Gallery has the works of Thomas Waterman Wood and other artists. The Vermont College of Fine Arts and the Savoy Theater, an independent cinema, add to the arts scene, and the Hunger Mountain Co-op is a community hub. The Kellogg-Hubbard Library and the Capitol City Farmers Market are local fixtures.

Parks

Hubbard Park, a 194-acre park on a hill above the city, has trails, picnic areas, and a stone observation tower, and the North Branch River Park and the Winooski River offer trails and swimming holes. The Montpelier Recreation Path runs along the river, and nearby Groton State Forest, Camel's Hump State Park, and the Barre granite quarries are within a short drive. In winter, cross-country skiing and sledding are popular.

Seasons and Weather

Montpelier has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 90 inches of snow. Fall foliage is spectacular in October, and spring brings maple sugaring in March and April.
