How to Get There

From South Burlington (3 mi): From I-89, take Exit 14W onto US-2 W (Williston Road) and follow it west through the University of Vermont campus into downtown Burlington and the Church Street Marketplace.

Overview

Burlington is the largest city in Vermont, on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain, with views across the water to the Adirondack Mountains, and it is known for its lively waterfront, its pedestrian Church Street Marketplace, its progressive politics, and its college-town atmosphere. It is the home of the University of Vermont and the birthplace of Ben & Jerry's ice cream, and its craft beer, farm-to-table restaurants, and live music scene have made it a favorite among visitors. Bernie Sanders, who later became a U.S. senator, served as its mayor from 1981 to 1989.

Visitors come to Burlington for the Church Street Marketplace, a four-block pedestrian mall with shops and restaurants, and for the waterfront on Lake Champlain. They also come for the Island Line Trail, for sailing and boat cruises, for the ECHO Leahy Center for Lake Champlain, and for the fall foliage and ski areas nearby.

History

The area was home to the Abenaki, and the town was chartered in 1763 and settled by Ethan Allen and his family, with Allen's Green Mountain Boys playing a key role in the early history of Vermont. The University of Vermont was founded in 1791, and in the nineteenth century, the city became a major lumber port, shipping timber from Canada and Vermont down Lake Champlain and the Hudson. In the late twentieth century, the city revitalized its waterfront and downtown, and became known for its independent businesses, its pioneering of local food, and its public-spirited city government. Ben & Jerry's was founded here in 1978 in a converted gas station.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Church Street Marketplace is the center of downtown, with outdoor cafes, street performers, and shops, and the waterfront at Waterfront Park has a boardwalk and views of the lake. The Ethan Allen Homestead Museum shows the home of the Green Mountain Boys' leader, and the Flynn Center for the Performing Arts, an Art Deco theater, hosts concerts. The Island Line Trail is a 14-mile recreation path along the lakeshore, and the Spirit of Ethan Allen III offers cruises on the lake.

Culture and Museums

The ECHO Leahy Center for Lake Champlain is a science museum and aquarium on the waterfront, and the Fleming Museum of Art at the University of Vermont has a collection of art from around the world. The Shelburne Museum, just south of the city, has historic buildings and a steamboat, the Ticonderoga, on dry land, and the Burlington City Arts and the Flynn Center are cultural venues. The city hosts the Discover Jazz Festival in June and the Burlington Farmers Market each Saturday.

Parks

Burlington has a number of parks along the lake. Waterfront Park has a boardwalk and a skate park, and North Beach has a sandy beach and a campground. Lake Champlain is popular for sailing, kayaking, and fishing, and the Island Line Trail crosses the lake on a causeway built on a former railroad bed. Rock Point and Oakledge Park are other spots, and the Green Mountains, with hiking and skiing, are within an hour.

Seasons and Weather

Burlington has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 80 inches of snow. Fall foliage in October is a highlight, and winter is popular for skiing and snowshoeing.
