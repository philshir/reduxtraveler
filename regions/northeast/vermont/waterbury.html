How to Get There

From Burlington (28 mi): Take I-89 S toward Montpelier, use Exit 10, and follow signs into downtown Waterbury at the intersection of Interstate 89 and Route 100.

Overview

Waterbury is a village and town in north-central Vermont, in the Winooski River valley between Montpelier and Stowe, and it is best known as the home of the Ben & Jerry's Ice Cream Factory, the most visited tourist attraction in the state. The town is also known for its craft beer, its cider, its historic downtown, and its location at the crossroads of Interstate 89 and the road to Stowe. Waterbury is a popular stop for visitors headed to the ski resorts of the Mad River Valley and Stowe.

Visitors come to Waterbury for the Ben & Jerry's factory tour, for the Cold Hollow Cider Mill, and for the craft breweries, such as the Alchemist, which makes the cult-favorite Heady Topper. They also come for Waterbury Reservoir, for hiking in Mount Mansfield State Forest, and for the downtown's shops and restaurants.

History

Waterbury was chartered in 1763 and settled in the 1780s, and it grew with the arrival of the Central Vermont Railway in 1849, becoming a railroad and trade center. In the nineteenth century, it was the home of the Vermont State Hospital, and Waterbury Center was a farming area. A devastating flood from the Winooski River during Tropical Storm Irene in 2011, and another flood in July 2023, damaged the downtown, and the community rebuilt both times. Ben & Jerry's, founded in Burlington, opened its factory in Waterbury in 1985, and it has helped make the town a destination.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Ben & Jerry's Ice Cream Factory offers tours that show how the ice cream is made and a "flavor graveyard" for retired flavors, and the Cold Hollow Cider Mill, in nearby Waterbury Center, makes apple cider doughnuts and cider. The downtown has restored brick buildings with shops, restaurants, and the Waterbury Historical Society's museum in the old train depot, and the Alchemist Brewery has a brewery and a visitor center. The Waterbury Reservoir is a popular spot for boating and swimming.

Culture and Museums

The Waterbury Historical Society operates a small museum on the town's history, and the Vermont Ski & Snowboard Museum in Stowe is nearby. The town hosts the Waterbury Arts Fest, and the Waterbury Center and Stowe areas have a number of local craft makers. The nearby Green Mountain Coffee Roasters' visitor center explains coffee roasting.

Parks

The Waterbury Reservoir and Little River State Park have a beach, a campground, and trails around the reservoir, and Mount Mansfield State Forest and Camel's Hump State Park have hiking trails to some of Vermont's highest peaks. The Winooski River and the Perry Hill trail system offer mountain biking and swimming. The Mad River Valley and Stowe, with their ski resorts, are a short drive.

Seasons and Weather

Waterbury has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 85 inches of snow. Fall foliage is a highlight in October, and heavy rain can cause flooding in the Winooski valley.
