Seasons:
All year; winter is renowned for skiing and snowboarding, summer for hiking and biking, and fall for spectacular foliage. Spring offers fewer crowds and scenic mountain views.

How to Get There

From Burlington (36 mi): Take I-89 S to Exit 10 in Waterbury, follow VT-100 N through Waterbury, and continue approximately 10 miles into downtown Stowe.

Overview

Stowe is a picturesque resort town in northern Vermont, at the foot of Mount Mansfield, the state's highest peak at 4,393 feet, and it is known as the "Ski Capital of the East," for its ski resort, its white-steepled church, and its charming village. Stowe is also known as the home of the Trapp Family Lodge, run by the family made famous by The Sound of Music, and for the Stowe Recreation Path, an easy trail through the countryside. The town is a year-round destination for skiing, hiking, shopping, and fall foliage.

Visitors come to Stowe for Stowe Mountain Resort and skiing on Mount Mansfield, for the Stowe Recreation Path, and for the dramatic cliffs of Smugglers' Notch. They also come for the Trapp Family Lodge, for waterfalls such as Moss Glen Falls, and for the craft beer, cider, and maple products of the area.

History

Stowe was chartered in 1763 and settled in the 1790s, and it was named for the Stowe family from England. For most of the nineteenth century it was a farming and logging community, and in the 1860s the first hotel, the Summit House, opened on Mount Mansfield. In the 1930s, the Civilian Conservation Corps cut ski trails on the mountain, and skiing began to attract visitors, with the first chairlift installed in 1940. The von Trapp family, Austrian singers who fled the Nazis, settled in Stowe in 1942 and opened their lodge, now a resort, in 1950. Stowe has since developed into a major ski and summer resort.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The Stowe Community Church, with its white steeple, is the town's best-known landmark, and the village has shops, galleries, and restaurants. Smugglers' Notch is a narrow mountain pass with 1,000-foot cliffs and boulders, and the road through it is closed in winter. Mount Mansfield has a toll road and a gondola to the summit area, and Gold Brook Covered Bridge, the "Emily's Bridge," is a historic covered bridge said to be haunted. The Trapp Family Lodge has a bakery and a brewery.

Culture and Museums

The Helen Day Art Center shows contemporary art in a former school, and the Vermont Ski and Snowboard Museum, in the village's old town hall, covers the history of skiing in the state. The Stowe Performing Arts presents concerts, and the Trapp Family Lodge has a museum of the family's history. Events include the Stowe Winter Carnival in January and the Stowe Foliage Arts Festival in October.

Parks

Mount Mansfield State Forest and Smugglers' Notch State Park have hiking trails, camping, and views, and the Long Trail runs along the mountain's ridge. The Stowe Recreation Path is a 5.3-mile paved trail through meadows and along a river, and Sterling Pond, Moss Glen Falls, and Bingham Falls are popular for swimming and walking. Skiing, snowboarding, and cross-country skiing are the main winter activities.

Seasons and Weather

Stowe has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and more than 100 inches of snow on the mountain. Fall foliage in late September and October is spectacular, and ski season runs from late November to April.
