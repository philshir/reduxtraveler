How to Get There

From Lebanon, NH (15 mi): Take I-89 S to Exit 1, follow US-4 W across the Connecticut River into Vermont, and continue directly into the village of Woodstock.

Overview

Woodstock is a classic New England village in central Vermont, on the Ottauquechee River, and it is often called one of the prettiest small towns in America. Its village green is surrounded by Federal-style houses, a white church, and a historic inn, and a covered bridge crosses the river at the edge of the green. Woodstock is also home to Billings Farm & Museum and the Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park, the only national park in Vermont, and it is a popular destination for fall foliage.

Visitors come to Woodstock for the village green and its shops, galleries, and restaurants, for Billings Farm & Museum, a working dairy farm, and for the park's historic mansion and managed forest. They also come for the Middle Covered Bridge, for the Woodstock Inn & Resort, and for nearby Quechee Gorge, with its 165-foot-deep gorge.

History

Woodstock was chartered in 1761 and settled in the 1760s, and it became the shire town of Windsor County, with a grand courthouse and elegant homes built by lawyers and merchants in the Federal style. In the nineteenth century, native son George Perkins Marsh, a diplomat and author of Man and Nature, an early work of conservation, grew up here, and the property later passed to Frederick Billings, a railroad president, who created a model dairy farm and a managed forest. Laurance Rockefeller, Billings's grandson-in-law, donated the land to form the national park, which opened in 1998. The first commercial ski tow in the United States was installed on a Woodstock hillside in 1934.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

The village green, called the Green, is the center of town, with the Woodstock Inn and the Dana House Museum nearby, and the Middle Covered Bridge, built in 1969 in the Town lattice style, spans the Ottauquechee River. Billings Farm & Museum is a working dairy farm with Jersey cows and exhibits on rural life, and the Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller Mansion has a collection of Hudson River School paintings. Quechee Gorge is a short drive east, and Mount Tom has a trail to views over the village.

Culture and Museums

The Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park tells the story of American conservation, with guided tours of the mansion and the carriage roads and forest, and the Billings Farm shows the history of Vermont agriculture. The Woodstock History Center in the Dana House has collections on local history, and the Pentangle Arts Council, the Town Hall Theatre, and local galleries add to the arts. Events include Wassail Weekend in December and the Winter Carnival.

Parks

The Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park has 20 miles of carriage roads and trails through one of the oldest professionally managed forests in the country, and Mount Tom, in the park, has a popular hike to a view over the village. Faulkner Park and the Kedron Valley offer more trails, and the Ottauquechee River is popular for fishing and paddling. Quechee State Park, with its gorge, is nearby, and the Woodstock Ski Touring Center has cross-country skiing in winter.

Seasons and Weather

Woodstock has a humid continental climate. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold and snowy, with highs in the 20s and 30s°F and about 70 inches of snow. Fall foliage in late September and October is the busiest season, and the village is especially pretty in winter, with holiday decorations.
