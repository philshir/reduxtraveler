Overview

Plymouth, Massachusetts, calls itself "America's Hometown" because it is the site of the first permanent European settlement in New England, founded by the Pilgrims who arrived on the Mayflower in 1620. Located on Cape Cod Bay about 40 miles south of Boston, it is associated with the story of the first Thanksgiving, with Plymouth Rock, and with the Wampanoag people who helped the colonists survive. Today the town is both a major historic destination and a harbor community with beaches, cranberry bogs, and a working waterfront.

Visitors come to Plymouth to see Plymouth Rock, to tour the Mayflower II, a replica of the Pilgrims' ship, and to visit Plimoth Patuxet Museums, a living history museum. They also come for Pilgrim Hall Museum, for Plymouth Beach and the harbor, for whale watching and cruises, and for the Thanksgiving celebrations in November.

History

The Pilgrims, English Separatists seeking religious freedom, sailed on the Mayflower in 1620 and first landed at Provincetown on Cape Cod, where they signed the Mayflower Compact, before settling at Plymouth in December, on the site of an abandoned Wampanoag village called Patuxet. Nearly half of the 102 passengers died during the first winter, and in the spring the survivors were helped by Squanto, a Patuxet man who spoke English, and by the Wampanoag sachem Massasoit, who made a treaty with them. The harvest feast of 1621, shared by the colonists and about 90 Wampanoag men, is the basis for the American Thanksgiving tradition. Plymouth Colony remained a separate colony until it merged with Massachusetts Bay in 1691, and the town later became a center for fishing, shipbuilding, and rope-making, and then a tourist destination.

Preserved Sites of the Pilgrims and Early Colonization

Plymouth preserves more sites of the Pilgrim landing and early colonization than any other place in America. Plymouth Rock, the traditional landing place identified in 1741, sits on the waterfront beneath a classical portico, and the nearby Mayflower II, a 1957 replica, is open for tours. Cole's Hill overlooks the harbor and has the graves of the settlers who died in the first winter, and Burial Hill, the site of the original fort, has gravestones from the 1600s. The Jabez Howland House, built in 1667, is the only remaining home in Plymouth known to have been occupied by a Mayflower passenger, and the Pilgrim Hall Museum, founded in 1824, is the oldest continuously operating public museum in the country, with the Pilgrims' possessions. Plimoth Patuxet Museums recreates the 1627 colony and a Wampanoag homesite, the Plimoth Grist Mill is a working reconstruction of a 1636 mill, and the National Monument to the Forefathers, an 81-foot granite statue dedicated in 1889, honors the Pilgrims.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Plymouth's waterfront has the Rock, the Mayflower II, and State Pier, and Water Street and Main Street have shops, restaurants, and historic buildings. Brewster Gardens is a park along Town Brook, named for Elder William Brewster, and the Plymouth Antiquarian Society operates historic house museums such as the Hedge House and the Spooner House. Whale watching and fishing boats leave from the harbor, and a seasonal ferry runs to Provincetown.

Culture and Museums

Pilgrim Hall Museum displays the objects associated with the Pilgrims, including the cradle of Peregrine White, the first English child born in New England, and Plimoth Patuxet Museums includes both the 17th Century English Village and the Wampanoag Homesite staffed by Native interpreters. The Plymouth Antiquarian Society, the Mayflower Society House, and the Plymouth National Wax Museum are other attractions, and the Plymouth Center for the Arts and the Spire Center for Performing Arts host events. America's Hometown Thanksgiving Celebration includes a parade in November.

Parks

Plymouth Beach is a three-mile barrier beach with views of the harbor, and the town has nine public beaches in all. Myles Standish State Forest, in the southern part of the town, has 12,000 acres of pitch pine forest, kettle ponds, and trails, and Morton Park and Brewster Gardens are popular for picnics. Cranberry bogs are a distinctive part of the landscape, particularly in autumn when the harvest takes place.

Seasons and Weather

Plymouth has a humid continental climate moderated by Cape Cod Bay. Summers are warm, with highs in the upper 70s°F, and winters are cold, with highs in the 30s°F and about 40 inches of snow. Summer and fall are the busiest seasons, and some attractions are closed or have reduced hours in winter.
