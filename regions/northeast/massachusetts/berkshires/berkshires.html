Pricing depends on the specific venue or activity; many outdoor areas are free, while museums and performances may charge admission ranging from $10–$30.

Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter — each season offers unique experiences.

How to Get There

From Springfield, MA (45 mi): Take US-20 W to MA-8 N, then follow signs for Pittsfield and continue into the heart of the Berkshires region.

Overview

The Berkshires are a highland region in western Massachusetts, celebrated for their blend of natural beauty and cultural richness. With rolling hills, dense forests, and charming towns, they have long attracted artists, writers, and vacationers seeking inspiration and tranquility.

The region is home to world-renowned institutions such as Tanglewood, the summer home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, and the Massachusetts Museum of Contemporary Art (MASS MoCA). Outdoor activities include hiking the Appalachian Trail, skiing in winter, and exploring historic estates like The Mount, Edith Wharton’s home, or wellness retreats such as the Kripalu Center for Yoga & Health.

Geography

The Berkshires fill Berkshire County, a roughly 50-mile-long highland between the Connecticut River Valley to the east and the Taconic Mountains and the New York border to the west. Mount Greylock, at 3,491 feet, is the highest point in Massachusetts. The Housatonic River winds through the central valley, and the Appalachian Trail crosses the county for about 90 miles. Forests of maple, beech, and birch cover most of the hills.

History

The Mohican (Stockbridge-Munsee) people lived in the area before English missionaries and settlers arrived in the 1700s. Stockbridge was founded as a mission town in 1734. Shays’ Rebellion in 1787 centered on Great Barrington, and the region later became a mill-and-paper-making center, with Dalton still producing paper for U.S. currency. In the Gilded Age, wealthy New Yorkers built grand summer “cottages” in Lenox and Stockbridge, and writers including Herman Melville and Nathaniel Hawthorne lived and worked here. W. E. B. Du Bois was born in Great Barrington in 1868.

Cities and Towns

Lenox and Stockbridge are classic New England towns with historic inns, Tanglewood, and the Norman Rockwell Museum. Great Barrington is known for its arts scene and restaurants, Williamstown for the Clark Art Institute and Williams College, and North Adams for MASS MoCA. Pittsfield is the county seat, and Lee and Great Barrington offer outlets and farm stands.

National Protected Areas

The Berkshires have no national park of their own, but the Appalachian National Scenic Trail runs through the county, passing over Mount Greylock and through the Housatonic Valley. The W. E. B. Du Bois Boyhood Homesite in Great Barrington is a National Historic Landmark. Much of the high country is in state forest or private conservation land, and the nearest national park units are in the Hudson Valley and Cape Cod.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Mount Greylock State Reservation has a war memorial tower, Bascom Lodge, and trails, and October Mountain State Forest is the largest state forest in Massachusetts. Bash Bish Falls State Park has the tallest waterfall in the state, and Beartown and Pittsfield state forests offer hiking and camping. Cultural sites include Hancock Shaker Village, Arrowhead (Herman Melville’s home), and The Mount.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

Summer is the cultural high season, with Tanglewood, Jacob’s Pillow Dance, and the Williamstown Theatre Festival running from late June through August. Fall foliage usually peaks in early to mid-October and draws large crowds. Winter brings skiing at Jiminy Peak, Bousquet, and Catamount, and cozy inns, while spring is mud season followed by maple sugaring in March and early blooms in May. The Appalachian Trail is best hiked from late spring to fall.
