Overview

Martha’s Vineyard is an island south of Cape Cod and a popular summer colony, with a year-round population of about 20,000 and a summer population of more than 100,000. Visitors come for the gingerbread cottages, whaling-era captains’ houses, clay cliffs, beaches, farms, and fishing villages.

The island has six towns, each with its own character, and it is reached by ferry from Woods Hole, Hyannis, Falmouth, and other ports, or by air. It is popular for biking, beaches, sailing, shopping, dining, and sightseeing, with a slower pace than the mainland.

Geography

The island is about 20 miles long and roughly 100 square miles, and sits about 7 miles off the southwestern coast of Cape Cod. It is part of the glacial moraine that includes Cape Cod and Nantucket, with outwash plains in the south, rolling hills in the north, and brightly colored clay cliffs at Aquinnah. Salt ponds and barrier beaches line the south shore, and Chappaquiddick is a small island attached on the east. The surrounding ocean keeps the climate milder than the mainland.

History

The Wampanoag have lived on the island for thousands of years, and the Aquinnah Wampanoag still live at Aquinnah. Bartholomew Gosnold named the island in 1602, probably for his daughter, and English settlers arrived in 1642 under Thomas Mayhew. Whaling made Edgartown wealthy in the 1800s, and Methodist camp meetings in Oak Bluffs in the 1830s led to the town’s Campground cottages and the island’s role as a summer resort. The island became widely known after Jaws was filmed there in 1974 and later as a presidential vacation spot.

Cities and Towns

Tisbury (Vineyard Haven) is the main port for people and cargo. Edgartown is a whaling town and the island’s largest, with Mytoi and Chappaquiddick nearby, and Oak Bluffs is known for gingerbread cottages, the Flying Horses Carousel, and nightlife on Circuit Avenue. West Tisbury is the agricultural center, Chilmark is rural and hilly, with the fishing village of Menemsha, and Aquinnah is the Wampanoag homeland with the Gay Head clay cliffs.

National Protected Areas

The island has no national park units, but the Gay Head Cliffs are a National Natural Landmark, and the Martha’s Vineyard Camp Meeting Association Historic District in Oak Bluffs is a National Historic Landmark. Nearby on the mainland, Cape Cod National Seashore is a day trip from Woods Hole, and several lighthouses, including East Chop and Edgartown Harbor, are on the National Register of Historic Places.

State Parks and Historic Sites

Joseph A. Sylvia State Beach is a popular beach between Oak Bluffs and Edgartown, and Manuel F. Correllus State Forest in the island’s center has biking and hiking trails. Conservation lands managed by The Trustees of Reservations, including Cape Poge, Wasque, and Mytoi, offer beaches and trails on Chappaquiddick. The Felix Neck Wildlife Sanctuary is run by Mass Audubon, and the Menemsha Hills Reservation has trails with ocean views.

Seasons and Best Time to Visit

Summer is the high season from Memorial Day through Labor Day, with crowded ferries, beaches, and towns, and advance reservations are essential. September is considered the best month for swimming and fewer crowds, and the Martha’s Vineyard Striped Bass and Bluefish Derby takes place from mid-September through mid-October. The Martha’s Vineyard Agricultural Fair is held in late August. Many businesses close from late fall to spring, and winter is quiet and windy. Spring offers biking and gardens in a more peaceful setting.
