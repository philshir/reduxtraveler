Freedom Trail

★5.0
  1. Northeast
  2. Massachusetts
  3. Boston
  4. Freedom Trail

Ingfbruno, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

2.5-mile-long (4.0 km) path through downtown Boston, Massachusetts, that passes by 16 locations significant to the history of the United States

Places to See on Freedom Trail

Daderot, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Boston Latin School

★ 5.0

Public exam school established on April 23, 1635, making it both the oldest public school in America and the oldest existing school in the United States

Boston Massacre Site

★ 5.0

Historic site marked by a memorial plaza and interpretive exhibits commemorating the 1770 confrontation between British soldiers and colonists that became a pivotal event leading to the American Revolution

Jacob Rottmann, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Bunker Hill Monument

★ 5.0

Erected to commemorate the Battle of Bunker Hill, which was among the first major battles between British and Patriot forces in the American Revolutionary War

Copp's Hill Burying Ground

★ 5.0

Second oldest cemetery in Boston contains more than 1200 marked graves, including the remains of various notable Bostonians from the colonial era into the 1850s

Eric Kilby from Somerville, MA, USA, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Faneuil Hall

★ 5.0

Marketplace and meeting hall, it was the site of several speeches by Samuel Adams, James Otis, and others encouraging independence from Great Britain

Granary Burying Ground

★ 5.0

Boston's third-oldest cemetery, is the final resting place for many notable Revolutionary War-era patriots, including Paul Revere, the five victims of the Boston Massacre, and three signers of the Declaration of Independence: Samuel Adams, John Hancock, and Robert Treat Paine

King's Chapel Burying Ground

★ 5.0

From Downtown Boston (0.2 mi): Walk along Tremont Street past Boston Common, and King’s Chapel Burying Ground is located directly beside King’s Chapel on the Freedom Trail.

King's Chapel

★ 5.0

Completed in 1754, is one of the finest designs of the noted colonial architect Peter Harrison, and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960 for its architectural significance

King of Hearts, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Massachusetts State House

★ 5.0

State capitol and seat of government for Massachusetts, completed in January 1798, is considered a masterpiece of Federal architecture

Old Corner Bookstore

★ 5.0

Historic commercial building built in 1718 as a residence and apothecary shop, and first became a bookstore in 1828

Domenico Convertini, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Old North Church

★ 5.0

Location from which the famous "One if by land, two if by sea" signal is said to have been sent, relating to Paul Revere's midnight ride

Thaidragon27, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Old South Meeting House

★ 5.0

Historic Congregational church building that gained fame as the organizing point for the Boston Tea Party on December 16, 1773

Mobilus In Mobili, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Old State House

★ 5.0

Historic building in Boston, built in 1713, it was the seat of the Massachusetts General Court until 1798 and is one of the oldest public buildings in the United States

Park Street Church

★ 5.0

Historic and active evangelical church founded in 1804 was the tallest building in the United States from 1810 to 1828 and was the first landmark travelers saw when approaching Boston

Beyond My Ken, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

Paul Revere House

★ 5.0

Colonial home of American patriot Paul Revere during the time of the American Revolution

Yuhan Zhang, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons; Image Size Adjusted

USS Constitution

★ 5.0

Wooden-hulled, three-masted heavy frigate of the United States Navy is the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel still afloat

Overview

The Freedom Trail is a 2.5-mile path of red brick and painted line through downtown Boston, linking 16 sites important to U.S. history from Boston Common to the Bunker Hill Monument in Charlestown. Stops include churches, burying grounds, meeting houses, and a warship. Journalist William Schofield proposed it in 1951. The National Park Service visitor center on the first floor of Faneuil Hall offers tours and free maps.

Trail Sites (south to north)

  • Boston Common, the Massachusetts State House, and Park Street Church
  • Granary Burying Ground, King's Chapel and its burying ground, and Boston Latin School
  • Old Corner Bookstore, Old South Meeting House, and the Old State House with the Boston Massacre Site
  • Faneuil Hall, Paul Revere House, Old North Church, and Copp's Hill Burying Ground
  • USS Constitution and the Bunker Hill Monument in Charlestown
