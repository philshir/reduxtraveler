John Fitzgerald Kennedy National Historic Site

★5.0

Birthplace and early childhood home of President John F. Kennedy, preserved as a restored house museum reflecting his family life and formative years.

How to Get There

From Boston: Take the MBTA Green Line (C branch) to Coolidge Corner, then walk about four blocks along Harvard Street and turn onto Beals Street. Driving directions lead via I‑90 or local roads into Brookline’s residential neighborhood.

Overview

John Fitzgerald Kennedy National Historic Site preserves the birthplace of John F. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States. The modest house at 83 Beals Street in Brookline is where Kennedy was born on May 29, 1917, and spent the first years of his life with his family. The house was built in 1909 and purchased by Joseph and Rose Kennedy in 1914. Rose Kennedy restored it in the 1960s, and guided tours show the bedroom where Kennedy was born, the parlor, the dining room and the nursery.

Access

The site is at 83 Beals Street in Brookline, about 4 miles from downtown Boston. Visitors take the MBTA Green Line C branch to Coolidge Corner and walk. Street parking is limited. Admission is free.

Seasons

The site is open Wednesday through Sunday from spring through fall and closed in winter. Spring and fall bring pleasant weather. Summer is warm. Tours are by ranger and leave at set times.

Things to Do

Tour the House

Rangers lead tours of the restored rooms.

See the Nursery

Period items recreate daily life.

Hear Rose Kennedy's Recordings

Narration tells about the family.

Walk the Neighborhood

Brookline streets show the Kennedys' early world.

Visit the JFK Library

The presidential library is in Dorchester.

Top Trails

Beals Street Walk

Length: Approximately 0.3 miles round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A short walk around the block with signs.

Coolidge Corner Walk

Length: Approximately 1 mile round trip
Elevation Gain: Minimal
Difficulty: Easy

A walk to the commercial center and the cinema.

Visitor Centers

The visitor center in the house has exhibits and rangers. Tours leave at set times, and admission is free. The site is small, so tours are limited.

Camping and Lodging

There is no camping or lodging at the site. Hotels are in Brookline, Boston and Cambridge. Camping is available at Wompatuck State Park.
