Aguadilla

Coastal city known for dramatic cliffs, expansive beaches, and aviation heritage shaped by trade winds and Atlantic exposure.

How to Get There

From San Juan, PR: Drive west along PR‑2 following the northern coast, then continue toward the northwest corner of the island where Aguadilla is reached via coastal highways.

Overview

Aguadilla is a coastal city of about 55,000 people on the northwest corner of Puerto Rico, where the Atlantic Ocean meets the Mona Passage. It is famous for its surf and beaches, for the long-standing claim that Christopher Columbus landed here in 1493, and for its former military base, Ramey Air Force Base. Today it is known for its airport, which serves the west coast, and for its relaxed beach and surf culture.

People come to Aguadilla to surf, snorkel and dive at beaches such as Crash Boat Beach, watch the sunset over the Atlantic, see the colorful murals and painted houses, visit the Punta Borinquen lighthouse ruins and enjoy fresh seafood. It is a base for exploring Rincón, Isabela and the northwest coast, and for whale watching from January to March.

History

The Taíno people lived on the northwest coast before the Spanish arrived. A local tradition holds that Columbus landed near Aguadilla on November 19, 1493, on his second voyage, to take on fresh water, and the name Aguadilla comes from the Spanish word for a watering place. The town was founded in the 1770s and grew as a port and market for sugar, coffee and tobacco. An earthquake and tsunami struck the northwest coast in October 1918 and caused heavy damage. In 1939 the U.S. military opened Borinquen Field, which became Ramey Air Force Base and was a major base during the Cold War until it closed in 1973. The former base is now the Rafael Hernández Airport and a golf course, and its surfing beaches are known worldwide.

Sightseeing and Popular Landmarks

Crash Boat Beach is the best-known site, with a long, brightly painted wooden pier that was once used by the military to launch rescue boats, and clear water good for swimming, snorkeling and diving. Nearby surf spots such as Wilderness, Surfer's Beach and Gas Chambers draw surfers in winter. The ruins of the Punta Borinquen Lighthouse (1889) stand on a cliff above the ocean near the old base, and the painted houses of Aguadilla brighten a hillside neighborhood. Downtown, the historic plaza and the waterfront promenade have restaurants and shops.

Culture and Museums

Aguadilla's culture is a mix of Puerto Rican traditions and surf and beach life. The city has a historic downtown with a colonial-era church, and it hosts festivals such as concerts and surf competitions. Local cuisine features fresh fish, mofongo and fried street food, and the former Ramey base area has memorials and a golf course that recall its military history.

Parks

Beaches are Aguadilla's main parks. Crash Boat is a public beach with facilities, and the Ramey beaches have long stretches of sand and surf. The Aguadilla cliffs along the coast have viewpoints and trails near the Punta Borinquen lighthouse ruins. Nearby, the Guajataca Tunnel and the beaches of Isabela offer more coastal scenery, and the Mona Passage has good whale watching from January to March when humpback whales pass offshore.

Seasons and Weather

Aguadilla has a tropical climate with warm temperatures all year, with highs in the mid to upper 80s °F and steady trade winds. Winter, from December to April, is the driest season and the best time for big surf, while summer is hot and the sea is calmer for swimming and snorkeling. Hurricane season runs from June to November, and Hurricane Maria in 2017 caused heavy damage in the area. Rip currents are strong at many beaches, so heed lifeguards and posted warnings.
