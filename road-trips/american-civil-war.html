Overview

The American Civil War left an enduring imprint on the nation's landscape, and its historic sites offer visitors a powerful window into one of the most consequential periods in United States history. Across preserved battlefields, military installations, historic homes, and interpretive museums, travelers can explore the stories of soldiers, civilians, political leaders, and enslaved people whose lives were shaped by the conflict. These sites help bring to life the causes, course, and consequences of a war that transformed the nation.

Many Civil War sites protect landscapes where pivotal campaigns and battles unfolded, allowing visitors to walk the same ground where historic events occurred. Museums and visitor centers feature artifacts, personal accounts, military equipment, and interactive exhibits that provide insight into the experiences of those who lived through the war. Together, these landmarks illustrate not only military strategy and battlefield action but also the social, political, and human dimensions of a conflict that tested the country's future.

For modern travelers, Civil War sites offer opportunities for both education and reflection. Preserved cemeteries, memorials, and historic structures serve as reminders of the immense sacrifices made during the conflict and the profound changes that followed, including the abolition of slavery and the preservation of the Union. Whether exploring a sprawling battlefield or a small historic museum, visitors encounter stories of courage, hardship, and resilience that continue to shape the American narrative today.

American Civil War Timeline (1861-1865)

The American Civil War was fought between the United States (Union) and the Confederate States from 1861 to 1865. The conflict centered on the preservation of the Union and the issue of slavery, becoming the deadliest war in American history. The following timeline highlights major military campaigns, battlefields, political centers, hospitals, prisons, naval sites, and surrender locations preserved today as historic sites across the United States.

1861: Secession and the Opening of the War

South Carolina

  • Fort Sumter National Historical Park (Charleston) - The Civil War began on April 12, 1861, when Confederate forces opened fire on Fort Sumter.
  • Fort Moultrie - Part of Charleston Harbor's defensive system and closely linked to the opening events of the war.
  • Secessionville Battlefield - Site of a significant 1862 Confederate victory protecting Charleston.

Virginia

  • Richmond National Battlefield Park - Preserves battlefields surrounding Richmond, the capital of the Confederacy.
  • White House of the Confederacy - Official residence of Confederate President Jefferson Davis.
  • Tredegar Iron Works - One of the Confederacy's most important industrial facilities, producing artillery and military equipment.
  • Manassas National Battlefield (First Bull Run) - Site of the First Battle of Bull Run in July 1861, the first major land battle of the war.

Missouri

  • Wilson's Creek National Battlefield - One of the earliest major battles west of the Mississippi River.
  • Carthage Battlefield - Site of an early Missouri engagement reflecting the state's divided loyalties.
  • Lexington Battlefield - Location of the 1861 Battle of Lexington, an important Confederate victory in Missouri.

1862: Fighting Across the Border States and the Mississippi Valley

Tennessee

  • Fort Donelson National Battlefield - Union victory that opened major river routes and elevated Ulysses S. Grant to national prominence.
  • Shiloh National Military Park - Site of one of the war's bloodiest battles up to that point in April 1862.
  • Stones River National Battlefield - Major Union victory that strengthened federal control of Middle Tennessee.

Arkansas

  • Pea Ridge National Military Park - Union victory that helped secure Missouri and much of the Trans-Mississippi West.
  • Prairie Grove Battlefield - Decisive engagement that established Union control over northwestern Arkansas.

Virginia

  • Gaines' Mill Battlefield - Largest battle of the Seven Days Campaign around Richmond.
  • Malvern Hill Battlefield - Final battle of the Seven Days Campaign.
  • Manassas National Battlefield (Second Bull Run) - Major Confederate victory during General Robert E. Lee's 1862 offensive.

Maryland

  • South Mountain Battlefield - Important precursor battle during Lee's first invasion of the North.
  • Antietam National Battlefield - Site of the bloodiest single day in American military history and the battle that led to the Emancipation Proclamation.

1863: Turning Points of the War

Pennsylvania

  • Gettysburg National Military Park - Site of the largest battle ever fought in North America and a decisive Union victory in July 1863.
  • Gettysburg National Cemetery - Final resting place of Union soldiers and location of Abraham Lincoln's Gettysburg Address.
  • Hanover Battlefield - Cavalry engagement connected to the Gettysburg Campaign.
  • Monterey Pass Battlefield - Site of a significant action during the Confederate retreat from Gettysburg.

Mississippi

  • Vicksburg National Military Park - Commemorates the successful Union siege that gave the North control of the Mississippi River.
  • USS Cairo Museum - Preserves a Union ironclad gunboat that operated during the Vicksburg Campaign.

Tennessee & Georgia

  • Chickamauga Battlefield (Georgia) - Major Confederate victory in September 1863.
  • Chattanooga National Military Park (Tennessee) - Preserves battles that opened the Deep South to Union invasion.
  • Lookout Mountain Battlefield - Site of the famous "Battle Above the Clouds."
  • Missionary Ridge Battlefield - Decisive Union breakthrough that secured Chattanooga.

Virginia

  • Brandy Station Battlefield - Largest cavalry battle fought on the North American continent.
  • New Market Battlefield - Later famous for the participation of Virginia Military Institute cadets.

1864: Total War and the Final Campaigns

Virginia

  • Wilderness Battlefield - Opening battle of Grant's Overland Campaign.
  • Spotsylvania Court House Battlefield - Site of intense trench and close-quarters fighting.
  • Fredericksburg Battlefield - An important battlefield revisited during the Overland Campaign.
  • Cold Harbor Battlefield - Known for one of the war's costliest Union assaults.
  • Petersburg National Battlefield - Site of a lengthy siege that eventually led to Richmond's fall.
  • Fort Harrison Battlefield - Important Union breakthrough against Richmond's defensive line.
  • Cedar Creek Battlefield - Major Union victory securing the Shenandoah Valley.
  • Kernstown Battlefield - Key battlefield of the Shenandoah Valley campaigns.

Georgia

  • Resaca Battlefield - First major engagement of the Atlanta Campaign.
  • Pickett's Mill Battlefield - Fierce fighting during Union advances toward Atlanta.
  • Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park - One of the strongest Confederate defensive positions in the Atlanta Campaign.

Tennessee

  • Spring Hill Battlefield - Site of a missed Confederate opportunity during the Franklin-Nashville Campaign.
  • Franklin Battlefield - Scene of one of the Confederacy's most devastating defeats.

Mississippi

  • Brice's Crossroads Battlefield - Confederate victory under Nathan Bedford Forrest.
  • Tupelo National Battlefield - Union operation intended to neutralize Forrest's cavalry force.

South Carolina

  • Honey Hill Battlefield - One of the final significant battles fought in South Carolina.
  • CSS Hunley Museum - Preserves artifacts linked to the Confederate submarine CSS Hunley, the first submarine to sink an enemy warship.

Georgia

  • Andersonville National Historic Site - Site of the Confederate military prison that became infamous for harsh conditions.
  • Camp Lawton Historic Site - Another major Confederate prison camp established late in the war.

1865: Collapse of the Confederacy and Surrender

Virginia

  • Five Forks Battlefield - Decisive Union victory that opened the way to Petersburg and Richmond.
  • Sailor's Creek Battlefield - Often called the "death knell of the Confederacy" because of heavy Confederate losses.
  • Appomattox Court House National Historical Park - Site where General Robert E. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to General Ulysses S. Grant on April 9, 1865.

North Carolina

  • Averasboro Battlefield - One of the final major Confederate attempts to slow Union forces in the Carolinas.
  • Bentonville Battlefield - Largest Civil War battle fought in North Carolina and one of the final major engagements of the war.
  • Bennett Place - Site of the largest Confederate surrender, where General Joseph E. Johnston surrendered his forces.
  • Fort Fisher State Historic Site - Preserves the Confederate fort whose capture closed Wilmington, the Confederacy's last major Atlantic port.

Western Theater and Trans-Mississippi Campaigns

Louisiana

  • Port Hudson State Historic Site - Site of the longest siege in American military history.
  • Mansfield State Historic Site - Major Confederate victory during the Red River Campaign.
  • Pleasant Hill Battlefield - Important battle fought during the same campaign.

Arkansas

  • Poison Spring Battlefield - Significant engagement of the Red River Campaign.
  • Jenkins' Ferry Battlefield - One of the last major battles fought in Arkansas.

Medical, Naval, and Support Sites

Hospitals

  • Ben Lomond Historic Site (Virginia) - Preserves one of the best-known Confederate field hospitals.
  • Pry House Field Hospital (Maryland) - Headquarters and hospital site associated with the Antietam Campaign.
  • National Museum of Civil War Medicine (Maryland) - Interprets wartime medical innovations and battlefield care.

Naval Sites

  • Hampton Roads Naval Battle Sites (Virginia) - Associated with the famous clash between the ironclads USS Monitor and CSS Virginia.
  • USS Cairo Museum (Mississippi) - Preserves one of the few surviving Civil War ironclads.
  • CSS Hunley Museum (South Carolina) - Interprets the history of the pioneering Confederate submarine.

War Summary

Fort Sumter → First Bull Run → Fort Donelson → Shiloh → Antietam → Gettysburg → Vicksburg → Chickamauga → Chattanooga → Wilderness → Spotsylvania → Cold Harbor → Petersburg → Atlanta Campaign → Franklin → Five Forks → Sailor's Creek → Appomattox Court House → Bennett Place.

This article uses material from preserved Civil War battlefields, military parks, museums, hospitals, forts, prisons, naval sites, and surrender locations maintained by the National Park Service, state historic sites, and local preservation organizations. These sites collectively preserve the history of the American Civil War's Eastern, Western, and Trans-Mississippi theaters.
