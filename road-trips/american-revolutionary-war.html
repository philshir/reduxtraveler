Overview

The American Revolutionary War gave rise to a new nation, and its historic sites allow visitors to explore the places where the struggle for independence unfolded. From preserved battlefields and military encampments to historic meeting halls and homes associated with revolutionary leaders, these landmarks tell the story of a conflict that reshaped North America and influenced democratic movements around the world. Visitors can discover how ordinary citizens, soldiers, and statesmen contributed to the fight for self-government and independence.

Many Revolutionary War sites preserve the landscapes where pivotal military campaigns were fought and where important political decisions were made. Museums and interpretive centers showcase artifacts ranging from weapons and uniforms to personal letters and historical documents, helping visitors understand both the military and civilian experiences of the era. Together, these sites provide insight into the challenges faced by the Continental Army, the role of foreign allies, and the complex political debates that accompanied the birth of a new nation.

For today's travelers, Revolutionary War sites offer a chance to walk through the earliest chapters of American history while reflecting on the principles that shaped the country's founding. Historic buildings, reconstructed forts, memorials, and preserved landscapes bring the eighteenth century to life, creating meaningful connections between past and present. Whether exploring a famous battlefield or a lesser-known historic landmark, visitors gain a deeper appreciation for the sacrifices, leadership, and ideals that helped define the United States.

American Revolutionary War Timeline (1775-1783)

The American Revolutionary War was fought between Great Britain and the thirteen American colonies seeking independence. Beginning with armed conflict in Massachusetts in 1775 and culminating with the British surrender at Yorktown in 1781, the war established the United States as an independent nation. The following timeline highlights key battlefields, military headquarters, government sites, and museums that preserve the story of the American Revolution.

1775: The Shot Heard Around the World

Massachusetts

  • Minute Man National Historical Park (Lexington & Concord) - Preserves the route and sites associated with the opening battles of the Revolution on April 19, 1775.
  • Lexington Battle Green - Site where colonial militia and British troops exchanged the first shots of the Revolutionary War.
  • Concord's Old North Bridge - Location of the famous confrontation where colonial forces pushed British troops back toward Boston.
  • Freedom Trail (Boston) - Connects numerous sites associated with revolutionary activity and Boston's role in the independence movement.
  • Bunker Hill Monument and Battlefield - Commemorates the June 1775 Battle of Bunker Hill, one of the war's earliest and most influential engagements.
  • Longfellow House–Washington's Headquarters National Historic Site - Served as George Washington's headquarters during the Siege of Boston.

1776: The Fight for Boston and the Declaration of Independence

Massachusetts

  • Dorchester Heights - Site where American forces placed artillery overlooking Boston, prompting the British evacuation of the city in March 1776.

Pennsylvania

  • Independence Hall (Philadelphia) - Meeting place of the Second Continental Congress and site where the Declaration of Independence was adopted.
  • Carpenter's Hall - Home of the First Continental Congress and an important center of colonial political activity.

1776-1777: Washington's Counteroffensive in New Jersey

New Jersey

  • Trenton Battlefield - Site of George Washington's surprise victory after crossing the Delaware River in December 1776.
  • Princeton Battlefield State Park - Location of Washington's important January 1777 victory that helped revive American morale.
  • Dey Mansion Washington's Headquarters - Served as Washington's headquarters during portions of the war.

1777: The Turning Point Campaigns

New York

  • Saratoga National Historical Park - Site of the American victory often regarded as the turning point of the war because it secured French support.
  • Fort Ticonderoga - Strategic fortress that played a significant role in the northern campaigns.
  • Fort Stanwix National Monument - Important defensive position that helped disrupt British plans during the Saratoga Campaign.
  • Oriskany Battlefield - Site of one of the war's bloodiest battles and a critical component of the Saratoga Campaign.
  • Bennington Battlefield State Historic Site (New York Side) - Associated with the American victory that weakened the British advance toward Saratoga.

Pennsylvania

  • Brandywine Battlefield Park - Site of the largest land battle of the Revolutionary War and a British victory that opened the road to Philadelphia.
  • Paoli Battlefield - Location of a surprise British attack during the Philadelphia Campaign.
  • Germantown Battlefield - Site of Washington's bold but unsuccessful attack against British forces near Philadelphia.

1777-1778: The Winter at Valley Forge

Pennsylvania

  • Valley Forge National Historical Park - Winter encampment of the Continental Army where troops endured severe hardships while becoming a more disciplined fighting force.
  • Fort Mifflin - Defensive fortification that played an important role in the defense of Philadelphia.

1778: The War in the Mid-Atlantic

New Jersey

  • Monmouth Battlefield State Park - Site of one of the largest battles of the war and a demonstration of the Continental Army's improved effectiveness after Valley Forge.
  • Red Bank Battlefield Park - Location of a successful American defense that delayed British operations along the Delaware River.

New York

  • Washington's Headquarters State Historic Site - One of several headquarters used by George Washington during the war.

1779: Fighting Along the Hudson River

New York

  • Stony Point Battlefield - Site of General Anthony Wayne's successful nighttime assault on British fortifications.

1778-1781: The Southern Campaign

Georgia

  • Kettle Creek Battlefield - Important Patriot victory that disrupted Loyalist recruitment efforts in Georgia.

South Carolina

  • Cowpens National Battlefield - Site of one of the most decisive American victories of the Southern Campaign.
  • Kings Mountain National Military Park - Patriot victory that marked a major turning point in the South.
  • Ninety Six National Historic Site - Preserves a key frontier settlement and important Revolutionary War siege site.
  • Eutaw Springs Battlefield - One of the final major battles fought in the Southern Theater.

North Carolina

  • Moores Creek National Battlefield - Early Patriot victory that helped secure North Carolina for the revolutionary cause.
  • Guilford Courthouse National Military Park - Major 1781 battle that weakened British forces despite being a tactical British victory.
  • Alamance Battleground - Although predating the Revolution, it is often interpreted as an important precursor to colonial resistance.

Virginia

  • Great Bridge Battlefield & Waterways History Foundation - Site of an early Patriot victory in Virginia that helped drive British authority from the colony.

1781: Yorktown and the End of Major Combat Operations

Virginia

  • Yorktown - Site of the decisive siege where British General Charles Cornwallis surrendered his army to American and French forces.
  • American Revolution Museum at Yorktown - Interprets the Yorktown Campaign and the broader struggle for independence.
  • George Washington's Mount Vernon - Home of the commander of the Continental Army and later first President of the United States.

Building a New Nation

New York

  • Federal Hall (New York City) - Associated with the early federal government and George Washington's first presidential inauguration following independence.

Pennsylvania

  • Museum of the American Revolution (Philadelphia) - Preserves and interprets the history, causes, and consequences of the Revolutionary War.

Revolution Summary

Lexington Green → Concord's Old North Bridge → Bunker Hill → Dorchester Heights → Trenton → Princeton → Fort Ticonderoga → Oriskany → Saratoga → Brandywine → Germantown → Valley Forge → Monmouth → Stony Point → Kings Mountain → Cowpens → Guilford Courthouse → Yorktown → Federal Hall.
