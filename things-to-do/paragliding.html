Paragliding

  1. Things To Do
  2. Paragliding

paragliding

Foot-launched aerial adventure that uses a fabric wing and air currents to glide through the sky while enjoying panoramic views and a sensation of free flight.

General Information

Pricing:
$120–$350 per person for a tandem introductory flight with an instructor; comprehensive beginner training courses typically range from $600–$1,800.
Locations:
Popular throughout the United States in mountainous and coastal regions, including California (Torrey Pines and San Bernardino), Colorado, Utah, Idaho, Arizona, Nevada, Hawaii, Tennessee, and Washington.
Seasons:
Best conditions are generally found during spring, summer, and fall, depending on the region. Coastal sites often provide longer flying seasons, while mountain locations are most active during warmer months.
Difficulty
Moderate. Tandem flights are suitable for most healthy adults, while solo paragliding requires formal instruction, good judgment, and the ability to manage changing weather conditions.
Necessary Gear or Equipment
Paraglider wing, harness, helmet, reserve parachute, appropriate footwear, weather-appropriate clothing, and often a variometer or flight instruments for experienced pilots.
Typical Duration:
Tandem flights typically last 15–45 minutes in the air, while a complete experience including preparation, safety briefing, transportation, and setup generally lasts 1–3 hours.

Overview

Paragliding is a form of unpowered flight that allows participants to launch from a hillside, mountain, or coastal cliff and soar through the air using a lightweight fabric wing. By taking advantage of natural air currents and thermals, pilots can remain airborne while enjoying expansive views of landscapes that are difficult to experience from the ground. Many first-time participants begin with tandem flights, where an experienced instructor controls the wing and navigation.

The United States offers numerous locations well suited for paragliding, ranging from the coastal bluffs of Southern California to the Rocky Mountains of Colorado and the scenic valleys of Utah and Idaho. Tandem flights typically cost between $120 and $350 per person, making them one of the most accessible ways to experience free flight. Those interested in becoming independent pilots can enroll in structured training programs that teach launching, landing, meteorology, airspace awareness, and flight safety.

Weather conditions play a critical role in paragliding, which is why spring through fall is generally considered the best time to participate in many parts of the country. The activity is moderately challenging because it requires awareness of wind and terrain, though tandem experiences are appropriate for most average adults. Flights can last from a few minutes to well over half an hour depending on conditions, providing a memorable combination of adventure, scenic beauty, and the unique experience of flying without an engine.
