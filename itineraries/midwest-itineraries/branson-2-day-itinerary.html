Branson: Live Shows, Ozark Lakes, and Old-Fashioned Family Fun

2 Days

Tucked into the Ozark Mountains of southwest Missouri, Branson is a small resort city of about 12,000 residents that draws millions of visitors each year with its dozens of theaters, family attractions, and scenic lakes. Known as the "Live Entertainment Show Capital of the USA," it offers everything from country music and gospel to comedy, magic, and Broadway-style productions, along with the 1880s-themed Silver Dollar City and the clear waters of Table Rock Lake and Lake Taneycomo. This two-day itinerary spends the first day at Silver Dollar City and Table Rock Lake, ending with a dinner cruise on a paddlewheel showboat, then spends the second day exploring downtown Branson, the Highway 76 Strip, and the Branson Landing waterfront before an evening at one of the city's famous live shows. Top of the Rock, Dolly Parton's Stampede, and the hiking trails of the Ruth and Paul Henning Conservation Area are left out to keep the pace relaxed.

Day 1

The first day centers on the Table Rock Lake area west and south of town, where Silver Dollar City, Table Rock Dam, and the showboat dock are a short drive apart. Many attractions operate from spring through the Christmas season, and some close or have reduced hours in January and February. Buy Silver Dollar City and Showboat Branson Belle tickets in advance, especially in summer and during the busy holiday season.

1

Spend the morning and early afternoon at Silver Dollar City, a 61-acre, 1880s-themed amusement park on the Indian Point peninsula of Table Rock Lake, about 9 miles west of Branson. Opened in 1960, the park blends roller coasters and water rides with an Ozark village where craftspeople demonstrate glassblowing, candle making, blacksmithing, and candy making at shops such as Brown's Candy Factory. Wander through districts like Main Street, Homestead Ridge, and Rivertown, catch a bluegrass or gospel performance, and enjoy hearty Ozark food for lunch. Single-day admission starts at around $95 for adults.

2

Before leaving Silver Dollar City, take the guided tour of Marvel Cave, included with park admission. The cave opened for tours in the late 1800s, decades before the park was built around its entrance, and it helped draw the first tourists to the Branson area along with Harold Bell Wright's 1907 novel The Shepherd of the Hills. The tour descends into the Cathedral Room, one of the largest cave entrance rooms in the country, and returns to the surface on a cable train. Expect many stairs and cool temperatures underground.

3

In the late afternoon, drive to Table Rock State Park on the shores of Table Rock Lake, one of the best lakes in the Ozarks for boating and fishing. The lake was created by Table Rock Dam, built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers from 1954 to 1958 on the White River. Stop at the Dewey Short Visitor Center near the dam for exhibits on the lake, then stroll the shoreline or visit the park's full-service marina, where boat, kayak, and paddleboard rentals are available. The park grounds are free and open from 6am to 10pm.

4

End the day aboard the Showboat Branson Belle, a paddlewheel-style vessel styled after nineteenth-century riverboats that cruises Table Rock Lake from its dock on Highway 165, just a few minutes from the state park. Enjoy a multi-course meal and a live show with music, comedy, and variety acts as the boat glides past forested coves and rolling Ozark hills. Evening cruises are especially popular as the sunset light reflects across the lake. Adult tickets typically start around $50 to $80.

Day 2

The second day explores the heart of Branson, from the historic downtown and Lake Taneycomo waterfront to the theaters and attractions along Highway 76, also called the Strip. Traffic on the Strip can be heavy in the evenings, so allow extra time to reach your show. Reserve show tickets and train seats in advance, particularly during the summer and the holiday season, when the Ozark Mountain Christmas lights and special shows draw large crowds.

1

Begin your morning at the Branson Scenic Railway, which departs from Branson's historic 1905 depot on Main Street downtown. Vintage dome cars and classic passenger coaches carry visitors on a narrated round-trip excursion through the rugged hills and wooded valleys of the Ozarks, crossing steel trestles and passing through hand-cut tunnels, limestone bluffs, and remote hollows that cannot be reached by car. Adult coach tickets typically start around $36, and dome seating is available for an additional charge.

2

Next, head to the Highway 76 Strip to visit the Titanic Museum Attraction, a half-scale replica of the famous ship. Upon entering, each visitor receives a boarding pass with the name of a real passenger or crew member, whose fate is revealed at the end of the tour. Galleries display artifacts from the ship, a replica of the grand staircase, and hands-on exhibits such as touching an iceberg and feeling the icy temperature of the water on the night the ship sank.

3

In the afternoon, return downtown to Branson Landing, an open-air shopping and dining district along the shore of Lake Taneycomo. Stroll the waterfront boardwalk lined with shops, local boutiques, and restaurants, and watch the synchronized fire-and-water fountain show, which combines music, lights, flames, and water displays throughout the day and evening. Lake Taneycomo is famous for its trout fishing, fed by the cold water released from Table Rock Dam. Admission and parking are free.

4

End your trip the way Branson is best known, with a live show at one of the city's many theaters. Choose from Sight & Sound Theatre's large-scale Biblical productions, with massive sets and live animals, or The Haygoods, a high-energy show performed by six siblings that combines live music, harmonies, lasers, and aerial stunts with more than 20 instruments. Other favorites include Presleys' Country Jubilee, a Branson tradition, and Pierce Arrow. Many shows run about two hours, making it easy to enjoy dinner on the Strip beforehand.
