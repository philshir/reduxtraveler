Gateway Arch National Park: The Gateway to the West on the Mississippi Riverfront

1 Day

Rising 630 feet above the west bank of the Mississippi River, the gleaming stainless-steel Gateway Arch is the tallest arch in the world and the tallest human-made monument in the United States. Designed by architect Eero Saarinen and completed in 1965, it marks the site where St. Louis was founded and honors the nation's westward expansion, the Louisiana Purchase, and the pivotal Dred Scott case. At just 91 acres, Gateway Arch National Park is the smallest national park in the country, and its compact grounds make it easy to explore in a single day on foot. This one-day itinerary combines the park's three main areas, the Old Courthouse, the Arch and its underground museum, and the Mississippi riverfront, and ends with a sunset walk across the historic Eads Bridge just beside the park. Nearby St. Louis attractions such as City Museum, Forest Park, and the Saint Louis Zoo are left out so you can enjoy the park at a relaxed pace.

Day 1

Start in the morning at the Old Courthouse on the western edge of the park, then cross the landscaped grounds to the Arch and spend the afternoon along the riverfront. Everything is within a short walk, and the paved paths are flat and accessible for visitors of all ages. Reserve tram tickets online in advance, especially in summer, when the most popular times sell out. Spring and fall bring the most comfortable weather, while summer visits are best planned for early or late in the day to avoid the heat.

1

Begin your day at the Old Courthouse, whose cast-iron dome has crowned downtown St. Louis since the mid-1800s and once made it the tallest building in Missouri. In 1846, Dred Scott and his wife Harriet sued for their freedom here, and their trials in this building led to the landmark 1857 U.S. Supreme Court decision that helped push the nation toward civil war. Walk through the restored courtrooms and galleries on westward expansion and the Dred Scott case, and join a ranger talk if one is scheduled. Admission is free, and the view east from the courthouse steps frames the Arch perfectly at the end of the park's green lawn.

2

Cross the grounds to the Arch and head underground to the Museum at the Gateway Arch, which opened in 2018 beneath the monument. Its galleries trace more than 200 years of history, from the Native peoples of the region and colonial St. Louis to Lewis and Clark, the Louisiana Purchase, and the pioneers who headed west. Exhibits also explain how the Arch itself was designed and built, and a documentary film in the theater captures the daring construction of the monument. Plan to arrive a little before your tram time so you can explore the museum before or after the ride.

3

Board the tram for the highlight of any visit, a ride to the observation deck at the top of the Arch. Small, futuristic capsules travel inside the legs of the monument and take about four minutes to reach the top, where narrow windows offer views of up to 30 miles on a clear day. Look east across the Mississippi River into Illinois and west over downtown St. Louis, Busch Stadium, and the Old Courthouse you visited that morning. The cozy capsules are not for the claustrophobic, but the views from 630 feet up are unforgettable.

4

Spend the afternoon strolling the landscaped Arch grounds, where tree-lined paths, ponds, and gardens designed by landscape architect Dan Kiley offer changing views of the Arch against the skyline. A loop around the grounds is about 1 to 1.5 miles. Then head down to the levee along the Mississippi River to watch barges drift past and see the Arch rising directly overhead. From spring through fall, consider a riverboat cruise from the levee for a different perspective of the Arch and the city from the water.

5

End the day with a walk onto the Eads Bridge, which crosses the Mississippi just north of the park. Completed in 1874, it was the first bridge to span the river at St. Louis and the first major bridge in the world built primarily of steel. Its pedestrian walkway offers one of the best views in the city, with the Gateway Arch and downtown skyline glowing in the evening light. Watching the sun set behind the Arch from the bridge is a memorable finale to a day at the Gateway to the West.
