Las Vegas & Utah's Mighty 5: Neon Nights and Red Rock Wonders

7 Days

This 7-day odyssey pairs the electric spectacle of the Las Vegas Strip with the silent grandeur of Utah's five national parks. The journey begins amid dazzling lights, faux-Roman columns, and dancing fountains, then shifts gears entirely as the road climbs out of the desert basin and into a landscape carved by wind and water over 200 million years. Along the way you'll stand beneath the delicate span of a 65-foot sandstone arch, watch the sun set fire to a canyon of hoodoos, and trace canyon rims where the earth simply drops away. Because five national parks and a world capital of entertainment cannot both be seen in full within a week, this itinerary has been distilled to the most iconic stops in each location — the sights that define why travelers cross oceans and deserts to see them. Fly into Harry Reid International Airport in Las Vegas, pick up a rental car for the week, and prepare for a trip that swings from velvet ropes to slickrock canyons and back again.

Day 1

Your adventure begins in the heart of the Las Vegas Strip, where elegance, spectacle, and world-renowned landmarks set the tone for the week ahead. Today is all about classic Vegas glamour — strolling between legendary resorts, admiring artistic interiors, indulging in luxury shopping, and witnessing iconic attractions that define the city's identity. From the neon glow of the world-famous welcome sign to floral installations, dancing fountains, Roman grandeur, and Parisian charm, this first day immerses you fully in the magic of Las Vegas while easing you comfortably into the rhythm of the trip. The evening culminates with an unforgettable dining experience at one of the city's most celebrated restaurants.

1

No trip to Las Vegas is complete without a photo beside this mid-century neon icon, standing at the south end of the Strip since 1959. Its retro starburst design and cheerful red script have become shorthand for the city itself, and an early-morning stop here — before the tour buses arrive — offers a quiet moment with one of the most photographed signs in America.

2

Clustered around a single intersection, this quartet of themed resorts showcases the imaginative excess that built modern Las Vegas. The Luxor's black glass pyramid and towering light beam sit across from Excalibur's turrets and Mandalay Bay's tropical façade, while New York-New York recreates a miniature Manhattan skyline complete with a roller coaster weaving through its towers. MGM Grand, with its bronze lion statue and sprawling casino floor, anchors the corner. Walking this stretch in under an hour offers a crash course in the architectural theatrics that make the Strip unlike anywhere else on earth.

3

The Bellagio is one of Las Vegas's most iconic resorts, inspired by the elegance of an Italian lakeside village. Inside, the Bellagio Conservatory features massive seasonal displays made entirely from fresh flowers and plants, changing several times a year. Outside, the Fountains of Bellagio present choreographed water performances set to music and lights on the resort's eight-acre lake, creating one of the most photographed attractions on the Strip. Time your visit near the top of the hour for a full show.

4

Caesars Palace brings ancient Rome to life through grand columns, statues, and opulent interiors. The resort is home to the Forum Shops, an upscale shopping mall designed like a Roman marketplace with painted sky ceilings that shift from dawn to dusk. Walking through Caesars offers an immersive experience that blends luxury retail, history-inspired décor, and classic Vegas glamour.

5

The Cosmopolitan stands out for its modern, artistic design and vibrant atmosphere. Known for contemporary artwork, stylish lounges, and terraces overlooking the Strip, this resort appeals to travelers seeking a chic, urban vibe. Its open-air balconies and prime views of the Bellagio Fountains make it a favorite stop for photos and people-watching.

6

Paris Las Vegas recreates the charm of the French capital with cobblestone walkways, Parisian-style cafés, and a half-scale Eiffel Tower. Visitors can stroll beneath replicas of famous landmarks or ride to the top of the tower for panoramic views of the Strip. The resort's romantic design and lively streetscape make it one of the most recognizable themed hotels in Las Vegas.

7

Just across the street, Planet Hollywood offers a more contemporary counterpoint to its neighbors, with a Hollywood-glam interior and direct access to the Miracle Mile Shops. It's a convenient spot to pause, grab a drink, and regroup before the evening's dining reservation.

8

Las Vegas is a world-class dining destination, and this day closes with a visit to one of its highly acclaimed restaurants. Options range from Michelin-recommended fine dining to celebrity-chef steakhouses and scenic dining rooms overlooking the Strip. These restaurants elevate the Vegas experience by combining exceptional cuisine with elegant, unforgettable settings.

Day 2

Today you'll explore the bold, high-energy attractions of the North Strip and historic Downtown, where cutting-edge technology, vintage neon, and old-school casino grit tell the fuller story of Las Vegas's evolution. From a colossal illuminated sphere and a needle-thin tower with cliff-edge thrill rides to the birthplace of the city on Fremont Street, the day offers a dynamic mix of past and future. As daylight fades, the focus shifts to one of the city's signature experiences — a jaw-dropping live show that blends artistry, athleticism, and spectacle in classic Vegas style.

1

The Sphere is one of Las Vegas's newest and most technologically advanced landmarks. Its massive LED exterior displays dynamic visuals visible from miles away, while the interior features immersive audio and visual technology unlike any venue on earth. The Sphere represents the future of live entertainment and has quickly become one of the city's most talked-about attractions.

2

The Venetian re-creates the canals of Venice indoors, complete with singing gondoliers and painted ceilings, and shares its promenade with Madame Tussauds Las Vegas for those who want a photo beside a wax celebrity double. A short walk north, Wynn Las Vegas offers a quieter, garden-filled contrast — manicured topiary, a private lake, and some of the most refined interiors on the Strip.

3

Recognizable by its enormous silver "cloud" canopy, Fashion Show is the Strip's largest shopping mall, home to flagship department stores and a runway that occasionally rises from the floor for live fashion shows. It's a good midday stop for air conditioning, retail therapy, or simply people-watching under the shade of its steel disc.

4

Rising high above the Strip, the STRAT Tower offers sweeping views of Las Vegas from its observation deck. For thrill-seekers, the tower's rides extend riders out over the edge of the deck, creating a heart-stopping experience unlike anywhere else. It's one of the best places to visually grasp the sheer scale of the city and the empty desert that surrounds it.

5

Circus Circus is a classic Las Vegas resort known for its carnival-style atmosphere. Inside, guests can find midway games, indoor amusement rides, and live circus performances under its pink-and-white big top. The resort offers a nostalgic glimpse into old-school Vegas and remains popular with families and travelers seeking a more playful casino experience.

6

Resorts World is a modern mega-resort offering sleek design, high-end shopping, and a diverse collection of global dining options. Its contemporary architecture contrasts sharply with older themed casinos, showcasing the evolution of Las Vegas into a modern luxury destination.

7

Standing over 500 feet tall, the High Roller is one of the world's tallest observation wheels. The slow, thirty-minute rotation allows riders to enjoy unobstructed views of the Strip and the surrounding desert. Evening rides are especially popular when the city lights illuminate the skyline below.

8

A short drive north, Downtown Las Vegas reveals the city's grittier, historic roots. The Fremont Street Experience is a pedestrian corridor capped by a canopy of LED screens, framed by vintage casinos like the Plaza Hotel and Casino. Thrill-seekers can zip beneath that canopy on the Slotzilla Zipline, while the nearby Neon Museum displays retired signage in an open-air "boneyard," and the Mob Museum, housed in a former courthouse, traces the city's organized-crime history through original artifacts and courtroom exhibits.

9

Las Vegas is renowned for its live entertainment, and this itinerary includes at least one top-tier show. Cirque du Soleil productions such as O, KA, Mystère, and Michael Jackson ONE combine acrobatics, music, and storytelling. Alternatives like Blue Man Group or Absinthe offer equally memorable theatrical experiences to close out your final Vegas night before heading into the desert.

Day 3

Trade the neon for sandstone this morning as you drive roughly three hours northeast from Las Vegas into southwestern Utah. The transformation is dramatic: strip malls and casinos give way to crimson cliffs and a canyon carved so deep the Southern Paiute called it "Mukuntuweap" — straight canyon. Zion National Park, Utah's oldest and most visited, will be your home for the next day and a half. Spend the afternoon and evening easing into the park's rhythm along the canyon floor before tackling its more strenuous trails tomorrow.

1

Zion National Park's centerpiece is Zion Canyon itself, a half-mile-deep gorge sliced through Navajo sandstone by the Virgin River over millions of years. The shuttle-only Zion Canyon Scenic Drive winds between towering rock walls streaked with desert varnish, offering your first proper introduction to a landscape that feels genuinely otherworldly after two days on the Strip.

2

This paved, easy path follows the Virgin River to the entrance of the Zion Narrows, where the canyon walls close in to less than 30 feet apart in places. Even without wading into the famous slot canyon itself, the Riverside Walk offers a shaded, family-friendly glimpse of the geology that makes the Narrows one of the most sought-after hikes in the American Southwest.

3

A moderate loop leads past a series of pools and seasonal waterfalls tucked beneath sandstone overhangs, connecting to the shaded Grotto Trail along the canyon floor. It's a manageable afternoon hike that rewards with hanging gardens of ferns and columbine clinging to damp canyon walls.

4

Close out the day on this moderate 3.3-mile round-trip trail near the South Entrance, which climbs to a bench overlooking the Towers of the Virgin and the town of Springdale below. As the sun drops, the sandstone cliffs blaze orange and red — one of the most rewarding sunset spots in the park.

Day 4

A full day dedicated to Zion's signature trails, tackling the park's most celebrated — and most demanding — landscapes before the road climbs east toward Bryce Canyon. Whether you choose the chain-assisted scramble of Angels Landing or a gentler alternative, the morning is built around Zion's vertical drama. By afternoon you'll trade Zion's narrow red canyon for Bryce's wide amphitheater of stone spires, arriving in time to watch the hoodoos catch their first golden light.

1

Angels Landing is Zion's most famous — and most feared — trail, climbing switchbacks known as Walter's Wiggles before a final half-mile along a knife-edge ridge secured by chains, ending on a summit with sheer 1,000-foot drops on either side. A permit is required for the final section. Travelers seeking a similar thrill with less exposure often choose the Hidden Canyon Trail instead, which offers its own chain-assisted scramble into a secluded hanging canyon.

2

Just past the Zion-Mount Carmel Tunnel, this short but rewarding trail leads to a viewpoint overlooking lower Zion Canyon without the crowds of the valley floor. Continuing east on the scenic highway, Checkerboard Mesa's cross-hatched sandstone pattern — the result of both wind and water erosion over eons — makes for an easy roadside stop and one of Zion's most photographed geological curiosities.

3

The roughly 1.5 to 2-hour drive from Zion to Bryce climbs in elevation from red-rock desert to ponderosa pine forest, a reminder of how dramatically Utah's landscape shifts within short distances. Watch for mule deer and pronghorn along Highway 89 and the Bryce Canyon scenic byway.

4

Nothing prepares first-time visitors for the sight of the Bryce Amphitheater: thousands of orange, pink, and white hoodoos packed into a natural bowl below the rim. Sunset Point offers one of the most accessible and dramatic overlooks in the park, and as the sun sinks, the spires seem to glow from within.

Day 5

Rise early for Bryce Canyon's finest hours, when low light rakes across the hoodoos and turns the amphitheater into a glowing maze of shadow and stone. After descending among the spires on foot, the road turns east again toward Capitol Reef, Utah's most underrated park — a 100-mile wrinkle in the earth's crust where waterpockets, natural bridges, and a pioneer-era orchard settlement tell a quieter story than its more famous neighbors.

1

Often combined into a single figure-eight route, the Queens Garden Trail and Navajo Loop descend directly into the amphitheater, passing beneath towering hoodoos and through the famous switchbacks of Wall Street, where slot-canyon walls rise nearly 200 feet on either side. This is widely considered Bryce's best short hike, offering an up-close encounter with formations you can only appreciate from the rim otherwise.

2

For a quieter perspective, follow a stretch of the Rim Trail out to Inspiration Point, where three tiered overlooks reveal the amphitheater from increasingly dramatic angles. Fairyland Point, tucked north of the main entrance, sees a fraction of the crowds and offers a similarly striking view over its own field of hoodoos.

3

The roughly two-hour drive east on Scenic Byway 12 is itself one of Utah's great road trips, crossing the slickrock spine of Boulder Mountain with sweeping views over the Waterpocket Fold before descending into Capitol Reef's quieter, less-crowded terrain.

4

Settled by Mormon pioneers in the 1880s, the Fruita Historic District preserves orchards, a one-room schoolhouse, and homestead buildings tucked improbably into a canyon of red cliffs. The Capitol Reef Scenic Drive threads through this same corridor, and a short hike on the Hickman Bridge Trail leads to a 133-foot natural bridge spanning a dry wash — a perfect introduction to the park before dusk.

Day 6

A morning among Capitol Reef's waterpockets and petroglyphs gives way to an afternoon drive east into Utah's canyon country proper. By evening you'll arrive in Moab, gateway to both Arches and Canyonlands, and spend golden hour among some of the most famous sandstone arches on the planet.

1

Before paved roads existed, Capitol Gorge served as the only wagon route through this stretch of the Waterpocket Fold, and the easy walk to the Pioneer Register still reveals names and dates carved into the canyon walls by early settlers and travelers. A short detour to Panorama Point offers a wide-angle view over the folded reef, best appreciated in the soft light of early morning.

2

The two-and-a-half to three-hour drive from Capitol Reef to Moab follows Utah's high desert plateau before dropping into the Colorado River valley, with the La Sal Mountains rising in the distance. Note that Arches now requires timed-entry reservations during peak season, so plan your arrival window in advance.

3

Arches National Park packs more than 2,000 documented sandstone arches into a single compact landscape. The trail through Park Avenue winds between towering rock fins resembling a canyon of skyscrapers, while Balanced Rock — a boulder the size of three school buses perched atop a narrow pedestal — makes for one of the park's easiest and most striking roadside stops. Nearby, the Windows section clusters several massive arches, including Double Arch, within a short walk of one another.

4

No image represents Utah more than Delicate Arch, the freestanding 65-foot span featured on the state's license plates. The three-mile round-trip hike across open slickrock is moderately strenuous but delivers travelers to a natural amphitheater where the arch frames the La Sal Mountains beyond. Sunset, when the sandstone turns deep red-orange, is the classic time to arrive — just bring a headlamp for the walk back.

Day 7

Your final day begins at sunrise beneath one of the most photographed arches in the world before exploring the vast mesa-top vistas of Canyonlands. From here, the journey turns homeward: a long but scenic drive of roughly six and a half hours retraces the route back to Las Vegas, where you'll return the rental car and catch your flight home, carrying with you memories that span from casino floors to canyon rims.

1

A short, easy half-mile trail leads to this compact arch perched directly on the edge of a cliff, framing a thousand-foot drop into Buck Canyon beyond. At sunrise, light reflecting off the canyon walls below sets the underside of the arch aglow in deep orange — a moment photographers travel across the country to capture.

2

The Island in the Sky mesa sits more than 1,000 feet above the surrounding terrain, offering some of the most expansive views in the entire national park system. At Grand View Point, the Colorado and Green Rivers have carved a labyrinth of canyons, buttes, and mesas stretching to the horizon — a landscape so vast it's easy to understand why this region remained one of the last areas of the continental United States to be mapped.

3

This unusual crater-like formation has puzzled geologists for decades, with theories ranging from an ancient salt dome collapse to a meteor impact. A short walk to the overlook reveals its strange, colorful rings of exposed rock — a fittingly mysterious final stop before leaving the park.

4

The drive back to Las Vegas retraces I-70 and I-15 through the same red-rock corridor that opened the trip, offering one last look at the Utah landscape before the desert flattens out toward Nevada. Plan for roughly six and a half hours of driving, with a dinner stop in St. George, Utah, or Mesquite, Nevada, breaking up the journey. Return your rental car at Harry Reid International Airport in time for an evening flight, or spend one final night near the airport before an early morning departure — either way, closing the loop on a week that spanned neon towers and ancient stone.
