Mid-Atlantic travel itineraries blend iconic cities, revolutionary history, scenic countryside, and coastal destinations into diverse regional experiences. From world-famous landmarks in New York City and Washington, D.C., to Pennsylvania’s historic sites, New Jersey’s shorelines, Maryland’s Chesapeake Bay, and Delaware’s charming small towns, each journey reveals a unique chapter of the American story.

Overview

The Mid-Atlantic stretches from New York's Finger Lakes and Long Island south to Washington, D.C., packing much of early American history into a compact region. Independence Hall in Philadelphia, the National Mall in Washington, and Baltimore's Inner Harbor are all within a few hours of each other.

Major cities include New York City, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Baltimore, and Washington, D.C., where the Smithsonian museums are free to enter. Outside the cities, Lancaster County offers Pennsylvania Dutch farmland, the Finger Lakes have wineries and waterfalls, and the Hamptons offer seaside getaways.

The cities along the Northeast Corridor are easy to connect by train, so many trips work well without a car. Summer is busy at the shore, while spring and fall are pleasant seasons for city sightseeing.
