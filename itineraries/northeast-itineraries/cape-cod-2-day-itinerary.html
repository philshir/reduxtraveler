Cape Cod: Lighthouses, National Seashores, and the Edge of New England

2 Days

Curving gracefully into the Atlantic like an outstretched arm, Cape Cod has captivated travelers for centuries with its maritime heritage, windswept beaches, fishing villages, and iconic lighthouses. From the bustling harbor of Hyannis to the dramatic dunes of Provincetown, the Cape offers a remarkable blend of history, natural beauty, and coastal culture. Here, rolling sand dunes meet the sea, whale ships once departed for distant oceans, and beacons of light continue to guide mariners along one of America's most celebrated coastlines.

This 2-day itinerary follows Cape Cod from west to east and then north to Provincetown, grouping attractions geographically to reduce driving while maximizing opportunities to experience the Cape's most iconic landmarks. Inspired by the storytelling of a National Geographic expedition and the practical organization of a TripAdvisor itinerary, this journey explores historic towns, national seashores, legendary lighthouses, and some of the most scenic landscapes in New England.

Day 1

Today explores Upper and Mid-Cape Cod, where maritime history, charming villages, and scenic coastal overlooks introduce the region's rich heritage. From Sandwich, the oldest town on Cape Cod, to Hyannis and the Cape's eastern shoreline, you'll uncover the stories that shaped this iconic destination.

1

Begin your Cape Cod adventure in Sandwich, the oldest town on the Cape. Founded in the seventeenth century, Sandwich retains much of its historic charm through picturesque streets, colonial architecture, and centuries-old community landmarks that reflect New England's early settlement history.

2

Continue to the Sandwich Glass Museum, where exhibits celebrate the town's important role in America's glassmaking industry. Beautiful glass creations, demonstrations, and historic displays tell the story of an industry that helped shape Sandwich during the nineteenth century.

3

Drive east to Hyannis, the commercial heart of Cape Cod and longtime summer destination of the Kennedy family. The John F. Kennedy Hyannis Museum explores the relationship between Cape Cod and America's thirty-fifth president through photographs, exhibits, and personal stories that capture an important period of American history.

4

One of Cape Cod's most beautiful lighthouses, Nobska Light overlooks Vineyard Sound and the passage between Cape Cod and Martha's Vineyard. The elegant beacon serves as a perfect introduction to the Cape's long maritime tradition.

5

Continue along Nantucket Sound to West Dennis Light, a classic New England lighthouse overlooking beautiful beaches and calm coastal waters. The setting captures the quintessential atmosphere of Cape Cod's southern shore.

6

End the day at Scargo Tower in Dennis, where panoramic views stretch across Cape Cod Bay, inland villages, and the surrounding coastline. From this elevated vantage point, the geography of the Cape becomes fully visible, providing a memorable perspective on the region's unique landscape.

Day 2

Today explores the Outer Cape, where Cape Cod National Seashore preserves some of the most spectacular coastal scenery in the northeastern United States. Towering dunes, dramatic ocean beaches, historic lighthouses, and the vibrant community of Provincetown create a fitting finale to your Cape Cod adventure.

1

Begin the day exploring Cape Cod National Seashore, a protected landscape of beaches, dunes, salt marshes, ponds, and maritime forests stretching along the Outer Cape. Established to preserve the Cape's extraordinary natural beauty, the seashore remains one of the region's greatest treasures.

2

One of Cape Cod's most photographed landmarks, Nauset Light stands dramatically above the Atlantic shoreline. The lighthouse's red-and-white tower has become a symbol of the Cape's rugged eastern coast.

3

Marconi Beach combines dramatic ocean views with historical importance. Named for wireless communication pioneer Guglielmo Marconi, the beach features towering cliffs and sweeping Atlantic vistas that rank among the most impressive on the Cape.

4

Continue north to Highland Light, Cape Cod's oldest lighthouse. Perched atop coastal bluffs, the beacon has guided vessels through these waters for generations while offering spectacular ocean views.

5

Arrive in Provincetown, the vibrant community located at the very tip of Cape Cod. Historically significant as one of the first landing areas visited by the Pilgrims, Provincetown is today known for its lively atmosphere, art galleries, waterfront scenery, and rich maritime heritage. Explore the Town Hall Square Historic District, where historic architecture reflects centuries of coastal history.

6

Rising prominently above Provincetown, the Pilgrim Monument commemorates the Pilgrims' first landfall in the New World. Climbing the monument rewards visitors with some of the finest panoramic views anywhere in New England.

7

Venture into the dunes to visit Race Point Light, where powerful ocean currents and dramatic coastal scenery define one of Cape Cod's wildest landscapes. The remote setting offers a profound sense of isolation and natural beauty.

8

Hidden among the dunes are the historic Dune Shacks of Peaked Hill Bars, simple structures long associated with artists, writers, and naturalists seeking inspiration in Cape Cod's wilderness. The surrounding dunes create a landscape unlike anywhere else on the East Coast.

9

Conclude your Cape Cod journey with views of Wood End Light and Long Point Light, two remote beacons guarding the entrance to Provincetown Harbor. Surrounded by ocean, sand, and sky, these lighthouses provide a fitting finale to an adventure through one of America's most iconic coastal regions.
