New Hampshire's White Mountains: Franconia Notch, Mount Washington, and the Kancamagus Highway

4 Days

This 4-day adventure explores the heart of New Hampshire's White Mountains, where dramatic mountain passes, historic covered bridges, cascading waterfalls, alpine summits, and some of New England's most scenic roads create an unforgettable journey. From the granite cliffs of Franconia Notch to the summit of Mount Washington and the winding beauty of the Kancamagus Highway, this itinerary combines iconic natural landmarks with rewarding hikes and historic sites. Organized geographically to minimize driving and maximize exploration, the route showcases the very best of central New Hampshire.

Day 1

Your White Mountains journey begins in Franconia Notch State Park, one of New Hampshire's most spectacular mountain corridors. Surrounded by towering peaks, deep forests, rushing streams, and glacially carved landscapes, today introduces the natural beauty that has made the White Mountains famous throughout New England.

1

Begin your adventure exploring the scenic mountain pass that forms the heart of the White Mountains. Steep granite walls, dense forests, and breathtaking vistas make Franconia Notch one of the most beautiful natural areas in New Hampshire.

2

Formed by thousands of years of swirling water, The Basin is a remarkable granite pothole carved by glacial runoff. The crystal-clear water and scenic woodland setting make it one of the park's most popular attractions.

3

Walk along elevated boardwalks through a narrow granite chasm where waterfalls and streams have carved a dramatic passage through the mountains. The Flume is among New Hampshire's most iconic natural attractions.

4

Set beneath towering cliffs, Echo Lake offers stunning reflections of the surrounding mountains. The peaceful shoreline provides excellent opportunities for photography and relaxation.

5

Conclude the day by ascending high above Franconia Notch aboard the aerial tram. From the summit, panoramic views stretch across mountain ranges, forests, and valleys that define the White Mountains region.

Day 2

Today follows the legendary Kancamagus Highway, a scenic road widely regarded as one of New England's greatest drives. Waterfalls, mountain vistas, covered bridges, and forested valleys create a journey through the heart of the White Mountain National Forest.

1

Begin the day driving one of America's most celebrated scenic byways. Forested mountains, winding rivers, and countless overlooks reveal the rugged beauty of the White Mountains.

2

Stop at one of New Hampshire's classic covered bridges, where traditional craftsmanship and picturesque surroundings capture the timeless character of rural New England.

3

One of the highway's most popular stops, Lower Falls features smooth granite ledges and cascading water surrounded by beautiful mountain scenery.

4

Here the Swift River forces its way through a narrow granite gorge, creating dramatic rapids and scenic viewpoints that showcase the power of mountain waters.

5

A short woodland walk leads to one of the most photogenic waterfalls in the White Mountains. The multi-tiered cascade tumbles through a narrow gorge sculpted by centuries of flowing water.

Day 3

Today's adventure explores Crawford Notch and the Presidential Range, where waterfalls, mountain railways, and New England's highest peak create some of the most memorable experiences in the Northeast.

1

Begin the morning at this beautiful series of cascades and natural pools. The smooth granite formations and shaded forest surroundings make it a favorite destination for visitors to the White Mountains.

2

Hike to one of New Hampshire's most rewarding overlooks. The summit provides a breathtaking view directly into Crawford Notch, framed by towering cliffs and densely forested mountains.

3

Ride the historic mountain railway to the summit of Mount Washington. Operating since 1869, the railway climbs through multiple climate zones on its journey to the highest peak in the Northeast.

4

Standing at the summit of New England's highest mountain is one of the defining experiences of the White Mountains. Panoramic views extend across multiple states and Canada on exceptionally clear days, while the summit environment is famous for its extreme weather.

Day 4

Your final day focuses on the region's most iconic hikes and mountain landscapes. Dramatic ridgelines, alpine terrain, and sweeping vistas provide a fitting conclusion to your White Mountains adventure.

1

Start the day with a relatively short hike to one of New Hampshire's most famous viewpoints. The cliff-top overlook delivers outstanding views of Echo Lake and the surrounding peaks.

2

Explore a fascinating series of boardwalks, caves, rocky passageways, and waterfalls hidden within a rugged mountain gorge formed by glacial forces.

3

Spend the day tackling one of the most celebrated hikes in the Northeast. The Franconia Ridge Trail follows a dramatic alpine ridgeline with continuous views in every direction, while Mount Lafayette stands among New Hampshire's most beloved summits.

4

If extending your journey southward or returning toward southern New Hampshire, conclude with a visit to Mount Monadnock. One of the most climbed mountains in the world, its isolated summit offers sweeping views and serves as a fitting finale to four days immersed in New Hampshire's mountain landscapes.
