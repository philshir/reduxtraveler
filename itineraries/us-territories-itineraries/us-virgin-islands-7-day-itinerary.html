U.S. Virgin Islands 7 Day Itinerary

Magens Bay, St. Thomas, USVI

U.S. Virgin Islands: Island Hopping Across St. Thomas, St. John, and St. Croix

7 Days

The U.S. Virgin Islands are three main islands, each with its own character. St. Thomas has the harbor town of Charlotte Amalie and the famous Magens Bay, St. John is mostly national park with hiking trails and snorkeling reefs, and St. Croix, the largest island, has Danish colonial towns, sugar estates, and Buck Island Reef National Monument. This seven-day itinerary spends two days on St. Thomas, two on St. John, and three on St. Croix. Ferries connect St. Thomas and St. John in about 15 to 20 minutes from Red Hook, while St. Croix, about 40 miles to the south, is reached by a short flight or seaplane from St. Thomas. Driving is on the left throughout the territory. Water Island, Hassel Island, and the British Virgin Islands are left out to keep the pace relaxed.

Day 1

Arrive on St. Thomas and spend the first day in Charlotte Amalie, the capital. The town is compact and easy to explore, and the hillsides above it provide wide views of the harbor.

1

Begin along the Charlotte Amalie waterfront, where Danish colonial warehouses have become shops, cafés, and galleries. Walk past Fort Christian, the island's oldest standing structure, and explore the lanes lined with jewelry, local crafts, and spices.

2

Climb to Blackbeard's Castle, a historic stone watchtower surrounded by tropical gardens, for panoramic views of the harbor and the town's red rooftops. It is a cool, shaded stop in the middle of the day.

3

In the late afternoon, ride the Skyride to Paradise Point, a gondola that carries you up to a hilltop lookout high above the harbor. The views over the cruise ships, the bay, and the surrounding islands are especially good as the sun drops.

Day 2

Today follows the north shore of St. Thomas, with the island's best views and beaches. The roads are winding, so allow plenty of driving time.

1

Start at Mountain Top, a hilltop stop with sweeping views and a popular open-air bar known for frozen tropical drinks. Continue to Drake's Seat, a scenic pull-off overlooking the curve of Magens Bay, where legend says Sir Francis Drake once watched for ships.

2

Descend to Magens Bay, a nearly mile-long crescent of white sand and calm turquoise water that is regularly ranked among the world's most beautiful beaches. Swim, snorkel, or rent a kayak, and stay through the afternoon.

3

On the way back, stop at Coki Point Beach, a small, sheltered cove with good snorkeling, and Coral World Ocean Park next door, which has an underwater observatory and encounters with sea turtles and stingrays. Head to Red Hook for the night so you are ready for the morning ferry.

Day 3

Take the early ferry from Red Hook to Cruz Bay on St. John and spend the day on the island's North Shore. Most of the island is Virgin Islands National Park, so pick up maps and conditions at the visitor center.

1

Arrive in Cruz Bay and stop at the Virgin Islands National Park Visitor Center for maps and ranger advice. Then follow the Lind Point Trail to Honeymoon Beach, a sheltered white-sand cove with calm water and a gentle reef, ideal for a first snorkel.

2

Rent a vehicle or catch a safari taxi along North Shore Road to Hawksnest Beach, a long, shaded beach with calm water and snorkeling over seagrass and coral close to shore.

3

End the day at Maho Bay, where shallow seagrass beds attract green sea turtles that graze within easy view of swimmers. The water is calm and welcoming, and the late afternoon light over the hills is beautiful.

Day 4

Spend the second day on St. John exploring the island's east end, where Danish plantation history and some of the best snorkeling in the territory combine. Return to St. Thomas by ferry in the late afternoon.

1

Begin at Annaberg Sugar Plantation, one of the best-preserved sugar plantation ruins in the Caribbean, with a windmill tower, boiling house, and stone ruins looking out over Leinster Bay.

2

Follow the easy Leinster Bay Trail to Waterlemon Cay, a renowned snorkeling spot with a healthy reef, sea turtles, and starfish in the shallows.

3

If you have energy left, drive to Salt Pond Bay on the southeast tip of the island and walk the Ram Head Trail to a dramatic windswept headland. Then return to Cruz Bay in time for the ferry to St. Thomas, and fly or take the seaplane to St. Croix the next morning.

Day 5

Fly or take the seaplane from St. Thomas to St. Croix and spend the day in Christiansted, the island's historic harbor town on the north shore. Rent a car for the rest of your stay.

1

Tour Christiansted National Historic Site, a cluster of Danish colonial buildings along the harbor, including Fort Christiansvaern, the yellow fort completed in the 1700s.

2

Walk the boardwalk along the harbor, past sailboats, seaplanes, and historic buildings, and stop for lunch in one of the waterfront restaurants.

3

Drive to the island's east end to Point Udall, the easternmost point of the United States, for sweeping views from the cliffs. If you wake early the next day, this is the first place in the country to see the sunrise.

Day 6

Today combines a boat trip to Buck Island with the beaches and history of the north shore.

1

Take a half-day boat trip from Christiansted to Buck Island Reef National Monument, where an underwater trail winds through elkhorn coral and schools of tropical fish and the island's white-sand beach is one of the best in the Caribbean.

2

Visit Salt River Bay, a protected harbor of mangroves and coral reefs that is the only known site in U.S. territory where Columbus's expedition landed, in 1493.

3

Spend the late afternoon at Cane Bay Beach, a palm-lined stretch of sand famous for the dramatic wall just offshore, which is popular with snorkelers and divers. Consider a guided night kayak trip on the bioluminescent bay afterward.

Day 7

The last day explores the west end around Frederiksted, with sugar estates, rum, and a famous pier. Head back toward the airport by late afternoon if you have an evening flight.

1

Begin at Estate Whim, a beautifully preserved 18th-century sugar plantation with a windmill tower and great house that explain the island's agricultural past.

2

Take a tour of the Cruzan Rum Distillery, where rum has been made from local sugar cane and molasses for generations, and finish with a tasting.

3

End in Frederiksted at Fort Frederik, the red Danish fort where emancipation was proclaimed in 1848, and walk out along Frederiksted Pier, a landmark shore-diving spot and one of the best places on the island to watch the sunset.
