St. Thomas 3 Day Itinerary

  1. Itineraries
  2. St. Thomas 3 Day Itinerary

Charlotte Amalie, St. Thomas, USVI

St. Thomas: Harbor Views, Famous Beaches, and Danish Colonial History in the U.S. Virgin Islands

3 Days

St. Thomas is the most visited of the U.S. Virgin Islands and the main gateway to the territory, with a busy cruise port, an international airport, and ferries to St. John and the British Virgin Islands. Green hills rise steeply above Charlotte Amalie's historic harbor, and the island's beaches range from the broad, calm sweep of Magens Bay to snorkeling coves on the east end. This three-day itinerary begins in historic Charlotte Amalie, spends the second day on the scenic north shore, and ends on the island's east end with a visit to Coral World and a sunset at Red Hook. Day trips to St. John and Water Island are left out to keep the schedule relaxed.

Day 1

The first day explores Charlotte Amalie, the capital, whose waterfront and hillsides are lined with pastel-colored Danish colonial buildings. Streets are narrow and the roads climb steeply, and driving is on the left, so a taxi or open-air safari bus is the easiest way to get around. Start early, since cruise-ship days can be crowded in the middle of the day.

1

Begin along the harbor in Charlotte Amalie, where old warehouses have become shops and cafés and the narrow lanes between them are lined with jewelry, spices, and local crafts. Stroll along the waterfront promenade and nearby Fort Christian, the red fort that is the island's oldest standing structure, and watch seaplanes and sailboats come and go across the bay. Many of the buildings date to the 1700s and 1800s, when the harbor was a busy Danish trading port.

2

Climb up the steps above town to Blackbeard's Castle, a historic stone watchtower built in the 1600s that is now a scenic attraction with tropical gardens and relaxed dining spaces. The tower's elevated position gives broad views over the harbor, the town's red rooftops, and the islands beyond. Nearby overlooks provide more panoramas, and the cool breezes and shaded gardens make this a pleasant midday stop.

3

After lunch, ride the Skyride to Paradise Point, a gondola that carries passengers up to a hilltop terrace about 700 feet above sea level. The ride offers sweeping views over Charlotte Amalie, the cruise ships, and the surrounding islands, and the lookout at the top is a good place for a cold drink and photographs. Late afternoon is a good time to visit, when the heat eases and the light softens.

4

End the day at Yacht Haven Grande, a waterfront complex of upscale shops, restaurants, and a promenade lined with superyachts. The harborfront dining is a relaxed place to watch the sunset over the bay and sample local seafood and Caribbean-inspired dishes before returning to your hotel.

Day 2

Today follows the island's north shore, with some of its best views and its most famous beach. The road climbs and descends in tight curves, so allow extra driving time and take the hairpin turns slowly. Bring a swimsuit, snorkel gear, and sunscreen.

1

Start the morning at Mountain Top, a hilltop stop at about 1,500 feet above sea level with sweeping views across the north shore and out toward the British Virgin Islands. It is known for its open-air bar and frozen tropical drinks, as well as duty-free shopping and local crafts. Come early to enjoy the views before the tour buses arrive.

2

A short drive away, Drake's Seat is a scenic pull-off with a stone bench that, according to local legend, is where Sir Francis Drake watched for enemy ships. The viewpoint looks straight down onto the curve of Magens Bay and across Pillsbury Sound to the neighboring islands, and it is one of the most photographed views in the U.S. Virgin Islands.

3

Descend to Magens Bay, a nearly mile-long crescent of white sand and calm turquoise water surrounded by green hills that is often ranked among the most beautiful beaches in the world. The shallow, protected water is ideal for swimming, and you can rent kayaks, paddleboards, and snorkel gear on the beach. A small fee is charged for entry, and the facilities include restrooms, showers, and a snack bar.

4

Finish the day at Hull Bay, a quieter, palm-fringed beach on the north coast known for its relaxed local atmosphere and stronger waves. In winter, swells make it popular with surfers, and fishing boats rest along the shore. Stay for a seaside dinner and watch the sun set over the water.

Day 3

The final day explores the east end of St. Thomas, where marine life and snorkeling beaches are the focus. This part of the island is quieter than Charlotte Amalie, and Red Hook, at its far end, is the departure point for ferries to St. John and the British Virgin Islands.

1

Begin at Coral World Ocean Park, where you can walk through an underwater observatory, meet sea turtles and stingrays, and watch tropical fish. Nearby Coki Point Beach is a small, sheltered cove with calm water and good snorkeling just offshore, and it is popular with families.

2

Continue to Lindquist Beach, one of the island's most scenic beaches, with soft white sand, a backdrop of green hills, and views across to St. Thomas's neighboring cays. The beach is part of Smith Bay Park, and the clear, gentle water is excellent for swimming and relaxing away from the busier beaches.

3

Head to Secret Harbor Beach on the southeast coast, a calm, crescent-shaped cove with clear turquoise water and reefs just offshore. It is one of the island's best easy snorkeling spots, with parrotfish, sea turtles, and rays often seen in the shallows, and a casual beach bar and snorkel rentals make it simple to spend the afternoon.

4

End the day in Red Hook, the harbor village at the island's east end that is the gateway to St. John, with its busy ferry dock, marina, and waterfront restaurants. For dinner, try Dinghy's Beach Bar and Grill, a casual, open-air waterfront spot serving Caribbean dishes, cocktails, and fresh local seafood, where you can watch the boats come and go as the sun sets.
