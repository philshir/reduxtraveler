San Juan 3 Day Itinerary

  1. Itineraries

Castillo San Felipe del Morro, Old San Juan, Puerto Rico

San Juan: Colonial Fortresses, Colorful Streets, and Caribbean Beaches in Puerto Rico's Capital

3 Days

San Juan is one of the oldest European-founded cities in the Americas, and it packs centuries of Spanish history, lively neighborhoods, and city beaches into a compact area. Old San Juan, the walled colonial district on a small island at the harbor entrance, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with blue cobblestone streets, pastel buildings, and two massive fortresses that now form San Juan National Historic Site. Beyond the walls, the Condado, Santurce, Ocean Park, and Isla Verde districts offer beaches, art, markets, and nightlife. This three-day itinerary spends the first day exploring Old San Juan on foot, the second day in Condado and Santurce, and the third day on the city's beaches before returning to the old city for sunset. Day trips to El Yunque rainforest and the bioluminescent bays are left out to keep the pace relaxed and are better treated as separate outings.

Day 1

Spend the first day in Old San Juan, where nearly everything is within walking distance. The streets are steep and paved with cobblestones, so wear comfortable shoes, and start early, since the fortresses are exposed to the sun and the cruise-ship crowds build by late morning. Free trolleys also circle the old city if you need a break from the hills.

1

Begin at El Morro, the massive six-level fortress that has guarded the harbor entrance since the 1500s. Walk through its ramps, tunnels, and sentry boxes, then climb to the upper ramparts for views across the bay and out to the open Atlantic. The sweeping lawn in front of the fort is a favorite place to fly kites, and Paseo del Morro, the walkway that follows the old city wall along the water below, offers a quieter way to take in the fortifications from the shoreline.

2

Walk east along Calle del Cristo, one of the old city's most picturesque streets, to the Cathedral Basilica of San Juan Bautista. The cathedral dates back to the 16th century, and it is the resting place of Juan Ponce de León, the island's first governor. Step inside to admire the vaulted ceilings and stained glass, then browse the small plazas, shops, and cafés around it before stopping for a midday meal.

3

Continue along Calle de la Fortaleza, the street lined with colorful colonial buildings that leads toward La Fortaleza, the governor's mansion and one of the oldest executive residences in continuous use in the Western Hemisphere. From here, walk down to the Puerta de San Juan, the red-painted gate that once welcomed ships' passengers into the walled city and is one of the few remaining gates of the old city. Pause at nearby Parque de las Palomas, a small plaza with views over the bay and a resident flock of pigeons.

4

End the day at Castillo San Cristóbal, the largest Spanish fortification in the Americas. Built to defend the city from attacks by land, the fort spreads across 27 acres of tunnels, dry moats, and gun batteries, and its upper levels give wide views over the city and the ocean. The late afternoon light on the stone walls is especially beautiful, and the fort is a good place to learn how Spanish engineers designed San Juan's defenses before heading to dinner in the old city.

Day 2

Today leaves the old city for the modern neighborhoods of San Juan. Condado and Santurce are a short ride from Old San Juan by car, rideshare, or bus, and together they show off the city's beaches, art, and food scene. Bring sunscreen and a swimsuit, and plan on a long lunch in the afternoon heat.

1

Start the morning in Condado, the upscale beachfront district known for its high-rise hotels, restaurants, and boutiques. Stroll along the sand at Condado Beach, where the Atlantic waves can be strong, then continue to Playita del Condado, a small, sheltered cove beside the old Dos Hermanos Bridge where the water is calmer and well suited to wading and paddleboarding.

2

Head into Santurce to the Museo de Arte de Puerto Rico, the island's largest art museum, housed in a restored 1920s hospital building with a modern wing and tropical sculpture gardens. Galleries trace Puerto Rican art from the colonial era to the present, with paintings, sculpture, and prints by island artists, and the shaded gardens are a cool place to rest between exhibits.

3

Have lunch around La Plaza del Mercado de Santurce, a historic market hall surrounded by food stalls, bars, and small restaurants. Inside, vendors sell fresh produce, spices, and tropical fruit, while the plaza outside is known for casual Puerto Rican dishes such as mofongo, empanadillas, and fresh seafood. The area becomes especially lively in the evening, when locals gather for drinks and music.

4

After the heat of the day, walk toward Calle Loíza, the neighborhood's artistic main street, where colorful murals cover the buildings and independent shops, cafés, and galleries line the sidewalks. Santurce is one of the best places in San Juan to see street art. Return to the plaza around La Placita de Santurce as night falls to enjoy live salsa and Latin music, one of the city's most popular evening scenes.

Day 3

Spend the final day on the beaches that line San Juan's coastline, from a protected snorkeling cove to wide stretches of sand near the airport. Public beaches are free, and chairs and umbrellas can be rented on most of them. Return to Old San Juan late in the day for a final sunset on the walls.

1

Begin the morning at Balneario del Escambrón, a public beach between Old San Juan and Condado with calm turquoise water protected by coral reefs. Shade trees line the sand, and the shallow, sheltered water is ideal for swimming and snorkeling, with a good chance of seeing tropical fish and sea turtles. Lifeguards are on duty during opening hours, and restrooms and showers are available.

2

Continue east to Ocean Park Beach, a wide, relaxed stretch of sand in a residential neighborhood. The steady breezes make it a favorite for kitesurfing and windsurfing, and the sand is lined with small guesthouses, beach bars, and casual restaurants. Stop here for a laid-back lunch and a swim, or watch the surfers and kitesurfers from the shore.

3

Farther east, Isla Verde Beach is one of the longest and broadest beaches in San Juan, with golden sand, palm trees, and a lively atmosphere in front of the resort hotels. The water is generally good for swimming and bodyboarding, and beachfront restaurants and water-sports rentals make it easy to spend the afternoon. It is also close to the airport, which makes it a convenient last stop for travelers with evening flights.

4

Return to Old San Juan in the late afternoon and wander Caleta de San Juan, a narrow, steep cobblestone passage with stone walls and shaded overlooks that open toward the harbor and the old fortifications. Finish at Parque de las Palomas, a small hillside plaza with sweeping views over the bay, where the pigeons gather as the sun sets behind the city walls. Then settle in for a final dinner in the old city.
