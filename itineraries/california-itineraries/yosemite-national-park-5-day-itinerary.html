Yosemite National Park: Granite Giants, Waterfalls, and the High Sierra

5 Days

Yosemite National Park is one of the world's most celebrated wilderness landscapes, a place where towering granite cliffs, ancient giant sequoias, alpine lakes, glacial valleys, and powerful waterfalls combine into a landscape unlike any other in North America. With its immense size and diverse ecosystems, Yosemite rewards visitors who explore beyond the famous valley floor. This five-day itinerary is organized geographically to minimize driving while maximizing time in the park's most spectacular regions: Yosemite Valley, Glacier Point Road, Mariposa Grove and Wawona, Tioga Road and Tuolumne Meadows, and the remote Hetch Hetchy wilderness. The journey balances iconic landmarks with lesser-known treasures, creating an experience that captures the full grandeur of Yosemite.

Day 1

Your first day explores Yosemite Valley, the heart of the national park and one of the most famous landscapes on Earth. Massive granite monoliths, roaring waterfalls, and glacier-carved cliffs surround the valley floor, creating the iconic scenery that has inspired artists, photographers, and adventurers for generations.

1

Begin at Tunnel View, Yosemite's most iconic overlook. From a single viewpoint, visitors can see El Capitan, Bridalveil Fall, Half Dome, and Yosemite Valley stretching into the distance. Few locations better introduce the scale and beauty of the park.

2

Continue into Yosemite Valley, where meadows, rivers, and soaring granite walls combine to create one of the world's most recognizable landscapes. Yosemite Village serves as the historic and cultural center of the park.

3

Visit Bridalveil Fall before experiencing the immense scale of El Capitan, the world's most famous granite monolith. Rising more than 3,000 feet above the valley floor, El Capitan is a legendary destination for climbers from around the globe.

4

Walk portions of the Valley Loop Trail before reaching Mirror Lake, where calm waters often reflect Half Dome and surrounding cliffs. The trail provides an excellent introduction to Yosemite's meadows and river ecosystems.

5

Explore views of Royal Arches before ending at Yosemite Falls, one of the tallest waterfalls in North America. The thunderous cascade serves as a fitting conclusion to your introduction to Yosemite Valley.

Day 2

Today ascends high above Yosemite Valley to Glacier Point and the extraordinary viewpoints along Glacier Point Road. Granite domes, dramatic overlooks, and some of the park's most iconic long-distance views make this one of the most rewarding days of the trip.

1

Begin at Glacier Point, where panoramic views encompass Half Dome, Yosemite Valley, Nevada Fall, Vernal Fall, and the High Sierra. This viewpoint has long been considered one of the most spectacular overlooks in the national park system.

2

Hike to Sentinel Dome for a 360-degree panorama of Yosemite before continuing to Taft Point, where dramatic cliffs drop nearly vertically to the valley floor thousands of feet below.

3

Follow portions of the famed Panorama Trail, considered one of Yosemite's most scenic routes. Continuous views of Half Dome, waterfalls, and granite cliffs provide a constantly changing perspective on the landscape.

4

Descend via the legendary Mist Trail, where stone staircases pass beside the powerful cascades of Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall. Water spray, granite walls, and rushing rivers combine to create one of Yosemite's classic hiking experiences.

5

Finish the day with viewpoints along the Four Mile Trail while enjoying unforgettable perspectives of Half Dome, perhaps Yosemite's most famous natural landmark and the symbol of the Sierra Nevada.

Day 3

Today explores southern Yosemite, where giant sequoias, mountain communities, and cascading waterfalls reveal a quieter side of the park. This region combines natural wonders with rich human history.

1

Begin in historic Wawona, one of Yosemite's oldest developed areas. Nestled among forests and rivers, it provides a glimpse into the park's early tourism and conservation history.

2

Explore Mariposa Grove, home to hundreds of giant sequoias including some of the oldest and largest trees on Earth.

3

Stand before the Grizzly Giant, one of Yosemite's most famous sequoias, before visiting the Fallen Monarch, a massive tree that has fascinated visitors for generations.

4

End the day hiking toward Chilnualna Falls, a beautiful waterfall cascading through polished granite terrain amid peaceful forest surroundings.

Day 4

Today's adventure follows Tioga Road through Yosemite's spectacular high country. Alpine lakes, granite domes, mountain passes, and expansive meadows showcase an entirely different side of the park.

1

Follow Tioga Road across the crest of the Sierra Nevada. Stop at Olmsted Point for dramatic views of Half Dome and Yosemite's glacially sculpted wilderness.

2

Visit two of Yosemite's most beautiful alpine lakes, where crystal-clear water reflects granite peaks and pine forests.

3

Explore Tuolumne Meadows, one of the largest subalpine meadows in the Sierra Nevada, before climbing Lembert Dome for expansive views across the high country.

4

These spectacular alpine lakes provide some of Yosemite's finest mountain scenery, surrounded by granite peaks and wildflower-filled meadows.

5

Experience two of Yosemite's most celebrated wilderness corridors, where rivers, granite peaks, and alpine meadows create classic High Sierra landscapes.

6

End the day among Yosemite's highest elevations. Mono Pass and Mount Dana provide extraordinary views extending across the Sierra Nevada and the Great Basin beyond.

Day 5

The final day ventures into Yosemite's remote northern wilderness at Hetch Hetchy, a dramatic glacial valley often compared to Yosemite Valley before the construction of O'Shaughnessy Dam. Rugged trails, waterfalls, and wilderness lakes provide a memorable conclusion to the journey.

1

Begin at Hetch Hetchy Reservoir and O'Shaughnessy Dam, where towering granite walls surround one of California's most important water systems.

2

Follow the lakeshore trail to Wapama Falls, one of the largest waterfalls in Yosemite's northern wilderness.

3

Explore some of Yosemite's most remote backcountry destinations. Alpine lakes, wilderness camps, rivers, and waterfalls provide a glimpse into the solitude that exists beyond the park's famous landmarks.

4

Conclude your Yosemite journey with views toward two of the park's most legendary summits: Clouds Rest and Half Dome. Rising above the surrounding wilderness, these granite peaks embody the grandeur, scale, and enduring beauty that make Yosemite National Park one of the world's great natural treasures.
