California travel itineraries reveal a state of extraordinary diversity, connecting Pacific coastlines, towering redwood forests, world-renowned cities, wine country, desert landscapes, and iconic national parks. From the cultural energy of Los Angeles and San Francisco to the natural wonders of Yosemite, Death Valley, and the Central Coast, these journeys showcase the countless experiences that make California one of America’s most celebrated travel destinations.

Overview

California offers more variety than almost any other state, from the redwood forests and rugged coastline of the north to the deserts of the southeast. Its nine national parks include Yosemite, Sequoia, Kings Canyon, Death Valley, and Joshua Tree, and its cities range from San Francisco and Los Angeles to San Diego.

Many itineraries combine nearby destinations: Yosemite with Sequoia and Kings Canyon in the Sierra Nevada, Highway 1 and Big Sur along the Pacific Coast, and Napa and Sonoma in Wine Country. Highway 395 follows the eastern side of the Sierra Nevada past Mono Lake and Mammoth Lakes.

Seasons matter. Summer is extremely hot in Death Valley and Joshua Tree, while high-elevation roads such as Tioga Pass in Yosemite close in winter. Some popular parks require reservations during peak season, so check the National Park Service website for current conditions and requirements before your visit.
