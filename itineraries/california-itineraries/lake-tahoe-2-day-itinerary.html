Lake Tahoe 2 Day Itinerary

  1. Itineraries

Lake Tahoe, CA/NV

Lake Tahoe: Emerald Bay, Sandy Beaches, and Alpine Views on the Jewel of the Sierra

2 Days

Straddling the California–Nevada line high in the Sierra Nevada, Lake Tahoe is the largest alpine lake in North America, about 22 miles long and 12 miles wide, and at 1,645 feet it is the second-deepest lake in the United States. Its crystal-clear, deep blue water, sandy beaches, and snow-capped peaks make it a year-round destination for swimming, paddling, hiking, and skiing. This two-day itinerary spends the first day exploring the state parks of the West Shore, from Emerald Bay to Sugar Pine Point, and the second day enjoying South Lake Tahoe, from a gondola ride above the lake to an afternoon on the beach and a drive along the East Shore. Strenuous hikes into the Desolation Wilderness to Mount Tallac and Lake Aloha, the ski resorts of the North Shore such as Palisades Tahoe, and the full drive around the lake are left out to keep the pace relaxed.

Day 1

The first day follows Highway 89 along the West Shore from South Lake Tahoe to Sugar Pine Point State Park. This itinerary is planned for summer, when Vikingsholm tours run and the beaches are warm, though the water stays cold year-round. Parking at Emerald Bay and the state parks fills early on summer days, so start early and bring cash or a card for state park day-use fees. Highway 89 at Emerald Bay can close in winter because of snow and avalanche danger.

1

Begin at Emerald Bay State Park, a National Natural Landmark and California's first underwater park, where views of the glacier-carved bay and tiny Fannette Island, the only island in Lake Tahoe, are among the most famous in the Sierra. After taking in the view from the overlook along Highway 89, hike the steep 1-mile trail down to Vikingsholm, a 38-room Scandinavian-style mansion built in 1929 on the shore of the bay. Tour the home in summer, then relax on the beach before the climb back up.

2

Across the highway from the Vikingsholm parking area, take a short hike up to Eagle Falls, where Eagle Creek tumbles over granite on its way down to Emerald Bay. The trail continues into the Desolation Wilderness, and the falls offer a sweeping view back over Emerald Bay and Lake Tahoe. In early summer, snowmelt makes the falls especially powerful.

3

Continue a short distance north to D. L. Bliss State Park, which was donated to the state in 1929 by the heirs of timber and railroad magnate Duane L. Bliss. Enjoy lunch and a swim at its sandy beaches, among the most beautiful on the lake, where the clear water glows in shades of turquoise. Walk the trail to Rubicon Point Light, the highest-elevation lighthouse in the United States, and see the giant balancing rock along a popular forest trail.

4

End the day at Ed Z'berg Sugar Pine Point State Park, which stretches almost two miles along the West Shore and includes 2,500 acres of forest and a sandy beach. Tour the grounds of the Ehrman Mansion, a Queen Anne–style summer home built in 1903 that now serves as the park's visitor center, and stroll along the lakeshore or the General Creek trail through tall pine forest as the evening light softens over the lake.

Day 2

The second day centers on South Lake Tahoe, the largest city on the lake's southern shore, and the East Shore just across the Nevada state line. The city is a lively mix of beaches, trailheads, restaurants, and casinos at Stateline. Gondola tickets can be purchased in advance online, and beaches can get crowded on summer weekends, so arrive early to find parking.

1

Start the morning with a ride on the gondola at Heavenly Mountain Resort, which straddles the California–Nevada line and rises to the highest elevation of any Tahoe ski area at 10,067 feet. The gondola departs from the village in the heart of South Lake Tahoe and climbs high above the city, with sweeping views of the lake below and the Desolation Wilderness and Carson Range beyond. Stop at the observation deck for one of the best views of Lake Tahoe, then continue to the top for summer activities and hiking trails.

2

In the late morning, head to Pope Beach, one of the most popular beaches on the South Shore, with a long stretch of sand backed by pine trees and views across the lake. Rent a kayak or stand-up paddleboard and glide over the crystal-clear water, where you can see the sandy bottom far below. After lunch, take a short side trip to nearby Fallen Leaf Lake, a peaceful alpine lake beneath Mount Tallac.

3

In the afternoon, drive north on U.S. 50 along the quieter East Shore in Nevada. Just past Zephyr Cove, the highway passes through the tunnel at Cave Rock, a 250-foot volcanic formation whose caves were carved by waves during the Ice Age. Sacred to the Washoe people, Cave Rock was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as a Traditional Cultural Property in 2017. Admire it from the nearby boat launch, and enjoy views across the lake to the granite peaks of the Sierra on the West Shore.

4

Return to South Lake Tahoe for dinner with a lake view, then stroll the lively area around Stateline, where lakeside casinos on the Nevada side meet the shops, restaurants, and entertainment of the Heavenly Village on the California side. Catch a concert at the Tahoe Blue Event Center or watch the sunset from the shore as the sky glows over the peaks of the Desolation Wilderness, a memorable finale to two days on the lake.
