Pacific Coast Highway & Big Sur: California's Most Scenic Coastal Journey

5 Days

Few road trips in the world rival the dramatic beauty of California's Pacific Coast Highway through Big Sur. Here, rugged mountains plunge directly into the Pacific Ocean, waterfalls tumble from forested cliffs, sea lions bask on remote beaches, and historic landmarks cling to windswept headlands overlooking endless ocean vistas.

This five-day itinerary follows a carefully structured journey through the most spectacular section of California's coastline, grouping destinations by proximity to maximize sightseeing while minimizing unnecessary driving. Inspired by the storytelling style of National Geographic and the practical pacing of a TripAdvisor itinerary, this route highlights iconic bridges, hidden beaches, historic lighthouses, state parks, wildlife viewing areas, and some of the finest coastal scenery in North America.

Day 1

Your adventure begins along the southern gateway to Big Sur. Today showcases rugged cliffs, secluded beaches, waterfalls, and some of the most scenic stretches of Highway 1. The dramatic meeting of mountains and ocean creates a spectacular introduction to California's legendary coastline.

1

Often referred to as the gateway to Big Sur, Ragged Point offers sweeping views of dramatic coastal cliffs rising above the Pacific Ocean. The overlook provides one of the first truly spectacular panoramas encountered along the highway.

2

A short walk leads to one of the most accessible waterfalls along the Pacific Coast Highway. Surrounded by lush vegetation, the falls tumble gracefully through a scenic canyon just steps from the roadway.

3

The largest sandy beach in Big Sur offers expansive shoreline views, rugged cliffs, and outstanding opportunities for beachcombing and photography.

4

Explore redwood groves, historic lime kilns, and beautiful waterfalls within one of Big Sur's most diverse state parks. The combination of coastal scenery and forested canyons makes this a memorable first-day destination.

Day 2

Today focuses on the heart of Big Sur, where iconic coastal landmarks, forested canyons, and some of California's most photographed viewpoints create an unforgettable day along the Pacific Coast Highway.

1

Famous for its purple sand and dramatic sea arch, Pfeiffer Beach is one of the most unique and beautiful shorelines on the California coast. Waves crash against offshore rock formations while the surrounding cliffs glow during sunset.

2

This beloved cultural landmark celebrates the life and work of author Henry Miller while serving as a unique gathering place for artists, writers, travelers, and musicians.

3

Big Sur's largest state park offers broad coastal meadows, river crossings, wildlife viewing, and beautiful oceanfront trails. Its varied landscapes showcase the region's ecological diversity.

4

Home to the world-famous McWay Falls, this park features one of California's most iconic coastal views where a waterfall cascades directly toward a pristine cove along the Pacific Ocean.

Day 3

Continue north through some of Big Sur's most famous landscapes. Dramatic cliffs, engineering marvels, historic lighthouses, and protected coastal reserves define today's exploration.

1

Towering above the Pacific, Soberanes Point offers dramatic views of crashing surf, rocky coves, and rugged coastal terraces that epitomize the beauty of Big Sur.

2

This rugged coastal preserve features panoramic coastal trails, wildflower-covered hillsides, secluded beaches, and breathtaking ocean scenery.

3

One of the most photographed bridges in the world, Bixby Creek Bridge gracefully spans a dramatic coastal canyon and has become the defining image of the Pacific Coast Highway.

4

Perched atop a volcanic rock promontory, Point Sur Lighthouse has guided ships along California's coast for generations. The surrounding historic park offers fascinating maritime history and coastal scenery.

Day 4

Today's itinerary explores the spectacular Monterey Peninsula and one of California's most important marine ecosystems.

1

Frequently described as the crown jewel of California's state park system, Point Lobos features extraordinary coastal scenery, turquoise coves, sea lion colonies, Monterey cypress groves, and some of the finest hiking on the Pacific Coast.

2

Spend time observing sea otters, harbor seals, seabirds, and marine mammals that thrive within the protected waters surrounding Point Lobos and the Monterey Bay region.

Day 5

The final day explores the San Simeon and Cambria region, where historic estates, elephant seal colonies, scenic beaches, and remote headlands provide a spectacular conclusion to the Pacific Coast Highway journey.

1

Perched high above the Pacific, Hearst Castle remains one of America's most extraordinary estates. Built for publishing magnate William Randolph Hearst, the hilltop complex blends European-inspired architecture, world-class art collections, and spectacular coastal views.

2

Located below Hearst Castle, this scenic beach offers beautiful coastal views, protected waters, and excellent opportunities to appreciate the region's maritime setting.

3

This historic lighthouse overlooks one of the most rugged and wildlife-rich sections of the Central California coast.

4

One of the most unique wildlife experiences on the California coast. Hundreds of northern elephant seals gather along the shoreline throughout the year, providing remarkable viewing opportunities.

5

Conclude the journey along one of California's most beautiful stretches of coastline. Moonstone Beach offers dramatic ocean scenery and coastal walks, while nearby Harmony Headlands provides expansive views of rolling coastal hills meeting the Pacific Ocean beneath a magnificent sunset.
