Southeast Utah: Sacred Lands, Desert Monoliths, and Legendary Roads

2 Days

Southeast Utah is where some of the most iconic landscapes of the American West converge. Vast desert valleys, towering sandstone buttes, sacred cultural landscapes, winding scenic roads, and some of North America's most breathtaking overlooks create an unforgettable journey through one of the least populated yet most visually stunning regions of the United States.

This two-day itinerary follows a logical route across the Four Corners region, combining the world-famous monuments of Monument Valley with the scenic backroads of Valley of the Gods, the dramatic overlooks of Goosenecks State Park, the steep switchbacks of Moki Dugway, the cultural significance of Bears Ears National Monument, and the remarkable stone bridges of Natural Bridges National Monument. The experience blends world-famous landmarks with hidden gems while minimizing unnecessary driving.

Day 1

Your journey begins among the legendary landscapes of Navajo Nation, where towering sandstone monuments rise dramatically from the desert floor. Today focuses on the iconic scenery that has defined the American Southwest in films, photography, and popular culture for generations. From the famous Forrest Gump Point to the scenic wonders of Monument Valley and Valley of the Gods, the day showcases some of the most recognizable landscapes on Earth before ending with breathtaking views from Goosenecks State Park.

1

The journey begins at one of the Southwest's most famous roadside viewpoints. This stretch of U.S. Highway 163 became legendary after appearing in the film Forrest Gump, where the vast desert highway points directly toward the towering monuments of Monument Valley. The perspective creates one of the most photographed scenes in the American West and serves as a perfect introduction to the region.

2

Monument Valley is the crown jewel of the Colorado Plateau and one of the most recognizable landscapes in the world. Massive sandstone buttes rise hundreds of feet above the valley floor, their red walls glowing beneath the desert sun. For generations, these sacred lands have been home to the Navajo people and have appeared in countless western films, photographs, and travel documentaries. Driving the scenic route reveals an ever-changing panorama of monoliths, mesas, and desert horizons.

3

Often described as Monument Valley's quieter cousin, Valley of the Gods offers extraordinary scenery with far fewer crowds. A scenic dirt road winds through a landscape of isolated sandstone towers, mesas, and sculpted formations that seem to emerge directly from the desert floor. The solitude and sweeping vistas create an atmosphere of exploration reminiscent of the Southwest before modern tourism.

4

End the day overlooking one of Utah's most astonishing geological spectacles. The San Juan River twists through a series of dramatic horseshoe-shaped bends nearly a thousand feet below the rim. Standing on the edge of the overlook, visitors can witness millions of years of erosion recorded in the exposed rock layers stretching toward the horizon. Sunset transforms the canyon walls into brilliant shades of orange, gold, and crimson.

Day 2

Today explores the rugged heart of southeast Utah, where dramatic mountain roads, sacred landscapes, and ancient geological formations reveal the region's deeper natural and cultural history. From the thrilling ascent of Moki Dugway to the archaeological and scenic treasures of Bears Ears National Monument and Natural Bridges National Monument, the day showcases some of Utah's most remarkable hidden wonders.

1

Few roads in America rival the drama of Moki Dugway. Carved directly into the cliffs of Cedar Mesa, this steep gravel road climbs more than one thousand feet through a series of switchbacks. Each turn reveals increasingly expansive views across Valley of the Gods, Monument Valley, and the distant desert landscape. The route itself is as memorable as the destinations it connects.

2

Bears Ears National Monument protects a vast and culturally significant landscape of mesas, canyons, archaeological sites, and sacred places important to numerous Indigenous tribes. Named for the twin buttes that rise above the surrounding terrain, the monument preserves thousands of years of human history alongside some of the most spectacular scenery on the Colorado Plateau. Traveling through the monument offers a deeper appreciation of the area's natural beauty and cultural heritage.

3

The journey concludes at Natural Bridges National Monument, home to three immense stone bridges sculpted by flowing water over countless centuries. Sipapu, Kachina, and Owachomo Bridges span deep sandstone canyons, forming some of the largest natural bridges on Earth. Scenic overlooks and hiking trails provide extraordinary opportunities to experience these geological masterpieces up close while enjoying the peaceful solitude that defines southeast Utah.
