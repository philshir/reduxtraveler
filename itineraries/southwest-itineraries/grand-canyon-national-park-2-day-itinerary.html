Grand Canyon National Park 2 Day Itinerary

  1. Itineraries
  2. Grand Canyon National Park 2 Day Itinerary

Bright Angel Trail, Grand Canyon South Rim, AZ

Grand Canyon National Park: South Rim Views and a Hike into the Canyon on the Bright Angel Trail

2 Days

Carved by the Colorado River over millions of years, the Grand Canyon stretches 277 miles across northern Arizona, reaching depths of more than a mile and exposing nearly two billion years of Earth's history in its colorful layers of rock. Most visitors only see it from above, but descending below the rim reveals the canyon's true scale. This two-day itinerary spends the first day exploring the South Rim's iconic viewpoints and historic village, then spends the second day hiking the Bright Angel Trail, the park's most popular corridor trail, from Grand Canyon Village down to Havasupai Gardens and Plateau Point and back. Desert View Drive and the Desert View Watchtower, about 25 miles east of the village, are left out, as is hiking all the way to the Colorado River and back in one day, which the National Park Service strongly discourages. Reaching the river and Phantom Ranch is best done as an overnight trip with a backcountry permit or Phantom Ranch reservation.

Day 1

The first day centers on Grand Canyon Village and the Rim Trail, which links the viewpoints with gentle, mostly paved paths. Park once at the visitor center and use the free shuttle buses to get around, since parking in the village fills quickly. A private vehicle pass costs $35 and is valid for 7 days. Keep the day easy to save your energy for tomorrow's hike, and use the evening to pack water, salty snacks, and layers.

1

Begin your morning at the Grand Canyon Visitor Center, where rangers can share trail conditions, shuttle information, and the day's ranger programs, and exhibits introduce the canyon's geology and the many peoples who have called it home. From the visitor center plaza, it is a short, easy walk to Mather Point, the first view of the canyon for many visitors. From its broad, guard-railed overlook, the canyon stretches outward in an endless display of layered rock, deep side canyons, and towering buttes, with small glimpses of the Colorado River far below. Arriving early rewards you with softer light and smaller crowds.

2

Follow the paved Rim Trail west from Mather Point for about 0.7 mile to Yavapai Point, one of the most expansive panoramas on the South Rim. Step inside the historic Yavapai Geology Museum, a stone observation station from the late 1920s, where a floor-to-ceiling window frames the inner canyon and exhibits explain how the canyon formed. From the windows and terrace, look for the Colorado River and the Phantom Ranch area far below. Continue west on the Trail of Time, an interpretive path that marks geologic time along the rim with rock samples from the canyon's layers, as it leads toward Grand Canyon Village.

3

Spend the middle of the day exploring the Grand Canyon Village Historic District, where landmark buildings were designed to blend with the landscape in the National Park Service Rustic style, many by architect Mary Colter. Stroll past the grand El Tovar Hotel, opened in 1905, browse Native American arts and crafts in Hopi House, and visit Kolb Studio and Lookout Studio, both perched right on the edge of the rim. Look for Buckey O'Neill Cabin, from the 1890s, the oldest surviving structure on the rim, and stop for lunch at El Tovar or Bright Angel Lodge.

4

In the afternoon, board the free Hermit Road shuttle at the Hermits Rest Route Transfer, just west of Bright Angel Lodge, since the 8-mile road is open only to shuttles and bicycles from March through December. Hop off at overlooks such as Powell Point and Mohave Point, where sheer cliffs drop thousands of feet and the Colorado River comes into view, then ride to the end of the line at Hermits Rest. Designed by Mary Colter in 1914, this rustic stone rest stop has a massive fireplace and a shaded porch with expansive west-facing views. The quieter western rim is a welcome break from the busy village.

5

End the day at Hopi Point, which projects farther into the canyon than any other viewpoint on Hermit Road and offers one of the widest panoramas on the South Rim. Look down on a long stretch of the Colorado River and across to the distant North Rim as the setting sun turns temples, buttes, and canyon walls red, orange, and gold. It is the most popular sunset spot on the west rim, so arrive early for a place along the railing and bring a warm layer, since temperatures drop quickly after dark. Check the shuttle schedule for the last bus back to the village.

Day 2

The second day is a strenuous hike of about 12.2 miles round trip with roughly 3,200 feet of elevation change, and it usually takes 7 to 10 hours. Unlike most hikes, the hard part comes last, since the climb back to the rim is uphill the entire way. Start at first light, and in summer avoid hiking between 10am and 4pm, when inner-canyon temperatures can be dangerously hot. Carry plenty of water and salty food, check with rangers on current conditions and water availability, and turn around at Three-Mile Resthouse if you are tired or it is hot.

1

Begin at dawn at the Bright Angel Trailhead in Grand Canyon Village, just west of Bright Angel Lodge and beside Kolb Studio, at about 6,860 feet. The trail follows a natural break in the cliffs along the Bright Angel Fault, a route used for centuries by the Havasupai people before it was improved for miners and tourists in the late 1800s. Within the first few minutes, pass through two short rock tunnels and look for ancient pictographs on the cliff wall above the trail as the switchbacks wind down into the canyon.

2

Descend the switchbacks to Mile-and-a-Half Resthouse, about 1.6 miles from the trailhead and more than 1,100 feet below the rim. The stone shelter offers shade, restrooms, and seasonal drinking water, which is usually shut off from about October to April. Stop for a snack and take in the view of the towering cliffs above, which already look dramatically different from below the rim. For a shorter outing, this makes a good turnaround point, about 3 miles round trip.

3

Continue down to Three-Mile Resthouse, about 3.1 miles from the trailhead at roughly 4,750 feet. Like the upper resthouse, it offers shade and seasonal water, and it looks out over the long descent ahead toward the green trees of Havasupai Gardens. This is the recommended turnaround point for many day hikers, especially in summer, when the National Park Service advises against hiking farther in the heat of the day. If you continue, refill your water and make sure you are feeling strong.

4

Below Three-Mile Resthouse, a final set of switchbacks known as Jacob's Ladder leads to Havasupai Gardens, formerly called Indian Garden, a shady oasis of cottonwood trees fed by a spring, about 4.9 miles from the trailhead at around 3,800 feet. The area has a campground, a ranger station, and drinking water, making it a good spot for lunch. From here, follow the mostly flat Plateau Point Trail across the Tonto Platform to Plateau Point, an overlook perched about 1,300 feet directly above the Colorado River, with sweeping views of the inner gorge. Plateau Point is about 6.1 miles from the rim and is the farthest point of the hike.

5

Retrace your steps on the long climb back to the rim, pacing yourself and resting often in the shade at Havasupai Gardens and the two resthouses. Eat and drink regularly, since the ascent typically takes about twice as long as the descent. Once back in Grand Canyon Village, celebrate with dinner at El Tovar or Bright Angel Lodge, then walk a few steps to Lookout Studio, perched on the edge of the rim, to watch the sunset light up the trail you just hiked as it winds down to the canyon floor.
