Virginia Beach & Norfolk: Coastlines, Colonial Heritage, Gardens, and Maritime History

4 Days

Where the Chesapeake Bay meets the Atlantic Ocean, Virginia Beach and Norfolk offer a fascinating blend of natural beauty, colonial history, military heritage, thriving waterfronts, and world-class cultural attractions. This region is where English settlers first arrived before moving inland to establish Jamestown, where majestic ships and naval history define the harbor, and where miles of beaches, maritime forests, and protected wetlands provide exceptional opportunities to experience Virginia's coastal landscapes.

This four-day itinerary is organized geographically to minimize driving while showcasing the area's most significant attractions. The journey begins among Virginia Beach's coastal parks and historic landmarks, continues through its iconic oceanfront district, then crosses into Norfolk's renowned museums, waterfront promenades, and historic churches before concluding among beautiful gardens and riverfront parks. The result is a balanced exploration of one of the Mid-Atlantic's most diverse coastal destinations.

Day 1

Your journey begins where American colonial history and coastal wilderness meet. Today's destinations explore the landscapes encountered by the first English settlers along the Chesapeake Bay. Maritime forests, scenic beaches, nature preserves, and one of the country's oldest lighthouses combine to create an unforgettable introduction to Virginia Beach.

1

Standing near the entrance to the Chesapeake Bay, Cape Henry Lighthouse has guided mariners since the eighteenth century and remains one of Virginia's most iconic landmarks. Visitors who climb to the top are rewarded with panoramic views of the Atlantic Ocean, Chesapeake Bay, and the surrounding coastline. The site marks an important chapter in both maritime and colonial history.

2

First Landing State Park preserves the location where English colonists first came ashore in 1607 before continuing to Jamestown. The park features scenic hiking trails through maritime forests, cypress swamps, dunes, and shoreline habitats. It is both one of Virginia's most historically significant landscapes and one of its most beautiful natural destinations.

3

Nestled along the Chesapeake Bay shoreline, First Landing Beach provides calm waters, soft sands, and spectacular sunset views. The beach offers a peaceful alternative to the bustling oceanfront and allows visitors to appreciate the same coastline encountered by early explorers more than four centuries ago.

4

This protected coastal preserve safeguards wetlands, tidal marshes, beaches, and wildlife habitats along the Lynnhaven River. Nature trails and observation points provide opportunities to view birds and native ecosystems while offering a quieter, less crowded perspective on Virginia Beach's coastal environment.

Day 2

Today focuses on Virginia Beach's cultural and family-friendly attractions. From marine life exhibits and historic homes to the lively Oceanfront Boardwalk, the day blends educational experiences with classic seaside entertainment and ocean views.

1

One of Virginia Beach's premier attractions, the Virginia Aquarium showcases thousands of marine animals and interactive exhibits that explore the ecology of the Chesapeake Bay and Atlantic Ocean. Visitors encounter sharks, sea turtles, rays, and countless aquatic species while learning about marine conservation.

2

The Thoroughgood House is among the oldest surviving colonial homes in Virginia. Preserved as a historic landmark, the property offers insight into seventeenth-century life and the early development of the region. Touring the home provides a tangible link to Virginia's colonial origins.

3

Stretching along the Atlantic shoreline, the Virginia Beach Oceanfront Boardwalk is the heart of the city's tourism district. Shops, restaurants, public art, live entertainment, and ocean views create a vibrant atmosphere that comes alive throughout the day and into the evening. A sunset stroll here is one of the quintessential Virginia Beach experiences.

Day 3

Crossing into downtown Norfolk, today's itinerary explores the city's impressive collection of museums, historic landmarks, and bustling waterfront district. Military history, fine art, religious heritage, and harbor views combine to showcase Norfolk's role as one of the East Coast's most important maritime cities.

1

Located along Norfolk's waterfront, Nauticus is a maritime discovery center featuring hands-on exhibits exploring naval history, ocean science, and maritime technology. Visitors can also tour the historic Battleship Wisconsin, one of the last battleships ever built by the United States Navy.

2

Dedicated to General Douglas MacArthur and the generations of Americans who served in the military, the MacArthur Memorial presents a fascinating look at leadership, military history, and twentieth-century world events. The museum is one of Norfolk's premier historical attractions.

3

The Chrysler Museum of Art houses an outstanding collection spanning thousands of years of artistic achievement. European masterpieces, American paintings, decorative arts, and world-renowned glass collections make it one of the finest art museums in the Mid-Atlantic region.

4

One of the most beautiful religious landmarks in Virginia, this historic basilica is renowned for its architectural elegance, stained-glass windows, and rich history. The church stands as an important cultural and spiritual landmark in downtown Norfolk.

5

Dating to the colonial era, St. Paul's Episcopal Church is the oldest surviving church building in Norfolk. The historic structure has witnessed centuries of American history and remains one of the city's most treasured landmarks.

Day 4

The final day showcases Norfolk's scenic side. Beautiful gardens, waterfront promenades, and peaceful public spaces provide a relaxing conclusion to the trip while highlighting the city's connection to the Elizabeth River and its reputation as one of Virginia's most attractive urban destinations.

1

Widely considered one of the finest botanical gardens in the country, Norfolk Botanical Garden features dozens of themed gardens, scenic waterways, woodland trails, and seasonal blooms. Its expansive grounds offer a tranquil escape and rank among the region's top attractions.

2

Located within Norfolk Botanical Garden, the Miyazaki Japanese Garden celebrates Norfolk's sister-city relationship with Miyazaki, Japan. Stone lanterns, carefully designed landscapes, and peaceful water features create a serene environment inspired by traditional Japanese garden design.

3

Overlooking the Lafayette River, the Hermitage Museum combines fine art, historic architecture, and elegant gardens. The picturesque waterfront estate provides a peaceful setting for appreciating both cultural collections and natural beauty.

4

Tucked along the waterfront, the Pagoda & Oriental Garden provides one of Norfolk's most distinctive and photogenic settings. Traditional Asian-inspired architecture, koi ponds, and landscaped gardens create a peaceful retreat in the heart of downtown.

5

Located on Norfolk's waterfront, Town Point Park serves as the city's premier gathering place for festivals, concerts, and public events. Expansive lawns and harbor views make it a wonderful place to relax while watching activity along the Elizabeth River.

6

End your trip with a stroll along the Waterside Promenade, where restaurants, marina views, naval vessels, and sunset reflections create a memorable finale. The waterfront beautifully captures the maritime character that defines Norfolk and the greater Hampton Roads region.
