Columbia River Gorge: Waterfalls, Clifftop Viewpoints, and Scenic Highway Adventures

5 Days

Carved by ancient floods and framed by towering basalt cliffs, the Columbia River Gorge is one of the Pacific Northwest's most spectacular landscapes. Waterfalls cascade from moss-covered canyon walls, scenic overlooks provide breathtaking river views, and historic roadways wind through some of Oregon and Washington's most dramatic scenery. This five-day itinerary follows the Columbia River Gorge from Troutdale to The Dalles, grouping attractions by proximity to minimize driving while maximizing time spent exploring waterfalls, trails, state parks, and historic landmarks. From the famous Multnomah Falls to hidden cascades, volcanic viewpoints, and dramatic cliffside overlooks, this journey showcases the very best of the Gorge. The Historic Columbia River Highway was America's first designated scenic highway and remains one of the region's greatest engineering and scenic accomplishments.

Day 1

Begin your Columbia River Gorge adventure at the western gateway of the Gorge. Today's route follows the Historic Columbia River Highway through some of Oregon's most famous overlooks and waterfalls. Dramatic river views, historic architecture, and stunning cascades provide an unforgettable introduction to the region.

1

Known as the Gateway to the Gorge, Troutdale serves as the perfect starting point for exploring the Historic Columbia River Highway and the natural wonders beyond.

2

Sitting high above the Columbia River, Crown Point offers one of the most iconic viewpoints in Oregon. Vista House, completed in 1918, was built as an observatory and rest stop along the Historic Columbia River Highway. Sweeping views extend both east and west through the Gorge.

3

Dropping dramatically over a sheer basalt cliff, Latourell Falls is one of the Gorge's most photogenic waterfalls and a perfect introduction to the region's volcanic geology.

4

A short forested walk leads to Bridal Veil Falls, one of the Gorge's most beautiful cascades. Nearby Guy W. Talbot State Park offers picnic areas and access to lush old-growth forest scenery.

5

End the day along the Columbia River shoreline with impressive views of the Gorge's towering basalt formations and broad river vistas.

Day 2

Today explores the heart of Oregon's famous Waterfall Corridor, where some of the Northwest's most celebrated waterfalls can be found within only a few miles of one another. Moss-covered cliffs, dense forests, and rushing water dominate the landscape.

1

The most famous waterfall in Oregon plunges dramatically in two tiers from towering cliffs above the Historic Columbia River Highway. It remains the signature landmark of the Columbia River Gorge.

2

One of the Gorge's most beautiful waterfalls, Wahkeena tumbles through a lush canyon filled with ferns, mosses, and towering evergreens.

3

These lesser-known waterfalls reward hikers with peaceful forest scenery and secluded viewpoints far from the crowds below.

4

Easily accessible from the highway, Horsetail Falls tumbles directly beside the roadway and serves as the gateway to several more hidden waterfalls.

5

Explore one of the Gorge's most unique slot-like canyons, where vertical basalt walls and lush vegetation create a magical Pacific Northwest landscape.

Day 3

Venture deeper into the central Gorge, where waterfall hikes lead through ancient forests and dramatic canyon landscapes. Today features some of the region's best hiking opportunities and hidden natural treasures.

1

Widely regarded as one of the premier hiking trails in the Pacific Northwest, Eagle Creek follows dramatic canyon walls, waterfalls, and lush forest scenery along one of the Gorge's most famous routes.

2

One of the most iconic waterfalls along Eagle Creek, Punch Bowl Falls pours into a spectacular turquoise basin surrounded by lush canyon walls.

3

Twisting through a narrow rock channel, this impressive multi-tiered waterfall is among the highlights of the Eagle Creek trail system.

4

These lesser-visited waterfalls provide outstanding scenery within one of the Gorge's most beautiful forest preserves, named after one of the founders of the Historic Columbia River Highway.

Day 4

Crossing between Oregon and Washington viewpoints, today's route explores dramatic river overlooks, unique geological formations, and some of the most impressive state parks on both sides of the Columbia River.

1

One of the Gorge's most recognizable volcanic formations, Beacon Rock rises dramatically above the river. Switchback trails provide extraordinary panoramic views.

2

This famous bridge links Oregon and Washington while offering dramatic views of the Columbia River and surrounding mountain scenery.

3

Considered one of the Gorge's premier hikes, Dog Mountain rewards hikers with sweeping views of the river corridor and spectacular spring wildflower displays.

4

This cluster of impressive waterfalls showcases the diversity of the Gorge's hydrology, from broad curtain-like cascades to waterfalls emerging directly through rock tunnels.

Day 5

The final day explores the eastern Gorge near The Dalles, where the landscape becomes drier and more open. Sweeping vistas, historical significance, and unique geological scenery provide a memorable conclusion to the journey.

1

One of the most historically significant communities in the Pacific Northwest, The Dalles served as a key trading and transportation hub for Native peoples, explorers, and Oregon Trail emigrants.

2

Expansive views of the Columbia River, rolling grasslands, and ancient cultural landscapes make this one of the most distinctive parks in the eastern Gorge.

3

Hidden gems of the Gorge, these scenic waterfalls offer quieter experiences away from the more heavily visited waterfall corridor.

4

Conclude the trip reflecting on the engineering marvel of the Historic Columbia River Highway while enjoying final river views and a relaxing walk along one of the Gorge's scenic parks. The highway remains one of America's most celebrated scenic roads.
