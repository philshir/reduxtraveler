Glacier National Park: Alpine Lakes, Glaciers, and the Crown of the Continent

5 Days

Carved by ancient ice and framed by towering peaks, Glacier National Park is one of North America's most spectacular mountain landscapes. Known as the Crown of the Continent, the park protects pristine lakes, hanging valleys, active glaciers, alpine meadows, and some of the most dramatic scenery in the Rocky Mountains. This five-day itinerary is organized by region to minimize driving while maximizing time spent among Glacier's most iconic vistas and hiking trails. Inspired by the immersive storytelling of a National Geographic expedition and the practical structure of a Tripadvisor itinerary, the route explores the park's famous Going-to-the-Sun Road, the glacier-carved valleys of Many Glacier, the remote wilderness of the North Fork, and the tranquil waters of Two Medicine, creating a comprehensive Glacier National Park experience.

Day 1

Your journey begins on the western side of Glacier National Park near Lake McDonald. Today's route explores ancient cedar forests, rushing creeks, and some of the park's most accessible but rewarding hikes. The day serves as an introduction to Glacier's remarkable landscapes before ascending into the high alpine environments that await later in the trip.

1

Begin at the largest lake in Glacier National Park. Surrounded by forested mountains and famous for its colorful shoreline stones, Lake McDonald offers a peaceful introduction to the park's grandeur. Early morning reflections often create mirror-like scenes across the water.

2

This accessible boardwalk trail winds through ancient cedar and hemlock forests that feel more like the Pacific Northwest than Montana. Towering trees, moss-covered trunks, and cool mountain air create one of the park's most enchanting environments.

3

Follow the trail beyond Avalanche Gorge into a valley surrounded by towering cliffs and cascading waterfalls. The hike culminates at Avalanche Lake, where waterfalls descend hundreds of feet from hanging valleys into crystal-clear alpine waters surrounded by rugged peaks.

4

End the day along McDonald Creek, where glacial waters rush through polished rock channels beneath dense forests. The creek's emerald waters and scenic viewpoints provide a relaxing conclusion to your first day in Glacier.

Day 2

Today follows the legendary Going-to-the-Sun Road, one of the most spectacular mountain drives in the world. Rising from forested valleys to alpine tundra, the route reveals many of Glacier's most iconic views and provides access to some of the park's signature destinations.

1

This engineering masterpiece traverses the heart of Glacier National Park. Around every corner, dramatic mountain scenery unfolds, including waterfalls, glacial valleys, and towering cliffs that create one of America's most unforgettable road trips.

2

One of Glacier's most photographed waterfalls, Bird Woman Falls plunges from a hanging valley thousands of feet above the roadway. Nearby, The Loop offers exceptional views into the heart of the park's rugged interior.

3

At the highest point on the roadway, Logan Pass introduces visitors to Glacier's alpine tundra ecosystem. The Hidden Lake Trail travels through wildflower meadows frequented by mountain goats and bighorn sheep before revealing one of the park's most celebrated lake vistas.

4

The eastern side of the park features some of Glacier's most iconic scenery. Sun Point overlooks deep-blue St. Mary Lake, while Wild Goose Island stands as one of the most photographed locations in Montana. The combination of mountain peaks and crystal-clear water creates postcard-perfect views.

5

End the day with a hike to two stunning waterfalls. The trail passes through recovering forest landscapes before arriving at powerful cascades framed by red rock cliffs and mountain scenery.

Day 3

Today explores Many Glacier, often described as the most spectacular region of the park. Dramatic peaks surround pristine lakes, glaciers cling to high cirques, and some of Glacier's most famous hiking trails begin here.

1

Begin the day at the historic Many Glacier Hotel, one of the grand lodges of the national park system. Standing beside Swiftcurrent Lake, the hotel offers extraordinary views of glacier-carved peaks reflected in calm waters.

2

This easy lakeside walk provides outstanding opportunities to observe wildlife while introducing visitors to the remarkable geology and natural history of the Many Glacier valley.

3

Glacier's most famous hike passes turquoise lakes, waterfalls, and alpine meadows before reaching the spectacular Grinnell Glacier. Along the journey, visitors encounter some of the most breathtaking scenery found anywhere in the Rocky Mountains.

4

Conclude the afternoon with a moderately easy hike to Redrock Falls, where water flows over colorful iron-rich stone formations. The surrounding meadows frequently provide opportunities to see moose, bear, and other wildlife.

Day 4

This day is dedicated to Glacier's legendary high-country hiking routes. These trails traverse alpine ridges, hanging valleys, and scenic overlooks often considered among the finest hikes in North America.

1

Widely regarded as Glacier's signature hike, the Highline Trail traverses the famous Garden Wall, an elevated ridgeline offering breathtaking views of valleys, glaciers, and distant peaks. Wildlife sightings are common throughout the route.

2

The historic Granite Park Chalet stands high above the valleys below and serves as a memorable destination along the Highline Trail. The remote mountain setting embodies the spirit of Glacier's backcountry.

3

Depending on hiking preference and energy level, the Iceberg Lake Trail offers another world-class alpine destination. Massive cliffs surround a glacier-fed lake where floating icebergs may remain well into summer.

4

Experienced hikers seeking additional challenge may choose routes involving Gunsight Pass or Siyeh Bend. These legendary backcountry crossings reveal spectacular alpine terrain and represent some of Glacier's most ambitious day hikes.

Day 5

The final day explores Glacier's remote regions. Far from the crowds of Going-to-the-Sun Road, these destinations reveal a quieter wilderness where pristine lakes, dense forests, and open valleys showcase the park's untamed character.

1

Begin in the rustic community of Polebridge, a gateway to Glacier's remote North Fork region. Dirt roads, mountain scenery, and a frontier atmosphere create an experience unlike anywhere else in the park.

2

Surrounded by wilderness and fewer visitors than Glacier's more famous lakes, Bowman Lake offers pristine scenery and remarkable reflections of surrounding mountains.

3

One of Glacier's most remote accessible lakes, Kintla Lake rewards travelers with deep wilderness solitude and some of the most pristine landscapes in the national park.

4

Conclude your Glacier adventure in the historic Two Medicine region. Sacred to Indigenous peoples and surrounded by rugged peaks, the lake's tranquil waters and dramatic mountain setting create a fitting finale to five days spent exploring one of the world's great mountain landscapes. Nearby Sacred Dancing Cascade adds one last peaceful stop before farewell.
