Kobuk Valley National Park: Arctic Sand Dunes and the Path of the Caribou

1 Day

About 25 miles north of the Arctic Circle in northwestern Alaska, Kobuk Valley National Park holds one of the great surprises of the Arctic: the Great Kobuk Sand Dunes, about 25 square miles of golden sand dunes up to 100 feet high. Formed from sand ground by ancient glaciers and blown into place by the wind, they are the largest active sand dunes in the Arctic. Each year, hundreds of thousands of caribou from the Western Arctic herd cross the Kobuk River on their migrations, as they have for thousands of years. With no roads, trails, or facilities, Kobuk Valley is one of the least visited national parks in the country, and the best way for an average traveler to experience it is a one-day fly-in trip from Kotzebue. This itinerary includes the Northwest Arctic Heritage Center, a flight over the Kobuk River valley, and a landing near the dunes. Multi-day float trips down the Kobuk River and backpacking in the Baird Mountains are left out, as they require wilderness experience and extensive planning.

Day 1

The day begins in Kotzebue, the hub of Northwest Alaska, which is reached by a flight of about two hours from Anchorage. Air taxis fly from Kotzebue into the park, about 80 miles to the east, and landings depend on weather and river conditions, so plan a flexible schedule and book well in advance. June through early September is the best time to visit, and late August through September brings the caribou migration across the Kobuk River. Bring bug protection, layers, and sturdy shoes for walking on sand and tundra.

1

Start your morning at the Northwest Arctic Heritage Center in Kotzebue, the visitor center for Kobuk Valley National Park and the other Western Arctic National Parklands. Exhibits introduce the region's wildlife and landscapes, the great caribou migrations, and the Iñupiat people who have lived along the Kobuk River for thousands of years and still hunt and fish in the park today. Rangers can share current conditions and help you make the most of your flight.

2

Board a small plane for the flight east over the broad valley of the Kobuk River, with the Baird Mountains to the north and the Waring Mountains to the south. From the air, the river winds through spruce forest and open tundra, and on clear days you may spot caribou, moose, or bears below. Look for Onion Portage, a historic caribou crossing and National Historic Landmark, where archaeologists have found layers of human settlement spanning thousands of years.

3

Land on a gravel bar along the Kobuk River near Kavet Creek and hike across the tundra and forest to the Great Kobuk Sand Dunes. Stepping onto rolling dunes of golden sand above the Arctic Circle is an unforgettable and surreal experience, and temperatures on the sand can be surprisingly warm in summer. Wander freely across the dunes, look for animal tracks in the sand, and take in the views of the surrounding mountains. Stay close to your pilot's schedule, as flights must depart before weather changes.

4

Return to Kotzebue and end the day along the waterfront on Kotzebue Sound, where fishing boats, drying racks, and the wide Arctic sky offer a glimpse of everyday life in one of Alaska's largest Iñupiat communities. In summer, the midnight sun lingers over the water late into the night, a fitting finale to a day spent exploring the Arctic.
